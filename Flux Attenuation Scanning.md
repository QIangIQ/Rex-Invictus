## Description

**Flux Attenuation Scanning (FAS)** is a high-order detection method that identifies objects through **localized deficits and distortions in ambient particle and field flux**, rather than through emitted or reflected signals.

It operates on the premise that all regions of space possess a measurable baseline flux:

- Cosmic particles (muons, hadrons)
    
- Neutrino background streams
    
- Solar and stellar particle winds
    
- Quantum fluctuation fields

Interpretation:

- **Flux deficit** → absorption or physical occlusion
    
- **Angular distortion** → scattering or refraction
    
- **Temporal inconsistency** → moving mass or active interference
    

FAS reconstructs targets via **inverse transport modeling**, producing a volumetric map of the object based on its exclusion signature.

---

## Usage

FAS is deployed as a **strategic-tier detection system** within the [[UOE]].

### Primary Applications

- **Anti-Stealth Detection**  
    Identifies vessels employing full-spectrum cloaking by detecting the absence of expected flux rather than emitted signatures.
    
- **Deep-Space Surveillance**  
    Passive detection across interstellar distances without reliance on signal return.
    
- **Mass and Structure Analysis**  
    Estimates internal density distribution through attenuation gradients.
    
- **Through-Obstruction Scanning**  
    Effective through nebulae, planetary crusts, and artificial interference fields.
    

---

### Operational Process

1. **Baseline Construction**  
    Compute expected flux field based on astrophysical environment.
    
2. **Distributed Sampling**  
    Sensor arrays measure real-time flux density and directionality.
    
3. **Differential Analysis**  
    Identify deviations from expected equilibrium.
    
4. **Inverse Reconstruction**  
    Solve for object geometry and motion from flux discontinuities.
    
5. **Multi-Domain Correlation**  
    Cross-reference across flux layers to eliminate noise and false positives.
    

---

### Strategic Characteristics

- Passive detection; no emission required
    
- Immune to electromagnetic, thermal, and most gravitational stealth
    
- Scales with sensor density and computational capacity
    
- Resolution increases with observation time and flux intensity
    

---

## Countermeasures

UOE cloaking systems achieve near-perfect spectral mimicry, but FAS targets **interaction absence**, not emissions. Countermeasures attempt to restore or obscure flux continuity.

### [[Probabilistic Phase Diffusion]]

**Function:**  
Randomizes interaction of incoming particles to blur attenuation boundaries.

**Effect:**  
Prevents clean geometric reconstruction.

**Limitation:**  
Creates non-natural noise patterns identifiable through multi-layer correlation.

---

### [[Quantum Vacuum Equalization]]

**Function:**  
Suppresses local vacuum fluctuation gradients to minimize detectable perturbations.

**Effect:**  
Reduces secondary detection channels.

**Limitation:**  
Energy-intensive; unstable beyond small volumes or short durations.

---

### [[Gravitational Compensation Lattices]]

**Function:**  
Offsets mass-induced distortions in surrounding fields.

**Effect:**  
Mitigates higher-order detection vectors tied to mass presence.

**Limitation:**  
Precision requirements scale exponentially with object mass.
