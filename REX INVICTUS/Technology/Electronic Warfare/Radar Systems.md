**[[Radar Systems]]** (radio detection and ranging) transmit radio energy and listen for echoes to find targets. Range comes from the echo's time delay, bearing from the antenna's pointing, and radial velocity from the Doppler shift.

## IRL

- **Key parameters** that [[Electronic Intelligence (ELINT)]] measures to identify a radar:
    - Carrier frequency and band (see [[Electromagnetic Spectrum (EMS)]]).
    - [[Pulse Repetition Frequency (PRF)]] and [[Pulse Width]].
    - **Scan pattern**: circular, sector, raster, conical or electronic. A switch from search scan to track is a key threat cue.
    - **Beam width**: narrower beams give finer angle accuracy.
    - Transmit power and modulation type (for example [[Pulse Compression]] chirps).
- **Types.** Early-warning and search radars, [[Fire Control Radar (FCR)|fire-control and missile-guidance radars]], airborne intercept radars, ground-mapping and synthetic-aperture radars.
- **Processing.** [[Doppler Filtering]] separates moving targets from clutter; pulse compression gives range resolution.
- **Countermeasures.** [[Chaff]], jamming ([[Electronic Attack (EA)]]) and stealth shaping.
- **History.** Developed in secret in the 1930s by Britain, Germany, the U.S. and others. Britain's Chain [[Rex Invictus|Home]] stations gave warning in the Battle of Britain (1940), and the cavity magnetron (1940) made compact microwave radar possible.
