The **[[Electromagnetic Spectrum (EMS)]]** is the full range of electromagnetic radiation, ordered by frequency (or wavelength). From low to high frequency: radio, microwave, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays and gamma rays. Frequency *f* and wavelength λ are tied by *c* = *f*λ, and photon energy rises with frequency.

## IRL

- **Radio frequency (RF).** About 3 kHz to 300 GHz. Most radar, communications and [[Electronic Warfare (EW)]] live here.
- **[[Radar Systems|Radar]] bands.** IEEE letter bands: L (1–2 GHz), S (2–4), C (4–8), X (8–12), Ku, K and Ka. Long-range search radars favor lower bands; [[Fire Control Radar (FCR)|fire-control]] and missile seekers favor X band and above for precision.
- **Infrared and visible.** Used by IR seekers, lasers and electro-optical sensors; see [[DIFM-CFT Smoke]] for the vault's IR countermeasure.
- **Ionizing end.** Above ultraviolet, radiation can ionize atoms, which matters for [[Radiation Hardening]] and [[Single Event Effects (SEE)]].
- **Spectrum as terrain.** Doctrine since the 2010s treats the EMS as a maneuver space to be contested, like air or sea.
