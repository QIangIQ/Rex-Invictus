[[Pulse Compression]] is a defining [[Signal Analysis|Technical Characteristic]] of certain [[Radar Systems]] in which a transmitted [[Radar Systems|Radar Pulse]] is encoded using a structured [[Linear Frequency Modulation (LFM)|Modulation Scheme]]—such as [[Linear Frequency Modulation (LFM)]], [[Phase Modulation (PM)]], or [[Phase Modulation (PM)|Binary Phase Shift Keying (BPSK)]]—and subsequently processed through a [[Matched Filter]] or [[Matched Filter|Correlation Receiver]] to produce a compressed output pulse with reduced effective [[Pulse Width|Pulse Duration]], thereby improving [[Range Resolution]] while preserving long-pulse detection performance.

Within [[Electronic Intelligence (ELINT)]], [[Pulse Compression]] is identified through [[Radar Warning Receiver (RWR)|Interception]] and detailed [[Signal Analysis]] of non-communication emissions from [[Radar Systems]].

---

## Detection of [[Pulse Compression]] in [[Electronic Intelligence (ELINT)]]

### During [[Radar Warning Receiver (RWR)|Interception]]

Sensors such as [[Electronic Support (ES)|Electronic Support Measures (ESM)]] or [[Radar Warning Receiver (RWR)]] capture the transmitted waveform.

---

### During [[Signal Analysis]]

[[Pulse Compression]] is revealed through:

- [[Signal Analysis|Frequency Analysis]] → Detection of continuous [[Electromagnetic Spectrum (EMS)|Frequency]] sweep (indicative of [[Linear Frequency Modulation (LFM)]])
    
- [[Signal Analysis|Time Domain Analysis]] → Measurement of extended [[Pulse Width|Pulse Duration]]
    
- [[Signal Analysis|Spectral Analysis]] → Measurement of wide [[Range Resolution|Bandwidth]]
    
- [[Signal Analysis|Pulse Analysis]] → Determination of internal modulation structure
    

These parameters collectively form part of the emitter’s [[Emitter Identification|Signal Fingerprinting]] profile.

---

## Role in [[Emitter Identification]]

Because [[Pulse Compression]] waveforms possess distinctive characteristics:

- Specific [[Range Resolution|Bandwidth]]
    
- Specific [[Range Resolution|Chirp Rate]]
    
- Specific phase coding patterns
    
- Specific [[Range Resolution|Compression Ratio]]
    

They enable:

- [[Emitter Identification]]
    
- Classification of [[Radar Systems|Radar System]] type
    
- Determination of Operational Capabilities
    

These characteristics are stored in [[Emitter Identification|Emitter Libraries]] and contribute to the construction of the [[Electronic Order of Battle (EOB)]].

---

## Intelligence Value of [[Pulse Compression]]

From an [[Electronic Intelligence (ELINT)]] perspective, detection of [[Pulse Compression]] implies:

- Advanced signal processing capability
    
- Enhanced [[Range Resolution]]
    
- Increased resistance to [[Electronic Countermeasures (ECM)]]
    
- Modernized [[Radar Systems|Radar System]] architecture
    

Thus, [[Pulse Compression]] is not merely a signal processing method, but an exploitable indicator of a radar’s [[Signal Analysis|Technical Characteristics]] and Operational Capabilities.

## Doctrinal ELINT-Aligned Definition

[[Pulse Compression]] is a structured [[Matched Filter|Radar Signal Processing Technique]] observable through [[Electronic Intelligence (ELINT)]] that involves the transmission of a long, internally modulated [[Radar Systems|Radar Pulse]]—typically using [[Linear Frequency Modulation (LFM)]] or [[Phase Modulation (PM)]]—followed by receiver-side matched processing to achieve reduced effective [[Pulse Width|Pulse Duration]]. Its measurable parameters, including [[Range Resolution|Bandwidth]], [[Range Resolution|Chirp Rate]], and internal coding structure, constitute identifiable [[Signal Analysis|Technical Characteristics]] used in [[Emitter Identification|Signal Fingerprinting]], [[Emitter Identification]], and assessment of [[Radar Systems|Radar System]] Operational Capabilities.
