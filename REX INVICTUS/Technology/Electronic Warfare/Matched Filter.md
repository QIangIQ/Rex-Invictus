A **[[Matched Filter]]** is the linear filter that maximizes signal-to-noise ratio for a known waveform in white noise. Its impulse response is the time-reversed complex conjugate of the transmitted signal, so its output is the correlation of the echo with a replica. A correlation receiver performs the same operation directly.

In [[Pulse Compression]] the matched filter collapses a long coded pulse ([[Linear Frequency Modulation (LFM)]] or [[Phase Modulation (PM)]]) into a narrow peak, which yields the fine [[Range Resolution]] of a short pulse with the energy of a long one. Dwight North described it in 1943.
