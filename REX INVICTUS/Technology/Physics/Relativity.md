**[[Relativity]]** covers Albert Einstein's two theories of space, time and gravity.

---

## Special Relativity

Published in 1905. The laws of physics are the same for all observers moving at constant velocity, and light in vacuum always travels at c, about 299,792 km/s. It follows that moving clocks run slow (time dilation), moving objects shorten along their motion, simultaneity depends on the observer, and mass and energy are equivalent (E = mc²). No signal can travel faster than light, which is why faster-than-light ideas such as [[Hadrian Tachyonics|tachyons]] and the [[Tachyon Bridge Network]] sit outside it.

## General Relativity

Published in 1915. Gravity is the curvature of spacetime by mass and energy. It predicts the bending of light, gravitational time dilation (clocks run slower deeper in a gravity well, a correction GPS satellites must apply), gravitational waves (detected in 2015) and the [[Black Hole]].

## Relativistic Delay

The practical lag created by both effects: signals limited to light speed take hours to cross a star system and years between stars, and ships moving near c age slower than those at home. Any interstellar polity like the [[UOE]] must route around it.
