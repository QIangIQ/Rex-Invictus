**[[Ionizing Radiation]]** carries enough energy to knock electrons from atoms. It includes particle radiation (alpha particles, protons, neutrons, heavy ions) and high-energy photons (X-rays and gamma rays from the top of the [[Electromagnetic Spectrum (EMS)]]).

## Sources
Space (trapped belts, solar particle events and galactic cosmic rays, which affect [[Low Earth Orbit]] and beyond), nuclear reactors, particle accelerators, nuclear accidents and nuclear weapons.

## Effects on electronics
- **Total ionizing dose (TID).** Accumulated charge trapped in oxides shifts transistor thresholds and raises leakage until a part fails. Measured in rad(Si).
- **ELDRS.** Enhanced low dose rate sensitivity: some bipolar parts degrade more under slow, space-like dose rates than in fast ground tests.
- **Displacement damage.** Neutrons and protons knock atoms out of the silicon lattice, hurting solar cells and optoelectronics.
- **Single events.** One particle strike can flip or destroy a device; see [[Single Event Effects (SEE)]].
