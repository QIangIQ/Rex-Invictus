A **[[Single-Event Upset (SEU)]]**, also referred to as a **[[Single Event Effects (SEE)|Single-Event Error (SEE)]]**, is a transient change of state in a microelectronic system caused by a single incident ionizing particle.

---
### Definition

A [[Single-Event Upset (SEU)]] occurs when:

- a **single ionizing particle** (e.g., [[Ionizing Radiation|ion]]s, [[Ionizing Radiation|electron]]s, [[Ionizing Radiation|photon]]s)
- strikes a **sensitive node** within an active microelectronic device

Examples of affected systems include:

- [[Semiconductor Devices|microprocessor]]s
- [[Semiconductor Devices|semiconductor memory]]
- [[Semiconductor Devices|power transistor]]s

---

### Mechanism

The upset is produced by:

- **ionization-induced charge deposition** near or within a critical node
- generation of **free charge carriers**
- disruption of the stored logical state

This typically affects:

- a **[[Semiconductor Devices|logic element]]**
- a **[[Semiconductor Devices|memory bit]]**

Resulting in:

- unintended **bit flips**
- temporary corruption of computational state

---

### Effects

The observable consequence is:

- an **output or operational error** in the affected device

This error is classified as:

- a [[Destructive Single-Event Effects|soft error]]

---

### Characteristics

- **Non-destructive** to hardware
- Does **not permanently damage** the device
- System functionality is recoverable after state correction

---

### Related [[Single Event Effects (SEE)|Single-Event Effects (SEE)]]

[[Single-Event Upset (SEU)]] is part of the broader class:

- [[Single Event Effects (SEE)|Single-Event Effects (SEE)]]

Other, **destructive** SEE variants include:

- [[Destructive Single-Event Effects|Single-Event Latch-up (SEL)]]
- [[Destructive Single-Event Effects|Single-Event Gate Rupture (SEGR)]]
- [[Destructive Single-Event Effects|Single-Event Burnout (SEB)]]
