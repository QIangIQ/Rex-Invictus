**[[Armor-Piercing Composite Rigid (APCR)]]** shot (U.S. term: HVAP, high-velocity armor-piercing) puts a dense tungsten-carbide core inside a light full-caliber body, usually aluminium. The low overall weight gives high muzzle velocity, and the core does the penetrating. It saw wide use in the Second World War, such as the German Pzgr. 40 and the U.S. 76 mm HVAP. Because the light body loses velocity quickly, it was soon replaced by discarding-sabot designs: first [[Armor-Piercing Fin-Stabilized Discarding Sabot (APFSDS)|APDS]], then [[Armor-Piercing Fin-Stabilized Discarding Sabot (APFSDS)]].
