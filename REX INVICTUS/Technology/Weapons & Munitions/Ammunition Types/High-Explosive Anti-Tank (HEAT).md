**[[High-Explosive Anti-Tank (HEAT)]]** warheads use a shaped charge: explosive packed behind a cone-shaped metal liner, usually copper. On detonation the liner collapses into a jet moving several kilometres per second, which pushes through armor by sheer pressure. Penetration does not depend on velocity, so HEAT suits slow rockets, missiles and grenades such as the RPG-7, the Panzerfaust and the Javelin's tandem warhead. Explosive [[Reactive Armor]], spaced armor and slat cages are designed to disrupt the jet. The principle is called the Munroe or Neumann effect.
