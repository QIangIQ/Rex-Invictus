**[[Flak]]** is anti-aircraft artillery, from the German *Flugabwehrkanone* ("aircraft-defense cannon"). In the Second World War guns like the German 8.8 cm Flak fired time-fuzed shells to burst near bombers; the U.S. proximity fuze (1943) later made bursts far more lethal. The word now means AA fire in general, and modern descendants are radar-directed autocannons and close-in weapon systems used for [[Point-Defence]].
