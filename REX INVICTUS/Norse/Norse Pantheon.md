The Norse Pantheon, also known as the Nordic Pantheon or Asgardian Pantheon, was one of the major Pantheons of [[Yggdrasil]] and one of the original great Divine powers of the planet. Its cosmological system encompassed the [[Nine Realms]], with [[Asgard]] serving as the principal Heavenly Realm under the rule of [[Odin]] while Helheim served as its primary [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] under Hela. The Pantheon contained both the Æsir and Vanir Gods alongside numerous supernatural civilizations, including Dwarves, Elves, Giants, Valkyries and other inhabitants distributed throughout its connected Realms.
For most of its history the Pantheon was ruled by Odin, one of Earth's original seven major [[God-Kings]] and a powerful Second Generation God associated with War, Death, Knowledge, Healing and Runes.[1] His regime possessed one of Earth's largest military systems, drawing not only upon the living Gods and supernatural inhabitants of the Nine Realms but also upon Valkyries, warriors accepted into Valhalla and vast numbers of dead warriors whose Souls had been converted into Divine military puppets.[2]
The Pantheon's internal stability was ultimately destroyed by Odin's treatment of [[Loki]]'s children and his attempts to prevent the prophecy of Ragnarok. In particular, Hela was confined to Helheim, [[Fenrir]] and [[Jormungandr]] became enemies of Odin, and the ancient Dragon of the End Nidhoggr remained imprisoned near the roots of Yggdrasil. Hela spent thousands of years preparing retaliation against Odin and eventually united several of the forces associated with the predicted Ragnarok against Asgard.[3][4]
The resulting Ragnarok ended the Norse Pantheon as an independent Divine civilization. Hela defeated Odin, captured most of his surviving family and Gods, while [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction secretly evacuated many of the other supernatural races and valuable inhabitants from Norse territory during the conflict.[5][6] After discovering that she had conquered a largely empty dimensional territory and having no desire to administer another Pantheon, Hela sold the Key of the Norse Pantheon to [[Shiva]].[6][7]
The sale transferred the territorial and dimensional ownership of the Norse Pantheon to Shiva, not Hela's captured Gods or the treasures she had already removed. Shiva consequently acquired the surviving Norse territorial system for the Hindu Pantheon, greatly expanding the latter's Divine Authority.[8][7] When the Hindu Pantheon was later annexed by the Elderblood Empire, the former Norse territories indirectly became Imperial territory as well.[9]
The Norse Pantheon therefore no longer exists as an independent political Pantheon. Its former Divine Realms remain cosmologically important, but its original Gods, populations, resources and sovereignty were divided among Hela, Victor's faction and the Hindu Pantheon following Ragnarok.

### Cosmological Structure

#### The Nine Realms
The Norse Pantheon was not a single conventional territory but a complex system traditionally referred to as the [[Nine Realms]]. These Realms were connected through the broader World Tree system associated with [[Yggdrasil]] and could be accessed through specialized paths, portals and the [[Bifrost]].
[[Ratatosk]] was noted as one of the few Beings capable of travelling throughout the Nine Realms using hidden routes connected to the World Tree, demonstrating that the different Norse territories were interconnected parts of a much larger dimensional system.[10]
The most important confirmed Norse territories include:
- [[Asgard]] — The principal Heavenly Realm and political center of [[Odin]]'s government.
- Helheim — The Norse [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] ruled by Hela.
- Valhalla — A Divine afterlife reserved primarily for worthy warriors and one of Odin's principal sources of military personnel.
- Fólkvangr — [[Freyja|Freya]]'s personal Celestial Domain and another destination for worthy dead warriors.[3]
- The Roots of Yggdrasil — A highly unusual dimensional region where Nidhoggr had been imprisoned and continuously interacted with the roots of the World Tree.[11][12]
Other Realms belonging to the traditional Nine Realms system contained races such as Giants, Dwarves, Elves and other supernatural civilizations, although the novel does not consistently detail every Realm individually.

#### World Tree System
The Norse Pantheon is closely associated with [[Yggdrasil]], but Yggdrasil should not be treated as exclusively belonging to the Norse Gods.
Yggdrasil is the Positive World Tree of Earth itself. Her influence extends through the planet's Divine systems rather than existing solely for [[Asgard]]. The Norse Pantheon nevertheless possessed a particularly close structural connection to her, with the [[Nine Realms]], [[Bifrost]] routes and several native supernatural species interacting directly with her branches and roots.
The Negative World Tree Qiliphoth likewise supports Earth's Negative-aligned dimensional systems and Underworlds. The existence of both [[World Trees]] therefore contributes to the wider cosmological structure that allowed Earth's Heavenly and Infernal Realms to exist.
This distinction became especially important during Ragnarok. Damage surrounding Nidhoggr and Yggdrasil's roots nearly destabilized the dimensional structure to the point that outside cosmic authorities had to intervene.[12][13]

### Asgard
[[Asgard]] was the political and military heart of the Norse Pantheon and the seat of [[Odin]].
From Asgard, Odin commanded the Æsir Gods, Divine military forces, Valkyries and the Pantheon's wider network of supernatural Realms. It possessed enough independence that Odin could completely seal the Pantheon's dimensional borders during Ragnarok in an attempt to prevent outside powers from interfering or exploiting the civil war.[2]
When the final conflict became unavoidable, Odin personally ordered the Pantheon sealed and refused [[Loki]]'s suggestion that they ask foreign powers for assistance. Odin regarded Ragnarok as an internal Asgardian matter and correctly understood that rival Pantheons would attempt to exploit any visible weakness.[2]
[[Freyja|Freya]] consequently stationed her Valkyries around the [[Bifrost]] entrance while Odin's armies prepared for Hela's assault.[2]
After Hela's victory, Asgard ceased to function as an independent Divine capital. Hela temporarily controlled the Realm before selling the territorial Key to [[Shiva]].[6][7]

### Nordic Hell
Helheim was the principal [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] of the Norse Pantheon and was ruled by Hela.
It was an enormous frozen and desolate Realm inhabited by Souls who were not accepted into Valhalla or [[Freyja|Freya]]'s Fólkvangr. These Souls were instead sent into Helheim, where the environment was defined by cold, darkness and supernatural creatures.[3]
[[Odin]]'s decision to imprison Hela within her own Underworld became one of the greatest strategic mistakes of his reign. Rather than permanently weakening her, confinement inside the Realm associated with her own Divine authority gave Hela thousands of years to accumulate strength, control Souls and prepare her eventual revenge.[4]
By the time of Ragnarok, Hela's familiarity with Death and the Underworld allowed her to mobilize enormous numbers of historical dead warriors who had made agreements with her before their deaths.
Helheim also remained cosmologically important after the destruction of Odin's government. Hela could not simply abandon the Norse system because it still participated in the death-and-rebirth cycle of Earth's Sector. Following the collapse of multiple other Pantheons, the surviving Asgardian/Norse afterlife had become one of only three principal Hell systems still operating in the Sector.[6]

### Valhalla and Fólkvangr
The Norse Pantheon's afterlife did not send every warrior to the same destination.
Valhalla received warriors judged worthy of joining [[Odin]]'s Divine forces, while Fólkvangr served as [[Freyja|Freya]]'s separate Celestial Domain for another portion of worthy dead warriors.[3]
Warriors who failed to qualify for either destination could instead be sent to Helheim or repurposed through other portions of the Pantheon's Soul system.
Odin exploited this structure militarily. Numerous dead warriors who were considered insufficient for Valhalla but still powerful enough to be useful were placed into artificial black armor and transformed into Soul-containing puppets for [[Asgard]]'s army.[2]

### Government

#### Odin's Rule
For most of the Pantheon's known history, the supreme ruler was [[Odin]].
Odin was widely recognized as a genuine God-King rather than merely a ceremonial monarch. He was intelligent, paranoid, highly experienced in warfare and extremely thorough when preparing for possible threats.[2]
His principal court included major Gods such as [[Thor]], [[Loki]], [[Freyja|Freya]], [[Heimdall]] and other members of the Æsir and Vanir.
Odin's authority was nevertheless heavily affected by his obsession with prophecy. His attempts to prevent Ragnarok led him to persecute or imprison several of the very Beings prophesied to cause his downfall. Hela later mocked the irony that Odin's attempt to avoid Ragnarok directly created the circumstances necessary for the prophecy to occur.[5]

#### Heaven and Underworld Division
The Norse Divine system could broadly be divided between its Positive-aligned Heavenly Gods and the Negative-aligned Beings associated with Helheim and Ragnarok.
This division should not be interpreted as a simple moral distinction. Positive and Negative describe cosmological and conceptual alignment rather than whether a God is morally good or evil.
A simplified structure before Ragnarok was:

### Military Forces
The Norse Pantheon possessed one of Earth's largest Divine military organizations before Ragnarok.

#### Odin's Army
[[Odin]]'s conventional military consisted of Gods, Valkyries, Valhalla warriors and other supernatural soldiers distributed throughout the [[Nine Realms]].
One particularly unusual component consisted of dead warriors converted into black-armored Soul puppets. These were warriors who had not been considered worthy enough to enter Valhalla but whose Souls were still powerful enough to be useful.[2]
Observers examining Odin's military could count more than 100,000 of these black-armored warriors alone. This number did not include the warriors residing in Valhalla, meaning Odin's complete military was significantly larger.[2]
The Pantheon's army additionally included:
- Major and Lesser Norse Gods;
- Demigods;
- Valkyries;
- Valhalla warriors;
- Soul-puppet soldiers;
- Divine constructs and equipment;
- and forces belonging to the supernatural races of the Nine Realms.
Odin had prepared these forces for years because he anticipated the eventual arrival of Ragnarok.[2]

#### Hela's Army
Hela's military differed completely from [[Odin]]'s conventional Divine forces.
As Goddess of Death and the ruler of Helheim, Hela spent thousands of years making agreements with exceptionally capable historical warriors before their deaths. When Ragnarok finally began, she summoned these individuals and armies back into battle.[14][15]
Her confirmed forces included:
- 30,000 Spartans led by [[Leonidas I]]. Leonidas originally commanded only 300 legendary warriors, but Hela's power and preparations expanded the force to approximately thirty thousand soldiers for Ragnarok.[14]
- More than 20,000 Wallachian warriors led by [[Vlad III Tepes]].[14]
- More than 700,000 Roman soldiers led by [[Julius Caesar]].[14]
- Various Souls and dead warriors accumulated throughout Hela's long existence.
- Creatures acquired through Helheim and her connections to Death.
- [[Creatures of the Abyss]] deployed during the war.
- [[Fenrir]], [[Jormungandr]] and Nidhoggr, three extraordinarily dangerous Beings associated with Ragnarok.[14]
Hela empowered the mortal armies she summoned with Divine Power and supernatural armor, allowing historical Human soldiers to participate in warfare against Gods.[14]

### Beasts of Ragnarok
The destruction of [[Odin]]'s Pantheon was heavily connected to several powerful Beings traditionally associated with Ragnarok.

#### Fenrir
[[Fenrir]] was one of [[Loki]] and [[Angrboða]]'s children and a Being associated with Destruction and END.
[[Odin]]'s earlier treatment of Fenrir made the Wolf fundamentally hostile toward [[Asgard]]. When Hela initiated Ragnarok, Fenrir abandoned his other responsibilities and returned to assist his sister.
During the war, Fenrir confronted members of Odin's family, including [[Balder]] and [[Vidar]].[14]

#### Jormungandr
[[Jormungandr]] was another of [[Loki]] and [[Angrboða]]'s children and one of the principal enemies predicted to participate in Ragnarok.
He entered the conflict directly against [[Thor]], reproducing the ancient hostility between the God of Thunder and the World Serpent.[14]

#### Nidhoggr
Nidhoggr was an ancient True Dragon possessing the authority of END and one of the most dangerous Beings associated with the Norse cosmological system.
He had been confined within a separate region surrounding the roots of [[Yggdrasil]], where he remained isolated for an immense period of time.
Hela carefully manipulated events to acquire favors from Nidhoggr. She sacrificed part of her own vitality to summon him and later delivered [[Ratatosk]]—a Being Nidhoggr deeply hated because of countless years of provocation—to obtain an additional favor.[11]
One favor was used to damage or destroy the [[Bifrost]], while another was preserved for the coming conflict against [[Odin]].[11]
Nidhoggr's removal from his prison caused an unexpected cosmological crisis. The unstable mixture of END Energy and the energies surrounding Yggdrasil's roots nearly caused the dimensional region to collapse into something capable of producing a new Primordial existence.[12][13]

### Ragnarok

#### Preparation
The final Ragnarok was not a spontaneous invasion.
Both sides had spent years—or in Hela's case, millennia—preparing.
[[Odin]] anticipated the possibility of Ragnarok and created enormous armies, secured the [[Bifrost]] and prepared [[Asgard]]'s defenses. His paranoia and experience as a God-King made the Pantheon far more prepared than most factions would have been under similar circumstances.[2]
Hela's preparation was substantially longer. Her imprisonment in Helheim allowed her to accumulate resources, Souls, agreements and allies across thousands of years.[4]
Her objective was not simply to conquer territory. Hela primarily wanted revenge against Odin and the family members who had persecuted herself and [[Loki]]'s children.

#### Sealing of the Pantheon
Once Ragnarok became imminent, [[Loki]] suggested seeking assistance from outside factions.
[[Odin]] refused.
He declared the war an internal Asgardian matter and ordered the complete closure of the Norse dimensional system. [[Freyja|Freya]] placed her Valkyries around the [[Bifrost]] entrance, preventing ordinary entry and exit.[2]
Odin's reasoning was strategically sound: weakened Pantheons were routinely exploited by rivals, and the supernatural world had already demonstrated how rapidly outside factions would take advantage of civil wars.
However, the isolation also meant [[Asgard]] would receive no meaningful foreign assistance once Hela revealed the full scale of her preparations.

#### Hela's Assault
Hela entered the final battle alongside [[Fenrir]], [[Jormungandr]] and Nidhoggr while also releasing her accumulated armies of the Dead.[14]
[[Thor]] fought Jormungandr, while [[Balder]] and [[Vidar]] engaged Fenrir. [[Odin]]'s forces initially attempted to contain the invading armies, but Hela continuously introduced additional assets.
The arrival of the 30,000 Spartans, Wallachian army and more than 700,000 Roman soldiers revealed that the forces Hela had displayed earlier represented only part of her preparations.[14]
Hela personally entered combat shortly afterward.
Against Odin's expectations, she had not spent her millennia solely planning from behind the scenes. She had also continuously increased her own combat ability. When Odin attempted to attack her forces directly, Hela intercepted and physically struck him away before manifesting her combat armor and weapon.[16]

#### Collapse of Odin's Forces
The battle rapidly deteriorated for [[Odin]].
Hela's personal power, resurrected armies and Ragnarok Beasts placed enormous pressure on [[Asgard]]'s forces. Her Darkness was capable of erasing warriors and the surrounding environment, while Nidhoggr and the other Beasts represented threats requiring entire groups of Gods to oppose.[16]
Odin eventually activated increasingly desperate countermeasures.
This caused the loyalty of several members of his faction to collapse. Gods who had originally sided with Odin began reconsidering whether following him would result only in pointless destruction.
[[Freyja|Freya]] and her Valkyries eventually abandoned Odin after his actions crossed the limits they were willing to accept.[5]
The civil war therefore became not merely a conflict between Odin and Hela but the complete political disintegration of Odin's government.

#### Defeat of Odin
[[Odin]] was ultimately defeated and mutilated.
By the conclusion of the battle, he had lost his arms and legs, while his legendary spear Gungnir had been broken. Despite these injuries, his nature as a powerful God-King prevented him from immediately dying.[5]
Hela openly mocked Odin for allowing his arrogance and obsession with prophecy to cause the exact future he had spent thousands of years trying to prevent.
Rather than immediately killing him, she captured Odin along with the surviving members of his family so that she could continue taking revenge upon them.[5]
The old Asgardian government effectively ended at this moment.

### Fall of the Norse Pantheon
Hela emerged as the undisputed victor of Ragnarok.
She defeated [[Odin]] and captured his children, other Gods and even members of the old family associated with [[Asgard]].[6]
Her victory was nevertheless far less complete than she initially believed.
While Hela had concentrated on destroying Odin's government, [[Victor Elderblood]]'s faction had secretly infiltrated the Norse dimensional system. Using abilities capable of evading even extremely powerful supernatural senses, Victor's forces removed many of the Pantheon's other inhabitants and resources without Hela noticing.[6]
When Hela attempted to begin subjugating the remaining races, she discovered that their settlements had become almost entirely empty.
The various populations that had once occupied these territories had disappeared.[6]
The operation included valuable Norse civilizations such as the Dwarves and Elves, whose skills and populations were subsequently incorporated into territories controlled by Victor's faction.[6][17]
Hela was consequently left controlling:
- the dimensional territory itself;
- captured Norse Gods;
- slaves and prisoners taken during Ragnarok;
- treasures and resources she had already secured;
- but few of the independent races she had originally intended to conquer.[6]
This transformed her triumph into what she personally considered a bitter victory.

### Cosmological Crisis
The destruction of the Norse Pantheon carried consequences extending beyond Asgardian politics.
During the events surrounding Nidhoggr and the World Tree's roots, the Norse dimensional system nearly destabilized completely. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] realized that destruction of the Realm at that particular point in Earth's history would have had consequences across the entire Sector.[13]
In earlier ages Earth possessed a much larger network of functioning Heavens and Hells. By the period of Ragnarok, however, only three major Heaven/Hell systems remained sufficiently important to maintain the planet's larger Soul cycle.[13][6]
Destroying the Norse system entirely could therefore have interfered with:
- Soul processing;
- reincarnation;
- the balance between Positive and Negative afterlife Realms;
- and potentially the wider death-and-rebirth cycle of the Sector.[13][6]
This was the principal reason Hela could not simply abandon the Pantheon after achieving her revenge.
Even if she did not care about governing [[Asgard]], someone still needed to maintain the dimensional territory and its function within the cosmic system.

### Hela's Temporary Rule
Following Ragnarok, Hela became the effective owner of the Norse Pantheon.
She had no genuine interest in becoming a conventional God-Queen. Her objective had always been revenge rather than administration.[6]
Hela nevertheless maintained the Pantheon temporarily because simply leaving it abandoned could attract intervention from the cosmic authorities responsible for maintaining the balance of Souls and reincarnation.[6]
She therefore continued operating the system while determining how to dispose of it.
Two months after Ragnarok, Hela publicly revealed the destruction of [[Asgard]] to the wider supernatural world.
The announcement shocked Earth's Gods. In less than a decade, three ancient Pantheons—the Greek, Egyptian and Norse Pantheons—had effectively ceased to exist as independent powers.[6]
Hela demonstrated her victory by appearing with captured Asgardian Gods. [[Thor]], formerly one of the greatest warriors of the Norse Pantheon, was displayed as one of her conquered subordinates.[6]
At that point, the political reality became undeniable:
Asgard as an independent Pantheon was gone.

### Sale of the Norse Pantheon
Hela eventually decided that the most profitable solution was to sell the dimensional territory.
She announced that the Key to the Norse Pantheon would be exchanged for whichever Divine artifacts, materials or resources interested her most.[6]
An important distinction existed between what Hela was selling and what she retained.
Hela had already removed and secured:
- the Pantheon's valuable treasures;
- resources she personally wanted;
- captured Gods;
- and other spoils of Ragnarok.
What she offered for sale was primarily the territorial dimensional system itself and the Key granting control over it.[6][8]
Hela explicitly reasoned that a Pantheon was defined by its Gods rather than by empty land. Selling the dimensions therefore allowed her to discard the burden of administration without surrendering the people and possessions she considered her personal property.[6]

### Acquisition by Shiva
The sale drew interest from several surviving [[God-Kings]].
Additional Pantheon territory had enormous long-term value because possessing more Divine territory increased Authority and provided room for future subordinate Gods and civilizations, particularly as Earth's Sector approached a higher stage of development.[18]
[[Shiva]] ultimately became the principal buyer.
Following negotiations, Hela produced the Key to the Norse Pantheon and Shiva signed a formal Contract for its purchase. Shiva transferred three highly valuable Essences to Hela in exchange for ownership of the Key.[19][7]
Once the Contract was completed, the territorial ownership of the former Norse Pantheon passed to Shiva.
The transaction represented one of the most significant territorial expansions of the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]]. After acquiring the former Norse territories, the Hindu Pantheon later possessed the second-greatest Pantheon Authority on Earth after [[Dragons Nest Pantheon]].[20]

### Imperial Era
[[Shiva]]'s acquisition did not restore the Norse Pantheon as an independent faction.
The old Norse Gods remained scattered, captured, dead or integrated into other powers, while Shiva primarily possessed the territory and dimensional Key.
When the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]] subsequently surrendered to [[Victor Elderblood]] and was annexed into the [[Elderblood Empire]], all of its Gods, resources, history and territories became Imperial possessions.[9]
Because Shiva's holdings included the former Norse Pantheon territory, those Realms became indirectly incorporated into the Elderblood Empire as part of the Hindu Pantheon's possessions.

### Associated Species
The Norse Pantheon historically governed or contained numerous non-Divine supernatural civilizations.
These included:
- Dwarves — Exceptional supernatural craftsmen whose expertise made them highly valuable throughout the supernatural world.
- Elves — One of the major supernatural populations inhabiting Norse territory.
- Dark Elves
- Fire Giants
- Ice Giants
- Fairies
- Valkyries
- various spirits and other native supernatural species.
Most of these populations ceased being subjects of the Norse Pantheon after Ragnarok.
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction secretly evacuated multiple races while Hela was occupied with the civil war, leaving her with little more than conquered Gods, prisoners and largely empty territories.[6]
By Chapter 973, the population transferred into Victor's territories had contributed to a civilization containing elements from several former mythological systems, including Norse races.[17]

### Political Legacy
The destruction of the Norse Pantheon marked a major turning point in Earth's supernatural political order.
[[Asgard]] had existed for thousands of years as one of the planet's greatest Divine powers. Its sudden collapse demonstrated that even the original major Pantheons could no longer assume their institutions were permanent.
The timing was particularly significant because the Norse Pantheon fell shortly after the Greek and Egyptian Pantheons.
Within less than a decade:
- the [[Greek Pantheon]] had collapsed;
- the [[Egyptian Pantheon]] had fallen under [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s control;
- and the Norse Pantheon had been conquered by Hela.[6]
This caused widespread concern among the surviving [[God-Kings]], who realized that Earth's ancient balance of power was disappearing.
The sale of Asgard to [[Shiva]] further demonstrated a new political reality: complete Pantheon territories could now be treated as transferable strategic resources rather than permanent sovereign states.
That transition ultimately foreshadowed Victor's later unification of Earth beneath the [[Elderblood Empire]].

### Trivia
- The Norse Pantheon was one of Earth's original major Pantheons and was ruled by [[Odin]], one of the original seven recognized major [[God-Kings]].[1]
- The Pantheon consisted of more than [[Asgard]] alone and was structured around the [[Nine Realms]].
- [[Ratatosk]] knew hidden routes throughout the Nine Realms and pathways associated with [[Yggdrasil]].[10]
- Yggdrasil is strongly associated with Norse cosmology but is actually Earth's World Tree rather than the personal World Tree of the Norse Pantheon alone.
- Odin possessed more than 100,000 black-armored Soul-puppet warriors before even counting his warriors in Valhalla.[2]
- These puppets were created from warriors who were not considered worthy of Valhalla but were still strong enough to be useful military assets.[2]
- Hela's Ragnarok forces included approximately 30,000 Spartans, more than 20,000 Wallachian warriors and more than 700,000 Roman soldiers.[14]
- The previous article's figure of 70,000 Wallachian soldiers is not supported by the Chapter 958 text; the chapter describes a force exceeding 20,000.
- Odin deliberately sealed the entire Norse dimensional system when Ragnarok began because he feared foreign Pantheons exploiting the civil war.[2]
- [[Freyja|Freya]]'s Valkyries were tasked with securing the [[Bifrost]] entrance.[2]
- Hela had prepared for Ragnarok for thousands of years.[4][14]
- Odin's attempt to avoid the Ragnarok prophecy ultimately helped create the conditions necessary for it to occur.[5]
- Hela defeated Odin but did not kill him immediately, instead capturing him and many members of his family for revenge.[5][6]
- The casualties among the Norse Gods themselves were considerably smaller than the apparent losses among the Pantheon's mortal and supernatural populations.[6]
- The public initially believed most Norse mortal races had been killed during Ragnarok, but [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction had secretly removed many of them while Hela was occupied with Asgard.[6]
- Hela retained the Gods, treasures and other assets she personally claimed from Ragnarok when she sold the Pantheon's territorial Key.[6][8]
- [[Shiva]] purchased the Norse Pantheon Key from Hela in Chapter 972.[7]
- The purchase was one of the reasons the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]] later possessed the second-greatest Pantheon Authority after [[Dragons Nest Pantheon]].[20]
- Following the annexation of the Hindu Pantheon, the former Norse territories ultimately became part of the [[Elderblood Empire]].[9]
- The Norse Pantheon no longer exists as an independent Divine faction. The current territorial owner and the surviving original Norse Gods should therefore not be treated as a single unified Pantheon.
- Hela conquered the Pantheon but deliberately refused to become its permanent God-Queen because she considered administration an unnecessary burden after completing her revenge.[6]
- The Norse afterlife remained cosmologically important even after Odin's defeat because abandoning it could disrupt Earth's death-and-rebirth cycle.[6]
- The collapse of the Norse dimensional system during Ragnarok nearly produced consequences severe enough to require intervention from higher cosmic authorities.[13]
