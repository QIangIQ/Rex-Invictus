### Appearances

#### Debut
Chapter 958
Vidar is a Third Generation Æsir God of the [[Norse Pantheon]], one of [[Odin]]'s sons and the God of Revenge.[1] He was one of the Divine warriors deployed by [[Asgard]] during Ragnarök, initially entering battle alongside his brother [[Balder]] against [[Fenrir]].[2]
Vidar was ultimately overwhelmed during the collapse of Asgard. Although Chapter 959 apparently depicts Fenrir using his humanoid transformation to catch the two Gods fighting him by surprise and erase them with his jaws, Vidar explicitly appears alive again in Chapter 961, where [[Vlad III Tepes]] has already defeated him, broken his body and impaled him through non-vital organs so that he would remain alive for Hela's planned torture of Odin's children.[3][1] The two chapters therefore contain a direct continuity conflict regarding Vidar's condition, and his old wiki listing of "Killed by Fenrir / Cause of Death: End" should not be treated as securely established canon.
During Hela's post-battle purge, Vidar became one of the sons of Odin deliberately kept alive and tortured rather than immediately executed. Hela intended to systematically break Odin's warrior sons before eventually killing them, while [[Victor Elderblood]] arranged to receive the corpses of Odin's children after Hela finished with them.[1] Vidar's eventual death is therefore strongly implied by the later fate of Odin's family, but the exact scene and method of his final death have not been directly shown.

### Physical Appearance
The novel has not provided a detailed canonical description of Vidar's physical appearance.
During Chapter 961, [[Vlad III Tepes]] is shown dragging Vidar by his hair after defeating him, confirming that Vidar possesses sufficiently long hair to be physically seized in this manner.[1] By that point his body had been completely broken from combat and torture.
No direct canonical information has been given concerning his hair color, eye color, height, skin tone, clothing or armor. The current artwork should therefore be treated as an artistic interpretation rather than a definitive reference for those details.

### Personality
Vidar receives very little direct dialogue, but Chapter 961 provides a useful glimpse into his personality through Vlad.
After brutally defeating him, Vlad remarked that Vidar became surprisingly lively when facing an enjoyable opponent, although Vidar apparently refused to openly acknowledge that side of himself.[1]
This indicates that beneath whatever reserved or serious demeanor Vidar normally maintained, he possessed a genuine warrior's enjoyment of challenging combat. His unwillingness to admit it suggests a degree of pride or emotional restraint rather than the openly battle-hungry personality displayed by warriors such as [[Leonidas I]].
Vidar also fought directly against [[Fenrir]] during Ragnarök despite the enormous danger represented by a Beast possessing an Aspect of The End.[2] At minimum, this demonstrates considerable courage and willingness to defend [[Asgard]] against an opponent capable of permanently erasing Gods.
More specific personality traits should not be assigned without additional material.

### Synopsis

#### Dragon God of Chaos
Vidar made his first direct appearance during the opening phase of Ragnarök, when Hela finally unleashed the military forces she had accumulated over thousands of years against [[Odin]] and [[Asgard]].
The initial Norse defense was progressing more successfully than Odin expected. [[Thor]] had engaged [[Jormungandr]], while [[Balder]] and Vidar entered combat against [[Fenrir]]. Odin's elite soldiers were also holding back Hela's ordinary undead forces, leaving Hela herself and Nidhoggr as the principal unresolved threats.[2]
That apparent advantage disappeared when Hela began revealing the true scale of her preparations. She summoned creatures from The Abyss, ancient Human armies and legendary commanders such as [[Leonidas I]], [[Vlad III Tepes]] and [[Julius Caesar]]. The battlefield rapidly became impossible for Asgard to control.[2]
Fenrir then abruptly changed tactics. His enormous body glowed and rapidly shrank, catching his opponents completely by surprise. Exploiting the opening, Fenrir used his speed to bite the necks of two Gods, with the narration stating that his jaws erased them from existence.[3]
Because Chapter 958 had immediately established Balder and Vidar as the pair fighting Fenrir, the scene appears to identify them as the two victims. Odin's wife screamed after witnessing the attack, reinforcing that the targets were members of Odin's family.[2][3]
However, Vidar explicitly reappears alive in Chapter 961.
Following Asgard's military defeat, Hela ordered the second phase of her plan. Important Gods were to be captured, blacklisted Gods detained and only those specifically designated for death immediately executed. Her historical commanders began carrying out the order throughout Asgard.[1]
Vlad III Tepes soon arrived near Leonidas I while dragging a heavily injured God by his hair. The captive was directly identified as:
Vidar, the God of Revenge and son of Odin.
[1]
Vidar's body had already been completely broken by Vlad.
Rather than kill him, Vlad created an impaling pillar and threw Vidar onto it with extraordinary precision. The spike passed through his body while deliberately avoiding every vital organ. Because of Vidar's Divine resilience, Vlad knew the wound would not kill him—which was exactly what he wanted.[1]
Vidar could still cough afterward, directly confirming that he remained alive despite his condition.
Vlad then commented that Vidar had been surprisingly lively while fighting an enjoyable opponent even though he refused to admit that side of himself.[1]
From a wider perspective, the purpose of Vidar's treatment became clear. Hela had ordered that Odin's children be tortured rather than granted immediate deaths. The Gods being executed after the initial battle were principally beings closely associated with Odin or his family, while his sons and daughters were deliberately preserved for a much more personal punishment.[1]
When [[Idun]] questioned why Hela needed to make them suffer, Hela openly explained that only [[Frigg]] had received the mercy of a quick death. Odin's remaining children would be kept alive while Hela systematically destroyed everything and everyone they valued. She wanted Odin to watch his warrior sons lose their spirits and become empty shells before she finally allowed them to die.[1]
Vidar therefore became one of Hela's living trophies and torture victims rather than simply another casualty of the battlefield.
[[Victor Elderblood]] later arrived and negotiated with Hela for the corpses of Odin and Odin's children after she completed her "special treatment" of them. Hela agreed, remarking that she had little practical use for Gods of their level once she was finished with them.[1]
This makes Vidar's eventual death after Ragnarök strongly implied. However, no later reviewed chapter directly depicts his final execution, and the exact means by which he ultimately died has not been established.

#### Continuity Issue: Fenrir and Vidar's Fate
Vidar's Ragnarök scenes contain a direct continuity problem.
Chapter 958 explicitly establishes:
[[Balder]] and Vidar were fighting [[Fenrir]].
[2]
Chapter 959 then states that Fenrir suddenly transformed, surprised his opponents and bit the necks of:
the two Gods
erasing them from existence.[3]
Taken naturally, those two Gods should be Balder and Vidar.
However, Chapter 961 unmistakably identifies a living God as Vidar, God of Revenge and son of [[Odin]], with Vlad deliberately impaling him in a manner specifically designed not to kill him.[1]
Because the later chapter provides an explicit named appearance, the wiki should not claim with certainty that Fenrir permanently killed Vidar in Chapter 959.
The safest current interpretation is:
- Vidar fought Fenrir alongside Balder in Chapter 958.[2]
- Chapter 959 contains wording implying Fenrir erased both opponents.[3]
- Chapter 961 nevertheless explicitly presents Vidar alive and captured.[1]
- Vidar was then deliberately kept alive for Hela's torture of Odin's children.[1]
- His final death occurs off-page or remains otherwise unspecified.
Until the novel or author clarifies the discrepancy, Cause of Death: End and Killed by Fenrir should not be treated as definitive.

### Skills & Abilities
Vidar is a Third Generation Norse God and the God of Revenge.[1] The novel provides few direct demonstrations of his personal Divinity because his only substantial scenes occur during the catastrophic final battle of [[Asgard]].

#### God Physiology
As an Æsir God, Vidar possesses the physical and spiritual properties common to Divine beings.
- Superhuman Physical Characteristics: Vidar possesses strength, speed, durability, stamina, reflexes and agility vastly exceeding ordinary Mortals.
- [[Immortality]] / Agelessness: As an ancient God, Vidar does not age according to ordinary Human biology.
- [[Divine Energy]]: Vidar possesses Divine Power as part of his existence as a God.
- Divine Soul: His existence includes the powerful supernatural Soul characteristic of genuine Gods.
- Enhanced Senses: Divine physiology grants Vidar supernatural perception beyond ordinary biological senses.
- Regeneration: As a God, Vidar possesses enhanced recovery and supernatural vitality, although no dedicated regeneration feat is individually highlighted.
- Extreme Resilience: Even after Vlad had completely broken his body and impaled him through the torso, Vidar remained alive because the spike deliberately avoided his vital organs. Vlad explicitly relied upon the natural resilience of a God when designing the torture.[1]
- High Pain Tolerance: Vidar remained conscious enough to react after suffering extreme physical damage and impalement.[1]

#### Divinity — <Revenge>
Vidar is directly identified in Chapter 961 as the God of Revenge.[1]
This establishes Revenge as the Concept associated with his Divine existence.
However, the novel has never directly shown Vidar activating his Divinity or explained its specific applications.
Confirmed:
- Divinity of Revenge
- Conceptual Affinity — Revenge
Not yet demonstrated:
- direct Revenge empowerment;
- damage reflection;
- vengeance-based power amplification;
- curse generation;
- fate manipulation;
- automatic retaliation;
- or any other specialized application commonly associated with revenge-based powers.
These should not be added until Vidar directly demonstrates them.

#### Combat Ability
Vidar was one of the Gods [[Odin]] trusted to directly engage [[Fenrir]], one of the principal Beings of Ragnarök and a combatant possessing an Aspect of The End.[2]
Although Vidar fought Fenrir together with [[Balder]] rather than alone, merely being assigned to the confrontation indicates that he was considered one of [[Asgard]]'s capable combat Gods.
Known combat characteristics include:
- Divine Combat Skill: Vidar could participate directly in Asgard's highest-level battlefield.
- Close-Quarters Combat: His battle with Fenrir necessarily placed him within direct physical combat range of the enormous Divine Wolf.
- Warrior Mentality: Vlad describes Vidar as becoming unusually lively when facing an enjoyable opponent.[1]
- Combat Enjoyment: Vidar appears to possess a hidden enjoyment of difficult battles, though he refused to openly admit it.[1]
The novel does not identify his preferred weapon or named Martial Art.

#### Resistance to Physical Trauma
Vidar survived extreme physical damage long enough to be used as a torture victim.
Vlad had already reduced his body to a completely broken condition before impaling him, yet Vidar remained alive once the spike avoided his vital organs.[1]
This demonstrates:
- High Physical Durability
- Resistance to Severe Bodily Trauma
- Divine Vitality
It does not establish immunity to bodily destruction or The End.

### Power
Vidar was a Major God of [[Asgard]] and one of the Divine combatants deployed directly against [[Fenrir]] during Ragnarök.
His exact placement among the strongest Third Generation Gods has never been stated.
The fact that Vidar and [[Balder]] were assigned together against Fenrir demonstrates that [[Odin]] regarded both sons as relevant combatants, but the fight also illustrates the enormous difference between ordinary Major Gods and a being empowered by The End.[2][3]
Fenrir was capable of drastically changing the battle the moment he adopted a smaller form and exploited his superior speed.
Vidar was later completely defeated by [[Vlad III Tepes]], who dragged him by his hair with his body already broken before casually impaling him for torture.[1]
This demonstrates that Vidar remained far below the upper tier of Hela's Ragnarök commanders once those individuals had been resurrected and empowered.
No source currently establishes Vidar as:
- God-King level;
- Primordial God level;
- Semi-Primordial;
- or comparable to Fenrir, Hela, Odin or Nidhoggr.

### Relationships

#### Odin
[[Odin]] is Vidar's father and former ruler.
Vidar fought on Odin's side during Ragnarök and was one of the sons deployed directly against Hela's most dangerous forces.[2]
Hela specifically targeted Odin's children after the battle because she wanted the suffering of his family to destroy him psychologically. Vidar was consequently kept alive and tortured rather than immediately killed.[1]
Hela intended Odin to personally watch his warrior sons lose their spirits before she eventually killed him last.

#### Balder
[[Balder]] is one of Vidar's brothers.
The two fought together against [[Fenrir]] during Ragnarök.[2]
Chapter 959 then describes Fenrir surprising the two Gods he was fighting and attacking their necks with his End-infused jaws.[3]
Because Vidar appears alive again in Chapter 961, the exact outcome of that scene remains inconsistent.

#### Thor
[[Thor]] is one of Vidar's brothers.
Both fought for [[Asgard]] during Ragnarök, although Thor was occupied primarily with [[Jormungandr]] while Vidar and [[Balder]] confronted [[Fenrir]].[2][3]
No direct conversation between the brothers has been shown.

#### Tyr
Tyr is one of Vidar's brothers and another warrior God among [[Odin]]'s sons.
During Hela's second phase after the initial battle, both brothers became targets of Hela's historical commanders. Vlad had already captured Vidar while [[Leonidas I]] challenged Tyr directly.[1]

#### Heimdall
[[Heimdall]] is one of Vidar's brothers and served as one of [[Asgard]]'s principal guardians.
No substantial direct interaction between them has been shown.

#### Vali
[[Vali]] is another of [[Odin]]'s sons.
Both Vali and Vidar were used by Hela as weapons against Odin, although in different ways. Vali had already been killed and preserved before Ragnarök, while Vidar was captured alive during the final war and deliberately tortured afterward.[3][1]

#### Fenrir
[[Fenrir]] was Vidar's primary opponent during the opening battle of Ragnarök.
Vidar and [[Balder]] entered battle against Fenrir together.[2]
In Chapter 959, Fenrir suddenly shrank his body into a smaller form, catching his opponents by surprise before attacking the necks of two Gods with his End-infused jaws.[3]
The scene appears to portray Vidar as one of the victims, but his explicit survival and capture in Chapter 961 create a direct continuity contradiction.
Unlike traditional Norse mythology, MTW does not portray Vidar as the warrior who successfully avenges [[Odin]] by killing Fenrir.

#### Vlad III Tepes
[[Vlad III Tepes]] personally defeated and captured Vidar during Hela's Ragnarök campaign.
By the time Vlad appeared carrying him, Vidar's body had already been completely broken. Vlad dragged him by the hair before creating an impaling pillar and throwing him onto it.[1]
Vlad deliberately aimed the spike so that every vital organ was spared, ensuring Vidar would remain alive and suffer.
He then casually commented on Vidar's hidden enjoyment of fighting strong or entertaining opponents.[1]
Vidar therefore became one of Vlad's torture victims during the post-battle phase of Ragnarök.

#### Hela
Hela was ultimately responsible for Vidar's capture and torture.
After defeating [[Odin]], she divided the surviving Gods into categories: important Gods were to be captured, blacklisted Gods detained and those on the death list executed. Odin's children were deliberately kept alive because Hela wanted to break them one by one in front of their father.[1]
Vidar was one of these children.
Hela explicitly stated that Odin's warrior sons would remain alive until they lost their spirit and became shells of their former selves.[1]
