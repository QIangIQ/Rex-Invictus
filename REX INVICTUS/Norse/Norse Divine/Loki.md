**[[Loki]]** is the trickster god of Norse myth, born of giants but counted among the Æsir of the [[Norse Pantheon]].

---

## Norse Myth (IRL)

Son of the giant Fárbauti and Laufey, he is blood-brother to [[Odin]]. He both causes and solves the gods' troubles: he gets [[Thor]] his hammer Mjölnir through a bet with the dwarves, and as a mare he gives birth to [[Odin]]'s eight-legged horse Sleipnir. With the giantess [[Angrboða]] he fathers [[Fenrir]], Jörmungandr and [[Hel]]. After he engineers the death of Baldr, the gods bind him with his son's entrails under a dripping serpent, and at Ragnarök he breaks free and leads the giants against the gods.

## PJO Canon

In *Magnus Chase and the Gods of Asgard* he is the main villain, a charming manipulator who tries to free himself and start Ragnarök. He is the father of [[Alex Fierro]]. See [[Magnus Chase]].

Loki, also known as Hveðrungr, is an ancient Jötunn, a Second Generation God, and one of the major deities of the [[Norse Pantheon]]. Best known as the God of Lies, God of Trickery and God of Mischief, Loki possesses Divine Authorities centered around deception, illusions and interference with the perceptions of others. He is also associated with Fire and is one of the oldest surviving supernatural figures belonging to the Norse Divine order.
Although born among the Jötnar rather than the Æsir, Loki became deeply integrated into [[Asgard]] through his relationship with [[Odin]], becoming the All-Father's blood brother and effectively an honorary member of the Æsir. His position within the Pantheon has always been complicated, however. Loki is simultaneously one of Asgard's ancient Gods and the father of several beings prophesied to bring about its destruction: [[Fenrir]], [[Jormungandr]] and Hela. Those children were feared and persecuted because of Ragnarök, creating a conflict between Loki's attachment to his family and his longstanding relationship with Odin and Asgard.
Behind the reputation of an irresponsible prankster, Loki possesses a strong attachment to his children. His decisions concerning them nevertheless caused enormous damage. Hela in particular came to regard Loki's attempts to distance himself and avoid provoking Odin as abandonment, eventually despising her father alongside the rest of Asgard after spending immense periods isolated in Helheim.[1] Even millennia later, Loki remained unwilling to willingly fight his children and initially intended to stay out of Ragnarök for precisely that reason.[2]
Loki was also one of the original members of the private supernatural chat group maintained by [[Aphrodite]], alongside Aphrodite, [[Freyja|Freya]], Kali and Susanoo. Through this group he became aware of [[Victor Elderblood]], initially treating the rising Vampire Count as another interesting mortal-supernatural curiosity before eventually witnessing Victor grow into a Being considerably beyond Loki's own level. Loki later became Victor's father-in-law through Hela.

### Physical Appearance
Loki normally appears as a handsome man approximately 180 centimeters tall. He possesses messy black hair, vivid green eyes and a relatively thin but well-defined physique. During his first physical appearance before [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]], Loki wore a black suit, giving him a considerably more modern appearance than the traditional Divine clothing associated with many ancient Gods.[3]
Despite his ordinary Human-like proportions, Loki's visible body should not be treated as a fixed biological form. Gods are fundamentally high spiritual existences capable of altering their physical manifestation, and Loki is particularly famous for taking advantage of that freedom. The Norse Gods themselves acknowledge that Gods can alter their sex when desired, although most prefer to retain the form corresponding to their birth. Loki is one of the notable exceptions and has historically manifested as a woman when circumstances suited him.[2]
His ability to freely alter his manifestation is most infamously associated with the circumstances surrounding Sleipnir, a subject Loki strongly dislikes having brought up by the other Gods.[4][2]

### Personality
Loki is playful, irreverent and habitually mischievous. He enjoys provoking people, making inappropriate jokes and using his Divine abilities for entertainment even when the target does not share his sense of humor. His idea of a "prank" can be extraordinarily dangerous by mortal standards. When Loki attempted to prank [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]], he intended to manipulate Victor's senses, interfere with his mind and exploit his fears before eventually revealing the deception and laughing about it. When Victor asked what had happened to ordinary mortals subjected to comparable jokes, Loki casually acknowledged that some had died.[3][5]
This behavior reflects a degree of the arrogance common among ancient Gods. Loki has existed for so long and possesses enough supernatural power that the privacy, psychological stability and even survival of much weaker mortals are not always things he instinctively treats with appropriate seriousness. His attempt to invade Victor's mind and his participation with [[Freyja|Freya]] in secretly observing Victor and [[Aphrodite]]'s private interactions are clear examples of this disregard for conventional boundaries.[3][5]
Loki is nevertheless not portrayed as fundamentally malicious. Aphrodite, who has known him for an immense period of time, described him as not truly bad but rather as an irritating bastard whose dangerous pranks frequently backfire upon himself.[2] His friends are extremely accustomed to his behavior, responding to his jokes with insults, threats and ridicule rather than genuine surprise. The casual hostility between Loki, Freya, Susanoo and Aphrodite is therefore generally representative of an extremely old friendship rather than actual hatred.[4]
He is also surprisingly reclusive. Despite his reputation and immense age, Loki had paid so little attention to developments in the Human World that he initially did not recognize the name "Alucard" when Victor entered the Gods' chat group. Aphrodite mocked him for effectively living beneath a rock, prompting Loki's curiosity and eventually motivating him to personally visit the Human World.[6]
Loki's greatest contradiction concerns his family. He has a genuinely strong attachment to his children and does not enjoy the hatred directed toward them by [[Asgard]]. He was particularly unwilling to participate in Ragnarök because the conflict would require him to fight [[Fenrir]], [[Jormungandr]] and Hela. Before the war, both Loki and Freya had expressed the intention of remaining outside the conflict, with Loki specifically refusing because he did not want to oppose his children.[2]
At the same time, Loki's historic methods of "protecting" those children were deeply flawed. Fear of [[Odin]]'s reaction to the Ragnarök prophecy caused Loki to distance himself from them rather than openly standing against Asgard. Hela experienced this as abandonment and spent enormous periods of time despising both her father and the Pantheon that imprisoned her.[1] Consequently, Loki's love for his family does not erase the harm his decisions caused them.
Loki is also more loyal to Asgard than his reputation suggests. Despite initially wanting nothing to do with Ragnarök, he was ultimately present beside Odin, [[Thor]], Freya and [[Heimdall]] while Asgard prepared for war. He attempted to approach the crisis pragmatically by suggesting outside assistance once the scale of the threat became clear, even while the other Norse Gods distrusted him because Fenrir, Jormungandr and Hela were on the opposing side.[2]

### History

#### Origins and Asgard
Loki was born a Jötunn, the son of Fárbauti and Laufey, and descended from the ancient Giant lineage associated with Ymir. Despite his Giant origins, he eventually established an extraordinarily close relationship with [[Odin]]. The two became blood brothers through ritual, allowing Loki to occupy a unique position within [[Asgard]] as a Jötunn accepted among the Æsir.
Over the course of his immense life, Loki became one of the major Gods of the Norse system and developed his famous Divinities over Lies, Trickery and Mischief. He also became associated with Fire. His ability to deceive even other Divine Beings became so refined that [[Aphrodite]] considered his Lies Divinity unusually troublesome; according to her, almost nobody could reliably see through Loki's falsehoods, with Odin's extraordinary eye being one of the few known exceptions.[6]
Loki formed relationships with several women and produced numerous children. The most consequential were his children with [[Angrboða]]: [[Fenrir]], [[Jormungandr]] and Hela. Each developed into an extraordinarily abnormal existence, and all three became associated with the prophecy of Ragnarök. Their existence caused the Norse Gods to fear them long before they had actually committed the destruction attributed to them.
The resulting persecution fractured Loki's family. Hela was confined to Helheim, Fenrir became the Beast destined to kill Odin, and Jormungandr became the serpent associated with the world's destruction. Loki attempted to survive the increasingly hostile environment around his children without directly confronting Odin, but his choices caused those children—particularly Hela—to view him as someone who had abandoned them when they most needed him.[1]

#### The Gods' Chat Group
Thousands of years later, Loki maintained close friendships outside the [[Norse Pantheon]] through a private communication group composed of several influential Gods. The principal members were Loki, [[Aphrodite]], [[Freyja|Freya]], Kali and Susanoo.[4]
Loki used the username AManOfCulture and immediately demonstrated the group's chaotic dynamic through his interactions with the others. Freya and Susanoo openly mocked him, while discussion of Loki's past repeatedly returned to the embarrassing circumstances surrounding Sleipnir. Loki attempted to dismiss the matter by claiming he had been young at the time and refused to continue discussing it.[4]
The group later became one of Loki's first points of contact with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]. When Victor entered the chat, Loki initially treated him as an entertaining newcomer and demanded that he provide a photograph. His reaction became significantly more interested after seeing Victor and learning that he was the newly established Vampire Count known as Alucard.[6]
Loki also witnessed something he personally could not explain when Victor communicated with Kali at the level of the Soul. Loki had known Kali for thousands of years but had never been able to naturally understand her unusual Soul-level communication, whereas Victor was capable of doing so despite having known her for almost no time. The incident provided Loki with an early indication that Victor was not an ordinary Vampire.[6]
During the same conversation, Aphrodite discussed Loki's Lies Divinity and emphasized how difficult it was to determine when he was lying. Loki proudly responded that such deception was expected from the God of Lies.[6]
Victor's existence eventually motivated Loki to leave his isolation and visit the Human World personally.

### Synopsis

#### Discoveries, Changes, And War
Loki physically met [[Victor Elderblood]] after traveling to the Human World alongside [[Freyja|Freya]]. Victor immediately identified him as a handsome man with messy black hair and green eyes, standing approximately 180 centimeters tall and wearing a black suit.[3]
Rather than introducing himself normally, Loki decided to test—or in his own words, prank—Victor by immediately attempting to place him under an illusion and interfere with his mind. The technique failed completely. Victor's continually evolving Vampire Progenitor Blood had developed enough resistance that Loki's conventional illusion and mind-control abilities could no longer meaningfully affect him.[3]
[[Aphrodite]] reacted considerably more violently than Loki expected. She entered a more openly Divine state and warned Loki not to casually interfere with Victor, whom she already regarded as her husband. Loki attempted to dismiss the entire situation as a harmless joke, but Victor was considerably less amused.[3]
Victor questioned exactly what Loki intended to do with the illusion. Loki explained that he planned to control Victor's senses, manipulate what he perceived, interfere with his thoughts and exploit his fears before eventually revealing the prank. When Victor asked what happened when Loki performed comparable tricks on mortals incapable of resisting him, Loki admitted that they sometimes died.[5]
The conversation exposed the enormous gap between Loki's Divine sense of humor and ordinary morality. To Loki, the experience remained a joke because he had not intended permanent harm; to Victor, the fact that weaker victims could die demonstrated why ancient Gods could not assume their intentions erased the consequences of their actions.
Loki and Freya were also reprimanded after Aphrodite learned that they had used their Divine senses to spy on private moments involving her and Victor. Freya unsuccessfully attempted to place most of the blame upon Loki, only for Aphrodite to make clear that both were responsible.[5]
During the same period, Loki demonstrated substantial supernatural knowledge. He understood the significance of a Vampire Progenitor and described such a being in terms of existing between Life and Death, wielding authority over the Souls of the dead and the Blood of the living.[5]
Loki continued using the Gods' communication group after Victor's rise. As Victor accumulated increasingly absurd levels of power, however, Loki's earlier willingness to casually tease him diminished considerably. By the later conversations between the group, Kali openly observed that Victor had already surpassed both Loki and Freya in direct strength. The two Norse Gods had become noticeably quieter around him because they were no longer entirely certain how they should treat the person who had once entered their group as a comparatively young supernatural newcomer.[7]
Aphrodite eventually changed Loki's group username from AManOfCulture to Chicken, continuing the long-running pattern of the chat members using the group itself to mock one another.[7]
Victor's progression also provided a practical demonstration of the limitations of Loki's greatest specialties. Later protections surrounding Victor and his family were explicitly capable of identifying and rejecting illusionary or mind-altering effects on the level of Loki's powers, making him an established benchmark for high-level Divine mental interference.[8]

#### King Among Kings
Loki accompanied [[Odin]] during the Third [[Supernatural Gathering]], appearing alongside [[Thor]] as part of the Norse delegation and helping provide security around the All-Father.[9]
By this point [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] was no longer the comparatively young Vampire Loki had attempted to prank during their first physical meeting. He had become one of the central forces influencing the Gathering, and Loki treated the situation with considerably greater caution than during their earliest interactions.
The Gathering also reinforced the complicated political position of the [[Norse Pantheon]]. Odin remained one of Earth's most powerful established Divine rulers, but numerous problems connected to Ragnarök, his children and Loki's family were continuing to develop beneath the surface.

#### Dragon God of Chaos
As the Norse crisis moved toward open war, the children of Loki became central to the coming destruction. [[Fenrir]], [[Jormungandr]] and Hela were not merely symbols from an old prophecy; they had become immensely powerful supernatural forces with genuine grievances against [[Asgard]].[10][11]
Loki understood this danger better than most of Asgard. He knew what his children were capable of and, unlike Gods who regarded Ragnarök primarily as an abstract prophecy, Loki had a direct familial relationship with the beings expected to destroy them.
Before the final preparations, Loki had told [[Aphrodite]] that he did not intend to participate in Ragnarök because he had no desire to fight his own children. [[Freyja|Freya]] independently held a similar position, regarding the prophecy and the entire conflict surrounding it as foolish.[2]
Despite those intentions, both eventually appeared in [[Odin]]'s war room as Asgard prepared for battle. Aphrodite was surprised to see them there precisely because both had previously indicated they wanted no involvement.[2]
Loki's presence did not mean he suddenly believed Odin's strategy was sound. He remained deeply pessimistic. He understood that Fenrir, Jormungandr and Hela were extraordinarily dangerous even individually, while Hela's alliance with the ancient Dragon Nidhoggr made the situation considerably worse.[2]
Loki suggested asking outside powers for assistance rather than attempting to resolve the entire crisis through Asgard alone. Odin rejected the proposal and chose to seal the Norse dimension, intending to face Ragnarök without external intervention.[2]
The atmosphere around Loki was particularly hostile. The other Norse Gods had difficulty trusting him because the enemies attacking them were his children. Even Odin, despite being Loki's blood brother, could not completely separate the father from the threat represented by his offspring.[2]
Loki nevertheless remained with Asgard during the preparations, demonstrating the fundamental contradiction that had defined much of his life: he did not want to fight his children, but neither was he willing to simply abandon the Divine society that had been his home for eons.
Ragnarök finally erupted after Hela's millennia of preparation. The conflict grew far beyond a simple rebellion. Hela possessed armies of the dead, agreements established across enormous spans of time, the cooperation of Nidhoggr and the support of her brothers. Asgard, meanwhile, gathered its remaining Gods and defenses around Odin.[12][13]
Loki remained associated with Odin's side during the conflict, despite the fact that doing so placed him opposite the children he had previously refused to fight. The surviving chapters focus considerably more heavily upon Odin, Hela, Fenrir, Jormungandr, Nidhoggr and the other principal combatants, and therefore do not provide a detailed individual duel for Loki comparable to those figures. His exact actions during every stage of the war remain unclear.
The important outcome is unambiguous: Hela won.
Odin's preparations collapsed beneath the consequences of decisions accumulated over thousands of years. Hela systematically destroyed his authority, eliminated or captured members of his family and ultimately seized control of the remnants of the Norse Divine order.[14]
Loki survived. Following the victory, Hela explicitly considered herself to have captured Odin's children and even her own "useless father", confirming that Loki was taken alive rather than killed during Ragnarök.[15]
The outcome carried considerable irony. Loki had spent ages attempting to navigate between Odin and the children whose existence frightened Asgard, only to ultimately become the prisoner of the daughter who believed he had abandoned her.

#### God Emperor Victor Elderblood
Following Ragnarök, Hela's relationship with [[Victor Elderblood]] developed dramatically. She eventually entered Victor's family, making Loki Victor's father-in-law and connecting the ancient Norse trickster directly to the Elderblood family through his daughter.[16]
No later chapter has yet given Loki an equally extensive independent role following his capture, and his exact circumstances within Hela's post-Ragnarök territory have not been explored in comparable detail.

### Skills and Abilities
Loki is an ancient God of the [[Norse Pantheon]] whose demonstrated powers primarily revolve around Lies, Trickery, Illusions and mental interference. Unlike Gods such as [[Thor]], Loki does not favor straightforward physical combat; when Thor questioned why he was not directly attacking their enemies, Loki explicitly described himself as a Trickster God who possessed his own methods of fighting before disappearing through an illusion-like effect.[17]
- God Physiology: Loki is an ancient God belonging to the Norse Pantheon. As such, he possesses the supernatural body, Soul, [[Divine Energy]] and Divinities inherent to a true God. (Individual applications should only be listed below where Loki has personally demonstrated or been stated to possess them.)
- Divinity of Lies: Loki is the God of Lies. His Divinity makes his lies extraordinarily difficult to perceive. [[Aphrodite]] stated that, as far as she knew, only [[Odin]] using his special eye could reliably see through Loki's lies.[6]
- Supernatural Deception: Loki's falsehoods are reinforced by his Divinity rather than depending entirely upon ordinary acting or verbal skill, making determining whether he is telling the truth extremely difficult.[6]
- Illusion Manipulation: Loki can create supernatural illusions and attempted to use them against [[Victor Elderblood]] during their first physical meeting. Victor's evolved Progenitor Blood rendered Loki's attempt ineffective.[3]
- Illusion-Based Concealment: During battle Loki stated that, as a Trickster God, he had his own methods of fighting and subsequently disappeared in a manner compared directly to an illusion.[17]
- Sensory Deception: Loki's illusions are capable of making victims perceive things that are not actually occurring. His abilities are dangerous enough that protections created by [[Roxanne Elderblood]] specifically allowed Victor's wives to distinguish Loki-level illusions from reality.[8]
- Mind Manipulation: Loki is capable of interfering with another person's mind. Aphrodite explicitly accused him of attempting to control Victor's mind during their first meeting, something Loki regarded as part of one of his usual pranks.[3][5]
- Mental Interference: Loki's pranks can involve altering the victim's perceptions and mental state rather than merely creating visible images.[3][5]
- Lethal Mental Effects: Loki admitted that mortals subjected to similar supernatural pranks had died, demonstrating that his mental and illusionary interference can become extremely dangerous to insufficiently resistant targets.[5]
- [[Divine Sense]]: Loki can use the supernatural perception available to powerful Gods. He and [[Freyja|Freya]] used their Divine Senses to observe Victor, an intrusion that Aphrodite later condemned as an invasion of Victor's privacy.[3][5]
- Supernatural Knowledge: Loki possesses extensive knowledge accumulated over his extremely long existence. When discussing Vampire Progenitors, he immediately described them as beings walking between Life and Death with authority involving the Souls of the dead and Blood of the living.[5]

#### Limitations
- Loki's Illusion and Mind Manipulation are not absolute. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s evolved Progenitor Blood was sufficiently resistant that Loki's initial attempt at manipulating him failed.[3]
- [[Roxanne Elderblood]] later developed mental and Soul protections for Victor's Clan that were explicitly capable of identifying Loki's illusions and preventing him from manipulating their minds.[8]
- By Chapter 725, Victor had already surpassed Loki and [[Freyja|Freya]] in raw strength.[7]

### Relationships

#### Odin
[[Odin]] is one of the most important figures in Loki's life. Despite Loki's Jötunn birth, the two became blood brothers through ritual and Loki subsequently spent eons associated with [[Asgard]] and the Æsir.
Their relationship is nevertheless deeply contradictory. Odin's obsession with preventing Ragnarök led directly to the persecution of Loki's children, placing Loki between his blood brother and his biological family. Rather than permanently severing ties with Odin, Loki continued operating inside Asgard while attempting to keep his children away from the All-Father's attention.
By the final Ragnarök crisis, Odin no longer trusted Loki completely because the central enemy forces were Loki's own children. Loki nevertheless remained in Odin's war room and attempted to advise him, including suggesting that Asgard seek outside assistance. Odin rejected the proposal and sealed the Divine dimension instead.[2]
Hela's victory ultimately destroyed the system both men had attempted to preserve. Odin was defeated and Loki was captured alive by his daughter.[15]

#### Hela Elderblood
Hela is Loki's daughter with [[Angrboða]] and the child with whom he possesses his most openly damaged relationship.
Loki genuinely cares for Hela, but his fear of [[Odin]] and Ragnarök caused him to handle her situation disastrously. Rather than openly opposing [[Asgard]]'s persecution, Loki distanced himself while Hela remained isolated in Helheim. Hela consequently came to regard him as an absent and useless father and included him among the Norse figures she despised.[1]
Loki's later behavior demonstrates that he had not stopped caring about her. He originally refused to participate in Ragnarök specifically because he did not want to fight his children, Hela included.[2]
That concern did little to change Hela's judgment of him. After successfully defeating Asgard, Hela captured Loki alive and explicitly referred to him dismissively as her useless father.[15]
Hela later entered a relationship with [[Victor Elderblood]], making Victor Loki's son-in-law.[18]

#### Fenrir
[[Fenrir]] is one of Loki and [[Angrboða]]'s children and the Beast prophesied to kill [[Odin]]. The Norse Gods' fear of Fenrir played a central role in the tensions that ultimately produced Ragnarök.
Loki understood Fenrir's danger far better than most of [[Asgard]] and was unwilling to casually treat his son as an enemy simply because prophecy identified him as Odin's killer. This familial attachment contributed to Loki's original refusal to participate in Ragnarök.[2]
By the time the final war arrived, Fenrir had become one of the principal forces opposing Asgard, turning Loki's worst familial conflict into reality.

#### Jormungandr
[[Jormungandr]] is another of Loki and [[Angrboða]]'s children and one of the principal beings associated with Ragnarök. Like [[Fenrir]] and Hela, he was feared because of prophecy long before the final destruction of [[Asgard]].
Loki was well aware of Jormungandr's destructive capabilities and included his son among the reasons the approaching war was far more dangerous than [[Odin]]'s confidence suggested.[2]

#### Angrboða
[[Angrboða]] is one of Loki's spouses and the mother of [[Fenrir]], [[Jormungandr]] and Hela. Their children became the most infamous branch of Loki's family because all three were associated with [[Asgard]]'s predicted destruction.
The novel has focused considerably more heavily upon the children than upon Loki and Angrboða's relationship itself, leaving much of their personal history unexplored.

#### Aphrodite Elderblood
[[Aphrodite]] is one of Loki's oldest known friends outside the [[Norse Pantheon]] and a fellow member of the Gods' private chat group.
Their relationship is extremely casual despite both being ancient major Gods. Aphrodite regularly insults, mocks and threatens Loki when his behavior becomes excessive, while Loki is comfortable teasing her in return.[4]
Aphrodite nevertheless understands Loki exceptionally well. During the approach of Ragnarök, she defended the idea that Loki was not truly evil, describing him instead as someone whose idea of humor involved dangerous pranks that frequently caused problems for everyone—including Loki himself.[2]
Her relationship with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] created one of the few occasions when Loki's behavior genuinely angered her. When Loki attempted to manipulate Victor's mind as a prank, Aphrodite immediately confronted him and warned him not to interfere with her husband.[3]

#### Freya
[[Freyja|Freya]] and Loki have known one another for an immense period of time and share a friendship characterized by constant insults and arguments.
Freya frequently threatens Loki or mocks his embarrassing history, particularly incidents involving his transformations. Loki responds in kind and appears completely accustomed to the hostility.[4]
The two also shared similar skepticism toward Ragnarök. Both initially told [[Aphrodite]] that they intended not to participate: Freya considered the prophecy and conflict foolish, while Loki did not want to fight his children. Their later appearance together in [[Odin]]'s war room therefore surprised Aphrodite.[2]

#### Susanoo
Susanoo is another longtime friend of Loki and member of [[Aphrodite]]'s Gods' chat group. Their conversations are usually filled with insults and jokes, with Susanoo freely calling Loki a degenerate when discussing his infamous history.[4]
Despite the mockery, the ease with which they communicate demonstrates a friendship extending far beyond ordinary political interaction between separate Pantheons.

#### Kali Elderblood
Loki has known Kali for thousands of years through the Gods' chat group and considers her part of his longstanding circle of Divine acquaintances.
Despite that enormous period of familiarity, Loki was incapable of directly understanding Kali's unusual Soul-level communication. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s immediate ability to do so surprised him and became one of Loki's earliest indications that Victor possessed a highly abnormal Soul.[6]

#### Victor Elderblood
Loki's relationship with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] changed dramatically as Victor evolved.
Their earliest interactions were extremely casual. Loki treated Victor like an interesting newcomer in the Gods' chat group, demanded photographs and eventually traveled to the Human World partly because of his curiosity.[6]
During their first physical meeting, Loki immediately attempted to use illusion and mind control on Victor as a prank. The attempt failed, and [[Aphrodite]]'s reaction made clear that Loki had badly misjudged the situation.[3]
As Victor continued growing, Loki became substantially more cautious. Victor eventually surpassed both Loki and [[Freyja|Freya]] in direct strength, while his later family protections were capable of countering powers comparable to Loki's mental abilities.[7][8]
The relationship acquired a familial dimension much later when Hela became one of Victor's women, making the God Emperor Loki's son-in-law.[19]

### Trivia
- Loki's original Gods' Chat Group username was AManOfCulture.[4]
- [[Aphrodite]] later renamed his account Chicken.[7]
- Loki had become so disconnected from the Human World that he initially did not recognize the name "Alucard" when [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] entered the Gods' chat group.[6]
- Loki's Lies Divinity is exceptionally difficult to overcome. Aphrodite stated that almost no one could see through Loki's lies, with [[Odin]]'s eye being one of the few known exceptions.[6]
- Loki's idea of a prank can be lethal to ordinary mortals. He admitted that some weaker people subjected to his illusionary and mental games had died.[5]
- Loki is famous among the Gods for changing sex, with the circumstances surrounding Sleipnir being a recurring source of ridicule from his friends.[4][2]
- Although usually portrayed as male, Loki's ability to adopt a female form is consistent with the spiritual nature of Gods, whose manifested sex is not as biologically fixed as that of ordinary mortals.[2]
- Aphrodite does not consider Loki fundamentally evil; according to her, he is primarily an irresponsible trickster whose dangerous pranks often backfire.[2]
- Loki originally intended to remain outside Ragnarök because he did not want to fight his children.[2]
- The other Gods of [[Asgard]] distrusted Loki during the final preparations because [[Fenrir]], [[Jormungandr]] and Hela were among the forces attacking them.[2]
- Loki was captured alive by Hela following her victory in Ragnarök.[15]
- Loki became Victor Elderblood's father-in-law through Hela.
