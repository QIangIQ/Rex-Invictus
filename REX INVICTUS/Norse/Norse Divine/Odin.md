**[[Odin]]**, also known as Wotan, Biflindi, Fjölnir and King Bileygr, is an ancient Second Generation Primordial God, the former God-King of the Norse Pantheon, former ruler of Asgard and patriarch of the Æsir. (Lore adapted from *The Secrets of the Immortal Nicholas Flamel*.)

---

## Real-World Background (IRL)

Odin is the chief of the Norse Æsir: god of wisdom, war, poetry and death, ruler of [[Asgard]]. He gave an eye at Mímir's well and hung on [[Yggdrasil]] to win the runes. In [[Magnus Chase]] he rules Valhalla.

### History
After the fall of [[Danu Talis]], he created his own Shadowrealm named [[Asgard]]. His Shadowrealm was fueled by the World Tree, [[Yggdrasil]], which also led down to the Shadowrealm of the Elder he loved, [[Hecate|Hekate]], and down even further to [[Niflheim]], the Shadowrealm of his nemesis, Hel, who is also his niece. After the destruction of Yggdrasil, and Asgard in the process, and the death of Hekate by the hands of [[Dr. John Dee]], Odin became furious with Dee and swore he would destroy him. He sent his two ravens, [[Huginn and Muninn]], after Dee in hopes of finding him and capturing him. When he discovered Dee with an ally named [[Virginia Dare]], he ordered the ravens to attack them. They chased after them with the help of a pack of [[Cucubuths]], and cornered them in an unfinished Shadowrealm. When Dee and Dare escaped with the help of [[Excalibur]] and [[Clarent]], Huginn and Muninn became trapped in the Shadowrealm, with the Cucubuths. They will remain there unless Odin wishes to go after his pets, as stated by Dee. He encountered Hel, his niece, in her damaged Shadowrealm and they swore revenge on Dee together, putting their past, where they were at each other's throats, aside. Later, his cousin, [[Mars Ultor]], joined them. He gave up one eye for wisdom to the Archon Mimir.

Odin, also known as Wotan, Biflindi, Fjölnir and King Bileygr, is an ancient Second Generation Primordial God, the former God-King of the [[Norse Pantheon]], former ruler of [[Asgard]] and patriarch of the Æsir. For an immense period of history he stood among Earth's most influential Divine rulers and was worshipped as the All-Father, the Father God of the Norsemen and a God associated with War, Runes, Knowledge, Death, Healing and martial combat.[3][2]
Odin was not merely a ceremonial sovereign. He was an ancient warlord, master of Runes, highly accomplished martial artist and one of the old [[God-Kings]] whose experience allowed them to understand threats that younger Divine generations frequently underestimated. When [[Victor Elderblood]] became the Progenitor of the [[Blood Dragons]], Odin immediately recognized how dangerous a Dragon Progenitor could become and began preparing anti-Dragon weapons rather than dismissing him as another powerful Vampire.[5]
However, the same mentality that made Odin an effective ruler also became his greatest flaw. He approached dangerous possibilities through control, suppression and preparation, and his fear of Ragnarök led him to persecute the very family foretold to participate in it. In particular, his treatment of Hela, [[Fenrir]] and [[Jormungandr]] created generations of hatred while accidentally giving Hela immense periods in which to cultivate her power and prepare his downfall.[6][7]
Ragnarök ultimately destroyed Odin's rule. After thousands of years of preparation, Hela dismantled Asgard through overwhelming military pressure, hidden alliances, resurrected armies and betrayals from within Odin's own circle. Odin was defeated personally by Hela, had all of his limbs removed, saw Gungnir destroyed and was left alive but completely crippled while his surviving family and Pantheon fell into Hela's hands.[8][1]

### Physical Appearance
Odin appears as an elderly man whose age reflects the ancient image associated with the All-Father despite his Divine immortality. His most distinctive feature is that he possesses only one functional eye; during the [[Supernatural Gathering]] he is explicitly described as an old man with one eye covered by a bandage.[3]
His missing eye is connected to the immense wisdom he sought throughout his existence. Hela later specifically mocked the irony of Odin having sacrificed an eye in the pursuit of wisdom only for his own arrogance to prevent him from seeing the betrayal and resentment developing directly around him.[1]
Despite his elderly appearance, Odin's body possesses the physical constitution of a Second Generation Primordial God and veteran warrior. His appearance therefore does not accurately represent his physical strength, reflexes or durability. When using his Runes seriously in combat, supernatural powers and markings can spread across his body as his [[Divine Energy]] reinforces him.[8]
Odin possesses golden Divine blood. Following his defeat during Ragnarök, his mutilated body was covered in golden blood while he nevertheless remained alive despite the catastrophic damage inflicted upon him.[1]

### Personality and Traits
Odin is extremely intelligent, knowledgeable, experienced and cautious. He survived long enough to become one of Earth's dominant [[God-Kings]] not merely because of his generation, but because he understood warfare, politics and the importance of preparing for threats before they became uncontrollable.
This characteristic is clearly demonstrated in his reaction to [[Victor Elderblood]] becoming a Dragon Progenitor. Odin did not dismiss Victor because he was comparatively young. As one of the ancient Gods familiar with Dragons, he understood that even an ordinary Dragon could require multiple powerful Gods to confront and that a Dragon Progenitor possessed something considerably more dangerous: the ability to potentially establish an entire race of Dragons under his authority. Odin consequently ordered the Dwarves associated with his Pantheon to begin prioritizing anti-Dragon weaponry.[5]
His caution can be perfectly rational. When an unknown powerful woman presented him with an extremely dangerous supernatural agreement, Odin refused to accept simply because she mocked him as cowardly. The contract carried consequences capable of threatening his very Soul if broken, and Odin considered signing something he did not fully control an unacceptable risk.[9]
At the same time, Odin's caution frequently develops into paranoia and an obsessive need for control. Rather than simply reject the mysterious woman's proposal and allow her to leave, he attempted to delay her while preventing her from taking the same offer to another Pantheon. Odin preferred an uncertain threat to remain where he could observe it rather than permit another ruler to obtain an advantage he himself had refused.[9]
Odin is also extremely proud. As the All-Father, a Second Generation God and ruler of an ancient Pantheon, he had spent enormous periods existing near the top of supernatural civilization. This gradually caused confidence born from legitimate accomplishments to become arrogance. Hela later compared this flaw directly to [[Zeus]], arguing that both God-Kings had become so accustomed to their own superiority that they stopped seriously examining the possibility that those beneath them could undermine them.[1]
His famous statement that he does not start wars but finishes them summarizes much of his worldview. Odin prefers preparation, deterrence and decisive retaliation over reckless aggression. However, his definition of “finishing” a threat can involve extreme measures, and once he considers something dangerous enough he has few moral objections to eliminating or suppressing it.[9]
Odin is deeply possessive toward both his political assets and his family. His jealousy could manifest even in relatively trivial situations; during a discussion with [[Frigg]] regarding Victor, Odin reacted negatively to his wife's interest in Victor's appearance and deleted images she had obtained of him.[9]
This possessiveness becomes considerably more serious when members of his family are harmed. The deaths of Hugin and Munin provoked genuine fury from Odin, and the eventual destruction of his family during Ragnarök caused some of the few moments where Hela could reliably disrupt his normal combat focus.[9][8]
Despite presenting himself as a wise ruler, Odin is capable of considerable hypocrisy. He justified brutal actions against [[Loki]]'s children as necessary to prevent Ragnarök while failing to acknowledge how his treatment of them actively produced the hatred that made Ragnarök possible. He condemned Hela and her siblings for a future they had not yet created, then responded to the consequences as proof that his original suspicion had been correct.[7][1]
His failure with [[Idun]] demonstrates another weakness in his leadership. Odin believed that his authority and the passage of time had settled grievances within [[Asgard]], but Idun never forgave the death of her brother at Tyr's hands or Odin's inadequate handling of the situation. Odin considered her one of the people he could trust, yet his inability to properly address the injustice created another betrayal Hela could eventually exploit.[10][1]
Nevertheless, Odin should not be reduced to an incompetent tyrant. Many of his decisions demonstrate genuine strategic intelligence. He understood that external Pantheons would exploit a Norse civil war and therefore isolated the Norse dimension; he reinforced his forces before Ragnarök; he recognized when Hela's END attacks were too dangerous to confront normally; and he delegated battlefield command when circumstances required it.[11][12][13][8]
Odin's downfall came not from lacking intelligence, but from believing his intelligence, age and position made him sufficiently capable of controlling every important variable.

### History

#### Ancient Era and Rise of the All-Father
Odin was born as a member of the Second Generation of [[Primordial Gods]], the son of [[Borr]] and Bestla. Alongside his brothers Vili and Vé, he belonged to one of the earliest generations of Norse Divine beings to arise after the foundational beings of the Pantheon.
Over the course of his immense life, Odin eventually became ruler of the Æsir and the dominant God-King of [[Asgard]]. His authorities over War, Knowledge and Runes, combined with his natural power as a Second Generation God, established him as one of the principal pillars of the Norse supernatural order.
Odin pursued knowledge aggressively. At some point during his ancient life he sacrificed one of his eyes for greater wisdom, leaving the one-eyed appearance for which he later became known.[1]
His position eventually expanded beyond that of a warrior-king. Odin became the religious and political center of Asgard, commanding Gods, Demigods, Valkyries, Einherjar and numerous supernatural races connected to the Norse realms.

#### The Prophecy of Ragnarök
One of the most consequential events of Odin's reign was his reaction to the prophecy of Ragnarök.
The prophecy associated several of [[Loki]]'s children with the future destruction of Odin and [[Asgard]]. Rather than waiting to determine whether the predicted future would actually occur, Odin treated the prophecy as a threat that needed to be controlled in advance.
Hela, [[Fenrir]] and [[Jormungandr]] consequently grew up beneath fear and suspicion because of events they had not yet committed. Hela was isolated within Helheim, while her brothers likewise became targets of containment and persecution.[6][7]
Odin believed these actions reduced the likelihood of Ragnarök.
In practice, they accomplished the opposite.
Helheim was the realm most compatible with Hela's Divine authorities over Death and the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]]. By confining her there for enormous periods, Odin gave Hela time, Souls and an environment through which she could continuously increase her power while her hatred toward Asgard deepened.[7]
The siblings' treatment also ensured that the family Odin feared would one day destroy him eventually possessed personal reasons to do exactly that.

#### The Growth of Asgard
For enormous periods after the prophecy, Odin continued ruling [[Asgard]] as one of Earth's most powerful [[God-Kings]]. His family expanded considerably, producing prominent Gods such as [[Thor]], [[Balder]], [[Vidar]], Tyr, [[Heimdall]] and [[Vali]].
Odin also gathered powerful followers, Divine Beasts and artifacts. His ravens Hugin and Munin functioned as supernatural observers capable of extending his perception beyond Asgard, while the Divine spear Gungnir became one of the most important symbols of his power.
His rulership was sufficiently stable that the ancient God-King gradually came to consider the Norse order effectively permanent. This long period of superiority contributed heavily to the arrogance Hela would later exploit.

### Synopsis

#### Demonic War
Odin's first direct involvement with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s story occurred remotely during the events surrounding the Demonic War.
While [[Thor]] was confronted by the unnatural powers Victor had obtained through his interaction with Hell, one of Odin's ravens appeared and Odin communicated through it. The ancient God immediately recognized the supernatural phenomenon surrounding Victor as exceptionally pure Negative Energy.[4]
Thor explained that the source was the new King of Hell and Second Vampire Progenitor. Odin, despite his age and experience, remained silent for several moments after processing the information, demonstrating how abnormal Victor's rapid growth already appeared even to one of the old [[God-Kings]].[4]
The encounter gave Odin one of his earliest opportunities to evaluate Victor indirectly, long before the latter became the threat Odin would later consider capable of destabilizing the balance between entire Pantheons.

#### King Among Kings

##### The Blood Dragon Progenitor
The conquest of the [[Greek Pantheon]] fundamentally changed Odin's assessment of [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]].
Odin had little reason to be terrified by the existence of another Vampire Progenitor. Ancient rulers such as himself had survived in a world where [[Vlad III Tepes|Vlad Tepes]] already existed, and [[God-Kings]] of Odin's generation possessed enough experience and power to account for extremely dangerous racial Progenitors.[5]
A Dragon Progenitor was different.
Odin understood the historical danger represented by Dragons far better than younger supernatural beings. Even an ordinary powerful Dragon could require multiple high-level Gods to confront safely. A Progenitor capable of producing an entire race of Dragons therefore represented a strategic threat extending far beyond Victor's personal combat strength.[5]
Victor's ability to create or elevate new [[Blood Dragons]] was particularly dangerous because it resembled, in racial terms, the world-building potential normally associated with powerful Creator Gods. If left unchecked, Victor could potentially transform isolated powerful individuals into an organized civilization of beings whose baseline racial quality already threatened Gods.[5]
Odin therefore ordered the Dwarves under the [[Norse Pantheon]] to prioritize the development of anti-Dragon weapons. The decision demonstrated both his strategic foresight and his instinct to prepare counters against a potential enemy before open conflict became unavoidable.[5]

##### The Death of Hugin and Munin
Odin's relationship with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] deteriorated further after the destruction of his ravens.
The ravens had been used as supernatural extensions of Odin's perception, allowing him to monitor distant events without physically appearing. Victor eventually detected and destroyed them, severing Odin's remote observation and infuriating the All-Father.[9]
When Odin opened his remaining eye following their destruction, golden Divine blood accompanied the reaction. More important than the physical response was Odin's anger: Hugin and Munin were not merely disposable surveillance tools but beings Odin considered part of his ancient household.[9]
His desire for retaliation increased accordingly.

##### The Outsider's Proposal
Around the same period Odin was dealing with another dangerous political problem. A mysterious and exceptionally powerful woman had presented him with a proposal involving a supernatural agreement.
Odin refused to accept immediately because the consequences of violating the agreement could extend to the destruction of his Soul. The woman mocked his caution, but Odin considered blindly entering an agreement of that scale fundamentally irresponsible.[9]
However, Odin also refused to allow her to simply leave and offer the same opportunity to another Pantheon. He delayed, questioned and attempted to maintain control over the situation while considering which other Divine faction she might approach if negotiations collapsed.
This interaction demonstrated Odin's characteristic mixture of intelligence and possessiveness. Refusing an unknown Soul-threatening contract was rational; attempting to prevent everyone else from obtaining the opportunity he refused reflected his deeper need to control potentially dangerous variables.[9]

##### The Second Supernatural Gathering
Odin personally attended the later [[Supernatural Gathering]] accompanied by [[Thor]] and [[Loki]]. His arrival publicly established the [[Norse Pantheon]] as one of the major Divine factions participating in the negotiations.[3]
By this point relations between the Norse faction and [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s side had become openly hostile. The Dragon Nest faction had learned of bounties associated with Norse forces targeting women belonging to Victor's family, with instructions that made the situation considerably worse than an ordinary assassination contract.[3]
Odin therefore entered a gathering where Victor already possessed substantial reasons to consider the Norse Gods enemies.
The situation became even more uncomfortable when [[Fenrir]] appeared. The ancient wolf looked toward Odin with obvious hostility, causing Odin to instinctively move as though to call Gungnir before remembering that direct fighting was prohibited inside the gathering.[14]
Victor's arrival demonstrated another important aspect of Odin's power. The pressure released by Victor was enough to bring lesser nearby Gods to their knees, including members of Odin's own group. Odin recovered from the initial shock and used his [[Divine Energy]] defensively, protecting himself and nearby allies from the overwhelming pressure.[14]
During the martial demonstrations surrounding the gathering, Odin and Thor immediately recognized the extraordinary level of [[Scathach Elderblood Scarlett|Scathach Scarlett]]'s martial arts. Both are specifically identified as Gods of [[Martial Arts]] and veteran warlords whose accumulated combat experience allowed them to recognize a peak Grandmaster without requiring lengthy analysis.[2]

#### Dragon God of Chaos

##### Preparations for Ragnarök
Long after the prophecy had first shaped his treatment of [[Loki]]'s children, Odin finally found himself confronting the Ragnarök he had spent so long attempting to avoid.
The approaching civil war was not a surprise in the ordinary sense. Odin understood that conflict was coming and gathered the principal powers still supporting his regime, including [[Thor]], Loki, [[Freyja|Freya]], [[Heimdall]] and [[Idun]].[15][11]
He also isolated the Norse dimension from outside intervention. Odin understood perfectly well how the supernatural political world functioned: if other Pantheons discovered that [[Asgard]] was exhausting itself in a civil war, they would attempt to steal resources, recruit valuable people or seize territory while the Norse Gods were weakened.[11][12]
Unfortunately for Odin, this prediction was completely correct. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction was already preparing to do precisely that.
Odin ordered Idun to increase production of her Apples so they could be distributed among the main army. The Apples represented an enormous strategic advantage because of their ability to strengthen and restore Divine combatants.[11]
Idun warned that forcing the process excessively was not without consequences. The power involved ultimately drew from the vitality of the surrounding Divine system, meaning reckless overproduction could cause long-lasting or potentially catastrophic damage to the Norse dimension itself.[12]
Odin nevertheless faced a situation in which losing the coming war would make preserving the realm irrelevant from his perspective. His preparations consequently prioritized defeating Hela over maintaining every long-term resource in perfect condition.
Unknown to him, several of the people and systems he believed remained under his control had already become compromised.

##### The Opening of Ragnarök
When Ragnarök finally entered open warfare, Odin deployed the accumulated military power of [[Asgard]].
His armies included Gods, Goddesses, Demigods, Valkyries and Human warriors who had earned places in Valhalla. He also fielded Soul-based constructs created from warriors who had not achieved enough to enter Valhalla but remained useful enough to be converted into military resources.[16]
At first, the battle did not appear hopeless.
[[Thor]] was occupied by Jörmungandr, while other members of Odin's family confronted the beings traditionally associated with Ragnarök. Asgard's forces possessed overwhelming numerical and individual power, and Odin believed the army accumulated under his rule could endure Hela's dead.[13]
Then Hela revealed how little Odin actually understood about the forces she had prepared.
Her army was not limited to the ordinary dead of Helheim. Hela had spent immense periods collecting contracts, Souls and armies belonging to historical figures from completely different civilizations. Entire military formations suddenly appeared beneath rulers such as [[Leonidas I]], [[Vlad III Tepes]] and [[Julius Caesar]].[13]
The battlefield expanded again when Hela opened passages for [[Creatures of the Abyss]], introducing forces Odin had not included in his conventional understanding of Ragnarök.[13]
For perhaps the first time during the conflict, Odin began realizing that Hela had not simply been waiting for the ancient prophecy to occur. She had spent thousands of years constructing a version of Ragnarök specifically designed to defeat him.

##### Battle Against Hela
Odin personally entered the decisive battle against Hela once her intervention became impossible to ignore.
He summoned Gungnir and activated his Runes, covering his body in multiple supernatural Powers as he prepared to fight the Goddess he had once considered containable.[8]
Hela's END-infused attacks immediately changed the nature of the confrontation. When Odin recognized what she was about to unleash against nearby warriors, he attempted to warn them, but ordinary reactions and physical defense were insufficient. Everything caught by the attack simply disappeared.[8]
Odin understood the danger. Fighting someone using an Aspect of The End meant that relying upon superior durability or Divine regeneration was not enough; one mistake could cause damage at a level conventional Godly defenses could not easily repair.
Nevertheless, Odin remained an ancient warlord and fought seriously rather than collapsing immediately. He used Gungnir, Runes, Divine Power and thousands of years of combat experience while attempting to adapt to Hela's unfamiliar fighting style.[8]
Hela deliberately attacked him psychologically at the same time.
Nidhoggr targeted Odin's family and [[Asgard]] itself. When Odin's attention moved toward the people he cared about, Hela exploited the opening and severed one of his arms. The attack did not merely cut flesh—the corresponding portion of Odin's Soul was also severed, producing agony capable of disrupting even an ancient God-King.[8]
Hela then continued dismantling Odin physically and emotionally rather than immediately killing him.
[[Frigg]] was deliberately targeted because Hela knew exactly how much her death would hurt Odin. Members of his family were killed or defeated across the battlefield while the empire he had built collapsed around him.[8]

##### Idun's Betrayal
The final humiliation did not come exclusively from Hela.
[[Idun]], one of the Gods Odin had trusted and relied upon during his preparation for Ragnarök, revealed that her loyalty had already disappeared.
Her resentment originated from the death of her older brother at the hands of Tyr. Odin had failed to resolve the crime and punishment in a manner Idun considered remotely adequate. She never forgot what happened, and Hela eventually gained another internal ally from an injustice Odin assumed had already become history.[10][1]
The betrayal affected Odin severely because he had trusted Idun at a level comparable to some members of his own family.
For Hela, it represented the entire point of her strategy: Odin believed himself wise enough to understand everyone around him, yet many of the people closest to his throne had been carrying grievances he never truly resolved.

##### Defeat
By the end of Ragnarök, Odin had been completely defeated.
All of his limbs had been severed, his body was covered in golden blood and the once-legendary Gungnir lay broken.[1]
Yet Odin remained alive.
The feat demonstrated the extraordinary vitality of a Second Generation Primordial God and Peak God-King. Even catastrophic mutilation, spiritual damage and the destruction of his Divine weapon were insufficient to immediately kill him.[1]
Hela used the moment to explain what she believed was the fundamental reason for his defeat.
Odin had sacrificed an eye for wisdom and spent eons cultivating knowledge, yet his pride made him incapable of seeing the obvious consequences of his own decisions. He had feared Ragnarök so intensely that he persecuted children for a future prophecy, turning them into precisely the enemies required to make the prophecy real.[1]
Had Odin treated [[Loki]]'s children differently, supported them or at minimum avoided giving them personal reasons to hate [[Asgard]], the events predicted as Ragnarök might never have unfolded in the manner he feared.
His attempt to prevent destiny had helped manufacture it.
Hela subsequently reduced the former All-Father to the humiliating nickname “Pochi”, treating the ruler who had once imprisoned her like a broken pet.[1]

##### Aftermath
The fall of Odin marked the effective end of the old Norse political order.
Hela controlled [[Asgard]] and the surviving members of Odin's family, while [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction had used the war to remove large numbers of valuable inhabitants, races and resources from the Pantheon before the conflict concluded.[17]
Odin remained alive but crippled and powerless to stop what followed.
Approximately two months later Hela openly offered the remnants of the [[Norse Pantheon]] for sale to the other major Divine factions. [[Thor]], likewise defeated and crippled, was displayed publicly as evidence that the old regime could no longer challenge Hela's authority.[18]
Hela had little intention of allowing Odin's personal suffering to end immediately. She intended to spend an extended period tormenting Odin, [[Loki]] and the surviving members of the family she considered responsible for her own family's persecution.[19]
Thus the former All-Father survived Ragnarök, but everything that had defined his power—his throne, Pantheon, family, armies, Divine weapon and political authority—was stripped away.

### Skills and Abilities
Odin is one of the most powerful traditional Gods originating from Earth's Divine civilizations. As a Second Generation Primordial God who reached the position of Peak God-King, his power dramatically exceeds that of ordinary Gods and he possesses thousands of years of combat, magical, political and military experience.
Although Hela ultimately defeated him, the result should not be interpreted as proof that Odin was weak. Hela spent millennia preparing specifically to destroy him, entered battle with END-based attacks capable of bypassing conventional Divine defenses, possessed hidden allies inside [[Asgard]] and systematically targeted Odin's emotional vulnerabilities while the rest of his family fought across multiple fronts.[13][8][1]

#### God Physiology
- Second Generation Primordial God Physiology: Odin belongs to the Second Generation of ancient Gods and possesses a Divine constitution vastly superior to ordinary supernatural beings.
- Immense Strength: Odin possesses physical strength appropriate to a Peak God-King and veteran [[Ares|God of War]].
- Immense Speed and Reflexes: His combat reactions allowed him to engage Gods capable of operating at the highest levels of Earth's native Pantheons.
- Immense Durability: Odin can withstand attacks that would completely destroy lesser Gods.
- Extraordinary Stamina: His Divine body can continue functioning through extreme levels of damage and prolonged warfare.
- Enhanced Senses: Odin possesses Divine perception further enhanced by his Knowledge Divinity, Runes and mystical eye.
- [[Immortality]] and Longevity: Odin has existed since before Human civilization and does not age according to ordinary biological limitations.
- Regeneration: Like other powerful Gods, Odin possesses supernatural recovery, although damage involving The End or direct spiritual severing can exceed what ordinary regeneration can immediately repair.
- [[Divine Energy]] Manipulation: Odin can generate, release and shape enormous quantities of Divine Power for offense, defense and the activation of his authorities.
- Divine Aura: Odin's presence carries the supernatural pressure expected of an ancient God-King.
- Blessing and Curse Bestowal: As an ancient major God, Odin possesses access to the general blessing and curse mechanisms associated with Divine authority.

#### God-King Physiology
Odin was not merely a powerful God but the recognized ruler of an entire Divine Pantheon.
- Peak God-King: Odin reached the upper level of traditional [[God-Kings]] and possessed the personal strength, accumulated authority and Divine Artifacts required to rule one of Earth's major Pantheons.
- Pantheon Authority: As ruler of [[Asgard]], Odin possessed extensive supernatural and political authority over the Norse Divine system.
- Divine Command: Odin could command the Gods, Demigods, Valkyries, Einherjar and supernatural forces belonging to his kingdom.
- Large-Scale Military Mobilization: During Ragnarök he mobilized virtually the complete military resources of Asgard, including Divine armies and Soul-based constructs.[16][13]
- Dimensional Sealing: Odin possessed sufficient authority over the Norse realm to close the dimension in preparation for Ragnarök, preventing ordinary external access and attempting to stop rival factions from exploiting the war.[11][12]
- Asgardian Lordship: His position granted him control over the administration and defenses of Asgard and its associated Divine civilization.

#### Divinities
Odin possesses several major Divine Domains accumulated throughout his existence.

##### War
Odin is one of the principal Gods of War of the [[Norse Pantheon]].
- War Divinity: Odin possesses supernatural authority associated with warfare, battle and martial conflict.
- Combat Empowerment: His War authority contributes to the extraordinary combat ability expected of the ancient Warrior King.
- Battlefield Awareness: Odin demonstrates the ability to rapidly evaluate changing military situations and recognize major threats during combat.
- Military Command: He is extremely experienced in directing Gods and large supernatural armies.
- Warlord Experience: Odin and [[Thor]] are explicitly identified as experienced warlords whose familiarity with combat allows them to recognize extremely advanced martial artists almost immediately.[2]

##### Runes
Odin is the God of Runes and one of the most accomplished users of Norse Rune-based supernatural techniques.
- Rune Manipulation: Odin can create, activate and manipulate supernatural Runes.
- Rune [[Magic]]: Runes can be used to produce supernatural effects beyond ordinary [[Divine Energy]] projection.
- Self-Enhancement: During his battle against Hela, Odin activated Runes that caused multiple supernatural Powers to spread across and reinforce his body.[8]
- Power Augmentation: Odin can use Runes to augment his existing physical and Divine capabilities.[8]
- Rune-Based Defense: His Rune system can be used as part of his defensive combat methods.
- Rune-Based Offense: Odin can incorporate Rune effects into direct combat and Divine weapon usage.

##### Knowledge
Knowledge is one of Odin's principal Divine Domains and one of the traits for which the All-Father is most famous.
- Knowledge Divinity: Odin possesses supernatural authority over knowledge and accumulated information.
- Supernatural Intelligence: His Divine nature, age and authority grant him enormous intellectual processing and accumulated experience.
- Mystical Perception: Odin possesses supernatural methods of detecting hidden information and deception.
- Eye of Omniscience: Odin's remaining mystical eye is associated with exceptional perception, wisdom and the ability to see through supernatural deception, including tricks produced by beings such as [[Loki]]. Despite the title, this ability should not be interpreted as literal unlimited omniscience.
- Wisdom: Odin sacrificed one of his eyes in his pursuit of greater wisdom.[1]
- Threat Analysis: Odin was able to understand the strategic consequences of [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] becoming a Dragon Progenitor immediately and correctly recognized that allowing him to create an entire Dragon race could destabilize the balance of power between the Pantheons.[5]

##### Death
Odin possesses a major Divinity associated with Death.
- Death Divinity: Odin possesses supernatural authority connected to Death as part of his traditional Divine portfolio.
- Soul Awareness: His position as ruler of a Pantheon containing Valhalla and several systems involving dead warriors gives him extensive familiarity with spiritual existence.
- Afterlife Administration: Odin's regime made military use of deceased warriors, including those worthy of Valhalla and lesser Souls converted into supernatural military constructs.[16]
The exact limits of Odin's direct Death manipulation have not been demonstrated as extensively as those of dedicated Death Gods such as Hela, and therefore should not be treated as equivalent to Hela's control over Souls or The End.

##### Healing
Odin possesses a Divine authority associated with Healing.
- Healing Divinity: Odin can apply Divine Power through the conceptual authority associated with healing.
The novel has not demonstrated this Domain with the same detail as his War or Rune abilities, so more specific applications should not be assumed without further feats.

##### Creation
Odin possesses at least some influence over the Concept of Creation. Creation is not exclusive to the Heavenly Father, and several sufficiently ancient or powerful Gods possess limited relationships with the Concept.[20]
Odin's influence over Creation should not be confused with the vastly more fundamental racial Creation abilities possessed by genuine Dragon Progenitors or dedicated Gods of Creation.

#### Martial Arts
- God of [[Martial Arts]]: Odin is explicitly recognized as a God associated with martial combat.[2]
- Master Martial Artist: Thousands of years of warfare have given Odin exceptional close-quarters combat experience.
- Advanced Martial Perception: Odin immediately recognized the extraordinary mastery displayed by [[Scathach Elderblood Scarlett|Scathach Scarlett]], identifying qualities that ordinary observers would be incapable of properly evaluating.[2]
- Combat Experience: Odin has accumulated eons of experience against Divine, supernatural and martial opponents.
- Adaptive Combat: He can rapidly modify his fighting approach according to an opponent's abilities.

#### Weapon Mastery
- Master Weapon User: Odin's immense military experience makes him proficient with numerous forms of weaponry.
- [[Spears|Spear]] Mastery: His greatest demonstrated weapon proficiency is with spears, particularly Gungnir.
- Divine Weapon Synchronization: Odin can channel his Divine Powers through his signature weapon.
- Close-Quarters Combat: He is capable of using Gungnir at close range against extremely powerful Divine opponents.

#### Raven Abilities
Odin's ravens serve as more than ordinary animals.
- Remote Observation: Odin can use his ravens to observe distant events without physically traveling to the location.[4]
- Remote Communication: Odin can speak through a raven directly, allowing him to communicate with [[Thor]] and others from a distant location.[4]
- Familiar Possession: Odin can project enough of his consciousness or perception through a raven for it to function as a remote extension of himself.[4]
- Information Gathering: Hugin and Munin served as part of Odin's enormous intelligence network.
- Long-Distance Surveillance: The ravens could operate outside [[Asgard]] while maintaining a supernatural connection with Odin.
Their destruction by [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] therefore represented both a personal loss and the elimination of important supernatural surveillance assets.[9]

#### Defensive Abilities
- Divine Barrier/Protection: Odin can release [[Divine Energy]] defensively and extend that protection to people nearby. When [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s overwhelming pressure affected the Gods at the [[Supernatural Gathering]], Odin recovered and protected himself and members of his group with Divine Power.[14]
- Rune Reinforcement: Odin can reinforce his own body with Rune-based Powers.[8]
- Spiritual Durability: Odin survived having part of his Soul severed simultaneously with his physical arm, although the injury caused extreme pain and remained extremely dangerous.[8]
- Extreme Divine Vitality: Even after losing all four limbs, suffering severe spiritual injuries and witnessing Gungnir's destruction, Odin remained alive.[1]

#### Soul-Based Military Techniques
Odin's Ragnarök forces included supernatural constructs produced from the Souls of dead warriors who had failed to qualify for Valhalla but were still considered useful enough for military purposes.[16]
- Soul-Golem Deployment: Odin could deploy large numbers of Soul-based constructs as expendable troops.
- Dead Warrior Mobilization: His Divine system allowed deceased Human warriors to continue serving the Pantheon after death.
- Einherjar Command: Odin possessed command over Human warriors elevated into Valhalla's military structure.
These abilities reflect the Norse afterlife system under Odin's rule and should not necessarily be interpreted as the same type of direct [[Necromancy]] practiced by Hela.

#### Strategic Abilities
- Master Strategist: Odin possesses enormous experience in war and supernatural geopolitics.
- Threat Assessment: He correctly identified the danger represented by a Dragon Progenitor before most younger supernatural factions fully understood [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s racial evolution.[5]
- Countermeasure Development: Odin immediately ordered the development of anti-Dragon weaponry in response to Victor's transformation.[5]
- Political Awareness: He correctly predicted that foreign Pantheons would attempt to profit from a Norse civil war.
- Dimensional Isolation Strategy: Odin sealed his realm to reduce foreign interference before Ragnarök.[11]
- Resource Mobilization: He ordered [[Idun]]'s Apples redirected toward strengthening his principal combat forces.[11][12]
- Battlefield Delegation: Odin was willing to assign command responsibilities to experienced Gods such as [[Freyja|Freya]] when necessary during Ragnarök.[13]
- Contingency Planning: Odin prepared weapons, soldiers, Divine resources and realm defenses in anticipation of multiple classes of threat.

#### Resistances
- High Physical Resistance: Odin's Second Generation Divine body can withstand extraordinary physical trauma.
- High Spiritual Resistance: He remained conscious and alive after END-enhanced attacks damaged his Soul directly.[8]
- Pain Tolerance: Odin continued functioning despite injuries that simultaneously affected both body and Soul.
- Divine Pressure Resistance: Although affected by [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s presence at the [[Supernatural Gathering]], Odin recovered quickly enough to protect both himself and others.[14]
- Immense Life Force: Complete loss of his limbs was insufficient to kill him.[1]

#### Weaknesses
- The End: Odin has no demonstrated immunity to END. Hela's END-infused attacks could bypass the advantages of ordinary Divine durability and regeneration, damage his Soul and threaten his existence directly.[8]
- Soul Damage: Odin can survive spiritual injury but is not immune to it. Hela's attack severing part of his Soul caused significantly greater agony than ordinary physical mutilation.[8]
- Arrogance: Odin's confidence in his own intelligence and ancient authority caused him to underestimate the possibility that people beneath him could prepare successfully against him.[1]
- Need for Control: Odin has difficulty allowing dangerous variables to exist outside his influence, sometimes causing rational caution to become counterproductive manipulation.[9]
- Family Attachments: Despite his ruthless political behavior, Odin genuinely cares about members of his family. Hela deliberately exploited this during Ragnarök by targeting [[Frigg]] and his children whenever she needed to break his concentration.[8]
- Internal Political Blindness: Odin underestimated how long resentment could persist among his own followers, most notably with [[Idun]].[10][1]

### Relationships

#### Hela
Hela became the greatest consequence of Odin's attempt to prevent Ragnarök.
Fearing the prophecy surrounding [[Loki]]'s children, Odin isolated Hela in Helheim and treated her existence as a future threat rather than as a child who had not yet committed the crimes predicted of her.[7]
The decision ultimately became catastrophic. Helheim strengthened Hela's Death-related abilities and gave her immense time in which to accumulate Souls, establish alliances and develop a plan designed specifically to destroy Odin.
Hela's hatred was intensely personal. She did not want merely to overthrow Odin; she wanted to force him to experience the persecution, helplessness and loss that she believed he had inflicted upon her family.
During Ragnarök she succeeded. Odin's family was destroyed around him, Gungnir was broken, his limbs were removed and the former All-Father was left alive for further punishment.[8][1]
The relationship consequently represents one of the central ironies of Odin's life: the person he imprisoned because he feared she would destroy him eventually destroyed him largely because he had imprisoned her.

#### Loki
[[Loki]] is Odin's brother and was historically one of the Gods operating beside him within [[Asgard]].
Despite their familial relationship, Loki's loyalty to Odin came at the expense of Loki's own children. Hela regarded her father as someone who repeatedly sided with Odin's regime rather than properly protecting [[Fenrir]], Jörmungandr and herself.
During the preparation for Ragnarök, Loki continued operating beside Odin.[11]
However, as the war collapsed, Odin's frustration toward Loki and his family became increasingly explicit. He ultimately recognized that the entire situation surrounding Loki's children had become one of the primary sources of Asgard's destruction.[13]

#### Frigg
[[Frigg]] was Odin's principal wife and one of the individuals closest to him.
Their relationship was not without ordinary marital conflict—Frigg's interest in [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s appearance, for example, provoked Odin's jealousy—but she remained one of the people whose safety mattered greatly to the All-Father.[9]
Hela understood this perfectly.
Rather than killing Frigg merely because she was an opposing Goddess, Hela arranged her destruction specifically because of the psychological effect it would have upon Odin. The loss successfully disrupted Odin emotionally during the decisive stage of Ragnarök.[8]

#### Thor
[[Thor]] is one of Odin's most powerful sons and among the greatest warriors produced by the [[Norse Pantheon]].
Odin and Thor share characteristics as Gods of War and [[Martial Arts]], and both accumulated enormous experience as veteran warlords.[2]
Thor frequently accompanied Odin during major political events, including the [[Supernatural Gathering]], and formed one of the principal military pillars of [[Asgard]] during Ragnarök.
Odin relied upon Thor heavily during the final war, but [[Jormungandr]] successfully occupied the God of Thunder, preventing Thor from freely returning to aid his father.[8]
Thor survived Ragnarök but, like Odin, was left defeated and crippled.

#### Idun
[[Idun]] was one of Odin's most trusted Goddesses and essential to [[Asgard]] because of the Apples connected to her authority.
Odin relied heavily upon her before Ragnarök, instructing her to accelerate the production of Apples in order to strengthen his army.[11][12]
Unknown to him, Idun had never forgiven what happened to her brother. Tyr had killed him, and Idun considered Odin's handling of the crime and its punishment completely inadequate.
Her eventual betrayal was devastating precisely because Odin believed she could be trusted.
Idun's actions became one of Hela's final demonstrations that Odin's supposed knowledge of the people around him was far less complete than he believed.[10][1]

#### Fenrir
Odin regarded [[Fenrir]] as one of the dangerous beings associated with Ragnarök and consequently contributed to the treatment that caused Fenrir's deep hatred of [[Asgard]].
Their hostility remained obvious thousands of years later. When Fenrir appeared at the [[Supernatural Gathering]] and directed a malicious look toward Odin, the All-Father instinctively prepared to summon Gungnir despite combat being forbidden there.[14]
Fenrir later joined Hela during Ragnarök and helped destroy the family and order Odin had attempted to protect.

#### Victor Elderblood
Odin's view of [[Victor Elderblood]] changed dramatically as Victor evolved.
When Odin first learned that Victor had become the Second Vampire Progenitor and King of Hell, he was shocked by the speed of the young Vampire's advancement but did not immediately treat him as an existential threat.[4]
That changed completely when Victor became the Blood Dragon Progenitor.
Odin understood enough about Dragons to realize that Victor's new Race changed the strategic equation. Victor was no longer merely one powerful individual. As a Progenitor, he potentially represented the beginning of an entire civilization of Dragons.[5]
Odin consequently initiated the production of anti-Dragon weapons and increasingly regarded Victor's faction as a geopolitical threat.[5]
Their relationship worsened after Victor destroyed Hugin and Munin and after Norse hostility became openly directed toward members of Victor's family.[9][3]
Ironically, Odin's attempts to prepare against Victor were never given the opportunity to become the decisive conflict he expected. Ragnarök destroyed the [[Norse Pantheon]] before a full war between Odin and the Elderblood faction could occur.

### Equipment

#### Gungnir
Gungnir is Odin's signature Divine spear and one of the greatest symbols of his authority as the All-Father.
The weapon possesses exceptional balance and supernatural accuracy, allowing it to strike targets with extraordinary reliability regardless of the ordinary limitations of the wielder. It is also capable of interacting with spiritual existences and harming Souls.
Odin combines Gungnir with his War Divinity, Rune abilities and immense combat experience, making the spear considerably more dangerous in his hands than its properties alone would suggest.
During Ragnarök, Gungnir directly clashed with Hela's END-infused weapon.[8]
The spear was ultimately broken during Odin's defeat, symbolically marking the destruction of his military and political authority alongside the fall of [[Asgard]].[1]

#### Draupnir
Draupnir is a Divine ring created by the Dwarves for Odin.
The ring possesses the supernatural ability to multiply itself and is connected to transportation throughout the [[Nine Realms]]. Odin can use it as part of the systems allowing him to travel between locations belonging to the Norse Divine structure.

### Companions and Familiars

#### Hugin and Munin
Hugin and Munin were Odin's supernatural ravens and important components of his intelligence network.
Odin could perceive distant events and communicate through his ravens, effectively using them as extensions of his senses and presence.[4]
Both ravens were ultimately destroyed by [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]], removing two of Odin's ancient companions and contributing significantly to the hostility between the All-Father and the Dragon Progenitor.[9]

#### Sleipnir
Sleipnir is the supernatural horse associated with Odin and served as one of the All-Father's Divine mounts.

#### Geri and Freki
Geri and Freki were wolves belonging to Odin's household and traditionally associated with the Warrior King.

### Trivia
- Odin sacrificed one of his eyes in the pursuit of wisdom. Hela later mocked the irony that someone who had paid such a price for wisdom failed to perceive the betrayal developing throughout his own household.[1]
- His title “Eye of Omniscience” should not be interpreted as literal omniscience. Odin possesses extraordinary supernatural perception and knowledge but repeatedly fails to know important information hidden from him.
- Odin was one of the first old [[God-Kings]] to immediately understand how dangerous [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s evolution into a Dragon Progenitor was and ordered anti-Dragon weapons developed in response.[5]
- Odin and [[Thor]] are explicitly described as Gods of [[Martial Arts]] and experienced warlords.[2]
- Odin's first appearance in Victor's story was indirect: he communicated through one of his ravens after detecting the extraordinarily pure Negative Energy surrounding Victor.[4]
- Hugin and Munin's deaths caused Odin to develop an even stronger personal grudge against Victor.[9]
- Although Hela was born as only a Third Generation Goddess, her millennia of preparation, END-based abilities and strategic advantages ultimately allowed her to completely defeat Odin despite his status as a Second Generation God-King.[8][1]
- Hela directly compares Odin's fatal arrogance to [[Zeus]], considering both rulers examples of ancient God-Kings whose immense authority eventually caused them to underestimate the consequences of their own actions.[1]
- One of the central ironies surrounding Ragnarök is that Odin's actions to prevent the prophecy helped produce the conditions necessary for it to occur. His persecution of [[Loki]]'s children gave Hela and her brothers personal reasons to seek [[Asgard]]'s destruction.[7][1]
- Even after losing all four limbs and suffering direct Soul damage, Odin remained alive, demonstrating the extreme vitality of a Peak God-King.[1]
- After his defeat, Hela mockingly renamed the former All-Father “Pochi” and treated him as a broken pet.[1]
- Odin's fall did not merely cost him his throne. Within months, Hela openly offered the [[Norse Pantheon]] itself for sale to the other Divine factions.[18]
- Odin remains alive following Ragnarök rather than being confirmed dead, but he is crippled and no longer possesses his former position as ruler of Asgard.
