### Appearances

#### Debut
Chapter 959
Vali was a [[Demigod]] of the [[Norse Pantheon]], the illegitimate son of [[Odin]] and one of the children personally targeted by Hela during her millennia-long plan to destroy the All-Father. Although Vali was not a full God, Odin favored him more than many of his other children because Vali's appearance and nature strongly resembled Odin himself during his youth.[1]
Vali's importance to the story comes primarily from Hela's use of him as a psychological weapon against his father. Long before the final battle of Ragnarök, Hela manipulated circumstances leading to Vali's death and secretly retained his Soul rather than allowing it to proceed normally through the afterlife.[1] She subsequently spent an extended period breaking and torturing him, later crediting Vali's resilience with helping her refine her torture techniques.[2]
During Ragnarök, Hela manifested Vali's lifeless body directly in front of Odin. His appearance immediately horrified the God-King, who understood that his son was already dead. Hela then casually crushed Vali's entire jaw into dust before restoring it moments later through her authority over Death, demonstrating both what she had done to him and how completely his remains were under her control.[2]
Chapter 967 later confirms that Vali's death had itself been part of Hela's larger strategy. She deliberately caused his death through several manipulations and kept his Soul specifically so she could eventually reveal what she had done to Odin and deepen his suffering.[1]

### Physical Appearance
Vali looked almost exactly like a younger version of [[Odin]]. Chapter 959 describes him as a man bearing a striking resemblance to the All-Father, while Chapter 967 clarifies that he was effectively an exact copy of Odin when Odin was younger.[2][1]
This resemblance was one of the principal reasons Odin became particularly attached to him despite Vali being a [[Demigod]] rather than a full God.[1]
When Hela manifested Vali during Ragnarök, he possessed a visibly lifeless appearance, immediately allowing Odin to understand that his son was dead.[2]
The novel has not provided sufficiently detailed information concerning Vali's exact hair color, eye color, height or clothing. His current artwork should therefore be treated as an artistic interpretation rather than a fully canonical design.

### Personality
Very little of Vali's personality is shown directly because he first appears after his death.
However, Chapter 967 reveals that Vali's nature strongly resembled [[Odin]]'s own personality when the God-King was younger. This resemblance extended beyond appearance and was important enough that Odin became more fond of Vali than many of his other children.[1]
The exact traits Vali inherited from the younger Odin are not individually listed. Consequently, characteristics such as arrogance, ambition, martial pride or cunning should not automatically be assigned to Vali unless later material directly confirms them.
Vali nevertheless possessed considerable resilience. Hela states that he "took ages to break" while she tortured him, and specifically credits the experience with improving her torture skills.[2] This indicates exceptional mental endurance and resistance to prolonged suffering even though the precise events of his imprisonment are not shown.

### History
Vali was born as one of [[Odin]]'s many children but was not a full God. Chapter 967 explicitly identifies him as Odin's bastard son and a [[Demigod]].[1]
Despite this technically inferior Divine status, Odin favored him because Vali resembled him extraordinarily closely. His appearance was effectively identical to Odin's younger self, while his nature was similarly close enough to Odin's that the God-King developed a particular fondness for him.[1]
At some point before Ragnarök, Vali became one of the targets incorporated into Hela's long-term revenge against Odin.
Hela spent thousands of years preparing Odin's downfall and designed her plans not simply to defeat him militarily but to systematically destroy the things he valued. Vali's position as a favored son made him an ideal target.
Through a series of unspecified manipulations, Hela caused Vali's death.[1]
She then retained his Soul rather than allowing it to disappear into the normal cycle of death and reincarnation. Hela kept Vali specifically so she could eventually reveal his fate to Odin and use the emotional damage as part of her revenge.[1]
Vali also became one of Hela's principal torture subjects. According to Hela, his resilience made him extraordinarily difficult to break, and the prolonged process helped her improve her own methods of torture.[2]
The precise amount of time Vali remained under Hela's control before Ragnarök has not been established.

### Synopsis

#### Dragon God of Chaos
Vali's role in Ragnarök began before the actual war, although neither [[Odin]] nor the audience initially knew he had become one of Hela's victims. Hela had spent millennia planning the destruction of Odin and constructed her revenge around more than military victory. She wanted to dismantle the God-King emotionally, spiritually and politically while forcing him to witness the destruction of his family and everything he had built.
Vali was one of the most personal pieces of that plan.
During Hela's direct battle against Odin, the God-King initially focused upon surviving her attacks, the invading armies, [[Fenrir]], Nidhoggr, the forces of The Abyss and the destruction spreading throughout [[Asgard]]. Hela then deliberately changed the battle from a military confrontation into a psychological attack.[2]
She raised her hand and caused a man to emerge from the ground.
Odin immediately recognized him.
Vali.
The resemblance between father and son was unmistakable. Vali looked strikingly similar to Odin, but there was no life remaining in his expression. Odin immediately understood what had happened to him.[2]
Hela gently touched the dead man's cheek and mocked Odin's arrogance. She explained that obtaining Vali had been surprisingly easy because Odin's own behavior repeatedly created opportunities for his enemies. Hela then revealed that Vali had been an exceptionally difficult person to psychologically destroy, stating that he had taken ages to break and that torturing him had substantially improved her skills.[2]
She then demonstrated exactly how completely Vali belonged to her.
Without hesitation, Hela crushed Vali's entire jaw, reducing it to dust directly in front of Odin. A moment later, the destroyed jaw was completely restored.[2]
Odin finally lost control of his emotions and accused Hela of killing his son.
Hela was delighted.
Vali's presence had never been intended to contribute military power to Ragnarök. His purpose was to make Odin experience hatred and helplessness while realizing that Hela had already taken one of the children he particularly valued long before the final war even began.[2]
The full context behind Vali's death was revealed after Ragnarök.
While reflecting on the success of her plans in Chapter 967, Hela specifically remembered the death of Vali, Odin's bastard son. She acknowledged that Odin had favored Vali more than many of his other children because the [[Demigod]] was essentially an exact copy of Odin when he was young, both in appearance and nature.[1]
Hela further revealed that Vali's death had not been accidental or simply the result of capturing him during the war. She had caused his death beforehand through several manipulations and then deliberately retained his Soul so that she could eventually use what she had done against Odin.[1]
Vali was therefore one of the clearest examples of the depth of Hela's preparation. Long before Odin understood that Ragnarök had truly begun, Hela had already manipulated the death of one of his favored sons, captured his Soul, broken him through prolonged torture and preserved him as a weapon intended specifically to attack Odin's emotions.

#### After Ragnarök
Vali remained dead following the fall of [[Asgard]].
Hela later declared that [[Odin]]'s captured children, Gods and treasures were her property. By Chapter 967, she explicitly continued regarding Vali's Soul as something she had preserved for her own purposes.[1]
Unlike Odin, [[Thor]] and several surviving members of the Norse family who were kept alive for prolonged punishment, Vali had already died before Ragnarök's conclusion.
No later chapter through Chapter 1170 establishes that Vali was resurrected as an independent living being.

### Skills & Abilities
Vali was a [[Demigod]] descended directly from [[Odin]]. Very little of his personal ability set is shown because his only direct appearance occurs after death.
The novel does not reveal a named Divinity, Element, weapon or unique supernatural technique belonging to him.

#### Demigod Physiology
Vali was explicitly not a full God but a [[Demigod]].[1]
As a Divine–Mortal hybrid descended from [[Odin]], he possessed supernatural physical and spiritual characteristics beyond those of ordinary Humans.
- Superhuman Physical Characteristics: Vali possessed enhanced strength, speed, reflexes, durability and stamina due to his Demigod nature.
- Enhanced Longevity: As a Demigod, Vali possessed a lifespan far beyond that of ordinary Humans.
- Enhanced Soul: His Divine ancestry granted him a supernatural Soul capable of surviving physical death and subsequently being preserved by Hela.
- Divine Heritage: Vali inherited supernatural potential directly from Odin despite not attaining the status of a complete God.
The exact degree of these abilities is unknown because Vali is never shown fighting while alive.

#### Exceptional Mental Endurance
Vali demonstrated extraordinary resistance to prolonged torture.
Hela states that:
He took ages to break.
[2]
She considered his resilience significant enough that repeatedly torturing him improved her own torture techniques.
This indicates:
- High Pain Tolerance
- Strong Willpower
- Mental Endurance
- Resistance to Torture
The feat does not establish immunity to Mind Manipulation or Soul Manipulation.
Hela ultimately succeeded in breaking him.

#### Soul Preservation
After Vali's death, Hela retained his Soul.[1]
This is not an ability belonging to Vali himself. It is the result of Hela's authority over Death and Souls.
Because Hela possessed his Soul, she was able to manifest his dead body during Ragnarök and repeatedly restore physical damage inflicted upon it.[2]

#### Regeneration (External; through Hela)
When Hela crushed Vali's entire jaw into dust, it was immediately restored.[2]
The restoration should not be indexed as proof that Vali possessed personal high-level Regeneration while alive.
The feat occurs while Vali is already dead and his body/Soul are completely controlled by Hela.
The restoration therefore belongs to Hela's Death Manipulation, Resurrection and Soul authority.

### Power
Vali's exact combat power is not demonstrated directly in the novel.
He was a [[Demigod]] rather than a full Norse God, but this did not necessarily make him insignificant. His father was [[Odin]], one of the strongest [[God-Kings]] of Earth's Sector, providing Vali with exceptionally powerful Divine ancestry.[1]
Previous wiki material has classified Vali within the Low-Level Combat God range. However, the chapters surrounding his direct appearance do not provide a personal battle, a Divine rank or a direct power comparison sufficient to independently reconstruct that classification from novel feats alone.
Consequently, his article should distinguish between:
- Race: Demigod — directly confirmed by Chapter 967.[1]
- Combat classification: Low-Level Combat God — existing supplementary/author ranking where applicable.
- Major God: Incorrect as a racial classification, because Chapter 967 explicitly states that Vali was not a full God.[1]
Without the external ranking, his exact combat level based only upon story feats remains unknown.

### Relationships

#### Odin
[[Odin]] was Vali's father.
Vali was one of Odin's illegitimate children and, despite being only a [[Demigod]], became one of the sons the All-Father favored more strongly than many others.[1]
The reason was deeply personal.
Vali was essentially a copy of Odin's younger self. His appearance strongly resembled Odin as a young man, and his nature was similarly close to that of the younger God-King.[1]
Hela deliberately exploited this affection.
Rather than merely killing a random son of Odin, she targeted someone whose death would personally hurt him. She caused Vali's death, preserved his Soul and later manifested his broken corpse directly in front of Odin during Ragnarök.[2][1]
Odin's reaction confirmed the importance of their relationship. The moment he understood Vali was dead, his anger immediately overwhelmed much of his usual composure.

#### Hela
Hela was Vali's killer, captor and torturer.
Their relationship was entirely antagonistic.
Hela selected Vali because he was useful in her larger campaign against [[Odin]]. Through several manipulations, she caused his death before Ragnarök and secretly retained his Soul afterward.[1]
She subsequently spent an extended period torturing Vali.
Hela described him as unusually resilient and stated that he took ages to break. His resistance forced her to repeatedly improve her methods, making Vali one of the individuals directly responsible for Hela becoming more proficient at torture.[2]
During Ragnarök, she manifested his corpse specifically to provoke Odin.
She caressed Vali's face while describing how she had broken him before crushing his jaw into dust and restoring it in front of his father.[2]
Vali was therefore less a military prisoner than a deliberately preserved psychological weapon in Hela's revenge against Odin.
