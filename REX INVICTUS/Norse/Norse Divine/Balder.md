**[[Balder]]** was a Third Generation Æsir God, Major God of the Norse Pantheon, son of Odin and Frigg, and one of Asgard's combat Gods during Ragnarök. (Lore adapted from *My Three Wives Are Beautiful Vampires*.)

> **Rex Invictus canon.** This material is adapted into the Rex Invictus world at the lowest priority: Rex Invictus wins any conflict, then the Flamel material, then MTWABV. Events of the MTWABV timeline (Victor's conquests, the fall of the pantheons, the [[Elderblood Empire]]) are distant background or rumor unless a chapter puts them on the page. ### Appearances

#### Debut
Chapter 958
Balder was a Third Generation Æsir God, Major God of the [[Norse Pantheon]], son of [[Odin]] and [[Frigg]], and one of [[Asgard]]'s combat Gods during Ragnarök. He fought alongside his brother [[Vidar]] against [[Fenrir]], one of the principal Beings responsible for the destruction of Asgard.[2]
Although Balder receives very little individual focus before his death, Odin considered him sufficiently capable to participate directly against Fenrir while [[Thor]] fought [[Jormungandr]] and the rest of Asgard's elite forces attempted to contain Hela's armies.[2] His opponent, however, possessed an Aspect of The End, giving Fenrir the ability to erase beings rather than merely inflict conventional physical damage.
During Chapter 959, Fenrir suddenly compressed his enormous wolf body into a much smaller form. The transformation happened quickly enough to take the Gods fighting him completely by surprise. Using the resulting increase in mobility, Fenrir bit through the necks of both opponents with jaws empowered by The End, causing them to disappear from existence.[1] Balder does not appear again afterward and is consistently treated as deceased.
There is a continuity problem involving Vidar, who is explicitly shown alive in Chapter 961 despite apparently being the second God attacked in the same Chapter 959 scene. This contradiction affects Vidar's fate, but there is currently no corresponding later appearance contradicting Balder's death. Balder can therefore still be safely indexed as having been killed by Fenrir while the exact identity or survival of the second victim remains inconsistent.

### Physical Appearance
The novel does not provide a detailed canonical description of Balder's appearance.
No reliable chapter currently establishes his hair color, eye color, height, skin tone, clothing or preferred armor. His current image should therefore be treated as an artistic interpretation rather than a definitive representation of his canonical appearance.

### Personality
Balder receives no direct dialogue during his brief appearance, leaving most of his individual personality unexplored.
His actions during Ragnarök nevertheless establish several basic traits. Balder willingly entered direct combat against [[Fenrir]], one of the greatest threats to [[Asgard]] and a Being associated with The End.[2] He did so alongside [[Vidar]] rather than avoiding the battle or remaining behind [[Odin]]'s defensive lines, indicating the courage and martial discipline expected from one of Asgard's Major Gods.
Beyond his willingness to fight for Asgard and obey Odin's military strategy, more specific traits such as kindness, arrogance, optimism or temperament should not be assigned without additional novel material.

### Synopsis

#### Dragon God of Chaos
Balder made his first and only substantial appearance during Ragnarök, after Hela finally began the war she had spent thousands of years preparing against [[Odin]].
At the opening of the conflict, [[Asgard]] initially appeared capable of resisting Hela's attack. Odin possessed enormous numbers of Divine warriors and Valkyries, while his most powerful children and allies divided themselves among the principal threats invading the realm. [[Thor]] engaged [[Jormungandr]], while Balder and [[Vidar]] fought [[Fenrir]] together.[2]
At this stage, Odin still believed the battle was progressing within manageable limits. His warriors were containing Hela's ordinary undead armies, and the principal enemies appeared to consist of Hela herself, Fenrir, Jormungandr and Nidhoggr.
The situation changed completely once Hela revealed the true extent of her preparations.
She summoned creatures from The Abyss, tens of thousands of Spartans under [[Leonidas I]], the Wallachian Royal Army under [[Vlad III Tepes]], hundreds of thousands of Roman soldiers under [[Julius Caesar]] and additional supernatural forces Odin had never included in his calculations.[2]
The battlefield immediately began collapsing around the Æsir.
Fenrir then abruptly changed his method of fighting. His enormous wolf body began glowing and rapidly shrinking. The transformation occurred so quickly that the Gods fighting him were caught completely by surprise.[1]
Fenrir immediately exploited the opening.
Using his newfound speed, he reached both Gods and bit into their necks. His jaws were infused with an Aspect of The End, and the narration states that the two Gods were erased from existence rather than simply physically killed.[1]
[[Frigg]]'s scream immediately echoed across the battlefield, attracting Odin's attention. When Odin used his Divine Senses to understand what had occurred, he became furious, but Hela immediately exploited his distraction and continued attacking him.[1]
Balder never appears again after this moment.
His death became one of the rapid personal losses that destabilized Odin during Ragnarök. Within moments, Odin was forced to witness the apparent destruction of his sons, the erasure of his palace and Frigg by Nidhoggr's attack, the destruction of Asgard's defenses and Hela's revelation that she had already killed and tortured [[Vali]].[1]
The sequence demonstrates one of the central purposes behind Hela's strategy. She did not merely intend to defeat Odin militarily; she deliberately arranged Ragnarök so that the God-King would be forced to watch his family and civilization collapse around him before she personally finished him.

#### Death and the Vidar Continuity Issue
Chapter 958 explicitly identifies the pair fighting [[Fenrir]] as:
- Balder
- [[Vidar]]
[2]
Chapter 959 subsequently states that Fenrir caught his two opponents by surprise and erased both with his jaws.[1]
Balder never appears again and is consistently treated as deceased.
Vidar, however, explicitly reappears alive in Chapter 961 after having been defeated by [[Vlad III Tepes]].[3]
The chapters therefore create a continuity contradiction regarding Vidar, but not necessarily Balder.
The safest interpretation for the wiki is:
- Balder and Vidar fought Fenrir together.[2]
- Fenrir attacked two Gods with End-infused jaws.[1]
- Balder disappeared permanently after the attack.
- Vidar inexplicably appears alive two chapters later.[3]
- Balder can still be treated as one confirmed victim of Fenrir, while Vidar's survival represents the inconsistency.

### Skills & Abilities
Balder was a Third Generation Æsir God, Major God and combat-oriented member of [[Asgard]]'s Divine forces. His limited screen time prevents a complete reconstruction of his personal supernatural abilities.

#### God Physiology
As a genuine God, Balder possessed the fundamental physical and spiritual properties associated with Divine beings.
- Superhuman Physical Characteristics: Balder possessed supernatural strength, speed, reflexes, durability, stamina and agility far beyond ordinary Mortals.
- [[Immortality]] / Agelessness: As an ancient God, Balder was unaffected by normal Mortal aging.
- [[Divine Energy]]: Balder possessed Divine Power as part of his existence.
- Divine Soul: His Soul possessed the supernatural quality associated with genuine Gods.
- Enhanced Senses: Balder possessed perception beyond ordinary Human senses.
- Regeneration: His Divine body possessed the supernatural healing and resilience generally associated with Gods.
- High Durability: Balder was capable of entering direct combat against [[Fenrir]] rather than being immediately incapable of participating in the battle.
Balder's ordinary Divine resilience offered no protection against the conceptual erasure produced by Fenrir's Aspect of The End.[1]

#### Divinity
The chapters surrounding Balder's appearance do not directly identify or demonstrate his personal Divinity.
The wider [[Norse Pantheon]] material traditionally associates Balder with positive Concepts such as Joy, Light, Purity and Summer, but the reviewed novel chapters do not show Balder directly activating those Concepts.
They should therefore not be expanded into demonstrated abilities such as:
- Light Manipulation;
- Purification;
- Emotion Manipulation;
- Summer Manipulation;
- or conceptual Joy-based abilities
without a direct supporting chapter.

#### Combat God
Balder is indexed within the existing power hierarchy as a Low Level Combat God.
Unlike non-combat Gods whose Divinities are primarily agricultural, social or administrative, Balder was directly deployed against one of Ragnarök's central monsters.[2]
Known combat applications include:
- Divine Combat: Balder could fight in direct supernatural warfare against Divine Beings.
- Close-Quarters Combat: His battle with [[Fenrir]] required fighting the enormous Divine Wolf at close range.
- Team Combat: Balder fought Fenrir alongside [[Vidar]].[2]
- Battlefield Experience: [[Odin]] considered him capable enough to serve as one of [[Asgard]]'s active defenders during Ragnarök.
The novel does not identify Balder's preferred weapon, Martial Art or a named combat technique.

#### Resistance to Conventional Damage
As a God, Balder possessed significantly greater resistance to ordinary physical and supernatural attacks than Mortals.
However, [[Fenrir]]'s killing attack did not rely upon normal physical damage.
Fenrir's jaws contained an Aspect of The End, and their victims were described as being erased from existence.[1]
Balder therefore demonstrated no resistance sufficient to counter:
- Existence Erasure
- The End
- Conceptual Destruction

### Power
Balder was a Major God and is indexed in the existing power hierarchy as a Low Level Combat God.
His placement is consistent with his battlefield role during Ragnarök: [[Odin]] assigned Balder and [[Vidar]] together to fight [[Fenrir]] rather than treating either God as individually capable of defeating the Beast of Ragnarök.[2]
Fenrir existed on a dramatically higher level.
By the time of Ragnarök, Fenrir was already powerful enough to threaten or defeat Gods of Balder's level while possessing an Aspect of The End. Once Fenrir changed into a smaller and faster form, Balder and his companion were unable to react before his jaws reached their necks.[1]
Balder consequently should not be scaled to Fenrir simply because he was assigned to fight him.
His demonstrated hierarchy is more accurately summarized as:
Character
Relative Position
Fenrir
Vastly superior; possessed an Aspect of The End and killed Balder once he seriously exploited his speed.[1]
Balder
Major God / Low Level Combat God
Vidar
Comparable battlefield assignment; fought Fenrir alongside Balder.[2]
No evidence places Balder at God-King, Primordial God or higher levels.

### Relationships

#### Odin
[[Odin]] was Balder's father, God-King and ruler.
Balder fought for Odin's faction during Ragnarök and was deployed directly against [[Fenrir]] as one of [[Asgard]]'s active combat Gods.[2]
Odin reacted with immediate rage when Fenrir's attack apparently destroyed his sons, but Hela prevented him from properly responding by continuing her assault the moment his attention shifted toward his family.[1]
Balder's death was therefore one of several personal tragedies Hela deliberately exploited to psychologically destabilize Odin during the war.

#### Frigg
[[Frigg]] was Balder's mother and Queen of [[Asgard]].
Following [[Fenrir]]'s attack upon the two Gods fighting him, Frigg's scream echoed across the battlefield.[1]
The scene strongly connects her reaction to the destruction of [[Odin]]'s sons immediately before Frigg herself became a target of Ragnarök.
Shortly afterward, Nidhoggr unleashed a breath of pure Darkness against Odin's palace, erasing the entire structure and everyone within it from existence. Frigg was killed during the attack.[1]
Balder and Frigg therefore died within the same rapidly collapsing phase of Ragnarök.

#### Vidar
[[Vidar]] was Balder's brother and combat partner during Ragnarök.
The two fought [[Fenrir]] together under [[Odin]]'s defensive strategy.[2]
Chapter 959 appears to depict Fenrir erasing both brothers, but Vidar inexplicably reappears alive in Chapter 961.[1][3]
This continuity problem should not be used to erase Balder's death from the article, as Balder himself never reappears.

#### Thor
[[Thor]] was Balder's brother and another major combatant during Ragnarök.
While Balder and [[Vidar]] fought [[Fenrir]], Thor was occupied with [[Jormungandr]], preventing him from assisting the rest of [[Odin]]'s family as Hela's strategy began overwhelming [[Asgard]].[2][1]

#### Tyr
Tyr was one of Balder's brothers and another combat-oriented Third Generation God.
Tyr survived the opening battle long enough to be confronted by [[Leonidas I]] during Hela's later purge of [[Asgard]].[3]
No direct interaction between Balder and Tyr is shown.

#### Heimdall
[[Heimdall]] was one of Balder's brothers and served as one of [[Asgard]]'s principal guardians.
After Balder's death and the rapid collapse of [[Odin]]'s forces, Odin ordered Heimdall to activate every remaining defensive system. Heimdall responded by raising the Roots of [[Yggdrasil]] throughout strategic areas of Asgard.[1]

#### Vali
[[Vali]] was another of [[Odin]]'s sons.
Vali had already been killed through Hela's manipulations before Ragnarök, although Odin did not learn this until after Balder's death. Hela manifested Vali's corpse during the battle specifically to further destroy Odin emotionally.[1]

#### Fenrir
[[Fenrir]] was Balder's final opponent and killer.
Balder and [[Vidar]] fought the enormous Divine Wolf together during Ragnarök.[2]
Fenrir later transformed rapidly into a smaller body, catching his opponents off guard. He then used his superior speed to reach their necks and erase the Gods through jaws empowered by The End.[1]
Balder never reappeared after this attack.
Unlike traditional Norse mythology, where Balder's death is normally associated with [[Loki]] and mistletoe before Ragnarök itself, MTW instead has Balder die directly during Ragnarök while fighting Fenrir.

- He is the son of [[Odin]] and [[Frigg]].

- Frigg screamed immediately after Fenrir's attack.[1]

- Traditional mythology commonly portrays Balder as exceptionally beautiful, beloved and nearly invulnerable to harm except through mistletoe. None of those specific mythological properties have been demonstrated by the MTW version and should not automatically be added as canon abilities.
- In traditional Norse mythology Balder normally dies before Ragnarök through [[Loki]]'s scheme. In MTW, he directly participates in Ragnarök and is killed by Fenrir.
