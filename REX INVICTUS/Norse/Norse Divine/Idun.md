### Appearances

#### Debut
Chapter 943
My brother's blood will be repaid with the blood of your family. That was the promise I made on that day in the face of your incompetence.
—Idun to [[Odin]], Chapter 961
Idun is an ancient Norse Goddess, the Goddess of Spring and Eternal Youth, and the protector and cultivator of the Apples of Youth. Although she was not one of [[Asgard]]'s principal combat Gods, Idun was considered one of the most strategically important members of the [[Norse Pantheon]] because her Apples strengthened Divine Beings, improved their physical condition and assisted Gods in understanding their own Divinities. Her importance was great enough that Odin, despite being interrupted in the middle of his Ragnarök war council, immediately allowed her to participate rather than dismissing her.[1]
Idun's role within Asgard extended far beyond simply guarding a magical fruit. The Apples were produced through a supernatural relationship between Idun's Divine Power, the trees she cultivated and the vitality of the Norse Dimension itself. Idun was capable of forcing the trees to produce additional fruit when necessary, but doing so extracted increasing quantities of vitality from the surrounding land. During Odin's preparations for Ragnarök, the process became catastrophically dangerous because the Dimension's vitality had stopped naturally recovering. Idun eventually warned Odin that further forced production would not merely leave the Pantheon without Apples for a million years; it could permanently drain the Dimension until its air, soil, water and natural environment became barren.[2]
Unknown to Odin, Idun had already become one of the hidden traitors inside his own faction. Her betrayal was motivated by the true death of her unnamed brother at the hands of Tyr, one of Odin's sons. Odin had handled the murder extraordinarily poorly, punishing Tyr with little more than house arrest. Idun never forgave the decision and eventually entered an agreement with Hela, helping facilitate Odin's defeat during Ragnarök in exchange for revenge and limitations on the unnecessary slaughter of other Norse Gods.[3]
Idun's betrayal was therefore considerably more complex than a sudden defection during the war. Despite harboring intense hatred toward Odin and his family, she objected to the indiscriminate killing of unrelated Gods and explicitly pressured Hela to fulfill their agreement once Odin had already been defeated. Hela subsequently ordered her armies to apprehend the important Gods designated on her list, imprison those she had blacklisted and reserve execution for those specifically placed on the death list.[3] This combination of compassion and deeply personal vengeance makes Idun one of the more morally complicated survivors of Ragnarök: she was willing to betray the Pantheon she had served for eons to avenge her brother, yet still attempted to prevent the conflict from becoming the indiscriminate extermination of every Asgardian.
Following Ragnarök, Idun ceased being an active member of Odin's former Norse regime. Her exact residence and political allegiance afterward have not been directly explored through Chapter 1165. She remains alive, but the story has not yet shown whether she permanently joined Hela, relocated to Dragons Nest, remained with surviving Norse Gods or established herself elsewhere.

### Physical Appearance
Idun is a female Goddess with long blonde hair.[1] Her full appearance has not received an extensive canonical description, and details such as her eye color, height, clothing and Divine transformation remain unknown.
During her debut she entered [[Odin]]'s war room in [[Asgard]] while the God-King was meeting with [[Thor]], [[Loki]], [[Freyja|Freya]] and [[Heimdall]]. Nothing in the scene describes Idun as physically imposing or armored for battle; her importance came from the Divine resource she controlled rather than from an immediately threatening warrior-like appearance.[1]
The current images used by the wiki should therefore be treated as artistic interpretations rather than exact novel references beyond her established blonde hair.

### Personality
Idun initially appears as a responsible, practical and unusually direct Goddess. She was willing to interrupt [[Odin]]'s Ragnarök war council because the rate at which he was consuming her Apple reserves had become unsustainable. Although Odin was the God-King of [[Asgard]], Idun did not hesitate to confront him when his military decisions threatened the future survival of the Dimension itself.[1][2]
Her concern was not limited to preserving her own trees. When Odin initially stated that he was willing to exhaust the current Apple reserves even if the Pantheon required a million years to recover, Idun angrily explained that he fundamentally misunderstood the problem. The surrounding environment itself was losing vitality, and further forced harvesting could render the entire Dimension permanently barren even if Asgard won Ragnarök.[2] This demonstrates that Idun understood her responsibility as extending beyond a military resource; the ecological stability of the Norse Dimension mattered more to her than blindly obeying Odin's wartime demands.
Idun is also highly knowledgeable about the Apple trees and immediately recognized changes in their vitality that the other Gods had failed to notice. She understood that temporary reductions in natural Energy were normally recoverable and became alarmed only when the Dimension stopped replenishing itself, showing that she had maintained and observed the system for long enough to distinguish normal fluctuation from a genuine existential problem.[2]
Beneath Idun's ordinarily responsible and comparatively benevolent demeanor exists an extraordinary capacity for hatred. The murder of her brother fundamentally changed her relationship with Odin's family. Tyr did not merely incapacitate the unnamed God; the narration explicitly clarifies that Idun's brother suffered true death and passed on completely. Odin then responded to the murder by placing his son under house arrest, an outcome Idun regarded as an unforgivable display of incompetence and favoritism.[3]
Idun preserved this resentment for an unknown but presumably significant period of time without openly exposing herself. When she eventually revealed her betrayal, her eyes were described as resembling a spiral of pure hatred, and she informed Odin that she had sworn to repay her brother's blood with the blood of his family.[3] Her ability to conceal this hatred from Odin until Hela's victory also demonstrates considerable patience and emotional self-control.
Despite her desire for revenge, Idun retained moral limits that distinguished her from Hela. Once Odin had effectively lost, Idun became frustrated by Hela continuing to torment him instead of completing their agreement. She demanded that Hela stop wasting time, rebuild the devastated realm and cease killing Gods who were not directly responsible for the conflict. Hela rejected Idun's description of any Norse God as "innocent" but nevertheless honored their agreement by dividing the survivors into groups meant for capture, imprisonment or execution.[3]
This reveals that Idun's betrayal was targeted rather than nihilistic. She wanted Odin's family to suffer for the injustice committed against her brother, but she did not share Hela's hatred toward the entirety of Asgard.
Idun can also be unexpectedly susceptible to [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s presence. When Victor Elderblood appeared after Odin's defeat, Idun was momentarily left breathless simply from seeing him and nearly suffered what the narration humorously described as a heart attack. Later, Victor manipulated Hela conversationally so convincingly that even Idun began looking at Hela as though she were an extraordinarily cruel woman despite Hela knowing Victor was deliberately acting.[3] This should not be mistaken for a romantic relationship, but it does demonstrate that Idun was not immune to Victor's supernatural charisma and social manipulation.

### Synopsis

#### Dragon God of Chaos
Idun entered the story during the final preparations for Ragnarök. Nyx had secretly infiltrated [[Asgard]] while [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction observed the Norse Gods preparing for the coming civil war. [[Odin]] gathered [[Thor]], [[Freyja|Freya]], [[Loki]] and [[Heimdall]] in his war room and refused the suggestion that he request assistance from other Pantheons, believing that outside powers would exploit Asgard's weakness if they learned how severe the crisis had become. The entire Norse Dimension was therefore sealed while Odin attempted to prepare a purely Asgardian response.[1]
Before the meeting could properly begin, Idun abruptly entered the room and informed Odin that the stocks under her control were declining far too rapidly because of his orders to strengthen the main army. Odin initially prepared to reprimand whoever had interrupted him but immediately changed his attitude after realizing it was Idun, with the narration describing her as a woman of exceptional importance to all of Asgard. He invited her to remain for the war council so that the resource crisis could be addressed alongside the military strategy.[1]
While secretly watching the conversation, [[Aphrodite]] Elderblood explained Idun's identity to Victor's other wives. Idun was the Goddess of Spring and Eternal Youth and the protector of the Apples of Youth, Divine fruits that helped Norse Gods develop a better understanding of their Divinity while keeping their bodies healthy and protecting them from various dangerous supernatural conditions. Aphrodite specifically mentioned resistance against substances such as Hydra Poison, Dragon Poison and Demonic Miasma, immediately revealing why Idun represented such an important strategic asset to a Pantheon preparing for war.[1]
Violet responded by casually proposing that they kidnap Idun. What initially sounded like one of Violet's jokes quickly became a serious discussion once Victor's faction acquired several Apples and examined their properties. The Elderblood family had already concluded that if the [[Norse Pantheon]] was determined to destroy itself through civil war, valuable people, races and supernatural resources should not simply be allowed to disappear with it.[1][2]
Idun's concerns became considerably more severe as Odin's war council continued. Odin stated that every available resource would be spent because the coming conflict would determine the future of the Pantheon. He was even willing to accept losing access to Idun's fruits for the following million years if exhausting the reserves allowed Asgard to win.[2]
Idun immediately told him that the problem was much worse than he understood. The vitality within her Apples was rapidly declining, and the deterioration was no longer limited to the trees. The soil, air, water and surrounding Nature itself were losing vitality. According to Idun, the entire Norse Dimension was dying.[2]
Normally, the vitality required to create the Apples could be replenished naturally. Idun could use her Divine Power to force additional trees to bear fruit without causing permanent harm because the Energy withdrawn from the Dimension eventually returned. During the Ragnarök preparations, however, that recovery had stopped. The previously renewable supply had effectively become finite, meaning that forcing the trees to continue producing at Odin's requested rate could consume what remained and leave Asgard completely barren.[2]
The situation was made more ironic by Nyx secretly stealing the exact Apples whose diminishing quantity Idun and Odin were discussing. Nyx ate them while invisible inside the war room and used War and Victor's other Heralds to transport baskets back to the observing Dragonesses. Idun stated that Asgard still possessed 722 Apples; after Nyx's theft, the actual number had already fallen to 666 without anyone in Odin's faction realizing what had happened.[2]
The stolen Apples were immediately tested. Even the Dragonesses—whose bodies and Energy reserves vastly exceeded those of ordinary Gods—found the fruit useful. [[Scathach Elderblood Scarlett]] could feel her Energy being slightly boosted after eating one, while [[Velnorah Xyphora Elderblood Thaloria II]] analyzed another and concluded that the Apples could become the basis of powerful enhancement medicines.[2]
Velnorah theorized that refining Idun's Apples alongside resources from Victor's territory could create an Elixir that improved comprehension of Divinity. Combining them with herbs grown near regions saturated by Victor's Dragon Energy could potentially strengthen the body as well. The complete formulation would be too powerful and biologically incompatible for ordinary non-Dragons, but diluted versions could theoretically be mass-produced as enhancement potions for Victor's armies.[2]
The discovery transformed Violet's kidnapping joke into a unanimous strategic proposal. Velnorah formally suggested abducting Idun, Violet immediately agreed, and Eleonor, Aphrodite, [[Scathach]] and Nyx supported the idea. Aphrodite specifically imagined Idun collaborating with [[Demeter]], the Fairy [[Valeria Alekerth]] and [[Gaia]] Elderblood, predicting that combining their specialties could produce unique resources unavailable anywhere outside Victor's faction. Sasha objected only to calling the plan "kidnapping", insisting that they were going to "invite" Idun instead, although everyone else immediately recognized that the practical distinction was nonexistent.[2]
Idun remained completely unaware of the conversation, experiencing only an unexplained shiver while Nyx watched her as though she were a valuable golden goose. Odin ultimately ordered Idun to stop allowing the Gods to consume the remaining Apples casually and preserve them for the Pantheon's highest-ranking elites once Ragnarök began.[2]
The Apples soon became useful to Victor for another reason. After receiving one of the stolen fruits through his Heralds, Victor presented it to Hela during their negotiations. Hela reacted with obvious desire because several dozen Apples would have been sufficient to restore her drastically depleted condition. Victor analyzed the fruit and discovered that he could reproduce its raw vitality relatively easily, but not the complete effect produced through Idun's Divinity.[4]
Victor concluded that Idun's Apples were fundamentally crystallized vitality enhanced by a unique Divine property. Idun's version both nourished a God's body and helped the recipient better comprehend their own Divine Concept, making the fruit simultaneously a healing resource and a tool for Divine progression. Victor could create an imitation containing even greater quantities of pure vitality, but his version lacked the secondary comprehension effect because that property came specifically from Idun's personal Divinity.[4]
This distinction established that Idun herself—not merely the species of tree—was the reason the Apples were so valuable.
When Ragnarök finally erupted, Idun's true position was revealed. Hela's preparations had extended much further into Odin's own faction than the God-King realized, and after Hela defeated and dismembered him, Idun appeared openly on her side.[3]
Odin was stunned by the betrayal because Idun had been someone he trusted deeply. Idun showed little sympathy for his condition and instead became irritated that Hela continued tormenting him rather than finishing their plan. She demanded to know why Odin was still alive, told Hela to stop playing with him and insisted that they begin rebuilding the devastated realm while ending the unnecessary killing of unrelated Gods.[3]
Hela replied that there were no innocent Gods but nevertheless acknowledged that Idun was referring to the terms of their agreement. She consequently ordered her commanders [[Leonidas I]], [[Julius Caesar]] and [[Vlad III Tepes]] to begin the second phase of the operation: Gods considered essential were to be apprehended, those on the blacklist were to be detained and only those explicitly placed on the death list were still authorized for execution.[3]
Odin demanded to know why Idun had joined Hela. Idun answered that she had never forgotten what happened to her brother. One of Odin's sons, Tyr, had killed him so completely that he did not merely enter a Divine coma; he suffered true death and passed on. Odin himself remembered the event and admitted internally that he had handled it terribly. Tyr's punishment amounted to little more than temporary house arrest despite permanently killing another God.[3]
Idun had never forgiven this injustice. She declared that her brother's blood would be repaid with the blood of Odin's family and revealed that she had made that promise on the day Odin failed to properly punish Tyr. Her hatred was strong enough that she ultimately betrayed the Pantheon she had served for eons in order to obtain revenge.[3]
Hela used the revelation to further humiliate Odin, telling him that Idun was not the only person close to him whose hatred had gone unnoticed because of his arrogance. The betrayal therefore became another example of Hela's central strategy during Ragnarök: rather than defeating Odin exclusively through superior strength, she weaponized the resentment created by centuries of his own decisions.[3]
Victor then appeared directly before Hela and Idun after the main battle. Idun was momentarily stunned by Victor's appearance, while Hela—already familiar with him—reacted far more cautiously. Their interaction did not establish a direct relationship between Victor and Idun, but it confirmed that she survived the battle and remained present during Hela's consolidation of the defeated Norse faction.[3]

### Skills and Abilities
Idun is primarily a non-combat Divine specialist whose established abilities revolve around Spring, Eternal Youth, vitality and the cultivation of the Apples of Youth. The novel has not shown her participating directly in battle, so generic combat powers should not be added simply because she is an ancient Goddess.
- God Physiology: Idun is an ancient Third Generation Norse Goddess. As a Divine Being she possesses a supernatural existence and Divine Power, but the novel has not individually demonstrated enough of her physical abilities to justify assigning her a generic list of superhuman strength, speed, regeneration or combat techniques.
- Divinity of Spring: Idun is explicitly identified as the Goddess of Spring.[1] Her demonstrated applications are connected primarily to Nature, vitality and the cultivation of the Apples of Youth rather than direct offensive manipulation.
- Nature/Vitality Sensitivity: Idun could perceive the deterioration of vitality throughout [[Asgard]]'s soil, water, air and surrounding natural environment before the other Gods recognized the extent of the problem.[2]
- Divine Cultivation: She uses her Divine Power to influence the trees responsible for producing the Apples of Youth and can forcibly accelerate their fruit production.[2]
- Environmental Dependency: Her accelerated cultivation does not create vitality from nothing. Producing excessive quantities of Apples withdraws vitality from the surrounding Dimension, creating potentially catastrophic consequences when natural recovery cannot replace what is consumed.[2]
- Divinity of Eternal Youth: Idun is explicitly identified as the Goddess of Eternal Youth.[1] Its clearest demonstrated manifestation occurs through the special properties she gives to the Apples of Youth.
- Vitality Bestowal: Idun's Apples provide considerable vitality to whoever consumes them, nourishing and restoring the recipient's body.[2][4]
- Health Preservation: [[Aphrodite]] explains that the Apples maintain a healthy Divine body and prevent or counter several adverse supernatural conditions.[1]
- Status-Effect Resistance: Known examples of conditions the Apples protect against include Hydra Poison, Dragon Poison and Demonic Miasma.[1]
- Energy Enhancement: Even a True Dragon such as [[Scathach]] could perceive a small increase in her Energy after consuming one of Idun's Apples.[2]
- Divine Concept Enhancement: The most valuable special property of Idun's Apples is their ability to help Gods obtain a better understanding of their own Divinity.[1][4]
- Divinity Comprehension: [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s direct analysis established that the fruit does more than restore vitality; it helps the God consuming it better comprehend their Divine Concept and therefore supports supernatural progression.[4]
- Unique Divine Imprint: Victor could easily reproduce the raw vitality composing the fruit but could not reproduce its conceptual-comprehension property merely through imitation. He explicitly recognized this secondary effect as a product of Idun's own Divinity.[4]
- Potential Elixir Creation: Velnorah concluded that the Apples could be refined alongside other materials into Elixirs designed to improve Divinity comprehension.[2]
- [[Apple of Youth]] Cultivation: Idun is the guardian and active cultivator of the Apples of Youth, making her one of Asgard's principal strategic support Gods.
- Forced Harvesting: Through Divine Power she can force the trees to produce additional fruit beyond their ordinary rate.[2]
- Large-Scale Production: Before Ragnarök, Idun had accumulated hundreds of Apples for use by Asgard's Divine forces, with 722 remaining before Nyx's theft reduced the actual stockpile.[2]
- Resource Management: Idun monitored both stored quantities and the condition of the trees closely enough to recognize that [[Odin]]'s wartime consumption rate could no longer be sustained.[1][2]
- Dimensional Vitality Conversion: Apple production ultimately draws vitality from the surrounding Norse Dimension. Under normal conditions this Energy replenishes naturally, but extreme overproduction can exhaust it faster than restoration occurs.[2]
- Potential Ecological Devastation: Idun warned that continuing the accelerated harvest after the Dimension stopped recovering could permanently drain its natural vitality and render the land barren.[2]
- Divine Knowledge — Natural Vitality: Idun possesses extensive specialized knowledge concerning the interaction between Divine cultivation, natural vitality and the health of a Divine Dimension. She recognized environmental deterioration that Odin and the other Gods had completely overlooked and correctly distinguished temporary Energy decline from irreversible depletion.[2]
- Strategic Planning / Deception: Idun successfully concealed her hatred of Odin and her alliance with Hela until the final stage of Ragnarök. Odin was genuinely shocked by her betrayal despite having known her for an immense period of time.[3] The exact duration of her agreement with Hela has not been revealed.

### The Apple of Youth
Main article: [[Apple of Youth]]
The Apple of Youth is the Divine resource most closely associated with Idun and the principal reason she held such strategic importance within [[Asgard]]. Although commonly treated as a magical fruit, [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s analysis demonstrated that its full properties depend directly upon Idun's Divinity rather than solely upon the tree from which it grows.[4]
Its currently demonstrated effects include:
- Providing large quantities of vitality and nourishing the body.[4]
- Maintaining the health of Divine Beings.[1]
- Protecting against several harmful supernatural substances and conditions, including Hydra Poison, Dragon Poison and Demonic Miasma.[1]
- Slightly increasing Energy even in extremely powerful recipients such as [[True Dragons]].[2]
- Helping Gods better understand their own Divine Concept.[1][4]
- Serving as a potential base ingredient for advanced Elixirs intended to enhance Divinity comprehension and physical development.[2]
Victor described the physical component of the fruit as essentially crystallized vitality. He could reproduce an imitation containing even greater raw vitality than the original, but his artificial version would function primarily as a healing item because he could not duplicate Idun's unique Divinity-based comprehension effect through simple reconstruction.[4]
This makes Idun herself substantially more valuable than possessing a finite stockpile of her Apples.

### Relationships

#### Odin
Idun served under [[Odin]] for an immense period and was considered sufficiently important to [[Asgard]] that Odin treated her with unusual respect even during his final preparations for Ragnarök.[1]
Their relationship was secretly destroyed by Odin's handling of her brother's death. Tyr, one of Odin's sons, permanently killed Idun's brother, yet Odin punished him only with house arrest. Idun interpreted the response as an unforgivable failure of justice and swore that Odin's family would eventually pay for her brother's blood.[3]
Odin apparently remained unaware of how deeply the incident had affected her. When Idun finally revealed herself as Hela's ally, he was genuinely unable to understand why she had betrayed him until she forced him to remember the murder.[3]
His failure to recognize Idun's hatred became another example of the arrogance Hela exploited to bring about his fall.

#### Hela Elderblood
Idun secretly allied herself with Hela before the final resolution of Ragnarök.
Their alliance was founded upon overlapping grievances against [[Odin]] rather than identical motivations. Hela wanted revenge against Odin and much of [[Asgard]] for the persecution of herself and her family, while Idun specifically wanted justice for her murdered brother.[3]
Idun also imposed limits upon their arrangement. Once Odin was defeated she demanded that Hela stop prolonging the killing, begin rebuilding Asgard and honor their agreement regarding the surviving Gods. Hela subsequently ordered important Gods to be captured, blacklisted Gods to be detained and only designated targets to be killed.[3]
Their interaction demonstrates that Idun was an ally rather than simply another subordinate following Hela's worldview.

#### Tyr
Tyr was the murderer of Idun's unnamed brother and the primary personal cause behind her betrayal of [[Odin]].
The death was absolute. Idun's brother did not merely enter the comatose condition sometimes experienced by defeated Gods; he truly died and moved on.[3]
Odin's decision to respond with little more than house arrest transformed Idun's grief into long-term hatred toward the ruling family. Her eventual cooperation with Hela was explicitly motivated by her promise to repay her brother's death with the blood of Odin's family.[3]

### Unnamed Brother
Idun possessed an unnamed brother who was killed by Tyr before the events of Ragnarök.
Almost nothing else has been revealed concerning him. His name, Divinities, generation, occupation and relationship with the other Norse Gods remain unknown. His death was nevertheless one of the hidden historical events that directly contributed to the eventual collapse of [[Odin]]'s rule.[3]

#### Victor Elderblood
Idun and [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] have had only limited direct interaction, but Victor's faction took an immediate interest in her because of the strategic value of her Divinity.
Victor's wives first observed her secretly through Nyx and rapidly concluded that recruiting or kidnapping her could greatly improve the Elderblood faction's ability to manufacture Divine medicines. [[Aphrodite]] specifically imagined combining Idun's talents with [[Gaia]], [[Demeter]] and Valeria.[2]
Victor later personally analyzed one of Idun's stolen Apples and discovered that its unique ability to improve comprehension of Divinity originated specifically from Idun herself rather than from simple vitality contained in the fruit.[4]
After Ragnarök, Victor appeared before Idun and Hela. Idun was briefly overwhelmed by his appearance and later became susceptible to his deliberately manipulative performance during his conversation with Hela.[3]
Despite the earlier plan to "invite" her, no chapter through Chapter 1165 confirms that Idun permanently joined Victor's faction.

#### Nyx Elderblood
Nyx covertly observed Idun while infiltrating [[Odin]]'s war council and became directly responsible for stealing a substantial number of her Apples.
Idun believed [[Asgard]] possessed 722 remaining Apples, but Nyx had already reduced the true quantity to 666 without anyone in the room noticing. She subsequently stole additional baskets and sent the fruit to [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s wives using War.[2]
After the Elderblood women decided that Idun should be recruited, Nyx agreed to handle the opportunity when one appeared and watched Idun with enough predatory interest that the unaware Goddess suddenly experienced an unexplained shiver.[2]

#### Violet Elderblood Snow
Violet was the first person in [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction to suggest kidnapping Idun after [[Aphrodite]] explained the value of the Apples of Youth.[1]
What initially sounded like a joke became an actual proposal after the Apples were tested. Violet strongly supported acquiring Idun for the faction and regarded her talents as too useful to risk losing during the destruction of the [[Norse Pantheon]].[2]

### Bragi
Bragi is listed as Idun's husband in existing Norse records associated with the character, but the main novel chapters currently examined do not substantially explore their marriage or his involvement in Idun's story. His status during and after Ragnarök has not been clarified in Idun's on-page material.

### Current Status
Idun survived Ragnarök and remains classified as Alive.
Her final substantial appearance occurs after [[Odin]]'s defeat in Chapter 961, where she openly reveals her alliance with Hela and participates in the immediate post-war decisions concerning the surviving Norse Gods.[3]
No later chapter through Chapter 1165 provides a definitive update concerning where she currently lives, whether she remained with Hela, whether she joined Dragons Nest, whether she continues cultivating the Apples of Youth or what became of Bragi.
Because she openly betrayed Odin and the old Asgardian regime, she is more accurately described as a former member of Odin's Norse faction rather than continuing to list her allegiance to the [[Norse Pantheon]] without qualification.
