**[[Borr]]** is an ancient God of the Norse Pantheon, a First Generation Primordial God and one of the old Divine Beings associated with the Concept of Creation. (Lore adapted from *My Three Wives Are Beautiful Vampires*.)

> **Rex Invictus canon.** This material is adapted into the Rex Invictus world at the lowest priority: Rex Invictus wins any conflict, then the Flamel material, then MTWABV. Events of the MTWABV timeline (Victor's conquests, the fall of the pantheons, the [[Elderblood Empire]]) are distant background or rumor unless a chapter puts them on the page. ### Appearances

#### First Mention
Chapter 521
Borr is an ancient God of the [[Norse Pantheon]], a First Generation Primordial God and one of the old Divine Beings associated with the Concept of Creation. His only direct mention in the main story occurs during the beginning of the Demonic War, when [[Shiva]] explains that Creation was never an authority exclusive to the Heavenly Father. Borr is specifically named alongside Shiva, [[Prometheus]] and [[Odin]] as one of the Gods who possessed influence over Creation and used that Concept to produce various creations, including members of the Human Race.[2]
Additional information supplied by the author identifies Borr with the Primordial Divinity of Death.[1] This places him among the ancient Norse [[Primordial Gods]] possessing authority over more than one major Divine domain, although the story has never shown Borr personally using either his Creation or Death powers in combat.
Unlike Odin, Hela, [[Thor]], [[Loki]], [[Fenrir]] and several other members of the Norse Divine order, Borr has never appeared directly within the narrative. His appearance, personality, exact history, current condition and complete relationship with the later Norse Gods therefore remain largely unknown. The page is retained primarily to index a canonically named ancient God whose Creation authority is directly established by the novel and whose Death Divinity is identified through an author statement.

### Physical Appearance
Borr has never made an on-page appearance and possesses no canonical physical description. His height, body type, hair color, eye color, clothing, Divine manifestation and any appearance associated with his Creation or Death Divinities remain unknown.
The image currently used for Borr is therefore an artistic interpretation rather than an official depiction.

### Personality
Nothing is currently known about Borr's personality. He has never spoken, appeared directly or received a perspective scene in the novel.
His beliefs concerning Humanity, the [[Norse Pantheon]], [[Odin]], the other Creator Gods or the use of his Divine authorities have likewise never been explored. Personality traits from traditional Norse mythology should not automatically be applied to the MTW character without independent confirmation from the novel.

### Background

#### Ancient Norse Primordial God
Borr belongs to the ancient Divine history of the [[Norse Pantheon]]. The setting's Primordial hierarchy places him among the First Generation or Original [[Primordial Gods]], the ancient Divine class that precedes later generations of Gods.
This classification makes Borr part of the foundational Divine era of the Norse Pantheon rather than a conventional later God. However, unlike the original Greek Primordials such as [[Gaia]], Nyx, [[Erebus]], [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]] and [[Ouranos]], the story has not yet explored the circumstances surrounding Borr's birth or the earliest history of his Pantheon in significant detail.
The exact mechanism through which Borr became a Primordial God is unknown. The novel has not established his parents, creator, place of birth or whether his Creation and Death Divinities were innate from the beginning of his existence.

#### Creation
The clearest canonical information concerning Borr comes from Chapter 521. During the meeting preceding the wider Demonic War, [[Shiva]] rejects the idea that Earth's Pantheons would remain uninvolved in a conflict threatening Humanity.
Shiva explains that all of Earth's major mythological systems had participated in the creation of Humans and that the Concept of Creation did not belong exclusively to the Heavenly Father. He specifically identifies:
- Shiva;
- [[Prometheus]];
- Borr;
- and [[Odin]]
as Gods possessing influence over Creation.[2]
The Gods used that Concept to produce various creations, including Human beings.[2] Borr therefore possesses direct canonical involvement with the Creation authority shared among several of Earth's ancient Pantheons.
The statement does not establish that Borr single-handedly created Humanity, nor that the entire Human Race originated exclusively from the [[Norse Pantheon]]. Humanity's creation involved multiple Divine traditions and Creator Gods, with the Heavenly Father possessing particularly important involvement in the Human lineage.

#### Human Creation
Borr's participation in Creation also places him among the Divine Beings responsible for the supernatural origins of Humanity.
Chapter 521 establishes that various Pantheons possessed their own Gods with influence over Creation and that these Gods created numerous things, with Humans specifically included among those creations.[2] Borr's role should therefore be described as participation in the broader Divine creation of Humanity rather than treating him as Humanity's sole creator.
The precise contribution made by Borr has never been explained. It is unknown whether he created particular populations, helped establish the Human body, contributed [[Divine Energy]], participated in the formation of the species alongside other Creator Gods or performed an entirely different role.

#### Death
The author has separately identified Borr as a Primordial God of Death.[1]
This makes Death another confirmed aspect of his Divine identity, but substantially less is known about this authority than his connection to Creation. Borr has never demonstrated Death Manipulation on-page, and no chapter currently explains how his Death Divinity functioned in comparison with other Death-related Gods or entities.
His authority should therefore not automatically be equated with:
- [[Hela Elderblood]]'s powers over Death and the Norse [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]];
- [[Thanatos]]' Divine relationship with Death;
- [[Odin]]'s Death-related Divinity;
- [[True Death]], the Primordial Entity governing the fundamental Universal aspect of Death;
- or the Judges of the Abyss and their role within the cycle of Souls.
Possessing a Death Divinity makes Borr a Divine user of that Concept; it does not make him the absolute Universal embodiment of Death.

#### Place in Norse History
Borr is one of the oldest known Gods associated with the Norse Divine lineage, while [[Odin]] became the later God-King and ruler of [[Asgard]].
The main narrative has not yet provided a detailed transition between Borr's ancient era and Odin's rule. Borr is not shown during the modern Norse storyline or the events surrounding Ragnarök, and the story does not explain whether he died before Odin's reign, disappeared, withdrew from the Pantheon or remained elsewhere.
For that reason, Borr's current status should remain Unknown rather than being listed as Alive or Deceased without a supporting chapter.

### Synopsis

#### Demonic War
Borr is first mentioned during the supernatural meeting immediately preceding the full escalation of the Demonic War. [[Diablo]] and Lilith attempted to prevent the other major supernatural powers from interfering in the conflict involving Humanity, Heaven and Hell.
[[Shiva]] dismissed the request as unrealistic because Earth's Pantheons possessed their own interests in Humanity. He explained that every mythology had been involved with Human creation in some manner and specifically rejected the idea that the Concept of Creation was exclusive to the Heavenly Father.[2]
Borr was named together with Shiva, [[Prometheus]] and [[Odin]] as one of the Gods who possessed influence over the Concept of Creation. These Gods had used that Concept to create various things, including Human beings.[2]
The statement establishes Borr as a genuine MTW character and Creator God even though he does not personally appear in the scene.

#### Dragon God of Chaos
Borr does not appear during the later Ragnarök storyline. The conflict instead centers upon [[Odin]], Hela, [[Fenrir]], [[Jormungandr]], Nidhoggr, [[Loki]] and the later generations of Norse Gods.
The absence of Borr during the destruction of Odin's regime does not by itself establish that he was dead. No later chapter currently explains his location or condition.

### Skills & Abilities
Borr has never participated in an on-page battle, so his complete abilities cannot presently be reconstructed. Only his confirmed Divine classifications and Concepts are indexed.

#### Primordial God Physiology
- God Physiology: Borr is explicitly identified among the Gods possessing influence over the Concept of Creation.[2]
- First Generation Primordial God Physiology: Borr belongs to the ancient Original Primordial generation of the Norse Divine lineage.
- [[Divine Energy]]: As a Primordial God, Borr possesses the Divine Energy required to support his Divinities. The size of his reserves and his personal degree of control have never been shown.
- Divine Soul: Borr possesses a Divine Soul capable of sustaining Primordial Godhood and multiple Divine Concepts. No individual Soul technique has been demonstrated.
- Agelessness: As an ancient Primordial God, Borr is not limited by ordinary Human aging.
- Supernatural Physical Characteristics: His Divine physiology places him vastly beyond ordinary Mortals, although no personal strength, speed or durability feat has been shown.

#### Creation
- Divinity — Creation: Borr possesses influence over the Concept of Creation. [[Shiva]] directly names him as one of several Gods whose Creation authority contributed to various creations, including Humanity.[2]
- Creation: Borr could use the Concept of Creation to produce new creations. The precise limits, process and Energy cost of this power have never been demonstrated.[2]
- Life Creation (Broadly confirmed): Humans are specifically included among the creations produced through the Creation authority wielded by Borr and other Creator Gods.[2] The exact portion of Human creation personally attributable to Borr remains unknown.
- Creation Energy: As a God connected to Creation, Borr belongs to the class of Divine Beings able to utilize the Creation-related Energy associated with that Concept. No personal display of this Energy has yet occurred.

#### Death
- Divinity — Death: Borr is identified by the author as a Primordial God of Death.[1]
- Death Authority: His Divinity grants a Divine relationship with the Concept of Death. The specific capabilities derived from this authority remain unknown because Borr has never used it on-page.
- Conceptual Authority: As a Primordial God possessing Death as a Divinity, Borr could exercise Divine authority through that Concept. This should not be expanded into specific abilities such as instant death, Soul destruction, resurrection denial or [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] control unless the story later demonstrates them.

### Power
Borr belongs to the First Generation Primordial God class and therefore originates from one of the oldest and highest natural Divine generations associated with Earth's Pantheons.
His exact combat power remains completely unknown.
No chapter has shown Borr fighting, releasing his [[Divine Energy]], confronting another Primordial God or performing a destructive feat. His Creation authority demonstrates significant conceptual capability, while his Death Divinity establishes another major Divine domain, but neither provides enough information to calculate a specific combat level.

### Relationships

#### Norse Pantheon
Borr belongs to the ancient [[Norse Pantheon]] and predates the modern Divine order most prominently associated with [[Odin]] and [[Asgard]].
His specific political position within the ancient Pantheon has not been explained. The novel does not currently state whether Borr ruled Asgard, founded the Norse Pantheon or directly governed its later Gods.

#### Odin
[[Odin]] is another Norse God explicitly identified in Chapter 521 as possessing influence over Creation.[2]
Traditional Norse mythology identifies Borr as Odin's father, and older versions of this wiki incorporated that genealogy into Borr's article and family tree. However, the currently verified MTW chapter material does not independently establish their father-son relationship.
Until a novel chapter or direct author statement confirms the relationship within MTW continuity, Odin should not be listed as Borr's confirmed son solely on the basis of external mythology.
The same caution applies to Bestla, Vili and Vé.
