### Appearances

#### Debut
Chapter 833: The [[Black Hole|Singularity]].
Frigg, also called Frigga and Friia, was a Second Generation Goddess, a Major Goddess of the [[Norse Pantheon]], the wife and principal Queen of [[Odin]], and the former Queen of [[Asgard]]. Unlike many figures surrounding Odin who operated primarily as warriors or subordinates, Frigg occupied a political position beside the All-Father and was trusted enough to participate directly in discussions involving foreign threats, alliances and the balance of power between Earth's Pantheons.[2]
Frigg should not be confused with [[Freyja|Freya]]. Although both belonged to the Norse Pantheon and possessed overlapping associations with traditionally positive Divine Concepts, their positions were fundamentally different: Frigg was Odin's Queen and a member of the Æsir royal household, while Freya was the Vanir Goddess who commanded the Valkyries. Frigg's only substantial direct appearance demonstrates that her principal influence within the story was political rather than military.[2]
Despite her limited screen time, Frigg became one of the most personally significant casualties of Ragnarök. Hela deliberately identified Odin's family as the place where he was most emotionally vulnerable and ordered Nidhoggr to attack the royal palace. Nidhoggr's breath of Pure Darkness erased the entire castle and everything within it from existence, including Frigg. Odin's immediate horror at her loss caused him to momentarily lose concentration against Hela, demonstrating the importance Frigg held in his life.[1] Chapter 961 further clarifies that Frigg had not personally wronged Hela; she was targeted because she was Odin's wife. Hela nevertheless gave Frigg a comparatively immediate end rather than subjecting her to the prolonged torture she intended for the remainder of Odin's family.[3]

### Physical Appearance
Frigg was described as a beautiful woman with long brown hair and vibrant green eyes.[2] The novel does not provide a detailed description of her height, physique, clothing or Divine equipment, and no canonical combat form has been shown.
Her appearance and reactions during her debut nevertheless portray her as a mature Goddess rather than a youthful-looking member of the younger Divine generations. She visibly blushed when [[Victor Elderblood]] was discussed, leading [[Odin]] to realize that she had previously acquired photographs of Victor's newly revealed Dragon form.[2]

### Personality
Frigg was composed, perceptive, pragmatic and politically cautious, particularly when contrasted with [[Odin]]'s pride and tendency to respond aggressively when his authority was challenged. Her debut immediately establishes this dynamic: after [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] destroyed the ravens Odin had been using to observe events surrounding the Witches' dimensional crisis, Odin became furious and prepared to involve himself personally. Frigg did not encourage his anger; instead, she warned him against recklessly beginning a conflict and argued that Victor needed to be properly evaluated before [[Asgard]] treated him as an enemy.[2]
She was notably comfortable disagreeing with the All-Father. Frigg did not behave like a subordinate waiting for Odin to make every decision, and Odin did not exclude her from highly sensitive discussions. When a mysterious outsider attempted to recruit him into a dangerous alliance, Frigg observed the negotiation, recognized the growing danger when Odin considered preventing the woman from leaving and restrained him from unnecessarily escalating the confrontation. Afterward, she immediately joined Odin in evaluating where the stranger would search for support next.[2]
Her political assessments were practical rather than based purely upon the individual strength of famous Gods. When Odin considered the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]] the most likely faction to receive the outsider's proposal, Frigg pointed out that its enormous power was offset by a lack of political unity: its strongest Gods could not simply be assumed to act together unless their own territory was directly endangered. The pair similarly considered the Celtic, Shinto and former Greek Pantheons before concluding that the developing situation would probably once again lead toward Victor.[2]
Frigg also possessed a teasing and relatively informal relationship with Odin. When Victor was mentioned, she casually described him as effectively being a Devil and visibly blushed. Odin immediately questioned whether she still possessed photographs of Victor, revealing that he had already deleted the images she had acquired. Frigg treated his jealousy as excessive and dismissed the matter as nothing more than pictures, showing that she was neither intimidated by Odin's possessiveness nor reluctant to mock him when she considered him unreasonable.[2]
Her calm nature should not be mistaken for detachment from Asgard. Frigg remained with Odin's household through the period leading into Ragnarök and was still residing within the royal palace when Hela attacked. The later severity of Odin's reaction to her erasure demonstrates that their marriage was not merely a formal political arrangement.[1]

### History
Very little of Frigg's ancient history is directly explored in the novel. By the time of her first appearance, she was already [[Odin]]'s established wife, Queen of [[Asgard]] and a senior Goddess of the Æsir. Her classification as a Second Generation God places her among an old Divine generation, while her position within the royal household gave her political access exceeding that of most ordinary Norse Gods.
The novel does not presently provide a detailed account of how Frigg became Queen, the beginning of her marriage to Odin or most of the mythological traditions conventionally associated with her. Claims concerning large lists of attendants, lovers or relatives should therefore not automatically be transferred from real-world mythology into the novel's continuity unless the story itself establishes them.
Her known narrative history is instead concentrated around two periods: the growing crisis surrounding [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] during the later era of Earth's Pantheons, and her eventual death during Hela's Ragnarök.

### Synopsis

#### King Among Kings
Frigg made her first directly verified appearance during the dimensional crisis surrounding [[The Arcane Realm]]. While [[Victor Elderblood]] was investigating the unstable connection created by the Witches, he discovered that [[Odin]]'s ravens were secretly observing the situation. Victor immediately destroyed the surveillance creatures with his Draconic power, causing a backlash that left Odin bleeding from the eyes and furious at Victor's interference.[2]
Odin ordered that [[Thor]] and [[Freyja|Freya]] be summoned and began taking the situation more seriously. Frigg was surprised that her husband had finally become personally interested in the developing crisis. When Odin indicated that he intended to involve himself directly, Frigg immediately cautioned him against allowing anger to turn the situation into an unnecessary war. Her concern was not sympathy for Victor but strategic uncertainty: the supernatural world still lacked a complete understanding of what Victor had become, and provoking him before determining his actual capabilities was reckless.[2]
The exchange demonstrated the difference between their temperaments. Odin confidently declared that he did not start wars but finished them, whereas Frigg continued treating the problem as something requiring information and political calculation rather than pride.[2]
Their discussion was interrupted by the arrival of an enormous mysterious woman who attempted to convince Odin to enter an alliance. Her proposed agreement offered substantial benefits but was enforced through a dangerous contract whose violation could destroy the offending party's Soul. Odin refused to recklessly bind himself to an arrangement whose full consequences he did not understand.[2]
When the outsider became dissatisfied and attempted to leave, Odin briefly considered preventing her departure. Frigg intervened through a simple gesture, signaling that escalating the encounter was unnecessary. Odin accepted her judgment and allowed the woman to depart rather than beginning a confrontation without sufficient information.[2]
Frigg immediately shifted the conversation toward intelligence analysis and asked which Pantheon the woman was most likely to approach next. Odin suggested the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]] because of its immense Divine strength. Frigg agreed that it contained extraordinarily powerful Gods but emphasized that it was considerably less politically unified than [[Asgard]]; the presence of powerful individuals did not guarantee collective intervention in a foreign problem. The [[Celtic Pantheon]] was considered too weak to be a likely decisive ally, the [[Shinto Pantheon]] was also judged insufficient for the outsider's apparent requirements, and the [[Greek Pantheon]] had already ceased existing as a coherent major power following the destruction of its previous regime.[2]
The discussion eventually returned to Victor, whose involvement in major supernatural incidents had become increasingly difficult for the old Pantheons to ignore. Frigg remarked that Victor was essentially a Devil himself and visibly blushed while talking about him. Odin immediately recognized the reaction and accused her of having kept additional pictures of Victor's Dragon form.[2]
Frigg replied that she no longer possessed them because Odin had already deleted the photographs himself. Odin remained irritated that his wife—and numerous other Norse Goddesses—had acquired the images in the first place, while Frigg treated his jealousy as unreasonable. Victor's new appearance had spread widely through the influence of the [[Blood God Religion]] and reached Nordic territory, where many female Divine beings had obtained copies. The situation became a source of irritation among several male Norse Gods and provided a rare domestic glimpse of Frigg and Odin outside their political roles.[2]
Although Frigg did not physically participate in Victor's conflicts during this period, the scene established her position clearly. She was not simply ornamental royalty: Odin consulted her during major political decisions, she could prevent him from making impulsive moves, and she possessed enough knowledge of other Pantheons to participate meaningfully in geopolitical planning.

#### Dragon God of Chaos
Frigg remained in [[Asgard]] during the events that eventually culminated in Ragnarök. By this point, Hela had spent millennia preparing to destroy [[Odin]]'s rule. Her objective went substantially beyond winning a military battle; she intended to systematically strip Odin of his family, allies, authority and dignity in retaliation for the persecution suffered by herself and her siblings.
During the decisive conflict, Hela confronted Odin while the wider Asgardian war unfolded around them. Her strategy employed forces and betrayals that had been prepared long before the battle began, including resurrected historical armies, hidden collaborators inside Asgard and the participation of Nidhoggr.[1]
At a carefully chosen point in the confrontation, Hela turned toward Nidhoggr and instructed the End Dragon that it was his turn, making clear that he already understood his assigned objective. Nidhoggr abandoned the immediate battlefield and flew directly toward Asgard.[1]
His destination was Odin's palace.
Nidhoggr released a breath composed of Pure Darkness against the royal castle. The attack did not simply demolish its walls or kill those inside through conventional force: the entire palace was erased from existence. Frigg was within the structure and was erased together with it.[1]
Odin immediately screamed Frigg's name after perceiving what had happened. The emotional shock caused him to lose awareness of Hela for a critical instant, allowing her to attack and sever his left arm. Hela deliberately refrained from simply killing Odin because immediate death would have contradicted the purpose of her revenge; she wanted him conscious long enough to experience the destruction of everything he cared about.[1]
The narration explicitly establishes that Nidhoggr's attack was aimed where it would hurt Odin most: his family. Frigg's death was therefore not accidental collateral damage during the fighting and was not the result of being randomly caught between Odin and Hela. Hela deliberately selected the palace because Frigg and members of Odin's household were there.[1]
Chapter 961 clarifies the nature of Frigg's fate even further. In the aftermath, her destruction is distinguished from normal death: she had been erased, as though her existence itself had been removed rather than merely leaving behind a dead Divine body or Soul. Hela also acknowledged that Frigg had not personally done anything to deserve her hatred. From Hela's perspective, Frigg's relationship with Odin was sufficient reason to make her part of the revenge.[3]
Frigg was nevertheless treated differently from most of the family Hela intended to punish. Hela regarded her erasure as a comparatively quick end, while the rest of Odin's household was intended to remain alive long enough to experience prolonged humiliation and torture before eventually dying. Frigg's elimination was consequently both an act of revenge against Odin and one of the few instances in which Hela did not deliberately prolong the suffering of her victim.[3]
Her death became one of the clearest demonstrations of the difference between defeating Odin physically and destroying him psychologically. Frigg never fought Hela or Nidhoggr directly, yet eliminating her immediately impaired Odin in the middle of battle more effectively than an ordinary attack because of the emotional importance she held for him.[1]

### Skills & Abilities
Frigg receives very little direct combat-focused screen time, so her abilities should be divided between powers established by her Divine classification and powers she personally demonstrates. Her status as a Major Second Generation Goddess does not justify automatically importing every ability attributed to Frigg in real-world mythology.

#### God Physiology
- Divine Physiology: Frigg was an ancient Æsir Goddess rather than a mortal or conventional supernatural life-form. Her existence was sustained through Divine nature and [[Divine Energy]], granting her the fundamental characteristics possessed by developed Gods.
- Superhuman Physical Characteristics: As a developed Goddess, Frigg naturally possessed physical capabilities beyond ordinary mortal species, although the novel never places her in a direct battle from which specific strength or speed feats can be calculated.
- Divine Energy Manipulation: Frigg possessed Divine Energy as one of the Major Goddesses of the [[Norse Pantheon]]. No named offensive technique using her personal Divine Energy has been shown.
- Divine Soul: Like other Gods, Frigg possessed a supernatural Divine existence and Soul rather than the ordinary spiritual constitution of a mortal. This did not save her from Nidhoggr's Pure Darkness because the palace and those within it were erased rather than conventionally killed.[1][3]
- Agelessness and Longevity: Frigg had existed for eons and belonged to the Second Generation of Norse Gods. She did not age according to ordinary mortal biology and would not normally have died from natural aging.
- Enhanced Divine Resilience: Mature Gods possess supernatural durability and survivability beyond ordinary beings. Frigg nevertheless receives no personal durability feat because the only lethal attack directed at her was an existence-erasing assault from Nidhoggr rather than conventional physical damage.[1]

#### Divine Classification
- Second Generation God: Frigg belonged to an older generation of the Norse Divine hierarchy and predates many of the younger Æsir who became active in later eras.
- High-Level God: Frigg is indexed within the High-Level Divine range and was a Major Goddess of [[Asgard]]. Her political importance should therefore not be mistaken for an absence of Divine power, although the novel does not provide a direct battle demonstrating her upper combat limit.
- Major Goddess: Her status as Queen and one of the principal female Deities of Asgard placed her above ordinary minor Gods in both political standing and religious significance.
The older classification of Frigg as a Second Generation Primordial God should not be used without direct evidence. In the later cosmology, a true Primordial Being is a fundamentally different class of existence associated with embodying and administering universal functions. Frigg is never demonstrated occupying such an office and is more consistently treated as a Second Generation High-Level Goddess.

#### Divinities
Frigg is indexed within the [[Norse Pantheon]] with Divine associations connected to several Concepts:
- Fertility
- Marriage
- Love
- Sexuality
- Prophecy
- Wisdom
These associations should not be converted into unsupported techniques. Frigg never receives a dedicated combat scene demonstrating Fertility Manipulation, supernatural marriage binding, Love Manipulation, prophetic visions, Wisdom-based omniscience or comparable specialized applications. The safest indexing is therefore that these represent her known Divine Domains, while their exact abilities remain unrevealed.

#### Political Intelligence
- Political Intelligence: Frigg demonstrated considerable political awareness during her first appearance, immediately treating [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] and the mysterious outsider as geopolitical problems rather than simply potential combatants.[2]
- Strategic Restraint: She recognized that beginning a war against Victor without first understanding his current strength and objectives was strategically unsound and directly warned [[Odin]] against doing so.[2]
- Threat Assessment: Frigg evaluated dangerous individuals according to capability, motivation and potential consequences rather than allowing Odin's wounded pride to determine [[Asgard]]'s response.[2]
- Pantheon Knowledge: She possessed enough knowledge of Earth's major Divine factions to discuss the Hindu, Celtic, Shinto and former Greek Pantheons, including the distinction between raw Divine strength and actual political unity.[2]
- Diplomatic Judgment: During the mysterious outsider's visit, Frigg recognized when the confrontation had reached a point where preventing the visitor from leaving would create unnecessary danger and persuaded Odin not to escalate the situation.[2]
- Political Forecasting: After the negotiations failed, Frigg immediately considered which supernatural faction the outsider would approach next and worked through the available candidates with Odin.[2]

#### Leadership and Royal Authority
- Queen of [[Asgard]]: Frigg occupied one of the highest non-kingly positions in the Æsir hierarchy and belonged to the core of Asgard's royal government.
- Royal Advisory Authority: [[Odin]] discussed major political and supernatural matters with Frigg privately and accepted her intervention even when it contradicted his immediate instincts.[2]
- Influence over Odin: Frigg was one of the comparatively few individuals shown capable of directly restraining or correcting Odin without behaving as a frightened subordinate. Her influence was sufficiently established that a simple gesture could persuade him not to escalate the confrontation with the mysterious outsider.[2]
- Emotional Importance: Frigg's importance to Odin had practical consequences during Ragnarök. Her erasure immediately broke his concentration during combat, allowing Hela to sever one of his arms.[1]

### Relationships

#### Odin
[[Odin]] was Frigg's husband, King and closest known political partner. Their relationship combined marriage, royal government and an unusually informal degree of personal familiarity. Frigg could warn him against reckless decisions, question his assessments and physically or verbally restrain him without displaying the submissive behavior expected from many of his subordinates.[2]
Their Chapter 833 interaction illustrates that Odin valued her judgment even when he disliked what she was telling him. He accepted her warning against unnecessarily escalating the confrontation with the mysterious outsider and discussed the probable actions of other Pantheons with her as a political equal. Their later argument concerning [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s photographs also exposes a far more domestic side to their marriage: Odin was intensely jealous while Frigg openly teased him for overreacting.[2]
The depth of Odin's attachment became undeniable during Ragnarök. The instant Frigg was erased, Odin screamed her name and momentarily ceased focusing upon Hela, creating the opening Hela needed to sever his arm.[1] Hela had predicted precisely this reaction and selected Frigg as a target because harming Odin's family was more valuable to her revenge than simply inflicting another physical wound.

#### Balder
[[Balder]] is indexed as Frigg's son within the Norse royal family. The novel does not provide enough direct interaction between them to construct a detailed mother-son relationship, and Frigg's limited appearances focus overwhelmingly upon [[Odin]] and the politics of [[Asgard]].

#### Freya
[[Freyja|Freya]] and Frigg were separate Goddesses with different positions inside the [[Norse Pantheon]]. Frigg was [[Odin]]'s Æsir Queen and political adviser, whereas Freya was the Vanir Goddess associated with the Valkyries. Their overlapping Divine associations and their shared connection to Norse mythology should not be used to merge their identities or responsibilities.
Chapter 833 reinforces the distinction when Odin orders Freya and [[Thor]] summoned while Frigg is already beside him participating in his political discussion.[2]

#### Victor Elderblood
Frigg never directly meets [[Victor Elderblood]] in a confirmed on-screen scene, but Victor became central to her debut because his destruction of [[Odin]]'s surveillance ravens triggered the discussion in [[Asgard]]. Unlike Odin, Frigg's immediate reaction was not to treat Victor as an enemy. She insisted that they first determine what he had become before considering war.[2]
Her interest was not entirely political. After images of Victor's Dragon appearance spread through Nordic territory, Frigg acquired several photographs. She visibly blushed when Victor was mentioned, causing Odin to expose the fact that he had discovered and deleted her images. Frigg regarded his jealousy as excessive and insisted that they were merely pictures.[2]
There is no evidence that this attraction ever became an actual relationship. Frigg and Victor did not become lovers, allies or enemies before her death.

#### Hela Elderblood
[[Hela Elderblood]] was indirectly responsible for Frigg's death and deliberately selected her as part of the psychological destruction of [[Odin]]. Hela's hatred was directed primarily at Odin and the Asgardian system responsible for persecuting her family rather than at Frigg personally.[1]
Chapter 961 explicitly makes this distinction clearer: Frigg had not personally done anything to Hela, but her status as Odin's wife made her an acceptable target in Hela's revenge. Hela considered Frigg fortunate compared with the rest of Odin's family because her erasure was immediate rather than prolonged through torture.[3]
Frigg therefore represents an important feature of Hela's Ragnarök: the war was not governed by ordinary battlefield morality. Hela deliberately harmed people connected to Odin because she wanted him to experience the same helplessness and loss she associated with the persecution of her own family.

#### Nidhoggr
Nidhoggr was Frigg's direct killer. At Hela's command, the End Dragon left the main confrontation and flew toward [[Odin]]'s palace. His breath of Pure Darkness erased the entire structure and Frigg together with it.[1]
There is no known personal relationship between Frigg and Nidhoggr before this event. He attacked her because eliminating Odin's family was part of the role assigned to him by Hela.

### Death and Legacy
Frigg's death is unusual even among the casualties of Ragnarök because the novel explicitly differentiates it from normal Divine death. Nidhoggr's attack did not leave a body behind or simply destroy the palace physically; the castle and Frigg were erased from existence.[1]
The following chapter reinforces this interpretation by treating Frigg as having been erased rather than ordinarily killed, to the point that her fate is described as if the existence of the woman herself had been removed. Hela considered this a quick death compared with what she planned for the remainder of [[Odin]]'s family.[3]
Her death also had immediate strategic value. Hela understood that Odin's emotional attachments were vulnerabilities and turned Frigg into a weapon against him without Frigg ever participating in their duel. Odin's moment of grief created a physical opening and symbolized the larger collapse of the world he had ruled: his family, palace, allies and authority were being dismantled around him one piece at a time.[1]
Frigg did not survive to witness the destruction and later sale of the [[Norse Pantheon]]. Her death therefore marks one of the final breaks between the old royal [[Asgard]] led by Odin and the post-Ragnarök order that emerged after his defeat.

### Trivia
- Frigg, Frigga and Friia are alternate names used for the same Goddess; they are not separate characters.
- Frigg and [[Freyja|Freya]] are different Goddesses. Frigg was [[Odin]]'s Queen and political partner, whereas Freya was associated with the Vanir and command of the Valkyries.
- Her directly described physical features are long brown hair and vibrant green eyes.[2]
- Chapter 833 is the first directly verified story appearance presently located for Frigg.[2]
- Frigg is one of the few characters shown openly advising Odin against a course of action rather than simply accepting his decision.[2]
- She correctly emphasized that the power of a Pantheon could not be judged only by how many individually powerful Gods it possessed; political unity also determined whether those Gods could realistically act together.[2]
- Frigg obtained photographs of [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s Dragon form after they circulated through Nordic territory, and Odin personally deleted them because of his jealousy.[2]
- She visibly blushed when Victor was discussed but never actually entered a relationship with him.[2]
- Frigg's death was not accidental crossfire between Hela and Odin. Hela deliberately sent Nidhoggr to attack Odin's family because she wanted to inflict psychological suffering upon him.[1]
- Frigg was erased together with Odin's entire palace by Nidhoggr's breath of Pure Darkness.[1]
- Odin's immediate reaction to her erasure caused him to lose concentration long enough for Hela to sever his left arm.[1]
- Chapter 961 confirms that Frigg's fate was more severe than conventional death and explicitly treats her as having been erased from existence.[3]
- Frigg had not personally wronged Hela. She was killed because she was Odin's wife and therefore someone whose destruction would hurt him.[3]
- Despite this, Hela regarded Frigg's immediate erasure as comparatively merciful; she intended to keep the remainder of Odin's family alive for substantially longer suffering.[3]
- The novel has not shown Frigg in a proper battle, so her exact combat style, weapons and specialized applications of her Divinities remain unknown.
- Although older indexing sometimes described Frigg as a Second Generation Primordial God, the story does not demonstrate her as a true Primordial Being or Regulator of Existence. She is more accurately indexed as a Second Generation High-Level Goddess unless later material states otherwise.
- The titles and relationships attributed to Frigg in real-world Norse mythology should not automatically be considered novel canon unless they are independently established in the story.
