**[[Thor]]** is the Norse god of thunder, son of [[Odin]], who defends gods and humans against the giants with his hammer Mjölnir.

## IRL (Norse myth)

He rides a chariot drawn by two goats, fishes up the [[Jormungandr|Midgard Serpent]], and dies killing it at Ragnarök. Thursday is named for him. In Riordan's *Magnus Chase* he is a crude, cheerful god who loses his hammer in *The Hammer of Thor*.

## UOE (Far Future)

At the first [[Panpantheonic Summit]], [[Ares]] challenged Thor to a duel, and [[Athena]] and [[Freyja]] had to prevent a war. See [[Norse Pantheon]].

### Appearances

#### Debut
Chapter 509
Thor Odinson, usually referred to simply as Thor, is a Third Generation God, Major God of the [[Norse Pantheon]], son of [[Odin]], husband of Sif, wielder of Mjölnir, and the Norse God of Thunder. Despite belonging to a considerably younger Divine generation than many of Earth's ancient rulers, Thor is an exceptional combat-oriented deity and is classified among the High-Level [[Combat Gods]], sharing the same general combat bracket as [[Ares]].[1]
Thor was regarded by Odin as [[Asgard]]'s strongest warrior and frequently served as one of the Pantheon's principal frontline combatants.[4] He also possessed the infamous title "The Genocide of [[Titans]]" after killing a large number of Jotuns, although Thor himself dislikes being called by that name and at least one of his Jotun lovers also despised the title.[3]
Thor's combat reputation extends beyond the Norse Pantheon. Before his proper introduction, he fought [[Shiva]], the Hindu God of Destruction, continuously for seven days and seven nights. Thor ultimately lost, and the conditions surrounding that defeat resulted in him accompanying Shiva to the later [[Supernatural Gathering]].[5] Rather than viewing the defeat as an unforgivable humiliation, Thor respected Shiva's strength and regarded losing to one of the world's strongest Gods as an opportunity to learn.[3]
Thor remained one of Odin's most important warriors until the final Ragnarök. During the war, he confronted [[Jormungandr]], his traditional enemy, but was ultimately defeated by the World Serpent and survived the battle.[6] Following Odin's defeat and the collapse of the old Norse order, Thor fell into Hela's custody. Approximately two months later, Hela publicly displayed the defeated God of Thunder on all fours with a leash around his neck during the gathering where she announced the sale of the Norse Pantheon.[2]

### Physical Appearance
Thor is an exceptionally tall and muscular man with long red hair, a large red beard, and brown eyes. His hair and beard are styled in a manner strongly reminiscent of ancient Norse warriors, reinforcing his distinctly Æsir appearance.[3]
During his first appearance, Thor wore a relatively simple but extremely sturdy suit of full armor. Despite lacking the ostentatious ornamentation favored by many Divine rulers, his physical size, battle-hardened appearance and sheer presence immediately identified him as a warrior rather than a ceremonial deity.[3]
His legendary Divine Weapon, Mjölnir, normally rests at his waist when not in use.[3] The hammer is further reinforced through Norse Runes placed upon it by [[Odin]], making it more than a simple Divine weapon and connecting it directly to Odin's mastery of Rune [[Magic]].[7]

### Personality
Thor is battle-loving, straightforward, hot-headed, competitive, arrogant and highly confident in his own strength. His mentality strongly reflects the traditional warrior culture of [[Asgard]]: when confronted with another powerful individual, his first instinct is often to determine how strong they are through battle rather than through prolonged diplomacy.
However, Thor's arrogance should not be mistaken for an inability to respect superior strength. His seven-day battle against [[Shiva]] ended in defeat, yet Thor did not respond with the resentment displayed by many other Gods. Instead, he considered Shiva one of the few Gods genuinely worthy of his respect and regarded surviving the battle as a valuable experience from which he could learn.[5][3]
This separates Thor from figures such as [[Zeus]], whose pride often prevented him from accepting humiliation. Thor may be proud, but his warrior mentality allows him to recognize when another fighter is simply stronger.
Thor also possesses a pronounced competitive streak toward Gods associated with Thunder and combat. During the hunt initiated by [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction, Thor found himself alongside Susanoo, [[Takemikazuchi]] and [[Taranis]]. Upon realizing how many powerful Thunder-related Gods were present, he immediately proposed settling which one of them was the strongest. [[Loki]] recognized the danger of allowing Thor's enthusiasm to derail the mission and was effectively present to prevent the God of Thunder from starting unnecessary fights.[4]
His tendency toward violence is accompanied by a crude sense of humor. Thor openly mocks Gods he dislikes, particularly Zeus. During the [[Supernatural Gathering]] he ridiculed Zeus' lack of mental discipline and made deliberately vulgar comments intended to provoke the Greek God while ensuring only a limited number of nearby Gods heard him.[8]
Thor is also sexually open and has had relationships outside his marriage, including at least one Jotun lover.[3] Before the later events of the story, he had also repeatedly shown interest in Natashia Fulger, who stated that Gods had bothered her in the past and that she had rejected their advances. During the Supernatural Gathering, Thor continued looking toward Natashia often enough for Victor Elderblood to recognize that Thor had not completely abandoned his interest in her.[9]
Despite this behavior, Thor is comically afraid of his wife's anger. When Victor's charm temporarily affected him in Chapter 873, Thor's immediate concern was what Sif would do if she discovered that her husband had been charmed by another man.[4]
Thor's personality also becomes more serious when genuine threats emerge. Although he enjoys fighting, he is capable of understanding the political consequences of Divine wars and obeying [[Odin]] when the survival of Asgard is at stake.

### History

#### Rise as Asgard's Warrior
Thor was born as one of the many Divine sons of [[Odin]] and eventually became one of the most famous warriors of the [[Norse Pantheon]].
Despite being only a Third Generation God, his natural combat ability, Divine Authority and centuries of experience allowed him to reach the High-Level Combat God bracket.[1] His reputation became sufficiently great that Odin ultimately regarded Thor as his strongest active warrior.[4]
Thor's title "The Genocide of [[Titans]]" originated from his campaigns against the Jotuns. He personally killed large numbers of the race, creating a reputation that followed him even among the Giants themselves.[3]
The title does not please Thor. His relationship with the Jotuns was more complicated than simple hatred, as he had at least one Jotun lover who also disliked hearing him called by the name associated with the destruction of her people.[3]

#### Battle with Shiva
Sometime before the later [[Supernatural Gathering]], Thor fought [[Shiva]].
The confrontation lasted seven days and seven nights, demonstrating both Thor's extraordinary combat endurance and the vast gap between ordinary Gods and the strongest Divine beings on Earth.[5]
Thor ultimately lost.
As a consequence of the battle, he accompanied Shiva as his escort during the gathering attended by several of Earth's most influential supernatural factions.[5]
Thor nevertheless did not resent Shiva for defeating him. By the time of his proper introduction, he openly respected the Hindu God and regarded defeat at the hands of such a powerful opponent as something from which a warrior could learn.[3]

### Synopsis

#### Discoveries, Changes, And War
Thor was first indirectly introduced through the account of his battle with [[Shiva]]. The two Gods fought continuously for seven days and seven nights, with Shiva eventually emerging victorious. Thor subsequently accompanied him during the supernatural diplomatic gathering connected to the major Pantheons.[5]
The battle established Thor's status before his proper appearance: although he lost, surviving a prolonged battle with one of Earth's strongest Gods demonstrated that Thor was far beyond the level of an ordinary Third Generation deity.

#### Demonic War
Thor made his proper debut during the [[Supernatural Gathering]], attending as the representative of the [[Norse Pantheon]] while accompanying [[Shiva]].[3]
His entrance immediately established his reputation. The supernatural leaders identified him as the Norse God of Thunder and "The Genocide of [[Titans]]", a title Thor visibly disliked.[3]
Thor appeared much less formal than several of the other Divine representatives. Rather than attempting to project the dignified aura of an ancient ruler, he behaved like a warrior attending a gathering because circumstances required him to do so.
His attention eventually shifted toward [[Victor Elderblood]] and Natashia. Victor noticed that although Thor appeared to be studying him, the God repeatedly looked toward Natashia instead.[9]
Natashia explained that powerful Gods had attempted to approach her in the past and that she had always rejected them. Victor nevertheless read Thor's continued gaze as evidence that the God had not entirely abandoned his interest in his wife.[9]
Victor immediately disliked him for it.
Thor also became involved in the increasingly hostile exchanges among the Gods present at the Gathering. He particularly enjoyed irritating [[Zeus]], mocking the Greek God's lack of mental discipline and making vulgar remarks at his expense whenever an opportunity appeared.[8]
Despite his crude behavior, Thor remained aware that the political situation was rapidly deteriorating. As the true scale of the conflict involving [[Diablo]], Lilith, Heaven and the various Pantheons became apparent, the gathering ceased being merely a diplomatic spectacle and became preparation for an actual supernatural war.
Thor later participated in the wider conflict under [[Odin]]'s orders as one of [[Asgard]]'s principal Divine combatants.

#### King Among Kings
By the time [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] organized the large-scale hunt against the surviving enemies associated with the Elder Gods and hostile factions, [[Odin]] contributed [[Asgard]]'s strongest warrior to the coalition:
Thor.[4]
The hunting force contained an extraordinary concentration of combat-oriented Gods. Heaven sent [[Azrael]] and Ariel, the [[Celtic Pantheon]] contributed [[Taranis]], and the [[Shinto Pantheon]] sent Susanoo and [[Takemikazuchi]].[4]
Thor's immediate reaction was exactly what those familiar with him expected.
Seeing multiple powerful Thunder-related Gods gathered in one location, he proposed finding out which one of them was truly the strongest.[4]
[[Loki]] was forced to stop the situation from turning into an unnecessary tournament before the actual mission had even begun.
The gathering of warriors was also one of the occasions on which Victor demonstrated the effects of his supernatural charm. Under the influence of a blessing derived from [[Aphrodite]], even individuals who would not normally be attracted to Victor could momentarily experience abnormal desire toward him.[4]
Thor was affected.
After realizing what had happened, his immediate horror was not directed toward Victor but toward the possibility of Sif finding out.[4]
Thor and Loki consequently acknowledged the need to develop greater resistance against supernatural Charm, with [[Freyja|Freya]] being considered an obvious person from whom they could acquire such training.[4]

#### Dragon God of Chaos
As Ragnarök approached, [[Odin]] gathered the remaining principal forces of [[Asgard]], including Thor, [[Freyja|Freya]], [[Loki]] and [[Heimdall]].[10]
Thor's prophesied opponent was [[Jormungandr]], one of Loki's children and the World Serpent.
Even before the battle began, [[Fenrir]] discussed the matchup with his brother. Because Jormungandr's enormous natural body would make him a comparatively easy target for the much smaller and highly mobile Thor, Fenrir advised him to rely upon a smaller humanoid form rather than simply attempting to overwhelm Thor through size.[11]
The decisive stage of Ragnarök finally began in Chapter 958, when Hela's armies and allies launched their accumulated plans against Odin's faction.[6]
Thor confronted Jormungandr as expected.
However, the battle did not end according to the traditional mutual-destruction scenario associated with their mythology. Jormungandr defeated Thor but did not kill him, leaving the God of Thunder alive after Asgard's forces collapsed.[6]
Thor's survival eventually became part of Hela's public humiliation of the old Norse order.
Approximately two months after Ragnarök, Hela summoned representatives of the major supernatural powers and announced her intention to sell what remained of the [[Norse Pantheon]].[2]
To demonstrate just how thoroughly Odin's regime had fallen, she brought Thor before the assembled supernatural rulers.
The once-proud God of Thunder and strongest warrior of Asgard was forced onto all fours with a leash around his neck, visibly demonstrating his defeated status beneath Hela.[2]
Thor was still alive, correcting the assumption that he died during Ragnarök. His humiliation instead served as one of the clearest symbols that the Asgardian order ruled by Odin had completely collapsed.

### Skills & Abilities
Third Generation Combat God Physiology: Thor is a Third Generation God whose combat ability far exceeds that of an ordinary deity of his generation. The Author's combat hierarchy specifically uses Thor alongside [[Ares]] as an example of a High-Level Combat God.[1]
- Divine Physical Characteristics: Thor possesses extraordinary strength, speed, reflexes, durability and stamina appropriate to an elite combat-oriented God. His ability to battle [[Shiva]] continuously for seven days and seven nights is one of the clearest demonstrations of his endurance.[5]
- High-Level Combat God: Thor occupies the upper combat brackets among ordinary Gods. The Author explicitly identifies him as one of the High-Level [[Combat Gods]] whom [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s [[Vampire Count Form]] could fight around the Chapter 630 benchmark.[1]
- Immense Combat Endurance: Thor remained capable of fighting Shiva for seven consecutive days and nights before finally being defeated.[5]
- Godly Longevity: As an ancient Third Generation God, Thor has existed for eons and possesses the immense lifespan characteristic of Divine beings.

#### Thunder Divinity
Thunder Manipulation: Thor is explicitly the God of Thunder, possessing a Divine Domain associated with Thunder.[3]
His status as one of Earth's major Thunder Gods is reinforced in Chapter 873, where he immediately views Susanoo, [[Takemikazuchi]] and [[Taranis]] as competing Gods within the same broad supernatural field and attempts to initiate a contest to determine which of them is strongest.[4]
The novel has not provided enough direct material to safely separate every mythological aspect sometimes attributed to Thor—such as independent Divinities of Sky, Hallowing, War, Strength or Storms—into confirmed individual Divine Concepts. These should therefore not be treated as separate demonstrated Domains until directly established in the novel.

#### Lightning Manipulation
Thor can generate and utilize Divine Lightning as part of his Thunder-related authority.
His Lightning is Divine in nature rather than merely elemental electricity, placing it within the supernatural framework used by the Gods.

#### Mjölnir
Mjölnir is Thor's famous Divine Weapon and his principal combat armament.[3]
The hammer is connected to the Norse Rune system and carries an enchantment placed upon it by [[Odin]].[7]
Because Mjölnir is a Divine Weapon forged and enchanted for one of [[Asgard]]'s strongest warriors, it functions as one of the primary channels through which Thor expresses his Divine combat Power.
The complete limits and every individual enchantment contained within the weapon have not yet been revealed.

#### Combat Mastery
Thor is one of [[Asgard]]'s most experienced and powerful frontline warriors.
[[Odin]] himself selected Thor as his strongest warrior when contributing personnel to [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s supernatural hunt.[4]
His known combat history includes:
- Fighting [[Shiva]] for seven days and seven nights.[5]
- Participating in the conflict surrounding the Demonic War.
- Being recognized as a High-Level Combat God.[1]
- Serving as one of Odin's principal warriors during Ragnarök.
- Fighting [[Jormungandr]] during the fall of Asgard.[6]

#### Resistance and Weaknesses
Thor possesses the spiritual resistance expected of a Major God, but his mental defenses are not absolute.
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s enhanced Charm temporarily affected him during Chapter 873, despite Thor not normally being attracted to men.[4] Thor and [[Loki]] subsequently considered acquiring training against supernatural Charm.
Thor can also be defeated through sufficiently overwhelming power. [[Shiva]] defeated him after a prolonged battle, while [[Jormungandr]] overcame him during Ragnarök.[5][6]

### Relationships

#### Odin
[[Odin]] is Thor's father, King and former ruler.
Thor remained one of Odin's most dependable warriors and was considered the strongest active fighter available to [[Asgard]].[4]
Odin repeatedly deployed Thor whenever raw Divine combat power was required, including during [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s hunt and the final preparations for Ragnarök.[4][10]
Thor ultimately failed alongside the rest of Odin's faction during Ragnarök, surviving while the old Asgardian government was destroyed.

#### Shiva
[[Shiva]] is one of Thor's friends, respected rivals and one of the few Gods whose strength Thor openly acknowledges.
Their most famous confrontation lasted seven days and seven nights before Shiva defeated Thor.[5]
Thor did not resent him for the loss. Rather than treating defeat as humiliation, he respected Shiva for being powerful enough to overcome him and considered the experience useful for his development as a warrior.[3]
Thor later accompanied Shiva during the [[Supernatural Gathering]] as a consequence of their battle.

#### Sif
Sif is Thor's wife.
Despite Thor's immense confidence around Gods, monsters and battlefield opponents, he displays considerably more caution regarding Sif's anger.
When [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s Charm temporarily affected him, Thor's immediate thought was that he would be in serious trouble if Sif discovered that her husband had been charmed by another man.[4]

#### Annasthashia Elderblood Fulger
Thor displayed romantic or sexual interest in Natashia long before and during the [[Supernatural Gathering]].
Natashia stated that Gods had previously attempted to bother her and that she rejected them. During the Gathering, Thor continued repeatedly looking toward her despite ostensibly observing [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]].[9]
Victor interpreted Thor's behavior as proof that the God had not completely given up on pursuing her and immediately became hostile toward the continued interest in his wife.[9]
There is no indication that Natashia ever reciprocated Thor's interest.

#### Zeus
Thor possesses a hostile and mocking relationship with [[Zeus]].
He frequently enjoys provoking the Greek God and shows little respect for Zeus' personality or habits. During the Gathering, Thor deliberately mocked Zeus' mental discipline with crude jokes while ensuring only nearby Gods such as [[Shiva]] and Zeus could hear him.[8]
Their relationship reflects the broader competitive hostility between several of Earth's major warrior Gods.

#### Jormungandr
[[Jormungandr]] became Thor's primary opponent during Ragnarök.
Their confrontation had been anticipated through Norse prophecy long before the war occurred. [[Fenrir]] specifically warned Jormungandr not to rely entirely upon his enormous body because Thor's smaller form and mobility would make such an approach dangerous.[11]
When the battle finally occurred, Jormungandr defeated Thor but chose not to kill him.[6]
This allowed Thor to survive Ragnarök despite the destruction of [[Odin]]'s rule.

#### Hela Elderblood
[[Hela Elderblood]] was one of the principal architects of [[Odin]]'s downfall and eventually became Thor's captor.
Hela had deep personal hatred toward Thor and most of [[Asgard]] because of the treatment she and her siblings received throughout their lives.
After Ragnarök, she retained Thor as one of the visible trophies of her victory.
During the gathering in Chapter 967, Thor was brought before the supernatural rulers on all fours with a leash around his neck, publicly reducing Odin's former strongest warrior to a humiliated captive.[2]
