### In Norse Mythology
In Norse mythology, Asgard was the home of the Aesir, a branch of Norse gods counterbalanced by the Vanir, who were seen as nature gods. It was connected to Midgard, the earth, by a rainbow bridge called the [[Bifrost]]. Asgard was ruled by [[Odin]], the chief of the Norse pantheon as a god of knowledge, lore, and magic. Asgard is said to be destroyed when Ragnarok, the Norse apocalypse, which translates to Doom of the Gods, comes. Loki, an adopted frost giant brother of Odin and a trickster god turned evil, will raise an army of the unworthy dead, along with his three monstrous children: Hel, who rules the unworthy dead, [[Fenrir]], a giant wolf chained in Asgard, and Jormungand, a sea serpent that encircles the earth. Many of the chief gods of Norse mythology, including Loki, Odin, Thor, the god of thunder, Tyr, the god of war, and [[Heimdall]], the ever-watching guard of the Bifrost, will die, and Thor's children will take refuge in the branches of [[Yggdrasil]] and come down when the fighting is over to repopulate the earth.

### Asgard
Asgard is the principal celestial realm and traditional seat of power of the [[Norse Pantheon]]. It forms part of the greater [[Nine Realms]] and historically served as the residence and political center of [[Odin]], the God-King of the Norse Gods, alongside many of the Pantheon's most important Æsir and allied Divine Beings.
Asgard was connected to the other Norse realms through the [[Bifrost]], a Divine Artifact possessing the Concept of Travel, while [[Yggdrasil]] and its dimensional structure provided a deeper connection between the Nine Realms. The destruction of the Bifrost isolated Asgard from the other realms and became one of the first major signs that the ancient Norse system was approaching its collapse.[1]
The realm later became the principal battlefield of Ragnarök. After thousands of years of preparation, Hela attacked Odin's regime with the forces she had accumulated throughout her imprisonment. While the Norse Gods fought one another, [[Victor Elderblood]]'s faction systematically evacuated useful populations and recovered valuable resources from the Pantheon. Odin was ultimately defeated, the old Asgardian order collapsed, and Hela later placed the surviving Pantheon territory itself up for auction rather than attempting to rebuild it.[2][3][4][5][6][7]

### Description
Asgard was the primary heavenly territory of the [[Norse Pantheon]] and the center from which [[Odin]] exercised authority over his Divine civilization. Unlike ordinary regions of the Mortal Realm, it belonged to the supernatural dimensional system of the [[Nine Realms]] and could not normally be reached by simply traveling through physical space.
The realm supported not only Gods but the military, resources, Divine infrastructure and populations accumulated by the Norse Pantheon across an enormous period of history. During the preparations for Ragnarök, [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction regarded Asgard as containing enough valuable people, resources and supernatural knowledge to make recovering its assets worthwhile even without taking the territory itself.[8][9]
Asgard should therefore not be understood merely as Odin's palace. The name is used for the greater Divine realm and civilization centered around the Norse Gods.

### Connection to the Nine Realms
Asgard was one part of the larger [[Nine Realms]], a supernatural network associated with the [[Norse Pantheon]] and [[Yggdrasil]].

#### Bifrost
The [[Bifrost]] was the principal Divine transportation system connecting the realms. It possessed the Concept and Divinity of Travel and was directly connected to the Pantheon itself.[10]
Its destruction was consequently far more significant than the loss of an ordinary portal. Once the Bifrost was destroyed, Asgard and the other realms became inaccessible through the normal system, severely restricting movement through the [[Norse Pantheon]].[11]

#### Draupnir
[[Odin]] possessed the ring Draupnir, which could transport him between locations throughout the [[Nine Realms]].[12] This gave the God-King an alternative means of dimensional movement independent of ordinary travel.

#### Yggdrasil
The deeper dimensional structure of the Pantheon remained connected through [[Yggdrasil]]. After the destruction of the [[Bifrost]], travel through the branches and hidden routes of the World Tree remained theoretically possible, although doing so could alert Yggdrasil to an intruder and allow her to expel them.[13]
[[Ratatosk]] possessed exceptional knowledge of these routes and was capable of navigating paths through Yggdrasil that were inaccessible to ordinary Gods and supernatural beings.

### Government
Asgard was historically ruled by [[Odin]], God-King of the [[Norse Pantheon]] and chief political authority over the Æsir.
The Divine hierarchy beneath him included many of the best-known Norse Gods, among them [[Thor]], [[Frigg]], [[Heimdall]], [[Balder]], [[Vidar]], Tyr, [[Vali]] and others. Figures such as [[Freyja|Freya]] and [[Idun]] also belonged to the Pantheon, though individual Gods could possess their own domains or residences elsewhere within the [[Nine Realms]].
Odin's government became increasingly unstable as Ragnarök approached. His treatment of Hela, [[Loki]]'s children and other members of the Pantheon created enemies who had spent thousands of years preparing specifically to destroy the system he ruled.[14][15][16]

### Military
Asgard possessed a substantial Divine military accumulated during [[Odin]]'s reign.
During the preparations for Ragnarök, Odin assembled Gods such as [[Thor]], [[Loki]], [[Heimdall]] and other major members of the Pantheon while strengthening the realm's defenses against Hela's expected invasion.[17]
His forces also included an enormous number of black-armored warriors containing Souls. More than 100,000 such warriors had accumulated under Odin's authority. They were individuals considered unworthy of entering Valhalla normally but were retained as useful Soul-based soldiers serving the Pantheon.[18]
Despite this preparation, Hela had spent an even greater period constructing counters to Odin's military and political advantages.

### Resources
Asgard and the wider [[Norse Pantheon]] possessed numerous Divine resources that became strategically important during its collapse.
One of the most valuable was [[Idun]], whose Divine authority allowed her to create the Apples of Youth. These fruits benefited Gods and Giants, supported their health and Divine development, and granted resistance against several harmful supernatural conditions.[19][20]
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s wives quickly recognized the value of both Idun and the resources surrounding the Pantheon. Their operations during Ragnarök consequently focused not merely on observing [[Odin]] and Hela but on ensuring that useful populations, knowledge and assets were not destroyed in the conflict.[21][22][23]

### History

#### Ancient Norse Order
Asgard served for ages as the center of the [[Norse Pantheon]] under [[Odin]]. Together with the other [[Nine Realms]], it formed one of Earth's major Divine civilizations.
The Pantheon included Gods, Giants, Dwarves, Elves, Dark Elves, Fairies and other supernatural populations distributed throughout its different realms.

#### Destruction of the Bifrost
The first major structural blow occurred when the [[Bifrost]] was destroyed.
Because the Artifact was directly connected to the Pantheon and embodied Travel, its destruction was considered extraordinarily difficult. Its loss nevertheless isolated Asgard and the other Realms from their normal transportation network.[24]
The event contributed to the gradual destabilization of [[Odin]]'s control and complicated outside access to the Pantheon.

#### Preparations for Ragnarök
Thousands of years of conflict between [[Odin]] and Hela eventually approached their conclusion.
Hela had used her imprisonment in Helheim to accumulate strength, allies and preparations specifically intended to destroy her father. Meanwhile, Odin fortified Asgard and gathered his Divine family in anticipation of Ragnarök.[25][26]
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction covertly observed these preparations rather than immediately choosing a side. Recognizing that the [[Norse Pantheon]] was likely to destroy itself, they planned to rescue valuable races and individuals while allowing Odin and Hela to exhaust one another.[27][28][29]

#### Ragnarök
Ragnarök finally erupted when Hela launched her long-prepared assault.
Her army included forces connected to [[Loki]]'s children as well as legendary dead warriors and rulers she could call through her authority over Death. Figures such as [[Julius Caesar]] and [[Vlad III Tepes]] participated alongside Hela's other forces, while Norse Gods including [[Balder]] and [[Vidar]] fought against enemies such as [[Fenrir]].[30][31]
The war rapidly dismantled [[Odin]]'s accumulated advantages. Hidden betrayals and Hela's preparations weakened the Asgardian forces from within while [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction exploited the chaos to evacuate useful inhabitants and resources.[32][33]
Odin was eventually defeated and Asgard's old government ceased to function.

#### Evacuation and Fall
By the conclusion of the war, [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s forces had removed a significant portion of the useful inhabitants, races and infrastructure from Asgard, leaving Hela with control over a territory far less valuable than the civilization [[Odin]] had once ruled.[34][35]
Rather than assume Odin's position as God-Queen and rebuild the [[Norse Pantheon]], Hela rejected the burden of governing it.

#### Auction of Asgard
Approximately two months later, Hela announced that she would auction the Asgardian Pantheon.[36]
Her reasoning distinguished the Gods from their territory: in her view, the dimension itself was not what made the [[Norse Pantheon]]; the Gods did. Hela therefore intended to remove the treasures and resources she considered hers and sell the remaining lands to another Divine faction.[37]
The decision marked the definitive end of Asgard as [[Odin]]'s independent celestial capital.

### Notes
- Asgard and the [[Norse Pantheon]] are related but not perfectly synonymous. Asgard is the Pantheon's principal celestial realm, while the Norse Pantheon encompasses its Gods, political organization and the greater [[Nine Realms]].
- Helheim is a separate infernal realm governed by Hela rather than another name for Asgard.
- The destruction of the [[Bifrost]] did not erase the Nine Realms themselves; it destroyed their principal transportation network.[38]
- The Pantheon survived the initial destruction of the Bifrost because deeper dimensional connections through [[Yggdrasil]] still existed.[39]
- Hela's decision to auction Asgard demonstrates that ownership of a Pantheon dimension and membership in the Pantheon are distinct concepts.[40]
