**[[Mystery Religions]]** were initiation cults of the Greco-Roman world. Members swore secrecy and went through rites that promised a better fate after death. [[Legum Magicae (XVIII-Alpha)]] lists several as recognized magical traditions.

## Eleusinian Mysteries
The oldest and most famous, for [[Demeter]] and [[Persephone]] at Eleusis near Athens, held for about two thousand years until the late 4th century AD. See [[Eleusinian Mysteries]].

## Dionysian Mysteries
Rites of [[Dionysus]] involving ecstatic dance, wine and the maenads. Euripides' *Bacchae* dramatizes them, and the Villa of the Mysteries fresco at Pompeii may show an initiation.

## Orphic Mysteries
Tied to the poet Orpheus. Orphics taught that humans carry a divine spark from [[Dionysus]] Zagreus, practiced vegetarianism, and buried initiates with gold tablets giving directions through the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] (avoid the spring of [[Lethe]], drink from Memory).

## Samothracian Mysteries
Rites of the Great Gods (the Kabeiroi) on the island of Samothrace, believed to protect sailors. Philip II of Macedon is said to have met Olympias there.

## Mithraic Mysteries
A Roman cult of Mithras popular with soldiers from the 1st to 4th centuries AD, held in cave-like mithraea showing Mithras slaying a bull. Initiates rose through seven grades from Raven to Father.

## Imperial Cult
The worship of Roman emperors and their genius, starting with the deified [[Julius Caesar]] and [[Augustus (Roman Emperor)|Augustus]]. Refusal to sacrifice was the test applied to early Christians.

## Haruspex
A Roman diviner trained in Etruscan lore who read the entrails of sacrificed animals, especially the liver (the bronze Liver of Piacenza is a model). Distinct from the [[Augur]], who read birds.
