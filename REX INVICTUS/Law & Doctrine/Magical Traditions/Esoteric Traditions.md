**[[Esoteric Traditions]]** gathers the real-world magical and mystical traditions named in [[Legum Magicae (XVIII-Alpha)]].

## Alchemy
See [[Alchemy]].

## Magic in the Greco-Roman World
Curse tablets (*defixiones*), love spells, amulets and the Greek Magical Papyri from Roman Egypt, which blend Greek, Egyptian and Jewish names of power. [[Hecate]] is a frequent patron.

## Egyptian Book of the Dead
A collection of funerary spells (about 1550 BC onward) to guide the dead through the Duat, including the weighing of the heart before [[Osiris]]. See [[Egyptian Pantheon]].

## Mesopotamian Divination
Babylonian priests read sheep livers, celestial omens and dreams, recorded in omen series such as *Enūma Anu Enlil*. The roots of Western astrology lie here.

## Asatru
Ásatrú ("faith in the Æsir") is a modern revival of Norse paganism, recognized as a religion in Iceland since 1973. See [[Norse Pantheon]].

## Seidr
Seiðr is Norse magic of prophecy and fate associated with [[Freyja]] and practiced mainly by women (völvas); [[Odin]]'s use of it was considered shameful.

## Runic magic
Runes were letters as well as charms. Sagas and inscriptions describe carving runes for healing, protection or curses, and [[Odin]] won them by hanging on [[Yggdrasil]].

## Druidry
The druids were the priestly class of the Gauls and Britons described by Caesar. Modern Druidry is an 18th-century revival.

## Shinto
The indigenous religion of Japan, centered on kami venerated at shrines. See [[Japanese Pantheon]].

## Onmyōdō
The Japanese court system of divination and protection based on yin-yang and the five elements; Abe no Seimei (921–1005) is its most famous practitioner.

## Dao
The Dao ("the Way") is the central concept of Daoism (Laozi's *Daodejing*), the natural order behind all things. Chinese internal alchemy and [[Cultivation Levels|cultivation]] fiction grow from it.

## Feng Shui
Chinese geomancy that arranges buildings and graves to harmonize with the flow of qi.

## Key of Solomon
*Clavicula Salomonis*, a Renaissance grimoire attributed to King Solomon, with rituals, pentacles and instructions for summoning spirits. The related *Lesser Key of Solomon* lists the 72 demons of the Ars Goetia, including [[Asmodeus]].

## Hieros Gamos
The "sacred marriage" between a god and goddess, or a king and a priestess, as in Mesopotamian rites of [[Inanna]] and [[Tammuz|Dumuzid]].

## Rosicrucianism
A 17th-century movement launched by manifestos (1614–1617) describing a secret brotherhood of the Rose Cross with hidden alchemical and mystical knowledge.

## Theosophy
Founded in 1875 by Helena Blavatsky and Henry Olcott, combining Western occultism with Hindu and Buddhist ideas.

## Western Mystery School
Initiatory orders of Western esotericism, such as the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn (1888), which shaped modern ceremonial magic and [[Major Arcana|tarot]].

## White Magic
[[Magic]] used for benevolent ends such as healing and protection, as opposed to harmful "black" magic.

## Witchcraft
Practices of folk magic, persecuted in the European witch trials of about 1450–1750; modern Wicca was founded by Gerald Gardner in the 1950s.

## Shamanism
Practices in which a practitioner enters altered states to contact spirits, named from the Siberian Evenki *šaman*.

## Animism
The belief that animals, places and objects have spirits, a term from E. B. Tylor (1871).

## Mysticism
The pursuit of direct union with the divine, found in Christian, Sufi, Kabbalistic and other traditions.
