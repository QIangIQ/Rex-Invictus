**[[Somatic Notation (Gesture)]]** is the movement form of a working: the caster's hands trace the glyph's chords in the air, layer by layer. It is a Rex Invictus design. The source corpus treats somatic components only as memory aids and gives no gesture system, so this note derives one from [[Spell Writing (Glyph Notation)]] in the same spirit. Every note in the [[Spell Registry]] lists its kata.

## The kata

A kata is the ordered list of strokes that draws a working's glyph.

1. **Frame.** The caster fixes the polygon in front of the body: 13 points on a circle about a forearm's length across, vertex 0 at upper right, numbered counter-clockwise as seen by the caster.
2. **Layers.** Strokes are drawn layer by layer, k1 (Tier) first and k6 (Duration) last, so the working's depth is set before its shape.
3. **Strokes.** Each stroke runs from vertex a to vertex b, where b is k steps clockwise from a. It is written a→b.
4. **Close.** The last stroke of k6 is held for a beat while the incantation's last syllable is spoken; the working releases on that beat.

A Fireball kata has 18 strokes: 3 for the tier, 2 for the school, 3 for fire, 5 for the sphere, 2 for the range and 3 for the duration.

## Stroke grammar

| Element | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Short edge strokes (k1) | Depth of the field minimum; one stroke per tier bit |
| Two-skip strokes (k2) | School; the hand crosses near the rim |
| Three- to four-skip strokes (k3, k4) | Damage and area; long chords through the inner field |
| Five- and six-skip strokes (k5, k6) | Range and duration; near-diameters, often drawn with both hands |

Line style follows the caster's written hand. A caster who writes non-centred arcs moves in arcs; a caster trained on straight chords moves in straight, sharp lines. Military schools teach straight chords because they are faster and easier to check by eye.

## Speed and concealment

A trained caster traces about four strokes per second. A one-action working of 15 to 20 strokes therefore takes four to five seconds of hand movement inside the six-second cast window. Reaction workings such as Shield are drilled as compressed kata: the k1 to k3 layers are prepared in advance (a held trigram) and only the last layers are traced on demand.

The kata is visible. Observers with training can read the k1 to k3 layers from the first second of movement, which is one of the inputs of [[Magical Countermeasures and Magical Intelligence (MCM-MINT)]]. Casters who need to hide a working cast with the hands inside a sleeve or behind cover, or let a device trace the kata for them, as the rotating glyph sets of UOE countermeasure suites do.

## Devices and kata

A worn or mounted glyph engine can trace kata faster than a hand, which is how equipment casts.
