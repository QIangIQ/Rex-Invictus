**[[Spell Speech (Incantation)]]** is the spoken form of a working. It follows *The Spell Saying Guide* (The Gorilla of Destiny, 2023): each of the six attributes of the [[Spell Attribute Set]] has a sound, and the sounds are strung together in a fixed order. The word works both as the verbal component a caster says and as the formal name an academic uses, the way a species has a Latin name. Every note in the [[Spell Registry]] gives its word.

## What the voice does

The Theory of Magic reading, which the guide repeats, is that components are memory aids more than ingredients: they help a caster recall the feel of the pattern. In Rex Invictus the word is still more than a mnemonic. It is a sound pattern that matches the glyph layer by layer, so a clean incantation keeps the working on its field minimum while it forms ([[Thaumic Field Model]]). A slurred or wrong word degrades the pattern, which costs field gain on over-curve workings ([[Parzival Energy Budget#Field gain]]).

Different traditions use different sound sets for the same attributes. The guide's own example is that dwarven mages in a cold mountain say "Daylight" differently from elves or humans. The registry uses the guide's set as the academic standard; [[Esoteric Traditions]] may keep their own.

## Grammar

**Word = Tier consonant + School vowel + Damage sound + "-" + Area sound + Range sound + Duration sound**

| Rule | Statement |
|---|---|
| Case | Tier sound keeps its capital; every later part is written in lower case except the area sound after the hyphen. |
| No damage | The damage sound becomes a break, written ’. |
| No damage and no area | The written ’-’ is removed and the two halves join. |
| Instantaneous | The duration sound is silent. |
| Upcasting | The new tier's consonant and *i* are put in front of the word, which keeps its first letter in lower case. |

### Examples

| Working | Parts | Word |
|---|---|---|
| [[Fireball (3-V-M-FRB)\|Fireball]] | L · u · ire · - · So · nosin · (silent) | **Luire-Sonosin** |
| [[Magic Missile (1-P-L-MM)\|Magic Missile]] | S · u · ton · - · ’ · mul | **Suton-’mul** |
| [[Shield (1-P-L-SHL)\|Shield]] | S · a · por · i (no damage or area) | **Sapori** |
| [[Misty Step (2-C-L-MS)\|Misty Step]] | H · e · por | **Hepor** |
| Fireball upcast to Tier 5 | T + i + luire-Sonosin | **Tiluire-Sonosin** |

The guide itself jokes that a villain shouting "Luire-Sonosin" lacks punch. Field casters are not trying to sound impressive.

## Homophones

Six attributes do not separate every working. The guide lists its repeated words: Halixa (Protection from Poison, Warding Bond), Hielixsi (Barkskin, Enhance Ability, Magic Weapon, Spider Climb), Hipor (Augury, Locate Animals or Plants), Si’-Sorporly (the three Detect workings), Sousinse (Bane, Bless, Hideous Laughter), Tielix (Awaken, Reincarnate), Tipor (Commune with Nature, Legend Lore), Tipore (Commune, Contact Other Plane) and Tumully (Wall of Force, Wall of Stone). The registry finds exactly these groups among the standard workings, and 12 more once the Science Spellbook and Rex Invictus workings are added.

The guide suggests an in-world fix: give the exceptions irregular forms. The registry form keeps the first member's word and adds a tail to the others: ’ra for the second, ’re for the third, ’ri for the fourth. So Bless is **Sousinse’ra** and Hideous Laughter **Sousinse’re**. The tails match the centre dots of the glyph ([[Spell Writing (Glyph Notation)#Duplicate glyphs]]).

## Rex Invictus extensions

- Tiers 10 to 14 take Zy, Ph, Th, Ks and Ps.
- The [[Pure]] affinity takes the vowel Eu and [[Composite]] takes Oi.
- The 18 derived damage types of [[Aspect Interpolation and Derived Damage Types]] and Radiation take new sounds (Tar, Fros, Spar and so on).
- Area, range and duration values the guide never needed take unused syllables, assigned in a fixed order so that no two values share a sound.

All of these are listed in [[Incantation Sound Tables]].

## Combinations

A combination is spoken as its members' words in casting order, joined by the binder's word when one is used. Practised teams shorten the chain to the first syllable of each member after the first, the spoken equivalent of a fire order.
