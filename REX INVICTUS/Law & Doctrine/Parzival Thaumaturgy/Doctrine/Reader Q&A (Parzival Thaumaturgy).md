**[[Reader Q&A (Parzival Thaumaturgy)]]** collects questions readers bring to [[Parzival Thaumaturgy]] from Baldur's Gate 3, tabletop D&D, anime and fantasy fiction, and answers each one from the wiki. It covers magic against magic, magic against the divine, and magic in modern combat. Answers state only what the wiki notes say, with figures and links. Where the wiki is silent or contradicts itself, the answer says so and points to the open items at the end, which are proposals for the author and not canon.

## Part 1: The author's example questions

### P1 · the author's example question
> Why don't people just use Cloak 24/7 so they can spellcast efficiently?

**Wiki:** Cloak lasts up to one minute under concentration and draws 40.9 Pz per cast, and under ULMTU-IV it ceases without the caster's support ([[Cloak (2-I-L-CLK)|Cloak]]). Holding it all day takes 1,440 casts. At one cast every 60 s, recast tension settles at N_eff = 1 / (e^(60/90) − 1) ≈ 1.06, so R ≈ 1.05 ([[Recast Tension and Combination Affinity]]). The day costs 1,440 × 40.9 × 1.05 ≈ 62,000 Pz, more than a whole Tier 5 reservoir of 52,212 Pz. A standard Tier 2 mage with 3,303 Pz affords about 77 minutes ([[Caster Capacity and Tiers]]). Cloak also cannot hide the draw itself: a damped working still shows at release or from a second angle ([[Practical Magical Combat]]). Cloak averages 6.8 Pz/s over its six-second cast, above the 6 Pz/s ELINT threshold, and a field spectrometer sees it at 0.64 km ([[Cloak (2-I-L-CLK)|Cloak]]; [[Arcane Spectroscopy]]). Doctrine uses it to buy a few seconds of doubt around one important cast ([[Practical Magical Combat]]).

### P2 · the author's example question
> Yeah that's cool, but doesn't that boil every fight down to "who finishes their mana first", since we can hypothetically just keep on countering?

**Wiki:** A counter matches the target's pattern and ignores its energy, so it is cheap. A pre-loaded counter against Fireball costs 0.05 × 2,586 ≈ 129 Pz ([[Magical Countermeasures and Magical Intelligence (MCM-MINT)|MCM-MINT]]). Fireball's reservoir cost is one Tier 3 quantum of 2,586 Pz, with the rest of its 13,101 Pz rated draw supplied as field gain ([[Fireball (3-V-M-FRB)|Fireball]]), so the exchange is about 2,586 / 129 ≈ 20:1; the MCM-MINT table prints 13,101 against 129 (see C3). Repeating one counter every 6 s settles recast tension at R ≈ 2.0, doubling its price ([[Recast Tension and Combination Affinity]]). Doctrine holds that fights are decided earlier: a decoy Fireball forms in plain view while Cloak and an illusion hide the real one, and a defender who counters the decoy wastes a chamber ([[Practical Magical Combat]]). Kinetic workings such as the Magical Railcannon are counterable only before release (MCM-MINT). Past the third rung of the counter ladder the contest does become a race of volume and reservoir (MCM-MINT).

*Not in the wiki:* which Fireball figure the reservoir actually pays is unsettled between the exchange table and the Fireball note; see C3 and G1.

### P3 · the author's example question
> Yeah, but wouldn't it be OP for me to focus on some awareness spells then?

**Wiki:** Open sensing is an active working under ULMTU-IV and must be fed for as long as it runs ([[Practical Magical Combat]]). Detect Magic recast every ten minutes costs about 850 Pz a day, and Arcane Eye about 6,500 Pz a day ([[Practical Magical Combat]]). True Seeing draws 805 Pz per hour, so 805 × 24 ≈ 19,300 Pz a day ([[True Seeing (6-D-L-TSU)|True Seeing]]). Only a Tier 6 caster can cast it, which makes it 19,300 / 123,266 ≈ 16% of their reservoir; Practical Magical Combat lists it as 37% of a Tier 5 reservoir (see C4) ([[Caster Capacity and Tiers]]). Detect Magic is cheap and narrow: it reaches 30 ft, and dense stone or metal blocks it ([[Detect Magic (1-D-L-DMT)|Detect Magic]]). Sustained sensing is a steady emitter hostile MINT can track, and under ULMTU-XIII it decays and must be re-formed, adding recast tension ([[Practical Magical Combat]]). Seeing a working also falls short of identifying it, since seven groups of standard workings share identical spectra ([[Arcane Spectroscopy]]).

### P4 · the author's example question
> That means I can permanently boost myself to always perceive the casting, like a third eye.

**Wiki:** An awareness working that reads every nearby casting is active, so under ULMTU-IV it ceases once the caster stops feeding it ([[Practical Magical Combat]]). Only a pattern that waits for a trigger can sit dormant at 1% of its active rate ([[Parzival Energy Budget]], rule C4). A permanent third eye therefore means paying every hour. Arcane Eye costs about 6,500 Pz a day, 12% of a Tier 5 reservoir ([[Arcane Eye (4-D-L-AE)|Arcane Eye]]; [[Practical Magical Combat]]). True Seeing costs about 19,300 Pz a day, 19,300 / 123,266 ≈ 16% of the Tier 6 reservoir it requires ([[True Seeing (6-D-L-TSU)|True Seeing]]; [[Caster Capacity and Tiers]]). Doctrine lists prepaid alternatives. A homunculus draws less through communion but falls to illusions of equal or higher tier. Perception tattoos behave like runes and draw visibly at each activation. An imbued glass eye sees nothing without an active working or a costly permanent transfiguration plus communion ([[Practical Magical Combat]]). The wiki does not price that permanent transfiguration.

*Not in the wiki:* the cost of a permanent transfiguration for a glass eye or third eye; see G20.

## Part 2: Kip asks

### K1 · Kip (Baldur's Gate 3 player)
> Okay, the obvious one first. Do your mages run on spell slots and long rests, or can they just keep casting until they pass out? Because if there's no slot limit, I'm going to have a lot of follow-ups.

**Wiki:** Slots are converted into a daily reservoir: the sum of the quanta a caster's slots would grant, with Q_T = 350·e^(T−1) Pz. A standard Tier 2 mage holds 3,303 Pz and a Tier 5 caster 52,212 Pz ([[Caster Capacity and Tiers]]). Each working draws its computed cost, for example Magic Missile at 5.6 Pz ([[Magic Missile (1-P-L-MM)|Magic Missile]]). Cantrips draw at a rate the field refills as fast as anyone can cast, so fatigue is their limit ([[Caster Capacity and Tiers]]). Spam meets two throttles. Peak sustained draw is about 160 Pz/s at Tier 2 ([[Caster Capacity and Tiers]]), and one cast every 6 s settles recast tension at R ≈ 2.0, doubling every price ([[Recast Tension and Combination Affinity]]). The wiki calls the reservoir "daily" without giving a rest or reset rule, and it does not say what happens to a mage who reaches zero. Whether the reservoir is spent as one pool or slot by slot is also unsettled (see C6).

*Not in the wiki:* a recovery rule and a rule for a mage at zero; see G2 and G3.

### K2 · Kip (Baldur's Gate 3 player)
> Is there a Counterspell? Like, if two mages are dueling, can one of them just go "nope" as a reaction and cancel the other guy's big spell, and can you counter the counter?

**Wiki:** Counterspell is a 3rd-tier abjuration with a 60 ft range. It injects a counter-phase field into a working still forming, collapsing patterns up to the third tier outright and contesting stronger ones ([[Counterspell (3-A-L-CNT)|Counterspell]]). A counter costs 0.05 × Q_T pre-loaded or 0.10 × Q_T cold, which is 129 or 259 Pz against Fireball ([[Magical Countermeasures and Magical Intelligence (MCM-MINT)|MCM-MINT]]). The Counterspell note prints its own draw as 59.2 Pz, while MCM-MINT prices the counter at 0.05 × Q_T (see C1). The threat trigram shows about one second into a four- to five-second cast (MCM-MINT). Countering the counter is the MCCM rung. Options include a false trigram for 0.01 × Q_T and a Shield on the working itself that absorbs the first counter, and a compressed kata closes in under a second (MCM-MINT). Under ULMTU-V a large power gap removes the option to counter; MCM-MINT names Divine Power while the hub speaks of power in general ([[Parzival Thaumaturgy]]), and neither sizes the gap (see C7).

*Not in the wiki:* how the contest against a stronger working resolves and how large a disqualifying gap is; see G4 and G9.

### K3 · Kip (Baldur's Gate 3 player)
> In BG3, if you break someone's concentration, their whole buff or hold spell drops. Do your mages have a concentration thing, and if so, can a sniper just plink a mage mid-cast to make the spell fizzle?

**Wiki:** Cloak, Haste, Hold Person and Invisibility carry a concentration flag. Their ULMTU-IV line reads "held by the caster's attention; the effect ceases without that support" ([[Haste (3-T-L-HST)|Haste]]; [[Invisibility (2-I-L-INV)|Invisibility]]). Concentration adds 0.25 handling points to the pattern cost ([[Parzival Energy Budget]]). A separate fragile flag marks workings that break under injury or interruption, and under ULMTU-XI a broken cast can misfire ([[Invisibility (2-I-L-INV)|Invisibility]]). A sniper who wounds a mage holding Invisibility therefore ends it. For a concentration-only working such as Haste, the wiki does not state whether a wound breaks it. A kata takes four to five seconds ([[Magical Countermeasures and Magical Intelligence (MCM-MINT)|MCM-MINT]]). Fatigue or a damaged glyph collapses field gain, and an over-curve working then fires at its quantum, about a fifth of rated strength for Fireball ([[Parzival Energy Budget]]). The sniper's main obstacle is a ward: a Tier 1 ward holds 350 Pz against 2.2 Pz for a 7.62 × 51 round, about 159 rounds ([[Arcane Active Protection]]).

*Not in the wiki:* whether a wound breaks a concentration-only working or counts as a damaged glyph; see G6.

### K4 · Kip (Baldur's Gate 3 player)
> Does magic missile always hit in your world? I'm asking because if there's some auto-targeting spell, a mage is basically a walking guided missile, and then why does anyone bother building the real ones?

**Wiki:** Magic Missile throws three force darts that fly straight from the caster's hand and burst on impact. They have no drop or drift, and they do not track; the source's never-miss behaviour is not canon ([[Magic Missile (1-P-L-MM)|Magic Missile]]). Tracking needs a separate guidance working bound to the dart, and each added member costs a link and adds a spectral line that enemy MINT reads ([[Practical Magical Combat]]). The working draws 5.6 Pz, with 2.1 Pz of effect energy for all three darts per its registry note, while Arcane Active Protection gives "about 2 Pz per dart" ([[Arcane Active Protection]]). A pre-loaded counter costs 17.5 Pz, and under ULMTU-XII a far stronger caster can undo it outright ([[Magic Missile (1-P-L-MM)|Magic Missile]]). Factory missiles stay the better buy. A Hellfire-class jet carries about 3,100 Pz, while the low-tier Anti-Mage Primer yields about a 900 Pz jet for about 1,840 Pz, 56% of a Tier 2 reservoir, after 18 s of visible casting ([[Arcane Active Protection]]).

### K5 · Kip (Baldur's Gate 3 player)
> Can a mage just shield against bullets? Because stopping one arrow is one thing, but a machine gun puts out hundreds of rounds a minute, so does the shield have like a hit counter, or does it just eat everything until the mage runs dry?

**Wiki:** A ward is a fixed tank of energy. It gets no field gain and holds only what the caster pays in, capped at the tier's quantum: 350 Pz at Tier 1 and 951 Pz at Tier 2 ([[Arcane Active Protection]]). Wards spread at 1,000 m/s per tier, and rifle bullets are slower, so each round meets the whole ward. A 7.62 × 51 round carries 2.2 Pz, so a Tier 1 ward lasts 350 / 2.2 ≈ 159 rounds and a Tier 2 ward 951 / 2.2 ≈ 432. A .50 BMG round at 11 Pz empties a Tier 1 ward in about 350 / 11 ≈ 32 ([[Arcane Active Protection]]). Restoring a spent ward takes a new cast with recast tension ([[Recast Tension and Combination Affinity]]). Three stacked Tier 2 wards triple the capacity against a rifle burst. A 30 mm round at 1,400 m/s outruns a Tier 1 ward's spread and passes it, while Tier 2 and Tier 3 wards stop it ([[Arcane Active Protection]]).

### K6 · Kip (Baldur's Gate 3 player)
> Here's my cheese strategy. If invisibility works on people, does it work on thermal cameras and radar, or would a drone operator just see a warm mage-shaped blob walking around?

**Wiki:** Invisibility steers incident light around one creature and its carried gear, and its note warns that thermal and other active sensors may still detect the subject ([[Invisibility (2-I-L-INV)|Invisibility]]). Greater Invisibility is likewise rated against visual sensors ([[Greater Invisibility (4-I-L-GIE)|Greater Invisibility]]). Cloak also damps the subject's arcane emission, lowering the optical and spectroscopic signature ([[Cloak (2-I-L-CLK)|Cloak]]). No note claims that any of these hides body heat, and none mentions radar. The casting itself gives the mage away: Invisibility averages 31.6 Pz/s over its six-second cast, far above the 6 Pz/s ELINT threshold, and a field spectrometer detects it at about 1.38 km ([[Invisibility (2-I-L-INV)|Invisibility]]; [[Arcane Spectroscopy]]). Invisibility is also flagged fragile, so it breaks under injury or when the subject strikes or channels another working ([[Invisibility (2-I-L-INV)|Invisibility]]).

*Not in the wiki:* whether invisibility defeats thermal imaging (the note says only "may") or radar; see G14.

### K7 · Kip (Baldur's Gate 3 player)
> Can a mage enchant a tank shell, or bless a whole artillery battery before a barrage? In BG3 I'd absolutely stack every buff on my best archer, so I want to know if the army can do that at scale.

**Wiki:** Bless is a 1st-tier enchantment giving up to three allies within 30 ft a small lift in coordination and resolve for up to one minute under concentration, for 6.3 Pz ([[Bless (1-E-L-BLSS)|Bless]]). It acts on gun crews, and shells are outside its scope. A battery takes many casts, and one cast every 6 s settles recast tension at R ≈ 2.0, so each Bless climbs toward 6.3 × 2.0 = 12.6 Pz ([[Recast Tension and Combination Affinity]]). Blessing munitions is a divine act. A round blessed by a patron satisfies Lex Fontis and counts as a counter-countermeasure against mortal-grade magic. An Artemisian round keeps to its marked quarry, and a Hephaestian casing resists wards up to the blessing's tier ([[Arcane Active Protection]]). The note marks these effects as "proposed defaults, not canon" and gives no cost or batch size. The defender's answer is a blessing from their own patron at equal or higher tier, or saturation with decoys, and divine involvement brings political costs ([[Arcane Active Protection]]).

*Not in the wiki:* a cost or batch size for blessed munitions, and any rule for mortal mages enchanting shells; see G10.

### K8 · Kip (Baldur's Gate 3 player)
> Okay, gods. If a mage prays to a god or makes a pact with one, can the god pull the plug mid-fight? Like Shadowheart's Shar situation, where your power comes from someone who might just change their mind.

**Wiki:** The wiki says nothing about pacts, prayer or a god revoking a mortal's power. A caster supplies only a small perturbation, and the thaumic field returns the quantum ([[Thaumic Field Model]]). A mortal's daily reservoir comes from the quanta of their tier's slots, 3,303 Pz for a standard Tier 2 mage ([[Caster Capacity and Tiers]]), and no god appears in that chain. Divine beings top their reservoirs from Ichor and Divine Energy, and a demigod's reservoir is their tier's reservoir scaled by a Divine Affinity factor of 0.5 to 3 ([[Caster Capacity and Tiers]]). The nearest mechanic is efficiency. A divine domain mismatch through DSEE or opposed Divine Affinity sets channel efficiency between 0.3 and 1.0, so at η = 0.3 every working costs 1 / 0.3 ≈ 3.3 times as much ([[Parzival Energy Budget]]). An Arcane Capacitor can also be charged from divine supply ([[Arcane Active Protection]]).

*Not in the wiki:* any rule for a patron withdrawing power from a caster; see G11.

### K9 · Kip (Baldur's Gate 3 player)
> Can a mage actually kill a god or a demigod? And if they can, does the mage get the god's powers, because in BG3 the whole endgame is basically about who gets to absorb the big scary power.

**Wiki:** The wiki does not describe a mage killing a god, and it has no rule for absorbing divine power. On scale, demigods in their prime sit at tiers 6 to 7, and major divine powers share tiers 8 to 9 with archmages ([[Caster Capacity and Tiers]]). Under ULMTU-V a large gap in Divine Power removes the option to counter ([[Magical Countermeasures and Magical Intelligence (MCM-MINT)|MCM-MINT]]), while the hub words the rule as power in general ([[Parzival Thaumaturgy]]); neither sizes the gap (see C7). Divine wards are costly to breach. A Lance of Longinus against an intact divine ward stack draws about 154,835 Pz, and the Anti-Mage Primer brings primer plus lance to about 6,700 Pz, 6,700 / 154,835 ≈ 4% of that ([[Combat Combination Analysis]]). Soul work costs 10⁴ Pz per tier above 3 ([[Parzival Energy Budget]]), and under ULMTU-XV soul and death work returns consequence on its maker ([[Wish (9-C-L-WSH)|Wish]]). The wiki does not classify deicide as soul work.

*Not in the wiki:* a deicide mechanic with any inheritance of power, and the size of the power gap; see G12 and G9.

### K10 · Kip (Baldur's Gate 3 player)
> Last one, and it's the exploit one. Is there anything like scrolls or potions, where a non-mage soldier can carry a spell and just use it? Because if so, I'd hand every grunt in the trench a stack of Misty Step scrolls and call it a day.

**Wiki:** The wiki has no scrolls or potions, and nothing on a non-mage waking a stored working at will. Stored energy sits in an Arcane Capacitor, a mana crystal charged only from the attuner's reservoir or from divine supply. A working may draw from it only if the caster already knows that working, and a crystal never raises tier ([[Arcane Active Protection]]). It serves as a mage's battery. Workings can be held dormant at 1% of their active rate and released by a trigger with nobody watching, which is how Mist Picket layers fire on a tripwire ([[Arcane Active Protection]]; [[Parzival Energy Budget]], rule C4). Doctrine also mentions passive curios that only need recharging ([[Practical Magical Combat]]). Stored charge is visible: a full Tier 4 crystal of 21,090 Pz shows on a spectrometer at 1 km × √(21,090 / 100) ≈ 14.5 km ([[Arcane Active Protection]]). Misty Step moves only the caster ([[Misty Step (2-C-L-MS)|Misty Step]]).

*Not in the wiki:* whether a non-mage can carry and trigger a stored working such as Misty Step; see G19.

### K11 · Kip (Baldur's Gate 3 player)
> Follow-up: hang on, the wiki says a Fireball draws 13,101 Pz, but a whole Tier 3 reservoir is only 9,427. So a Tier 3 mage can't afford a single Fireball out of their own tank? I'm guessing the field covers the gap, since Fireball drops to "about a fifth" when gain collapses, and 2,586 times five is about 13,000. So what does the caster actually pay from their reservoir, and if it's only the quantum, isn't the hundred-to-one counter exchange ratio more like twenty-to-one?

**Wiki:** A working is supplied by its slot's quantum ([[Caster Capacity and Tiers]]). When its total exceeds the quantum it runs on field gain, and if gain collapses Fireball fires at its quantum, about a fifth of rated strength ([[Parzival Energy Budget]]). The Fireball note gives a field gain of 5.07 and calls the excess over 2,586 Pz field gain ([[Fireball (3-V-M-FRB)|Fireball]]); 2,586 / 13,101 ≈ 0.20. The caster supplies only the perturbation that tips the field ([[Thaumic Field Model]]). On this reading a Tier 3 mage spends 2,586 Pz of 9,427 per Fireball, and the exchange against a 129 Pz pre-loaded counter is 2,586 / 129 ≈ 20:1. The MCM-MINT exchange table prints 13,101 against 129, setting delivered energy against reservoir spent ([[Magical Countermeasures and Magical Intelligence (MCM-MINT)|MCM-MINT]]), and Practical Magical Combat repeats the "hundredth" (see C3). A pool reading allows 9,427 / 2,586 ≈ 3.6 Fireballs, though the caster holds two Tier 3 slots (see C6).

*Not in the wiki:* what a below-curve working such as Magic Missile debits, its 5.6 Pz draw or a full quantum; see G1.

### K12 · Kip (Baldur's Gate 3 player)
> Follow-up: the wiki calls the reservoir "daily" and has no rest rule. In BG3 I abuse long rests constantly, so I have to ask: is it a rolling 24 hours, or does it reset at a fixed time? Because if it resets at midnight, I'm dumping my whole tank at 23:55 and getting a fresh one at 00:01, which is two reservoirs for one fight.

**Wiki:** The wiki does not say. It defines the reservoir as "how much field return a caster can command in a day" and gives no reset time, rolling or fixed ([[Caster Capacity and Tiers]]). What it does allow is banking. An Arcane Capacitor stores charge from the attuner's own reservoir, up to three quanta of their tier: about 7,760 Pz at Tier 3, against a full Tier 3 reservoir of 9,427 Pz. It charges no faster than peak draw, about Q_T over 6 s, so a Tier 3 mage fills one in roughly 7,760 / (2,586 / 6) ≈ 18 s ([[Arcane Active Protection]]; [[Caster Capacity and Tiers]]). The crystal leaks 1% of its charge per hour and glows for MINT at about 1 km × √(7,760 / 100) ≈ 8.8 km. If struck or dispelled it releases a quarter of its charge into its holder, and it never raises tier ([[Arcane Active Protection]]). Carrying stored energy into the next day is canon within that cap, and it is visible.

*Not in the wiki:* a reset rule for the daily reservoir; see G2.

### K13 · Kip (Baldur's Gate 3 player)
> Follow-up: combine the ward numbers with the materia rule. Passive stuff like Shield can sleep in materia at 1% of its active rate and fire on a trigger with nobody watching, which is how Mist Picket works on a tripwire. So why can't a mage pre-bind a Tier 2 ward into a grunt's vest, set to trigger on incoming fire, and hand out a whole crate of them? That's 432 rifle rounds of armour per soldier, and the soldier never has to cast anything.

**Wiki:** No note forbids one such vest; dormant binding is canon ([[Parzival Energy Budget]], rule C4; [[Arcane Active Protection]]). The limit is supply. A ward holds only what its maker pays in, up to 951 Pz at Tier 2 ([[Arcane Active Protection]]). A standard Tier 2 mage holds 3,303 Pz from four Tier 1 slots and two Tier 2 slots ([[Caster Capacity and Tiers]]). Counted by slots that funds two full Tier 2 wards; counted as a pool it funds 3,303 / 951 ≈ 3.5 before pattern cost and recast tension (see C6). Each dormant pattern leaks at 1% for MINT to see ([[Magical Countermeasures and Magical Intelligence (MCM-MINT)|MCM-MINT]]). Arcane Active Protection prices a ward dispel at 5%, 47.6 Pz at Tier 2, while MCM-MINT charges a closed working a cold counter of 95.1 Pz plus 20% of remaining upkeep (see C2). Mage Armor collapses if the subject puts on conventional armour ([[Mage Armor (1-P-L-MA)|Mage Armor]]), and the wiki does not say whether a ward bound into a vest shares that weakness.

*Not in the wiki:* how many dormant wards one maker can keep bound and how long they last; see G19 and G13.

### K14 · Kip (Baldur's Gate 3 player)
> Follow-up: the wiki says a god-blessed Artemisian round "keeps to its marked quarry", and also that the auto-hit Magic Missile isn't canon here. So a demigod blessing ammo just brings back the never-miss dart, with no cost or batch size in the record. And if ULMTU-V says a big gap in divine power means you can't counter at all, does a Tier 2 mage on the other side get any answer to that bullet, or is the only counter "go find your own god"?

**Wiki:** The Artemisian line sits in a table marked "proposed defaults, not canon", with no cost or batch size ([[Arcane Active Protection]]). Taken at face value, the Artemisian round defeats "lures and decoys of every kind" and makes no claim against wards. Lures form the soft-kill layer, and the later layers still apply: a Tier 2 ward holds 951 Pz against a 5.56 mm bullet's 1.2 Pz, about 951 / 1.2 ≈ 790 rounds of capacity ([[Arcane Active Protection]]). The blessing that defeats wards is the Hephaestian casing, and only up to the blessing's tier. The listed counter-counter is a blessing from the defender's own patron at equal or higher tier, or saturation with more decoys than the blessing can sort ([[Arcane Active Protection]]). MCM-MINT ties the ULMTU-V gap to Divine Power while the hub words it as power in general, and neither sizes it ([[Magical Countermeasures and Magical Intelligence (MCM-MINT)|MCM-MINT]]; [[Parzival Thaumaturgy]]) (see C7). The wiki does not say whether the rule covers a blessed physical round.

*Not in the wiki:* a cost and counter model for blessed rounds, and the size of the power gap; see G10 and G9.

## Part 3: Ness asks

### N1 · Ness (tabletop game master)
> When two mages go at it, what decides who wins? Is it raw power, or is there something like a dispel check where the weaker caster can still get lucky?

**Wiki:** Combat doctrine states that tier is not destiny: a caster who understands a working's attributes can unmake an enemy working for a fraction of its cost ([[Practical Magical Combat]]). A fight runs as a chain of identification before release, an anti-phase counter, deception with cloaks and decoys, then escalation up the counter ladder ([[Magical Countermeasures and Magical Intelligence (MCM-MINT)|MCM-MINT]]). A counter matches the pattern and ignores the energy, so a pre-loaded counter costs 0.05 × Q_T, which is 0.05 × 2,586 ≈ 129 Pz against a Fireball ([[Fireball (3-V-M-FRB)|Fireball]]). Reservoir against reservoir that is 2,586 / 129 ≈ 20:1, while the MCM-MINT table prints 13,101 Pz against 129 Pz (see C3). Raw power decides only across a vast power gap, where ULMTU-V removes every counter ([[Practical Magical Combat]]). Counterspell resolves stronger targets "by a contest with no extra energy modeled" ([[Counterspell (3-A-L-CNT)|Counterspell]]). The wiki does not define how that contest is decided, so it contains no dice-style dispel check.

*Not in the wiki:* how a counter against a stronger working resolves, or how wide a "vast" gap is; see G4 and G9.

### N2 · Ness (tabletop game master)
> Is there an action economy at all? If one mage can throw ten spells in the time another throws one, I don't see why the slow guy would ever show up.

**Wiki:** A standard cast is a kata of four to five seconds, and a spectrometer reads its threat trigram about one second in ([[Magical Countermeasures and Magical Intelligence (MCM-MINT)|MCM-MINT]]). Field practice spreads a quantum over one six-second cast, giving a peak draw of about 160 Pz/s at Tier 2 and about 3,200 Pz/s at Tier 5 ([[Caster Capacity and Tiers]]). A compressed kata with a pre-drawn trigram closes in under a second for the cost of dormant upkeep, and layer scrambles raise misfire risk under ULMTU-XI (MCM-MINT). Recast tension taxes volume: one working every 6 s settles at N_eff ≈ 14.5, and R = 1.05^14.5 ≈ 2.0, so the fast caster pays double ([[Recast Tension and Combination Affinity]]). Volume has a defined job in saturation, where cheap workings drain the defender's ready counters before the real one goes (MCM-MINT). Slow low-tier casting below the 6 Pz/s ELINT threshold goes unseen ([[Arcane Spectroscopy]]). The wiki does not say whether one caster may trace two katas at once.

*Not in the wiki:* a rule on overlapping casts; see G7.

### N3 · Ness (tabletop game master)
> Can a mage keep more than one effect running at once, or is there a hard limit like concentration? If there's no limit, why doesn't everyone just stack buffs before a duel and stay stacked forever?

**Wiki:** Several effects can run at once. Combinations bind several workings into one effect through a binder ([[Recast Tension and Combination Affinity]]), and a missile picket layers several workings on one position ([[Arcane Active Protection]]). Many workings carry a concentration flag under ULMTU-IV, and the wiki sets no cap on how many one mind can hold. Cost limits stacking. Haste lasts up to a minute at 51.3 Pz, so holding it all day takes 1,440 × 51.3 ≈ 73,900 Pz before tension, more than a Tier 5 reservoir of 52,212 Pz ([[Haste (3-T-L-HST)|Haste]]; [[Caster Capacity and Tiers]]). Every recast also raises recast tension. A running Haste is a closed working, and stripping it costs the unravel price of about 262 Pz, a cold counter plus 20% of remaining upkeep ([[Haste (3-T-L-HST)|Haste]]). Dormant patterns run at 1% of their active rate and still show to MINT ([[Parzival Energy Budget]]; [[Magical Countermeasures and Magical Intelligence (MCM-MINT)|MCM-MINT]]). Haste also carries an after-effect that makes timing matter.

*Not in the wiki:* a cap on concurrent concentration workings; see G5.

### N4 · Ness (tabletop game master)
> Can a mage cast without anyone noticing, no words and no gestures? If they can, how does anybody in your world ever prove who started a fight?

**Wiki:** Components vary by working. Counterspell is somatic only and Misty Step is spoken only ([[Counterspell (3-A-L-CNT)|Counterspell]]; [[Misty Step (2-C-L-MS)|Misty Step]]), and the UOE fields throat-mic incantation and gauntlet kata ([[Parzival Thaumaturgy]]). Arcane Spectroscopy puts any first-tier cast at about 350 / 6 ≈ 58 Pz/s, ten times the 6 Pz/s ELINT threshold, while the registry computes each rate from its own draw, such as Cloak at 6.8 Pz/s ([[Cloak (2-I-L-CLK)|Cloak]]) (see C5). *Not in the wiki:* the evidential use of MINT logs to prove who cast first; see G17.

### N5 · Ness (tabletop game master)
> When a mage deals with a god or a demigod, is the god's power the same kind of thing as the mage's power, just bigger? Or is it a different system that the mage's tricks can't touch?

**Wiki:** Divine power works on the same field. Divine casters work the same patterns with a Divine Affinity lean, and their Divine Power sets what may be countered at all under ULMTU-V ([[Parzival Thaumaturgy]]). A divine being's Divine Energy and Ichor deepen the field minima it casts into, which lowers its effective cost ([[Thaumic Field Model]]). Its reservoir is that of its caster tier scaled by a Divine Affinity factor between 0.5 and 3, so a tier-7 demigod at factor 3 holds 264,466 × 3 = 793,398 Pz ([[Caster Capacity and Tiers]]). Mortal counters therefore reach divine workings in principle. A large gap in Divine Power removes the chance to resist ([[Bless (1-E-L-BLSS)|Bless]]) and the counter option ([[Magical Countermeasures and Magical Intelligence (MCM-MINT)|MCM-MINT]]). MCM-MINT names Divine Power, while the hub speaks of "comparable power" and [[Practical Magical Combat]] of a "vast" gap in power generally (see C7). No note gives the size of the gap.

*Not in the wiki:* how large a power gap must be to remove counters; see G9.

The wiki does not say whether cancelling field return touches Ichor, or whether a Divine Power gap protects a god inside the sphere. It contains no account of gods hunting mages.

*Not in the wiki:* Antimagic Field against Ichor and divine beings, see G9; divine policy toward mages has no open item.

### N7 · Ness (tabletop game master)

*Not in the wiki:* whether Wish can erase a divine being, and the size of the power gap; see G9 and C7.

### N8 · Ness (tabletop game master)

**Wiki:** Protection must be up beforehand. ### N9 · Ness (tabletop game master)

### B3 · Bram (anime and fantasy reader)

**Wiki:** Tiers are hard ceilings, and the wiki has no desperation rule. Divine beings scale their reservoirs by a Divine Affinity factor of 0.5 to 3 ([[Caster Capacity and Tiers]]). > Follow-up: okay, I'll be honest, fatigue collapsing field gain really hurt me. But the wiki says the middle stage, competence, is about holding a pattern cleanly enough to get field gain on over-curve workings. So what stops a hero from reaching that stage in the middle of a fight, in one perfect moment of focus, instead of while he's panicking? Does the wiki say learning has to be slow, or is that just how it usually goes?

**Wiki:** The wiki gives the path no clock. ULMTU-XVIII requires only "a defined path from access to mastery", and the stages carry no duration ([[Caster Capacity and Tiers]]). Field gain is judged cast by cast: Fireball's excess "is legal only while the caster holds the full 3rd Tier pattern; a degraded pattern falls back to the quantum" ([[Fireball (3-V-M-FRB)|Fireball]]). A caster who holds a pattern cleanly on one cast under stress therefore takes the gain on that cast, and no rule forbids it. Two limits apply. The pattern must already be known, since workings have fixed, repeatable means under ULMTU-VII ([[Parzival Thaumaturgy]]). Gain never raises tier, because a Tier-T caster reaches tier T and below ([[Caster Capacity and Tiers]]). Training appears only inside channel efficiency, 0.6 to 0.95, with no rate of improvement ([[Parzival Energy Budget]]). The wiki does not state whether one clean hold makes competence permanent.

*Not in the wiki:* a time scale or learning rate for the mastery path; see G8.

### B12 · Bram (anime and fantasy reader)
> Follow-up: the drone answer scared me, so now I want to flip it around. If a familiar is basically a little drone that can't be jammed, can I send my familiar up to hunt the enemy's drones and see them through its eyes? I'm picturing a spirit owl dogfighting a quadcopter, and I need to know whether that's a real tactic or just me.

**Wiki:** The spotting half is supported. [[Find Familiar (1-C-H-FFN)|Find Familiar]] gives a shared-sense channel over a telepathic link, and the registry treats the familiar as a scout and relay that cannot be jammed electronically. The link reaches 30 m and [[Magic Missile (1-P-L-MM)|Magic Missile]] reaches 120 ft (about 37 m), so the familiar spots close to its caster, who then shoots. A dart kills a drone for about 2 Pz, though darts fly straight and a weaving drone at 20 to 40 m/s is a hard shot ([[Arcane Active Protection]]). The dogfight half has no support. The wiki gives the familiar no attack and no flight speed, and its one offensive use is carrying touch-range workings forward. It is flagged fragile. Replacing it takes a 1 hour ritual and 212 Pz ([[Find Familiar (1-C-H-FFN)|Find Familiar]]).

*Not in the wiki:* familiar attack, flight speed or combat rules; see G20.

### B13 · Bram (anime and fantasy reader)
> Follow-up: wait, go back to the Lance of Longinus, because dropping the bill from about 154,835 Pz down to 6,700 is the most anime thing I've heard all day! Who actually opens the divine ward stack first? Could a whole squad of weaker mages break it open together so one person gets to land the big hit?

**Wiki:** The wiki splits the job between two workings. The opener is the Anti-Mage Primer, a combination costing 1,838 Pz, which is 56% of a Tier 2 reservoir or 19% of a Tier 3 one, and a Tier 2 battle mage carries one ([[Combat Combination Analysis]]). The Lance of Longinus is an 8th-tier working ([[Parzival Thaumaturgy]]), so the finisher is a far stronger caster. Through the opened stack the Lance draws about 4,835 Pz, so 1,838 + 4,835 = 6,673 Pz, about 4.3% of the 154,835 Pz unprimed figure ([[Combat Combination Analysis]]). UOE doctrine against Armoured Titans and Behemoths uses the same order, cheap layered breachers first and the killing working second. The wiki does not state whether several casters can pool one primer. An array sharing a load leaks a concentrated signature that MINT catches at once ([[Practical Magical Combat]]). Countered in flight, the primer's binder costs the attacker 1,838 Pz and the defender 48 Pz ([[Magical Countermeasures and Magical Intelligence (MCM-MINT)|MCM-MINT]]).

*Not in the wiki:* rules for several casters sharing one working; see G16.

### B14 · Bram (anime and fantasy reader)
> Follow-up: the wiki says a struck or dispelled crystal dumps a quarter of its charge back into the mage. So could a desperate mage overcharge a crystal and throw it, or set it off on purpose next to an enemy, like a last-stand self-destruct? Or does that backlash only ever hit whoever was holding it?

**Wiki:** Under ULMTU-XI a struck or dispelled crystal releases a quarter of its charge into its holder, and the wiki names no other target ([[Arcane Active Protection]]). Overcharging has no route. A crystal holds at most three quanta of the attuner's tier and charges only from the attuner's own reservoir or divine supply, at no more than peak draw. It leaks 1% an hour. A full Tier 4 crystal holds about 21,090 Pz, so a rupture puts 21,090 / 4 ≈ 5,272 Pz into its holder. The wiki does not state whether a thrown crystal makes the catcher its holder. The reverse threat is explicit. A charged crystal glows as a MINT target at r = 1 km × √(E/100), and a full Tier 4 crystal is seen at about 14.5 km, so an enemy can find it and strike or dispel it ([[Arcane Active Protection]]).

*Not in the wiki:* a rule for throwing or deliberately detonating a crystal; see G20.

# Open items for the author

## Contradictions in the wiki

**C1. Counterspell's own draw.**
[[Counterspell (3-A-L-CNT)|Counterspell]] models its draw at 59.2 Pz and states that stronger targets are "resolved by a contest with no extra energy modeled". [[Magical Countermeasures and Magical Intelligence (MCM-MINT)|MCM-MINT]] prices every counter at 0.05 x Q_T of the target pre-loaded, which is 129 Pz against any Tier 3 working, and the Counterspell note's own MCM profile prints that 129 Pz.
Suggested resolution: Use the MCM-MINT rate of 0.05 x Q_T for every counter. Add a line to the Counterspell note saying 59.2 Pz is the bare working priced against a Tier 3 pattern.

**C2. Ward dispel rate.**
[[Arcane Active Protection]] rule 5 lets an enemy dispel a ward at 5% of its quantum pre-loaded or 10% cold. [[Magical Countermeasures and Magical Intelligence (MCM-MINT)|MCM-MINT]] ties those rates to a working still forming and charges a cold counter plus 20% of remaining upkeep once a working has closed.
Suggested resolution: Apply the unravel price to any ward already standing, since Arcane Active Protection rule 5 itself defers to "the counter rates in MCM-MINT". G13 gives the full reconciliation.

**C3. Fireball cost to the caster.**
The exchange table in [[Magical Countermeasures and Magical Intelligence (MCM-MINT)|MCM-MINT]] charges the attacker 13,101 Pz against a 129 Pz counter, and [[Practical Magical Combat]] calls a counter "about a hundredth of the evocation". [[Fireball (3-V-M-FRB)|Fireball]] lists a slot quantum of 2,586 Pz and calls the output above it field gain, and [[Caster Capacity and Tiers]] says a working is supplied by its quantum.
Suggested resolution: Have the reservoir pay the quantum, so the exchange is about 20:1 (2,586 / 129). Add a "reservoir spent" column to the MCM-MINT table.

**C4. True Seeing's share of a reservoir.**
[[Practical Magical Combat]] lists [[True Seeing (6-D-L-TSU)|True Seeing]] at about 19,300 Pz a day as 37% of a Tier 5 reservoir (19,332 / 52,212). True Seeing is a 6th-tier working, and [[Caster Capacity and Tiers]] lets only a Tier 6 caster cast it.
Suggested resolution: Restate the share as about 16% of a Tier 6 reservoir (19,332 / 123,266 = 15.7%).

**C5. First-tier emission rate.**
[[Arcane Spectroscopy]] states that a first-tier working cast in six seconds runs at about 58 Pz/s, ten times the ELINT threshold. Each registry note computes emission from its own draw: [[Cloak (2-I-L-CLK)|Cloak]] at 6.8 Pz/s and [[Magic Missile (1-P-L-MM)|Magic Missile]] at 0.93 Pz/s. The line in [[Parzival Energy Budget]] that a below-curve remainder "bleeds off as light and heat" gives the 58 Pz/s figure some footing.
Suggested resolution: Use the registry rates, since every published detection range rests on them and low-tier stealth depends on them. Reword the "bleeds off" line so the unpaid remainder emits nothing (see G1).

**C6. Reservoir as pool or as slots.**
[[Caster Capacity and Tiers]] defines the reservoir as the sum of slot quanta (9,427 Pz at Tier 3, from 4 x T1 + 3 x T2 + 2 x T3) and converts slots into a budget so tactics "can be stated as a fraction of a mage's budget". [[Combat Combination Analysis]] and [[Practical Magical Combat]] spend it as a pool, while vest pricing in earlier answers counted slots.
Suggested resolution: Treat the reservoir as a pool. Rule explicitly whether a Tier 3 mage with two Tier 3 slots may cast a third Fireball (9,427 / 2,586 = 3.6).

**C7. Scope of the power gap rule.**
[[Magical Countermeasures and Magical Intelligence (MCM-MINT)|MCM-MINT]] says a large gap in Divine Power removes the option to counter. [[Parzival Thaumaturgy]] states "every working can be opposed by one of comparable power", and [[Practical Magical Combat]] says "unless the power gap is vast". Neither sizes the gap.
Suggested resolution: Write one rule covering a gap in power of any kind, sized as in G4 and G9.

**C8. Magic Missile dart energy.**
[[Magic Missile (1-P-L-MM)|Magic Missile]] gives 2.1 Pz of effect energy for all three darts together (3 × 3.5 × 0.40 × 0.5). [[Arcane Active Protection]] says "about 2 Pz per dart" when it discusses killing drones.
Suggested resolution: keep the registry's 2.1 Pz for the whole volley and change Arcane Active Protection to "about 0.7 Pz per dart, about 2 Pz for the volley".

## Easy-to-miss figures

- 30 mm cannon: a Tier 1 ward does not stop a 30 mm round (1,400 m/s outruns its 1,000 m/s spread), and Tier 2 and Tier 3 wards do stop it ([[Arcane Active Protection]]).
- Stripping a standing working costs the unravel price (cold counter + 20% of remaining upkeep): running [[Haste (3-T-L-HST)|Haste]] 262 Pz, [[Cloak (2-I-L-CLK)|Cloak]] 95.1 Pz, [[Arcane Eye (4-D-L-AE)|Arcane Eye]] 739 Pz, a standing Tier 1 ward at least 35 Pz; the 5% rate applies only to a working still forming.
- The Fireball counter exchange is about 20:1 reservoir to reservoir (2,586 vs 129 Pz), while the [[Magical Countermeasures and Magical Intelligence (MCM-MINT)|MCM-MINT]] table prints 13,101 vs 129 Pz.
- [[True Seeing (6-D-L-TSU)|True Seeing]] held all day costs about 19,300 Pz, about 16% of a Tier 6 reservoir.
- The ULMTU-V gap rule is worded as power in general in the hub and [[Practical Magical Combat]], and it is unsized (see C7).

## Proposed rulings for the author (proposal, not canon)

**G1. What the reservoir pays** (raised by: K11, N11)
Gap: The wiki does not say what a caster's reservoir is debited for a working above or below its tier quantum.
Proposal: The reservoir debits min(E_total, Q_T) x R / η. A Fireball costs a Tier 3 caster 2,586 Pz and the field supplies the other 10,515 Pz while the pattern is held cleanly; Magic Missile costs 5.6 Pz. The unpaid remainder of a below-curve quantum emits nothing, so registry emission rates stand. The MCM-MINT exchange table gains a "reservoir spent" column: 2,586 against 129 Pz, about 20:1.
Status: suggested with amendment. The clause making the remainder bleed as light is dropped, since it would lift every first-tier cast to 58 Pz/s and remove low-tier cover.

**G2. When the reservoir refills** (raised by: K1, K12)
Gap: The wiki gives a "daily" reservoir with no recovery or reset rule.
Proposal: Recovery is continuous at 1/24 of the reservoir per hour, awake or asleep: 138 Pz/h at Tier 2, 2,176 Pz/h at Tier 5. There is no fixed reset. Recovered capacity is perturbation potential and emits nothing.
Status: suggested to adopt. It closes the midnight double-reservoir exploit.

**G3. A mage at zero** (raised by: B1, K1)
Gap: The wiki does not say what happens when a reservoir is empty.
Proposal: A caster may overdraw to minus 10% of their reservoir, at η ≤ 0.6 with no field gain. At minus 10% they fall unconscious until recovery returns them to zero, 2.4 hours at any tier. No working converts life force into Pz.
Status: suggested to adopt. It gives a last cast with a real penalty.

**G4. Counter against a stronger working** (raised by: N1, N12, N14)
Gap: Counterspell and Dispel Magic "contest" workings above Tier 3 with no defined resolution.
Proposal: No dice. A counter succeeds if the pattern was read correctly and its price fits inside the window at peak draw (Q_c / 6 per second). The window runs from trigram read to closure, about 3 s, giving 0.5 x Q_c; the pre-drawn part of a ring trigram counts as already paid. MCM-MINT's 0.3 to 0.6 s is release time only. A pre-loaded counter (0.05 x Q_t) reaches about 2.3 tiers above the counterer and a cold counter (0.10 x Q_t) about 1.6 tiers. Against a compressed kata the window shrinks to about 1 s and a pre-loaded counter reaches about one tier up. A Tier 5 caster facing Wish has a four-tier gap and cannot counter it.
Status: suggested with amendment. The window is defined as trigram read to closure, with pre-drawn ring layers counted as paid.

**G5. Concentration cap** (raised by: N3, N14)
Gap: The wiki has no limit on how many concentration workings one caster holds.
Proposal: A caster may hold 1 + floor(T/3) concentration workings: one at Tiers 1 and 2, two at Tiers 3 to 5, three at Tiers 6 to 8, four at Tier 9. Each one beyond the first lowers η by 0.1. Dormant patterns do not count. A Tier 4 stack of Cloak and Arcane Eye over a bound ward is legal at η = 0.9.
Status: suggested to adopt. It gives a usable number and keeps low tiers single-task.

**G6. Wounds and concentration** (raised by: K3)
Gap: "Fragile" workings end on injury, and concentration has no wound rule.
Proposal: Fragile workings end on any hit, including one a ward absorbs. Concentration workings end on a wound that reaches the body past wards and armour. A wound during a kata degrades the glyph, so the working fires at its quantum with no field gain, and a scrambled or compressed kata misfires under ULMTU-XI.
Status: suggested to adopt. A sniper must empty the ward first, which keeps rifles relevant.

**G7. One cast at a time** (raised by: N2)
Gap: The wiki is silent on overlapping katas.
Proposal: A caster traces one somatic kata at a time and may overlap it with one spoken-only working, provided combined draw stays under peak. The rule must state whether releasing a ring trigram counts as the caster's one kata.
Status: suggested with amendment. The ring-trigram case needs an explicit answer.

**G8. Competence in one moment** (raised by: B3, B11)
Gap: Field gain is judged cast by cast, and ULMTU-XVIII sets no timescale for reaching competence.
Proposal: A clean hold requires η ≥ 0.8 at the moment of casting. The first clean hold of a known working under stress grants permanent competence in it. Nothing raises tier. This proposal is flagged as giving an advantage with no cost. The alternative is that a clean hold under stress earns field gain on that cast only.
Status: flagged against. Permanent competence from one moment carries no cost.

**G9. Size of the power gap** (raised by: N5, K14, N6, N7)
Gap: ULMTU-V removes counters across a "large" Divine Power gap that is never sized, and whether Antimagic Field touches Ichor is open.
Proposal: Effective tier T_eff = T + ln(Divine Affinity factor); mortals have factor 1. A gap of 3 or more in T_eff removes every counter and every resistance. Below that, G4 governs: a tier-7 demigod at factor 3 (T_eff 8.1) can be countered by a Tier 6 mage and not by a Tier 5. Antimagic Field suppresses divine workings unless the being stands three effective tiers above its caster, and never touches Ichor itself.
Status: suggested with amendment. The clause letting Wish erase a being below T_eff 9 is dropped, since a roughly 1 MPz local working deleting a demigod breaks the scale.

**G10. Blessed munitions** (raised by: K7, K14)
Gap: The blessing table is labelled "proposed defaults" and is unpriced.
Proposal: A blessed round carries a dormant pattern held by a sympathy link to its patron, 0.1 Pz/s active (rule C2) and 3.6 Pz/h dormant (rule C4). The patron pays the pattern cost 0.01 x Q_Tb x (1 + h) per round. A Hephaestian round negates ward capacity only up to the energy prepaid into it, at Q_Tb per round. Each Artemisian round prepays 1/10 of the blessing's quantum, which limits a tier-7 demigod at factor 3 to about 56 rounds from a full reservoir (264,466 x 3 / 14,120), and the mark on its quarry is a visible working. Every blessed round shows on a spectrometer and can be countered under G4 and G9.
Status: suggested with amendment. At 1% per round a tier-7 demigod could bless over 500 Artemisian rounds a day.

**G11. A patron withdrawing power** (raised by: K8)
Gap: The wiki says nothing on pacts or revocation.
Proposal: A mortal whose reservoir is scaled by a patron's affinity factor drops to factor 1 on revocation, and crystals charged from divine supply drain at once. A hostile patron can impose its domain mismatch, η down to 0.3, only on workings in its own domain.
Status: suggested to adopt. Patrons can withdraw favour without switching off mortal magic.

**G12. Killing or sealing a god** (raised by: K9, B8, B9)
Gap: The wiki is silent on deicide and on sealing a divine being.
Proposal: Killing a divine being costs soul work under ULMTU-XV equal to the being's tier quantum x its affinity factor, on top of breaching its wards; a tier-7 demigod at factor 3 costs 141,200 x 3 = 423,600 Pz. Nothing transfers to the killer. Sealing is a working against will (ULMTU-XIV), so it cannot sleep in materia; it costs 1% of the being's quantum per hour, 1,412 Pz/h for a tier-7 demigod, and leaks a signature like any held pattern.
Status: suggested with amendment. The original 10⁴ x (T minus 3) cost was linear, while every other cost scales by e per tier.

**G13. Stripping a standing ward** (raised by: N8, N13)
Gap: Arcane Active Protection says 5% and MCM-MINT prices a closed working at the unravel rate (C2).
Proposal: A ward still dormant on its trigger counts as unclosed and costs 5% pre-loaded or 10% cold. Once released it is closed and costs the unravel price, reading "remaining upkeep" as remaining stored charge. A full released Tier 1 ward costs 35 + 70 = 105 Pz to strip and a full Tier 2 ward 95.1 + 190.2 ≈ 285 Pz. A battered ward is cheaper. Reach is limited by G4.
Status: suggested to adopt. It reconciles both notes and makes the dormant picket the exposed stage.

**G14. Invisibility against thermal and radar** (raised by: K6)
Gap: Thermal "may" detect invisible casters, and radar is not addressed.
Proposal: Invisibility and Greater Invisibility act on visible light only. Masking body heat is a separate bound member priced as the light term, about 100 W or 240 Pz an hour, plus a link cost, and it adds a line to the spectrum. No registered working affects radar.
Status: suggested to adopt. Thermal optics keep their value.

**G15. Illusions against missile seekers** (raised by: B6)
Gap: Only the laser-spot lure is described.
Proposal: An illusion fools a visible-band or TV seeker. An infrared seeker needs a heat-matched decoy at about 0.07 Pz/s of field heat, bound in advance. Semi-active laser seekers answer only to the Seeker Lure. Every decoy is a working, visible to enemy MINT and counterable.
Status: suggested to adopt. Seekers stay useful against a networked defender.

**G16. Shared castings and arrays** (raised by: B13)
Gap: Arrays leak signature, and pooling between casters is unwritten.
Proposal: When several casters share one working, each pays their share plus a link of 0.01 x Q_max to the lead, with recast tension charged per caster. The signature uses the total draw at one point.
Status: suggested to adopt. Teamwork costs more than its parts.

**G17. Proving who cast first** (raised by: N4)
Gap: MINT fuses position and caster tier, and its evidential use is undefined.
Proposal: Spectra carry no personal handwriting, since glyphs are standardised. Logs establish the working with its place, time and an estimated caster tier. Treaty practice accepts logs fused from two independent apertures as evidence; naming the individual needs physical proof.
Status: suggested to adopt. Investigation stays possible.

**G18. Auras of people, mages and machines** (raised by: B2, B7)
Gap: The wiki describes sensing of workings only.
Proposal: An idle mage emits nothing, and neither does a drone. Only workings show, along with any stored charge a person carries. Recovered reservoir capacity is invisible (G2).
Status: suggested to adopt. It protects the rule that low-tier mages survive by being hard to find.

**G19. Warded vests and bearer-triggered items** (raised by: K10, K13)
Gap: Nothing limits pre-bound wards in issued kit, and non-mage triggering is unaddressed.
Proposal: The bearer supplies no perturbation, so the maker prepays the full slot quantum for every bearer-triggered item, and the item leaks 1% of its charge an hour like a crystal. Each bearer item stays tied to one of the maker's slots until spent, so a Tier 2 maker fields two Tier 2 vests. Without the slot tie, a Tier 2 mage doing nothing else would sustain about 14 Tier 2 ward charges (138 Pz/h recovery / 1% leak ≈ 13,760 Pz; / 951 ≈ 14). Each full vest shows at about 3.1 km. A bearer Misty Step needs its own registry entry and costs the maker 951 Pz.
Status: suggested with amendment. The original figure of about eight wards was wrong, and about 14 is close to a crate, so the slot tie is needed.

**G20. Crystals, permanent senses and familiars** (raised by: B14, P4, B10, B12)
Gap: Rupture goes to "its holder", and permanent transfiguration and communion are unpriced.
Proposal: Rupture always goes to the attuner through the attunement link, so a thrown crystal wounds its thrower. A permanent effect lowers the reservoir maximum by Q_T of the working's tier while it stands, so an Arcane Eye-grade glass eye costs 7,030 Pz of capacity. A permanent sense still emits at its active rate and can be unravelled. Communion is priced as divination at 0.001 Pz/s x range / 10 m. A familiar has the speed and temperament of its animal form.
Status: suggested with amendment. A silent permanent sense would remove the detection cost that P4 depends on.
