Leonidas I was the ancient Human King of Sparta who, long after his mortal death, entered into an agreement with [[Hela Elderblood]], allowing the Norse Goddess of Death to preserve and later summon him as a powerful Spirit. He became one of Hela's three principal historical commanders during Ragnarök, fighting alongside [[Vlad III Tepes]] and [[Julius Caesar]].[2][3]
Unlike several of the historical figures Hela approached, Leonidas showed little interest in receiving wealth, immortality, political power, or protection for himself. The prospect of eventually participating in a war against actual Gods was already enough to secure his interest. His primary condition concerned his men: Leonidas wanted Hela to ensure that the Spartans who followed him would receive proper lives when they eventually entered their next reincarnations.[3]
Hela fulfilled her side of the agreement by preserving Leonidas and later bringing him into Ragnarök together with an army of approximately 30,000 Spartans. She further strengthened Leonidas and his soldiers with her own Divine Power, allowing warriors who had originally been mortal Humans to directly battle Gods, Valkyries, Divine soldiers, and other supernatural forces.[2]
Leonidas' most notable individual feat during Ragnarök was his confrontation with Tyr, one of [[Odin]]'s sons and a genuine Third Generation [[Ares|God of War]]. Despite the enormous difference between Leonidas' original Human existence and Tyr's Divine nature, the Hela-empowered Spartan King defeated Tyr with remarkable ease.[4]
His reputation continued to survive long after Ragnarök. During the development of the [[Elderblood Empire]], [[Aphrodite]] Elderblood used Leonidas and the historical reputation of Sparta as the ideological basis for naming the Empire's principal invasion force the Spartan Legion, emphasizing the willingness of the Spartans to enter desperate battles without hesitation.[1]

### Physical Appearance
Leonidas is an imposing man standing approximately two meters tall with an extremely muscular and battle-hardened physique.[2]
He retains the visual identity historically associated with Spartan warriors. His equipment includes a distinctive Spartan helmet, cape, minimal lower-body battle clothing, sandals, a large round shield, and a long spear.[2]
[[The Spartans]] summoned alongside him possess similar equipment, causing the resurrected army to visually resemble an idealized supernatural version of the ancient Spartan military rather than a conventional modern force.
Following Hela's empowerment of her resurrected historical armies, Leonidas and his soldiers were covered in supernatural armor created through Hela's Divine Power. The armor carried the black-and-green visual characteristics associated with Hela's Death authority and elevated the formerly mortal warriors into soldiers capable of surviving a Divine battlefield.[2]

### Personality
Leonidas is fearless, disciplined, direct, fiercely loyal to his soldiers, and almost completely defined by the mentality of a warrior.
His agreement with Hela demonstrates that Leonidas places extraordinary value upon battle itself. When Hela approached him with the possibility of someday participating in a conflict vastly greater than the wars he experienced while alive, Leonidas required very little convincing. The chance to fight Gods was already an acceptable reward.[3]
However, this desire for battle does not make Leonidas selfish.
While other legendary individuals negotiated with Hela for benefits affecting themselves or their descendants, Leonidas' principal concern was the fate of the warriors who followed him. He requested that Hela ensure his men would receive good lives in their future reincarnations.[3]
This reflects the same leadership mentality that defined him in life: Leonidas does not view his soldiers as expendable tools existing only to glorify their King. If they follow him into an impossible battle, he considers their fate part of his responsibility.
Leonidas is also extremely confident when facing superior opponents.
The appearance of Gods during Ragnarök did not intimidate him. Instead, fighting Divine Beings represented precisely the kind of battle he had accepted Hela's offer in order to experience.[2][3]
His enthusiasm spread throughout the resurrected Spartan army. When Leonidas rallied his warriors during Ragnarök, the entire force responded with the declaration:
“WE ARE SPARTA!”[2]
The statement summarizes Leonidas' mentality better than any elaborate speech: his identity remains inseparable from the warriors he commands and the martial culture he represents.

### History

#### Mortal Life
Leonidas was originally a mortal Human and King of the Greek city-state of Sparta.
He belonged to the Agiad royal line and was traditionally considered a descendant of the [[Demigod]] hero Heracles. He ruled Sparta during the Greco-Persian Wars and became famous for commanding the Spartan force that fought at the Battle of Thermopylae.
Leonidas and the warriors who remained with him ultimately died during the battle.
His death did not erase his reputation.
The legend of Leonidas and the Spartans endured for millennia as one of Humanity's most famous examples of military discipline, courage, and willingness to fight against overwhelming odds. This reputation eventually attracted the attention of both Gods and Death-related supernatural beings.

#### Agreement with Hela
At some point before Leonidas' final mortal death, [[Hela Elderblood]] approached him as part of the enormous preparation she had begun for the future Ragnarök.[3]
Hela had spent centuries and millennia searching throughout Human history for exceptional warriors whose Souls would be valuable when her war against [[Odin]] finally began.
Leonidas was an obvious candidate.
Rather than demanding an elaborate reward, Leonidas accepted because Hela offered him the opportunity to participate in a future war against Gods.[3]
His personal request was directed toward the men who followed him. Leonidas wanted his subordinates to have good lives after their deaths and eventual reincarnations.[3]
Hela accepted the condition.
Leonidas' Soul was consequently preserved under Hela's Death authority until she required him.

### Synopsis

#### Dragon God of Chaos
Leonidas finally returned to the world during the climax of Ragnarök.
Hela had spent thousands of years accumulating hidden assets while [[Odin]] believed the Goddess of the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] remained trapped and politically isolated. Once the decisive stage of her rebellion began, she revealed that her armies were far larger and more diverse than Odin had imagined.[2]
Hela summoned three legendary Human rulers:
- Leonidas I
- [[Vlad III Tepes]]
- [[Julius Caesar]][2]
Each commander appeared with an enormous military force.
Leonidas emerged commanding approximately 30,000 Spartans, transforming the legendary three hundred associated with his mortal history into a supernatural army one hundred times larger.[2]
Vlad appeared with tens of thousands of Wallachian soldiers, while Caesar commanded hundreds of thousands of Roman warriors. Together, the three armies formed one of Hela's greatest hidden military assets.[2]
The spectacle stunned the Gods observing Ragnarök.
[[Aphrodite]] Elderblood immediately recognized Leonidas and noted that [[Ares]], the Greek God of War, had spent a considerable period searching for the legendary Spartan King.[2]
Hela then released her Divine Power across the historical armies.
Dark supernatural Energy enveloped Leonidas and his soldiers, forming black-and-green armor around them and elevating their strength far beyond anything they possessed while alive.[2]
The transformation was necessary because their enemies were no longer ordinary Humans.
They were entering a battlefield occupied by Gods.
Leonidas showed no hesitation.
He raised his weapon and rallied his army, causing tens of thousands of Spartans to answer with a unified declaration of their identity.[2]
Hela also summoned [[Creatures of the Abyss]] onto the battlefield. Some of these enormous creatures were used as mounts by her historical commanders and warriors, with Leonidas riding one of the Abyssal beings into combat.[2]
The combination was almost surreal: an ancient Human king, resurrected as a Spirit, covered in Divine armor, leading tens of thousands of empowered Spartans while riding a creature originating from one of the dead regions of the Universe.
Leonidas later confronted Tyr, the Norse God of War and one of Odin's sons.
The thematic contrast was obvious.
Tyr was a genuine Divine embodiment of War.
Leonidas had been born Human.
Nevertheless, Hela's empowerment and Leonidas' accumulated martial experience made the nominal racial difference largely irrelevant.
Leonidas defeated Tyr with surprising ease.[4]
The victory became one of the clearest demonstrations of how dangerous Hela's resurrected historical champions had become. She had not merely summoned famous Humans for symbolic value; she had turned them into warriors capable of directly defeating established Gods.
After Ragnarök, further information regarding Hela's preparations revealed why Leonidas had willingly participated.
Unlike figures whose agreements with Hela required complicated political arrangements, Leonidas wanted essentially one thing for himself:
a greater war.[3]
The idea of participating in a battle against Gods appealed to him enough that he did not require a personal reward. His concern was instead directed toward ensuring that his warriors would be properly rewarded after their service.[3]

#### Those Who Rule Reality
Leonidas' legacy remained important even after the supernatural world moved far beyond the scale of Ragnarök.
During the enormous accelerated development of the [[Elderblood Empire]], [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] and his Empresses reorganized the Imperial military into specialized Legions.
The primary direct-warfare and invasion force eventually became the First Legion: the Spartan Legion.[1]
[[Aphrodite]] Elderblood was responsible for supporting the name.
When Victor showed displeasure toward preserving terminology associated with the old Greek world, Aphrodite explained that the name was intended to honor the history and symbolism of Sparta rather than any individual God or failed Pantheon.[1]
She specifically cited Leonidas as proof of what the name represented.
To Aphrodite, the Spartans embodied the mentality required from an Imperial invasion Legion: warriors capable of entering desperate situations with smiles on their faces rather than breaking beneath impossible odds.[1]
Leonidas' conduct during Ragnarök had demonstrated that this reputation was deserved.

### Skills & Abilities
Resurrected Heroic Spirit Physiology: Leonidas is currently a deceased Human maintained as a functional Spirit through [[Hela Elderblood]]'s supernatural authority over Death.[2][3]
His current capabilities should not be confused with those he possessed while mortal. During Ragnarök, Hela directly empowered Leonidas and his soldiers with [[Divine Energy]], elevating them enough to participate in combat against Gods.[2]

#### Hela's Divine Empowerment
- Divine Power Bestowal: Hela poured her [[Divine Energy]] into Leonidas and his army during Ragnarök, greatly increasing their physical and supernatural capabilities.[2]
- Divine Armor: Hela's Power manifested supernatural black-and-green armor around her resurrected historical warriors.[2]
- God-Level Physical Characteristics: While empowered by Hela, Leonidas became capable of directly fighting beings in the Divine range. His defeat of Tyr, a Third Generation [[Ares|God of War]], is the clearest demonstrated example.[4]
- Enhanced Strength: Leonidas possesses physical power vastly exceeding anything available to him during his mortal life after receiving Hela's Divine reinforcement.[2][4]
- Enhanced Speed and Reflexes: His empowered Spirit body allows him to engage Divine combatants whose physical abilities operate far beyond normal Humans.
- Enhanced Durability: Leonidas is capable of existing and fighting on the Ragnarök battlefield alongside Gods, Divine soldiers, Valkyries, Spirits and supernatural monsters.[2]

#### Spirit Physiology
As a Spirit preserved through Hela's Death authority, Leonidas does not possess an ordinary living Human body.
His current existence is dependent upon supernatural Soul preservation and manifestation rather than natural biology.[3]
- Post-Mortem Existence: Leonidas can continue acting, fighting and communicating despite having died thousands of years earlier.
- Soul Preservation: Hela retained Leonidas' Soul until the conditions of their agreement required his return.[3]
- Spiritual Manifestation: Hela can summon Leonidas into a functional battlefield form alongside his army.[2]
The exact consequences of completely destroying Leonidas' manifested body have not been demonstrated, so conventional Spirit immortality or unlimited resurrection should not be attributed to him without further evidence.

#### Martial Mastery
Leonidas was one of Humanity's most famous historical warrior-kings and possesses extensive experience in direct warfare.
- [[Spears|Spear]] Mastery: Leonidas' principal battlefield weapon is the Spartan spear.[2]
- Shield Mastery: He uses the large round shield characteristic of Spartan heavy infantry.[2]
- Close-Quarters Combat: Leonidas' fighting style is built around disciplined close combat rather than supernatural ranged techniques.
- Formation Warfare: As the King and commander of the Spartan army, Leonidas possesses exceptional experience coordinating warriors operating in disciplined military formations.
- Battlefield Experience: His entire mortal reputation was built upon warfare against numerically superior enemies, while his resurrected existence later expanded that experience into combat against Divine beings.

#### Military Leadership
Leonidas commands approximately 30,000 empowered Spartans during Ragnarök.[2]
His soldiers retain exceptional loyalty toward him and respond immediately to his commands.
Leonidas' ability to inspire warriors is one of his defining strengths. His forces display no hesitation upon learning that their enemies are literal Gods; instead, the opportunity intensifies their enthusiasm for battle.

#### Abyssal Mount
During Ragnarök, Leonidas rode one of the [[Creatures of the Abyss]] summoned through Hela's arrangements.[2]
These creatures originate from regions connected to dead portions of the Universe and are considerably more dangerous than ordinary supernatural animals.
The Abyssal creature should be considered a temporary battlefield mount rather than Leonidas' personal natural ability.

### Power and Scaling
Leonidas' current combat level is dramatically higher than his mortal Human state because of Hela's supernatural reinforcement.
His most important scaling feat is his victory over Tyr.[4]
Tyr was:
- A genuine Third Generation God.
- A Major member of the [[Norse Pantheon]].
- The Norse [[Ares|God of War]].
- One of [[Odin]]'s Divine sons.
Leonidas was able to defeat him without the fight being presented as an exceptionally difficult confrontation.[4]
This places Hela-empowered Leonidas firmly within the combat range necessary to threaten established Third Generation Gods.
However, this level should not automatically be attributed to Leonidas' original Human incarnation. His Ragnarök performance is explicitly associated with Hela's resurrection and Divine Power Bestowal.[2]

### Relationships

#### Hela Elderblood
[[Hela Elderblood]] is Leonidas' current master, summoner and the Goddess responsible for his continued existence as a Spirit.
Their relationship is based upon a voluntary supernatural agreement rather than conventional enslavement.[3]
Hela approached Leonidas before his ultimate death and offered him the opportunity to participate in a future war on a scale far beyond anything available during his mortal lifetime.
Leonidas accepted enthusiastically.
He requested no grand personal reward. Instead, he asked Hela to ensure that the warriors who followed him would receive good lives in their later reincarnations.[3]
Hela subsequently preserved his Soul and summoned him during Ragnarök as one of her principal commanders.[2]

#### The Spartans
Leonidas' relationship with his soldiers is central to his identity.
He does not consider himself separate from the Spartan warriors he commands. Even when negotiating with Hela regarding a future war, his primary concern was ensuring the future welfare of the men who would follow him.[3]
During Ragnarök, Hela expanded the Spartan force associated with Leonidas to approximately 30,000 soldiers and empowered the entire army with Divine Power.[2]
Their famous collective declaration of "WE ARE SPARTA!" emphasizes that Leonidas and his soldiers continue to identify themselves primarily through their shared warrior culture rather than through Hela's Norse affiliation.[2]

#### Tyr
Tyr was Leonidas' Divine opponent during Ragnarök.
The matchup placed an empowered historical Human warrior against a genuine Norse [[Ares|God of War]].
Leonidas defeated Tyr with remarkable ease.[4]
The victory is one of Leonidas' greatest supernatural feats and one of the strongest demonstrations of Hela's ability to elevate historical warriors far beyond their mortal limitations.

#### Ares
[[Ares]] never directly meets Leonidas on-screen during the Ragnarök chapters, but [[Aphrodite]] Elderblood revealed that the Greek God of War had been searching for Leonidas for a considerable period.[2]
The exact reason Ares wanted to find him has not been revealed.
Given Leonidas' historical identity as one of Sparta's most famous kings and Sparta's cultural association with warfare, the connection attracted immediate attention from Aphrodite when Leonidas appeared.

#### Julius Caesar and Vlad III Tepes
[[Julius Caesar]] and [[Vlad III Tepes]] are the other two principal historical rulers summoned by Hela during Ragnarök.[2]
The three form the leadership core of Hela's resurrected Human armies:
- Leonidas commands the Spartans.
- Vlad III commands the Wallachian forces.
- Julius Caesar commands the Roman army.
All three entered agreements with Hela under different terms and were preserved specifically because of the military value they would provide during her future war.[2][3]
