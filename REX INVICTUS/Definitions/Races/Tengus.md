Tengus, also referred to as Crows or Ravens, are a group of avian Yōkai native to Japan and led by Yomi-Hime, the Queen of the Tengu. For thousands of years, the Tengu maintained a mutually beneficial relationship with the Shinto Pantheon. In exchange for the protection of the Gods, Yomi-Hime's Clan served as their messengers and used its members to observe both Humans and supernatural beings throughout Japan.[1]
Rather than functioning as independent judges who enforce every law of Japanese supernatural society, the Tengu historically acted as an information, surveillance and communication network attached to the Shinto Gods. They monitored potentially dangerous supernatural developments, carried orders between the Divine and Mortal Worlds and reported events that could threaten Japan's supernatural balance. Their duties did not require them to interfere in every dispute between Yōkai; during Haruna's rise, for example, the Tengu deliberately ignored her earlier challenges against other Yōkai because she had not been interfering with Humans.[2]
Following the political transformation of Japan, the alliance between the Shinto Gods and Dragon's Nest, and the eventual establishment of the Elderblood Empire, the Tengu became part of the wider supernatural order surrounding [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s civilization through their continued connection to the Shinto Pantheon.

### Physical Appearance
Tengu possess distinctly avian characteristics associated with crows and ravens, although their exact manifestation varies between individuals. Ordinary Tengu have been shown appearing in a much more bird-like form; one of [[Yomi-Hime]]'s messengers appeared as a crow dressed in priestly robes while travelling through the Shinto Divine Realm.[1]
Higher-ranking Tengu can possess a considerably more humanoid appearance. Yomi-Hime, their Queen, appears as a woman with long black hair and two enormous raven-like wings extending from her back.[3] Tengu are also capable of sustained flight and are regularly seen observing events from the air or from elevated structures.[2]
The novel has displayed both crow-like and humanoid-winged Tengu, but has not explicitly established that every member of the species can freely transform between both appearances.

### Organization

#### Yomi-Hime
The recognized leader of the Tengu is [[Yomi-Hime]], who is explicitly identified as their Queen.[1][3] She represents the Clan in negotiations involving the Shinto Gods and is responsible for protecting the Tengu as a people.
Yomi-Hime was historically married to [[Tsukuyomi]], although the marriage originated from the political relationship between the Tengu and the Gods rather than ordinary romance. Their union had been arranged under the authority of the Shinto leadership, while the broader agreement itself had originally been proposed by Yomi-Hime thousands of years earlier.[1]

#### Pact with the Shinto Gods
The Tengu's relationship with the [[Shinto Pantheon]] originated as a mutually beneficial agreement:
- The Tengu received the protection of the Shinto Gods.
- The Tengu became official messengers of the Gods.
- Members of the Clan were used to observe Humans and supernatural beings.
- Information gathered by the Tengu could be transmitted to the Gods, allowing the Pantheon to follow events occurring throughout Japan.[1]
This arrangement should not be confused with unconditional devotion. Individual Tengu are capable of disagreeing with the decisions of the Gods, and [[Yomi-Hime]] herself retained loyalty toward the wider Yōkai population despite her alliance with the Pantheon.[1]

#### Reconnaissance Network
By the time of the Demonic War, the Tengu's scouting abilities remained important enough that [[Amaterasu]] specifically offered [[Yomi-Hime]] and her Tengu to Haruna as reconnaissance support while negotiating the defense of Japan. Amaterasu directly characterized the Tengu as experts in scouting.[3]
Their strategic function therefore extends beyond carrying messages. The Tengu form an established aerial intelligence network capable of observing supernatural activity and relaying information between different authorities.

### Personality
As a group, the Tengu are generally disciplined, observant and pragmatic. Their historical responsibilities require them to monitor supernatural conflicts without becoming involved in every confrontation they witness. During Haruna's rise, they were aware that she had been challenging Yōkai throughout Japan to increase the status of her [[Hyakki Yakō]], yet they chose not to interfere because those actions remained confined to the supernatural community and were not affecting Humans.[2]
Their loyalty is nevertheless not completely mindless. A Tengu messenger serving [[Yomi-Hime]] and [[Tsukuyomi]] became visibly less enthusiastic after learning that Tsukuyomi intended to abandon Yōkai caught in [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] and [[Scathach]]'s retaliation, demonstrating that individual members can possess their own opinions regarding the Gods they serve.[1]
Yomi-Hime likewise showed concern for the Yōkai whom Tsukuyomi dismissed and was disturbed by the prejudiced manner in which he regarded her species. Her priority ultimately remained the survival of her own Clan, reflecting the practical nature of the ancient agreement between the Tengu and [[Shinto Pantheon]].[1]

### Synopsis

#### Discoveries, Changes and War
The Tengu first became directly relevant during the [[Hyakki Yakō]] led by Haruna in Shibuya. They watched the enormous procession from the sky while evaluating the threat represented by Haruna's rapidly growing military force. Although they had already received reports that Haruna was challenging Yōkai throughout Japan to build the prestige necessary to command a Hyakki Yakō, they had previously chosen not to interfere because her activities had not directly affected Humans.[2]
Their passive observation became considerably more difficult once Haruna arrived accompanied by three Eastern Dragons. One of the Dragons immediately became irritated by the Tengu occupying the same airspace. Recognizing that challenging Dragons for control of the sky would be disastrous, the Tengu abandoned the air and repositioned themselves on surrounding buildings, from where they continued monitoring the gathering.[2]
The appearance of Haruna's army caused the observing Tengu to reassess the situation. What had previously appeared to be a series of challenges between Yōkai had developed into the movement of an organized supernatural military force capable of threatening large portions of Japan. Nevertheless, Haruna's objective remained directed toward the Yōkai hierarchy rather than Humanity, and the Tengu continued primarily as observers.[2]
The true structure behind the Tengu was revealed during the aftermath of [[Victor Elderblood]] and [[Scathach]]'s retaliation in Japan. A Tengu messenger travelled hurriedly to the Shinto Divine Realm to report to [[Yomi-Hime]] and [[Tsukuyomi]]. Using a talisman, the messenger displayed events occurring throughout the Mortal World and informed them that Victor's faction was systematically hunting those responsible for spreading information concerning Ophis and [[Nero Elderblood]].[1]
Tsukuyomi ordered the Tengu to carry warnings to Inari while the Shinto Gods deliberately avoided escalating the conflict against Scathach and Victor. During the discussion, Tsukuyomi finally explained the political foundation of the Tengu's position. Thousands of years earlier, Yomi-Hime had sought an arrangement with the Gods under which her Clan would receive Divine protection. In return, the Tengu became messengers and observers responsible for watching both Humans and supernatural beings on behalf of the Pantheon.[1]
The conversation also demonstrated that the Tengu were not merely extensions of the Gods' will. The messenger reacted negatively when Tsukuyomi dismissed the deaths of other Yōkai, while Yomi-Hime openly questioned whether he intended to abandon their people. Tsukuyomi instead instructed her to protect the Tengu themselves, believing their intelligence network would remain valuable after the conflict ended.[1]

#### Demonic War
The Tengu returned to prominence during the Demonic War, when Japan was being destabilized by Demonic incursions and much of the [[Shinto Pantheon]]'s normal administrative structure had been disrupted. With several Gods injured and [[Tsukuyomi]] incapacitated, [[Amaterasu]] sought the assistance of Haruna and the unified Yōkai forces to protect Japan.[3]
During the negotiations, Amaterasu brought [[Yomi-Hime]] directly before Haruna. Haruna identified her as the Queen of the Tengu and commander of the Ravens, although their relationship was immediately tense because Haruna viewed Yomi-Hime's historical cooperation with the Gods as a betrayal of fellow Yōkai.[3]
Personal hostility did not prevent practical cooperation. Amaterasu proposed placing Yomi-Hime and the Tengu at Haruna's disposal as scouts while Haruna's forces assumed a larger role in defending Japan from dangerous supernatural entities. Their established expertise in reconnaissance made them particularly valuable for locating threats and supporting the Yōkai defenders.[3]
This arrangement represented an important change in the Tengu's position. Historically they had primarily gathered intelligence for the Shinto Gods; during the crisis, that same intelligence network was directly attached to Haruna's military operations, creating practical cooperation between the old Shinto-aligned Tengu and the newly unified Yōkai faction.[3]

#### Dragon's Nest and the Elderblood Era
The supernatural order surrounding the Tengu changed considerably after the Demonic War. Haruna ultimately became one of the major leaders associated with [[Victor Elderblood]], while the [[Shinto Pantheon]] entered an increasingly close political relationship with Dragon's Nest. The Tengu consequently ceased existing within the same isolated political environment that had defined their ancient pact with the Shinto Gods.
When Victor later unified Earth's supernatural powers under the [[Elderblood Empire]], the Shinto Pantheon and the supernatural peoples of Japan became part of the broader Imperial civilization.[4] As a Shinto-aligned Yōkai people already incorporated into Japan's supernatural structure, the Tengu therefore fall within the modern Elderblood political sphere while retaining their own Queen and traditional identity.
Their ancient specialization remains particularly valuable in the new era: the same aerial mobility, reconnaissance and communication network once used to observe Japan on behalf of the Shinto Gods provides a natural intelligence role within the much larger supernatural civilization that succeeded the old order.

### Skills & Abilities
As Yōkai, the Tengu possess supernatural characteristics beyond those of ordinary Humans. The following abilities are directly demonstrated or strongly associated with their established role in the novel:
- Supernatural Physical Characteristics — As supernatural Yōkai, Tengu possess physical capabilities beyond ordinary Humans.
- Flight — Tengu possess avian characteristics that allow them to fly. Large groups were able to monitor Haruna's [[Hyakki Yakō]] from the skies, while [[Yomi-Hime]] possesses two enormous raven wings.[2][3]
- Aerial Mobility — Their ability to operate from above makes the Tengu particularly suited to surveillance, rapid travel and reconnaissance throughout Japan.[2][3]
- Enhanced Reconnaissance — The Tengu specialize in scouting and the observation of supernatural activity. [[Amaterasu]] explicitly considered them experts suitable for supporting Haruna's defense of Japan.[3]
- Surveillance — Under their ancient agreement with the [[Shinto Pantheon]], members of the Clan were assigned to watch both Humans and supernatural beings.[1]
- Information Gathering — Tengu observers collect reports concerning supernatural factions and movements, allowing them to track developments such as Haruna's campaign to build a Hyakki Yakō.[2]
- Divine Messenger Duties — For thousands of years the Tengu acted as couriers between the Shinto Gods and their agents, transmitting commands and warnings throughout the Pantheon's sphere of influence.[1]
- Talisman Usage — At least one Tengu messenger possessed and used a supernatural talisman capable of displaying events occurring in the Mortal World to Yomi-Hime and [[Tsukuyomi]].[1]
- Yōkai Energy — As members of the Yōkai race, the Tengu possess the supernatural nature associated with Japanese Yōkai, although the individual techniques and quantity of Yōki possessed by ordinary Tengu have not been extensively demonstrated.

### Relationships

#### Yomi-Hime
[[Yomi-Hime]] is the Queen and recognized leader of the Tengu. Her decisions established their ancient relationship with the [[Shinto Pantheon]], and her primary concern during political crises remains the security and continued existence of her Clan.[1]

#### Shinto Pantheon
The [[Shinto Pantheon]] historically served as the Tengu's principal political protector. The relationship was explicitly transactional rather than simple subordination: the Gods protected [[Yomi-Hime]]'s Clan, while the Tengu served as messengers, observers and intelligence agents.[1]

#### Otsuki Haruna and the Hyakki Yakō
The relationship between the Tengu and Haruna was initially distant and politically tense. The Tengu monitored her rise without interfering while her conflicts remained internal to the Yōkai community.[2]
The relationship became more complicated because Haruna regarded Yōkai who served the Gods as traitors to their own people, particularly [[Yomi-Hime]] and [[Hashimoto Genji]]. Despite this distrust, the Demonic War forced both systems to cooperate, with Yomi-Hime's Tengu providing scouting support to Haruna's forces during the defense of Japan.[3]

#### Elderblood Empire
Following the unification of Earth, the Tengu became part of the larger political order governed by the [[Elderblood Empire]] through Japan's supernatural factions and the [[Shinto Pantheon]].[4] They nevertheless continue to exist as a distinct Yōkai group under [[Yomi-Hime]] rather than losing their cultural identity.

### Trivia
- The terms Crow and Raven have both been used in connection with the Tengu. [[Yomi-Hime]] is specifically described with raven wings and is identified as the commander of the Ravens.[3]
- The Tengu's agreement with the Shinto Gods predates the main story by thousands of years.[1]
- Yomi-Hime herself originally approached the Gods and proposed the relationship that eventually made the Tengu their messengers and observers.[1]
- The Tengu do not automatically intervene in conflicts between Yōkai. Haruna's earlier challenges were deliberately ignored while they remained separate from Human affairs.[2]
- Despite being servants and allies of the Gods, individual Tengu can disagree with Divine decisions.[1]
- The Tengu are specifically regarded as experts in scouting and reconnaissance.[3]
- During Haruna's first major [[Hyakki Yakō]], the Tengu immediately surrendered the airspace when confronted by Eastern Dragons rather than provoke creatures regarded as rulers of the sky.[2]
