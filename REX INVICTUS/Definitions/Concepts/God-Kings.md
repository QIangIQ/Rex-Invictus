**[[God-Kings]]**, also referred to as Kings of Gods, Pantheon Leaders, Kings of the Pantheon, and God-Queens when the position is occupied by a Goddess, are the supreme Divine authorities of organized Pantheons. (Lore adapted from *My Three Wives Are Beautiful Vampires*.)

GOD-KINGS
God-Kings, also referred to as Kings of Gods, Pantheon Leaders, Kings of the Pantheon, and God-Queens when the position is occupied by a Goddess, are the supreme Divine authorities of organized Pantheons. A God-King normally acts as the Pantheon's highest representative, governs its Gods and heavenly or Divine territories, and possesses authority recognized by the supernatural systems governing Pantheon existence.
The term has a second and completely separate meaning: God-King is also an old supernatural power classification. A being can therefore possess God-King-level strength without ruling any Pantheon, while an actual Pantheon Leader can possess personal power far above the ordinary God-King combat range.[1]

### Overview
The title God-King fundamentally refers to the supreme Divine ruler of a Pantheon. The individual represents the Gods belonging to that Pantheon, occupies the highest administrative position within its Divine hierarchy, and usually presides over its Heaven, Paradise, Divine realm, or other positive-side supernatural territory.
However, the terminology used throughout the story is complicated by the fact that God-King is simultaneously used as a general measurement of supernatural power.
[[Scathach]]'s explanation made the distinction explicit: Gods called God-Kings were not necessarily identical in strength. Some received the designation because they possessed enormous amounts of Energy, while others were referred to as God-Kings because they were the actual rulers of Pantheons.[1]
Consequently, all of the following can be true at the same time:
- A being can be the God-King of a Pantheon without belonging to a species or generation called "God-King".
- A being can possess God-King-level power without ruling Gods.
- A God-King can possess combat strength considerably above the ordinary God-King power range.
- A Primordial God can serve beneath a politically superior God-King.
- A God-King can be replaced even while remaining alive.
- A Pantheon can lose its God-King without the concept of God-Kings elsewhere disappearing.
- Different civilizations can use entirely different names for positions functionally comparable to God-Kings.
MOST IMPORTANT DISTINCTION
God-King (Office) ≠ God-King (Power Level)
The office describes who rules a Pantheon. The power level describes how powerful a being is within an older supernatural classification system. The same words are used for two related but non-identical concepts.

### The Two Meanings of God-King
OFFICE
Pantheon God-King
Definition: The supreme recognized God or Goddess governing an organized Pantheon.
Examples: [[Zeus]], [[Odin]], [[Amaterasu]], [[Heavenly Father]], [[Victor Elderblood]].
Determined by: Divine rulership, political authority, control or recognition of the Pantheon, succession, conquest or equivalent systemic recognition.
Does not determine: Exact combat power.
POWER CLASSIFICATION
God-King Class
Definition: A broad supernatural strength range above the ordinary God and Combat-God classes.
Examples: [[Hades]], Yama, [[Enma]], [[Poseidon]], [[Tasha Fenrir Elderblood]] and others have possessed or entered this general power range without necessarily ruling a Pantheon.
Determined by: Energy, Concept mastery, combat capability and other strength-related characteristics.
Does not determine: Ownership of a Pantheon.

### Divine Generation and God-Kings
God-King is not a Divine generation.
Godhood possesses its own distinctions involving ancestry, origin, Divine Concepts and Primordial lineage. The office of Pantheon Leader can overlap with any of those classifications.
Examples include:
- [[Zeus]] ruled the [[Greek Pantheon]] despite belonging to a later Divine generation than ancient [[Primordial Gods]] such as [[Gaia]], Nyx, [[Ouranos]] and Kronus.
- [[Odin]] was both a Second Generation Primordial God and King of the [[Norse Pantheon]].
- [[Heavenly Father]] is an extremely ancient Primordial Divine existence while also serving as the supreme ruler of the [[Biblical Pantheon]].
- [[Amaterasu]]-Omikami is a Second Generation Primordial Goddess while simultaneously functioning as God-Queen of the [[Shinto Pantheon]].[5]
- [[Victor Elderblood]] became God-King through conquest and creation of a new Pantheon despite his Divine origin being radically different from the traditional Gods of Earth.[6]
Thus:
Divine Generation determines origin and natural Divine status.
God-King determines rulership.
A Primordial God does not automatically become a God-King, and a non-Primordial God is not automatically barred from becoming one.

### Pantheon Authority
God-Kings possess more than an ordinary political title. The position is recognized by the supernatural infrastructure governing Pantheons.
Following [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s destruction and replacement of the [[Greek Pantheon]], other Pantheon Leaders immediately became aware that the old Greek Pantheon had disappeared. Their positions gave them awareness of changes occurring through the greater System in relation to organized Pantheons.[7]
This demonstrates that Pantheon leadership possesses a metaphysical and systemic component.
A God-King is not merely the person whom the other Gods happen to obey politically. The office is connected to the supernatural identity and continued existence of the Pantheon itself.

#### Supreme Divine Authority
A recognized God-King generally occupies the highest administrative position among the Gods belonging to their Pantheon.
This can include authority over:
- Pantheon policy.
- Divine diplomacy.
- Heaven or equivalent Pantheon territory.
- Divine military forces.
- Pantheon representatives.
- Alliances with other supernatural factions.
- Distribution of institutional responsibilities.
- Major conflicts involving the Pantheon as a whole.
- The Pantheon's relationship with other recognized Divine factions.
This authority should not automatically be interpreted as irresistible mind control over every subordinate God. Earth Pantheons repeatedly demonstrate that Gods can disobey, conspire against, abandon or betray their own Pantheon Leaders.

#### Heavenly Authority
God-Kings frequently preside over the positive or celestial side of their religion's supernatural structure.
For example, [[Heavenly Father]] stands above the Biblical Heavens and occupies the highest authority associated with that Divine realm.[8][9]
Similarly, [[Zeus]] historically ruled [[Olympus]], [[Odin]] ruled [[Asgard]] and [[Indra]] ruled Svarga.

#### Pantheon Recognition
The God-King commonly serves as the entity through which the Pantheon is externally represented.
During supernatural gatherings, diplomacy and large-scale inter-Pantheon affairs, God-Kings or their designated representatives speak for the Divine faction rather than merely acting as individual Gods.

#### System Awareness
Chapter 826 demonstrates that Pantheon Leaders possess at least some awareness of how the System recognizes major Pantheon-level changes.[7]
When [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] erased the political identity of [[Olympus]] and replaced the old Pantheon, other God-Kings sensed that something fundamental had changed.
This distinguishes Pantheon leadership from merely being the strongest warrior in a group.

### Functions of a God-King
Although every Pantheon possesses its own culture and governmental structure, several functions repeatedly appear around recognized Pantheon rulers.

#### Divine Leadership
The God-King acts as the highest authority among the organized Gods of the Pantheon.
The actual amount of control varies dramatically. [[Zeus]]' authority was continuously challenged by the dysfunctional relationships within [[Olympus]], while [[Heavenly Father]]'s Heaven possessed a much more centralized structure.

#### Pantheon Representation
God-Kings represent their Pantheon in negotiations, supernatural gatherings, alliances and wars.
The individual may delegate this role, but the ultimate authority normally returns to the recognized Pantheon Leader.

#### Military Authority
A Pantheon Leader can command Divine armies, assign major warriors and determine whether the Pantheon enters wars or alliances.
This does not mean the God-King is automatically the best combatant in the faction.

#### Territorial Authority
God-Kings are usually associated with control over the central Divine territory of their Pantheon.

#### Preservation of the Pantheon
Because the God-King is attached to the Pantheon's organized structure, the position carries responsibility for the survival and development of the Divine civilization rather than merely the personal advancement of its ruler.
The repeated failures of several ancient Earth God-Kings came from neglecting this responsibility in favor of pride, personal indulgence, internal rivalries or stagnation.

### God-King and Primordial God
Main article: [[Primordial Gods]]
God-King and Primordial God describe fundamentally different things.
God-King
A political and supernatural office identifying the leader of a Pantheon.
The term is also reused as a power classification.
Primordial God
A Divine origin/generation or exceptionally advanced state associated with older, more fundamental Gods and Concepts.
It does not automatically grant leadership of a Pantheon.
This distinction creates several possible combinations:
- A God-King who is not a Primordial God.
- A God-King who is simultaneously a Second Generation Primordial God.
- A God-King who is an Original/First Generation Primordial God.
- A Primordial God who serves beneath another Pantheon Leader.
- A Primordial God with no Pantheon at all.
[[Odin]] and [[Amaterasu]] demonstrate the overlap between Second Generation Primordial status and Pantheon leadership.
[[Zeus]] demonstrates that the God-King position itself does not require Primordial ancestry.

### God-King as a Power Level
The second use of the term is a general supernatural strength classification.
Chapter 690 explains that beings called God-Kings could receive that description either because of immense Energy or because they actually governed a Pantheon.[1]
The old Divine progression surrounding this range can be represented approximately as:
High-Level God
→
High-Level Combat God
→
Low God-King
→
Mid God-King
→
High God-King
→
Peak God-King
The placement of Combat-God classes beneath God-King demonstrates that the ranking system does not measure Energy alone. [[Combat Gods]] are specifically oriented toward battle and can outperform less skilled beings with comparable or greater reserves.
The author's official power-ranking explanation repeatedly stresses that supernatural fights cannot be determined from raw Energy alone. Divinities, racial characteristics, Divine Weapons, armor, techniques, experience, special advantages and compatibility can drastically alter an encounter.[10]

#### God-King Power Stages
The God-King power range is commonly divided into four stages:
Stage
Description
Low-Level God-King
Entry stage of the God-King power range. Beings at this level possess Energy and/or overall supernatural capability comparable to rulers among the older Pantheons.
Mid-Level God-King
Intermediate God-King classification representing development beyond the lower threshold.
High-Level God-King
Upper God-King range. Concept mastery, accumulated Energy and combat specialization become increasingly important because the differences between individuals grow extremely large.
Peak God-King
Highest traditional God-King stage before the rankings transition toward Primordial-God classifications. The boundary between exceptionally powerful God-Kings and lower Primordial-God-level beings can become difficult to judge using simple Energy measurements.
These labels describe combat/power progression. They do not establish four different grades of political God-King.
A ruler of a Pantheon is still simply that Pantheon's God-King regardless of whether their personal strength would be classified as Low, High, Peak or beyond the God-King power range entirely.

#### Combat Ability and Energy
The reason God-King scaling is difficult is that Energy represents only one component of supernatural combat.
Relevant factors include:
- Quantity of Energy.
- Energy quality.
- Divinity or Concept.
- Level of Concept mastery.
- [[Martial Arts|Martial arts]].
- Combat experience.
- Racial physiology.
- Regeneration.
- Resistances.
- Divine Weapons.
- Anti-Divine weapons.
- Blessings.
- Pantheon or territorial advantages.
- Preparation.
- Compatibility between powers.
- Mentality and willingness to kill.
- Specialized forms or transformations.
[[Scathach]]'s explanation in Chapter 690 specifically rejects treating God-Kings as interchangeable simply because they share a broad classification.[1]
The official ranking likewise notes that Divine Weapons can push a God considerably above the level implied by their normal classification.[10]
Consequently:
Same Rank ≠ Same Combat Ability
God-King is a broad bracket. The outcome of battle still depends on the beings involved.

### Seven High-Level God-Kings
Chapter 1059 refers to seven High-Level God-Kings while [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] was observing the recurrence of the number seven throughout supernatural structures.[11]
This statement has historically been misinterpreted.
It does not establish that only seven Pantheon Leaders existed on Earth.
It also does not explicitly identify the seven as [[Zeus]], [[Odin]], [[Indra]], [[Buddha]], [[Amaterasu]], the [[Heavenly Father]] and the [[Jade Emperor]].
The statement concerns seven High-Level God-Kings, which uses God-King as a power classification. The chapter itself does not provide an exhaustive named roster for that sentence.[11]
The distinction is necessary because Earth already possessed or had possessed additional Divine factions, including:
- [[Celtic Pantheon]]
- [[Egyptian Pantheon]]
- Mesopotamian Divine factions
- [[Elder Gods Pantheon]]
- Victor's later [[Dragons Nest Pantheon]]
- Smaller Pantheons lacking the full major-Hell structure
Therefore:
The "seven High-Level God-Kings" are not a canon-confirmed list of Earth's only seven Pantheon Leaders. Any exact seven-name roster attached to that statement without direct chapter identification is speculative.[11]

### Succession
God-King is an office and can therefore change hands.
Known methods through which Pantheon leadership can change include:
- Hereditary or generational succession
- Overthrow
- Conquest
- Resignation
- Death
- Political replacement
- Systemic destruction of the previous Pantheon
- Creation of a new Pantheon

#### Kronus and Zeus
Kronus preceded [[Zeus]] as King of the Greek Gods.
Zeus later overthrew the older Divine order and became the recognized King of [[Olympus]], retaining the office until the destruction of the [[Greek Pantheon]]'s political structure.

#### Zeus and Victor
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s conquest demonstrated the most extreme known form of Pantheon succession.
After taking ownership of [[Olympus]]' dimension, Victor formally ended the old Pantheon and declared that a new Pantheon would rise beneath him:
"As the King of the Dimension, I declare... Olympus shall cease to exist, and a new pantheon shall rise from the ashes, a pantheon led by me."[6]
The event did not simply replace [[Zeus]] with Victor as another Greek God-King.
The [[Greek Pantheon]] itself ceased to exist as the previous System-recognized Pantheon.[7]
Victor then went on to establish Dragon's Nest as a separate multiracial Pantheon.

#### Indra and Shiva
[[Indra]] remained the formal King of Gods in the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]] despite [[Shiva]] possessing much greater raw importance and combat power.
This alone proves that:
Strongest God ≠ Automatically God-King
Indra eventually relinquished the position, allowing Shiva to formally become King of Gods.[2]

#### Shiva and Kali
The Hindu leadership changed again in Chapter 1152.
After discovering [[Shiva]]'s secret attempt to reconstruct one of [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s ancestors from Akashic information, Victor treated the experiment as a serious breach of trust. Kali was consequently elevated above Shiva as principal ruler of the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]].[3]
Shiva was assigned to frontline combat during the coming war and later accepted Kali's leadership, admitting that combat suited him better than administration.[4]
The text describes Kali as the principal ruler. It is therefore safest to distinguish between her current supreme leadership and the older explicit "King of Gods" wording used for Shiva rather than pretending Chapter 1152 never changed the hierarchy.

#### Destruction of a Pantheon
The disappearance of the [[Greek Pantheon]] demonstrated that Pantheon succession does not always preserve the institution itself.
A sufficiently complete conquest can destroy the old Pantheon's systemic identity and replace it with an entirely different Divine organization.[6][7]

### God-Kings Beyond Earth
The office or function represented by God-Kings is not uniquely terrestrial.
[[Ikor-Kar-VI]] led [[Nightingale]]'s [[Elder Gods Pantheon]], demonstrating that organized Divine civilizations outside Earth's familiar mythologies can possess their own Pantheon rulers.[15][16]
Ion later provided another example on an extraterrestrial world.[17]
These discoveries establish that Pantheons are a broader cosmic phenomenon rather than structures belonging only to Human religions.
However, later exploration demonstrates another important point:
The term "God-King" is not necessarily universal.
Different sectors and civilizations can organize their Divine factions through different hierarchies, names and ranking systems.

### Victor Elderblood and Dragon's Nest
Main article: [[Dragons Nest Pantheon]]
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] represents an unusual form of God-King because his Pantheon did not develop from a single historical religion, Divine species or Human culture.
After conquering [[Olympus]], Victor ended the former [[Greek Pantheon]] as a political structure and declared the creation of a new Pantheon beneath himself.[6]
Dragon's Nest was later formally recognized by the greater System as a Pantheon.[14]
Unlike the old Pantheons, membership was not restricted to a single mythological lineage.
Dragon's Nest incorporated or accepted members originating from:
- Dragons
- Vampires
- Gods
- Goddesses
- Demons
- Angels
- [[Werewolves]]
- Youkai
- Witches
- [[World Trees]]
- extraterrestrial civilizations
- other supernatural races
Victor therefore became a Pantheon Leader by creating a completely new Divine order around his own civilization rather than inheriting a pre-existing throne.

#### Victor's Position
By the Imperial era, the phrase God-King is technically far too limited to describe [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s personal power.
He eventually became:
- Dragon God of [[Chaos]]
- God Emperor
- Outer God / Eldritch Chaos
- Sector Overlord
- Emperor of the [[Elderblood Empire]]
- supreme authority over Dragon's Nest
Nevertheless, God-King remains one of his offices because he is still the supreme God and founder of his Pantheon.
This demonstrates again that office and strength are separate.
Victor did not cease to be Pantheon Leader merely because his personal existence advanced far beyond the old God-King power scale.

### Imperial Pantheons
One of the largest later developments is the distinction between:
belonging to [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s Empire
and
belonging to Victor's Pantheon.
By Chapter 1163, the Shinto, Biblical and Hindu Pantheons remained distinct Divine factions while existing under the political supremacy of Victor's Empire.[12]
They were subordinate to the Emperor without being absorbed into Dragon's Nest as institutions.
This distinction was demonstrated through Imperial religious techniques.
[[Andrew Walter]] could borrow power from Gods specifically belonging to Dragon's Nest. Although the Shinto, Hindu and Biblical Pantheons served beneath Victor's Imperial order, their ordinary Gods remained recognized as members of separate Pantheons.[12]
For example:
- [[Amaterasu]] could be accessed because she personally belonged to Dragon's Nest as one of Victor's Dragon Goddesses.
- Kali could likewise be accessed as a member of Dragon's Nest.
- Susanoo remained identified as belonging to the [[Shinto Pantheon]].
- [[Shiva]] remained identified as belonging to the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]].[12]
IMPERIAL DISTINCTION
Empire ≠ Pantheon
A Pantheon can submit politically to Victor and become part of the Elderblood Imperial sphere while still retaining its own Gods, internal hierarchy and Pantheon identity. Dragon's Nest is the Emperor's personal Pantheon; the Empire is the much larger civilization surrounding it.[12]
This is one of the most important later corrections to the old conception of God-Kings.
A ruler such as Kali can simultaneously be personally integrated into Dragon's Nest while holding supreme authority over a subordinate but still distinct Pantheon.

### Hindu Succession
The [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]] provides the clearest example of how the office changes independently from raw power.
[[Indra]] — Formal King of Gods despite [[Shiva]] being individually much more powerful.
↓ resignation
Shiva — Formally succeeds Indra as King of Gods in Chapter 1063.[2]
↓ demotion / administrative replacement
[[Kali Elderblood]] — Elevated above Shiva as principal ruler of the Hindu Pantheon in Chapter 1152.[3]
This sequence demonstrates three separate principles:
- Raw strength does not automatically determine who is God-King.
- A living God-King can voluntarily surrender the office.
- A later ruler can be placed above the current God-King without requiring the former ruler's death.

### God-King Power Classification in the Imperial Era
The traditional God-King power system remains useful when discussing the older supernatural world, but by the later Elderblood era it is no longer sufficient as a universal military measurement.
The Empire adopted a broader threat-assessment system capable of categorizing supernatural beings, technology, armies and extraterrestrial civilizations under common threat codes.
By Chapter 1163, individuals such as [[Frederick Winter]] and [[Andrew Walter]] were evaluated using Imperial V-level threat classifications rather than merely being called Gods or God-Kings.[12]
Likewise, beings capable of destroying galaxies could occupy the same broad Imperial threat category while still possessing enormous differences in actual combat ability.
This later system reflects the same principle originally explained by [[Scathach]]:
A single label cannot perfectly describe real combat strength.
The God-King classification therefore remains historically and conceptually useful but should not be treated as the final universal power system of the setting.

### Relationship with Faith
A God-King normally governs a religious Pantheon, meaning Faith, worship and Divine civilization can become politically important to the office.
However, becoming a God-King is not the same thing as possessing the greatest amount of Faith.
Faith is a source of Divine influence and Energy that can strengthen Gods, empower religious institutions and support Divine manifestations, but the office of Pantheon Leader is tied to rulership rather than simply the number of worshippers an individual possesses.
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] provides an extreme example. His [[Blood God Religion]] became one of the largest religious structures in his civilization, but his authority as Dragon's Nest Pantheon Leader originates from his position as founder and sovereign rather than simply the existence of his worshippers.

### Relationship with Divine Weapons
Divine Weapons can drastically affect the practical combat capability of God-Kings.
The official ranking explanation specifically emphasizes that special weapons can allow a God to challenge beings whose ordinary classification would appear higher.[10]
This is one reason the power-level usage of "God-King" should not be interpreted rigidly.
Examples of variables include:
- [[Zeus]]' Divine Lightning weaponry.
- [[Odin]]'s Gungnir.
- anti-Divine weapons.
- Pantheon artifacts.
- Concept-enhancing relics.
- defensive Divine equipment.
A God-King with exceptional equipment can therefore perform substantially above what their raw Energy suggests.

### Authority Does Not Equal Strength
Several historical cases directly demonstrate that the strongest member of a Pantheon does not necessarily occupy its throne.

#### Indra and Shiva
[[Shiva]] possessed far greater personal power than [[Indra]], yet Indra remained formal King of Gods until voluntarily transferring the office.[2]

#### Zeus and the Greek Primordials
[[Zeus]] ruled [[Olympus]] despite the existence of older entities such as [[Gaia]] and Nyx whose nature and power could exceed ordinary Olympian Gods.

#### Victor's Dragon's Nest
By the later Imperial era, [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] stands overwhelmingly above his Pantheon, but internal combat rankings among his wives do not directly determine governmental office.
For example, one later assessment considered [[Scathach Elderblood Scarlett]] among the highest combat threats in Dragon's Nest despite other Goddesses possessing more raw Energy.[19]
Thus:
Political hierarchy, Energy hierarchy and combat hierarchy are three different things.

### Common Misconceptions
✘ “God-King is a species.”False. God-King is primarily a Divine office and secondarily a power classification.
✘ “Every God-King is the same strength.”False. Chapter 690 explicitly rejects treating the label as a precise equality of power.[1]
✘ “Every God-King-level being rules a Pantheon.”False. [[Hades]], Yama, Tasha and multiple others demonstrate otherwise.
✘ “The strongest God is automatically God-King.”False. [[Indra]] remained King of Gods while [[Shiva]] was substantially stronger.
✘ “All [[Primordial Gods]] are God-Kings.”False. Primordial status concerns Divine origin/power, not Pantheon rulership.
✘ “A God-King is always stronger than a Primordial God.”False. Political office and Divine power generation are separate systems.
✘ “There are exactly seven God-Kings.”False. Chapter 1059 mentions seven High-Level God-Kings, not the total number of Pantheon Leaders.[11]
✘ “Shiva is still the undisputed current ruler of Hinduism.”Outdated. Kali was placed above him as principal ruler in Chapter 1152.[3]
✘ “All Pantheons inside [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s Empire are Dragon's Nest.”False. Shinto, Hindu and Biblical Pantheons remain distinct even while politically subordinate to Victor.[12]
✘ “God-King is the universal title for every cosmic Pantheon ruler.”False. Upper-Sector factions such as Black Eclipse use different structures and titles.[18]

### Importance
God-Kings are important because they combine three concepts that are often incorrectly treated as one:
Divine authority, political rulership and supernatural power.
The Pantheon Leader is the central point through which organized Gods interact with other civilizations. The position can influence alliances, wars, celestial territories and the continued systemic recognition of the Pantheon itself.
At the same time, the power-class usage of God-King represents one of the major thresholds in the older supernatural ranking system, historically separating powerful conventional Gods and [[Combat Gods]] from the beings approaching Primordial levels.
The term therefore remains important even after [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s Empire outgrows the old Earth-based hierarchy.

### Trivia
- God-King can mean either a Pantheon office or a power classification. Context is required whenever the term appears.[1]
- A female Pantheon ruler can be referred to as a God-Queen.
- God-King is not a species.
- God-King is not a Divine generation.
- A God does not need Primordial ancestry to become a God-King.
- Being a Primordial God does not automatically make a being a God-King.
- [[Zeus]] ruled older [[Primordial Gods]] despite originating from a later Divine generation.
- [[Odin]] combined God-King office with Second Generation Primordial-God status.
- [[Amaterasu]] likewise combines God-Queen status with Primordial ancestry.[5]
- God-King-level beings do not necessarily rule Pantheons.
- The God-King power range contains Low, Mid, High and Peak subdivisions.
- High-Level Combat God traditionally precedes the God-King range in the older progression system.
- Energy is not sufficient to determine actual battle strength.[1][10]
- Divine Weapons can allow Gods to fight substantially above their ordinary Energy classification.[10]
- A Pantheon's strongest warrior does not automatically become its ruler.
- [[Indra]] and [[Shiva]] provide the clearest example: Shiva was stronger while Indra remained formal King of Gods.
- Shiva later replaced Indra as God-King.[2]
- Kali subsequently replaced Shiva as the principal ruler of the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]].[3]
- Kali and Amaterasu personally belong to Dragon's Nest despite the Hindu and Shinto Pantheons remaining institutionally distinct from [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s Pantheon.[12]
- Rulers of Hells and God-Kings of Pantheons are normally separate offices.
- A Ruler can possess God-King-level strength without becoming the Pantheon's God-King.
- Victor became Pantheon Leader by destroying the previous Olympian political order and creating an entirely new Pantheon.[6]
- The disappearance of the [[Greek Pantheon]] was sensed by other Pantheon Leaders through the System.[7]
- Dragon's Nest was subsequently recognized as a genuine Pantheon by the greater System.[14]
- [[Ikor-Kar-VI]] demonstrates that [[Nightingale]] possessed a Pantheon ruler independent from Earth's religions.[15][16]
- Ion demonstrates that extraterrestrial planets can possess their own Pantheon Leaders.[17]
- <Darkness> demonstrates that a Pantheon ruler in the Upper Sectors does not need to use the title God-King.[18]
- The phrase seven High-Level God-Kings in Chapter 1059 should not be turned into a speculative list of exactly seven Pantheon Leaders.[11]
- By the later Imperial era, the old God-King power levels coexist with more comprehensive Imperial threat classifications because the setting's military scale has expanded far beyond the supernatural hierarchy originally used on Earth.[12]
