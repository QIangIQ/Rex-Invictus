## MTWABV Canon

Divine Power Archive
GOD POWER LEVELS
The baseline hierarchy of ordinary Divine development
God Power Levels are the conventional stages used to describe the development of an ordinary God after achieving genuine Godhood. The baseline hierarchy contains only four stages: Minor God, Low-Level God, Mid-Level God and High-Level God. These classes are best understood as measurements of a God's developed Divine existence—particularly the growth, understanding and control of their Divinities—rather than as titles of religious importance, age, generation or a simple quantity of Energy.[1][2]
Article scope — Baseline Gods only: This page catalogues the four ordinary Divine classes and the mechanics that determine progression through them. Specialized battle-oriented classifications, ruler-level Divine states and higher cosmological orders are separate systems and are intentionally not incorporated into this hierarchy.

### Overview
Godhood in [[MTWABV Series|My Three Wives Are Beautiful Vampires]] is not a fixed state in which every deity is automatically comparable to every other deity. Becoming a God establishes a new Divine existence, but it is only the beginning of further development. [[Hestia]] explains that once a Being attains Divinity, the next stage of growth consists of learning about, understanding and developing the Concepts that define that God. How far a God progresses in their Divinity is itself a source of status and authority among Gods.[2]
The hierarchy should consequently be read as a developmental classification. A God can possess several Concepts and still remain relatively low in the hierarchy if those Divinities are poorly understood, while another God can reach a much higher class through exceptional development of a smaller number of Concepts. [[Persephone]] is the clearest example: before becoming the Ruler of the [[Greek Underworld]], she possessed numerous Divine Concepts but was explicitly only a Low-Level Goddess because she lacked proficiency in them.[3]
Likewise, raw reserves do not independently determine classification. In the Author's auxiliary ranking, [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] possessed quantities of Energy far beyond his contemporary technical classification, while his actual fighting effectiveness changed drastically depending on control, technique and transformation. The same explanation stresses that combat cannot be reduced to comparing two numerical power values; experience, technique, psychology, weaknesses, equipment and unusual abilities can all overturn an apparent difference in raw Power.[1]

### What Determines a God Class?
There is no evidence that the four baseline stages are produced by a single numerical statistic. The story repeatedly presents Divine progression as the combined development of the Being and their Divinities. The most important demonstrated factors are:
- Understanding of Divinity: Gods become stronger as they deepen their understanding of the Concepts that constitute their Divinity. [[Hestia]] identifies continued conceptual understanding as the principal journey that begins after attaining Godhood.[2]
- Control and proficiency: Merely possessing a Concept does not mean that the God has properly developed it. [[Persephone]] possessed many Divinities while remaining Low-Level because she was not proficient with them.[3]
- Quality of the principal Concept: Different Concepts provide radically different applications. A specialized authority such as Luck, Love, Death or another abstract phenomenon can remain threatening even when the user does not possess the greatest reserves or direct destructive ability.[1]
- Development of the Divine existence: Advancement can occur through enlightenment, transformation and major changes to the God's body and Soul. [[Hassan-i-Sabbah]] developed from a mortal into a High-Level God through his journey of self-discovery and continued understanding of his Concepts.[4][2]
- External resources and circumstances: Faith, artifacts, territories, Divine weapons and other amplifiers can dramatically improve practical performance without necessarily changing the God's underlying baseline class.[1]
Classification rule of thumb
More Divinities ≠ higher class.   Older God ≠ stronger God.   Greater Energy ≠ superior fighter.   Higher religious status ≠ higher God class.

### Minor God
Minor God — Nascent Divine Development
Minor Gods are genuine Divine Beings whose Divinities remain insufficiently developed compared with established Gods. They are not Demigods or incomplete pseudo-deities; they possess real Godhood, but the Concepts attached to that Godhood have not yet reached a substantial level of development.
Chapter 627 provides one of the clearest descriptions of the class. Among the fifty Greek Goddesses brought to [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction, only a smaller group possessed Major Divinities in a Concept, while the remaining women were described as Minor Goddesses without fully developed Divinities.[5] The distinction is therefore primarily developmental rather than a statement that a Minor God is not truly Divine.
Minor Gods can compensate for their lower development through versatility. Because their Divinities are less rigidly established, the Greek Minor Goddesses were described as being comparatively free to learn crafts and specialize in other disciplines instead of being as restricted by strongly developed Divine functions.[5]
[[Tyche]] was explicitly still considered a Minor Goddess in Chapter 843 despite possessing the extraordinarily useful Divinity of Luck. Victor valued the nature of her Concept enough to grant her exceptional protection and ordered her to continuously develop it.[6] Her later history demonstrates that Minor God is not a permanent ceiling: after immense development, Chapter 1154 describes Tyche as having risen from a Minor Goddess to a state in which she nearly embodied her own Concept.[7]

#### Characteristics
- Complete Godhood and legitimate Divinity.
- One or more Concepts may exist, but they remain comparatively undeveloped.[5]
- Can possess highly useful or dangerous powers despite low overall Divine development.
- Greater freedom to learn non-Divine specialties was specifically noted among the Greek Minor Goddesses.[5]
- Should not be treated as interchangeable with Demigods, whose Divine characteristics originate from a different condition.

### Low-Level God
Low-Level God — Developing Divine Mastery
Low-Level Gods have progressed beyond nascent Godhood but still possess limited proficiency, understanding or development in the Divinities that define them. Their Divine existence is established enough to stand above the undeveloped Minor class, yet their Concepts have not been refined to the level associated with the middle and upper baseline stages.
[[Persephone]] provides the clearest direct explanation. Before undergoing the trial that transformed her position within the [[Greek Underworld]], she was explicitly described as a Low-Level Goddess. The important point was that Persephone already possessed many Concepts for Divinities; what kept her at Low-Level was that she was not proficient in them.[3] This makes her one of the strongest pieces of evidence that the number of powers or Concepts a God possesses is not the same thing as their class.
The difference between a Low-Level God and enormously more developed Beings can become extreme. When [[Victor Elderblood]] entered the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]] in Chapter 1085 without fully suppressing his existence, High-Level and even [[Primordial Gods]] struggled beneath his presence, while nearby Low-Level Gods simply died from the pressure.[8]

#### Characteristics
- Divinities are established but remain imperfectly developed.
- A God may possess numerous Concepts while still remaining Low-Level if proficiency is poor.[3]
- Represents a meaningful step above undeveloped Minor Godhood without implying elite Divine mastery.
- The class can be crossed rapidly when a God's Divine existence fundamentally changes.

### Mid-Level God
Mid-Level God — Established Divine Development
Mid-Level Gods form the established middle of the baseline Divine hierarchy. Their principal Divinities have advanced beyond the incomplete development characteristic of the lower stages, giving them a more mature combination of [[Divine Energy]], Conceptual authority and usable Divine abilities.
The Author's auxiliary ranking used the term "average god" when describing the level [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] could fight through perfect control around Chapter 630, giving Susanoo, [[Apollo]], [[Tsukuyomi]] and Inari as examples of that comparison range.[1] This should be treated as a historical benchmark rather than a permanent present-day classification for every named character; the comparison describes their place at that point in the story and characters can later grow, weaken or acquire additional states.
The terminology remained relevant much later. In Chapter 1117, Victor assessed Edward as capable of fighting Mid-Level Gods, describing that degree of force as something that would once have been considered elite even though the standards of the [[Elderblood Empire]] had risen dramatically by that period.[9] Edward's statement is a combat comparison rather than evidence that he is himself a Mid-Level God, but it confirms the class as a recognized benchmark independent of species.

#### Characteristics
- The central established stage of ordinary Godhood.
- Greater proficiency and Conceptual development than the Low-Level range.
- Often functions as the practical "average God" benchmark in comparisons.[1]
- Being capable of fighting a Mid-Level God does not automatically make the combatant a Mid-Level God; species, physiology, techniques and other power systems can produce comparable fighting strength.[9]

### High-Level God
High-Level God — Advanced Baseline Divinity
High-Level Gods occupy the highest stage of the baseline God hierarchy covered by this article. Their principal Divinities have reached an advanced level of understanding and development, allowing them to exert substantially greater authority over the Concepts associated with their Godhood.
[[Hassan-i-Sabbah]] is one of the clearest examples. After revealing his Divine nature in Chapter 791, Agnes directly identified him as a High-Level God.[4] [[Hestia]] later explained why the achievement was so exceptional: Hassan had not merely attained Godhood as a former Human; within roughly a millennium he had also continued developing his Concepts far enough to become High-Level, something difficult even for Beings born as Gods.[2]
The class is therefore not restricted by Divine ancestry. A mortal who attains genuine Godhood and progresses sufficiently far in their Concepts can reach High-Level, while an ancient deity who fails to develop properly can remain in a much lower class.
Tasha demonstrates that the hierarchy also permits sudden advancement. When she completed her True Divine Form, her Divine development jumped from Low-Level Goddess to High-Level Goddess rather than slowly passing through every intermediate stage.[10] Her later transformations moved her outside the baseline hierarchy covered by this article, but the Low-to-High transition remains one of the clearest demonstrations that these stages describe achieved development rather than mandatory sequential "levels" that must each be occupied for a fixed period.

#### Characteristics
- Advanced development of one or more principal Divinities.
- Can be reached by both born Gods and ascended mortals.[4][2]
- Represents exceptional Conceptual progress within ordinary Godhood.
- Advancement into this class can occur gradually through understanding or abruptly through a transformative breakthrough.[10]
- A High-Level God can still possess enormous differences in practical fighting ability compared with another High-Level God because class alone does not account for every combat factor.[1]

### Divinity and Conceptual Progression
Godhood is the beginning, not the end.
[[Hestia]]'s explanation in Chapter 792 establishes the central principle behind the baseline hierarchy: after a Being becomes a God, they must continue learning about the Concepts that define them. Progress in Divinity is therefore an ongoing process rather than an automatic consequence of age.[2]
For mortals, the road to Godhood can begin through self-discovery and Enlightenment. Hestia explains that when a Being achieves the necessary understanding of themselves, their Soul and body fundamentally change and genuine Godhood is attained. From that point onward, they stand at the beginning of the same conceptual journey faced by Gods born Divine: discovering their Concepts and developing an increasingly complete understanding of them.[2]
This development is not performed in isolation. Gods embodying the same or overlapping Concepts can interfere with one another. Hestia explains that a sufficiently developed deity may hinder younger Gods from progressing in the same Concept, while an exceptionally insightful God may still advance despite that interference.[2]
[[Aphrodite]] is used as the principal example. Her authority over Beauty and Love had reached the top of those particular conceptual hierarchies, allowing her to hinder others seeking to develop the same Concepts. Hestia describes this as making Aphrodite the High-Level Goddess of Beauty and Love.[2] This wording is important: a statement that someone is High-Level in a particular Divinity should not automatically be transformed into an unsourced claim about every aspect of that God's total fighting capability.

#### Major Divinity Is Not the Same as High-Level God
Chapter 627 identifies several Greek Goddesses as possessing Major Divinities while contrasting them with Minor Goddesses whose Divinities remained undeveloped.[5] The term therefore describes the development or importance of a Divine Concept and should not automatically be used as a synonym for the complete High-Level God classification.
This distinction prevents several common errors:
- A God with a Major Divinity is not automatically proven to be a High-Level God.
- A famous or culturally important deity is not automatically a High-Level God.
- Possessing several Divinities does not automatically place someone above a God who has mastered fewer Concepts.
- A statement about being a High-Level deity of a specific Concept should be preserved in that context unless the story separately establishes the character's overall class.

### Class and Combat Ability
God Power Level is a useful indicator of Divine development, but it is not an automatic prediction of who wins a fight.
The Author explicitly separates raw Power from the outcome of battle. Technique, experience, psychological exploitation, weaknesses, Divine weapons and unusual abilities can allow a combatant to threaten someone who appears superior by a simpler comparison.[1] This is particularly important for Gods because Divinities can possess highly asymmetric effects.
[[Aphrodite]] is given as a direct example in the Author's explanation. Although she was not presented as being as effective as [[Ares]] in straightforward combat, her overwhelming authority over attraction, Love and Beauty made her dangerous even to Beings possessing greater direct fighting strength unless they possessed a method of resisting her influence.[1]
Accordingly, the following concepts should remain separate:

### Divine Generation and God Class
A God's generation describes origin and lineage. It does not independently determine their current class.
This is demonstrated from both directions. ### Common Misconceptions
Frequently confused classifications
“A Third-Generation God must automatically be High-Level.”
No. Divine ancestry and generation do not replace demonstrated development. [[Persephone]] is direct evidence that a deity of important Olympian lineage can remain Low-Level.[3]
“A God with many Divinities must be stronger.”
No. Persephone had numerous Concepts but lacked proficiency. Development and understanding matter more than simply counting abilities.[3]
“Major Divinity means High-Level God.”
Not automatically. Chapter 627 uses Major Divinity to distinguish developed Concepts from those of Minor Goddesses, while Chapter 792 separately discusses High-Level development.[5][2]
“A famous Pantheon deity is automatically High-Level.”
Religious prominence, mythology and political office are not substitutes for a chapter-supported power classification.
“More Energy means a higher God class.”
Not necessarily. The Author's ranking explicitly separates pure Energy from technical classification and practical combat ability.[1]
“Anyone who can fight a Mid-Level God is a Mid-Level God.”
No. Chapter 1117 states that Edward could fight Mid-Level Gods, but the statement is a combat benchmark and does not transform his race or Divine status into that classification.[9]
“Every God has to progress Minor → Low → Mid → High one stage at a time.”
No. Tasha directly moved from Low-Level to High-Level after a major Divine transformation.[10]

### Summary
Minor God
Genuine Godhood with Divinities that remain comparatively undeveloped.
Low-Level God
Developing God whose Concepts exist but remain limited in proficiency or maturity.
Mid-Level God
Established "average" Divine range with meaningfully developed and usable Divinities.
High-Level God
Highest baseline stage, characterized by advanced development of the God's principal Divinities.
The hierarchy is therefore not a list of social ranks or a rigid numerical battle system. It is a shorthand for the maturity of ordinary Godhood. The progression from Minor to High-Level reflects increasingly developed Divinities and Divine existence, while the outcome of an actual confrontation remains dependent upon the complete abilities and circumstances of the individuals involved.[1][2]
