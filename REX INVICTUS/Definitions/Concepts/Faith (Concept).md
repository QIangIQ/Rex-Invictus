**[[Faith (Concept)]]** is a metaphysical phenomenon and form of supernatural Energy produced from sufficiently powerful belief, conviction and certainty. (Lore adapted from *My Three Wives Are Beautiful Vampires*.)

Events of the MTWABV timeline (Victor's conquests, the fall of the pantheons, the [[Elderblood Empire]]) are distant background or rumor unless a chapter puts them on the page. Faith is a metaphysical phenomenon and form of supernatural Energy produced from sufficiently powerful belief, conviction and certainty. Although Faith is most commonly associated with religion, Gods, the Inquisition and Onmyoujutsu, it is not intrinsically dependent upon worship of the [[Heavenly Father|Biblical God]] or even upon worshipping a recognized deity. Faith can be directed toward a God, another person or oneself, with its fundamental requirement being an unwavering belief strong enough to transcend ordinary psychology and acquire genuine supernatural influence.[1][2]
[[Abe-no-Seimei]] described Faith as an unshakable confidence and belief in something. According to him, its exact interpretation changes from individual to individual, but its essence remains constant. When conviction and belief are cultivated to sufficiently extreme levels, they can produce supernatural phenomena capable of bending Reality and allow the practitioner to access what Seimei referred to simply as the "source".[2]
Later revelations established that Faith is not merely a state of mind required for religious techniques. It exists as actual Energy capable of being accumulated, redirected and stored; it can establish a connection through which mortals invoke or borrow power from Divine Beings; collective Faith can contribute to the birth of powerful Gods and influence the Divinities developed by existing Gods; and Faith directed toward one's own existence can nourish the Soul and assist the spiritual development necessary for reaching Divinity.[3][4][5][6]
Faith should consequently be understood as a broad metaphysical principle rather than a single spell, element or religious ability. Different supernatural systems use the same underlying phenomenon in different ways.

### Nature

#### Belief and Conviction
Faith begins with genuine belief. Merely claiming to believe something or intellectually accepting that a God exists does not necessarily produce the same supernatural result as true conviction. Seimei's explanation treats Faith as something rooted deeply enough in the individual that their certainty becomes capable of causing supernatural change.[2]
The object of that belief is secondary to the conviction itself. Maria demonstrated this exceptionally early in the story. Unlike [[Carlos Reiss]], she lacked the natural potential normally required to easily access the power used by the Church's [[Hunters of Artemis|Hunters]]. Maria nevertheless possessed an intense and unquestioning Faith in Carlos. Despite not believing in the [[Heavenly Father|Biblical God]], she could use Hunter abilities because, within her own worldview, Carlos occupied that position; she explicitly considered him her God.[1]
Faith therefore does not fundamentally operate according to religious labels. A person does not gain power merely because an institution declares their beliefs legitimate, nor is supernatural Faith necessarily invalid because the target is not officially recognized as a deity.

#### Objects of Faith
Faith has been demonstrated in several distinct forms:
- Faith in a God: The most traditional application. Inquisitors and Onmyōji use their belief to establish the basis for supernatural techniques connected to Divine Beings.[2]
- Faith in another individual: Maria's Faith in Carlos allowed her to use Hunter abilities despite having no genuine belief in the God worshipped by the Church.[1]
- Faith in oneself: Hassan defined Faith in oneself and one's own existence as a spiritual conviction capable of nourishing the Soul and assisting the journey toward Divinity.[5]
- Collective Faith in a Divine identity: Large populations consistently perceiving a God through a particular role can influence the Divinity associated with that God.[6]
- Faith shaped by myth and legend: Collective stories, expectations and worship can accumulate around a developing Divine identity and contribute to the natural formation of powerful Gods.[3]
These forms are manifestations of the same underlying principle rather than unrelated powers.

#### Faith Is Not Simple Knowledge
Knowing that Gods objectively exist does not automatically constitute Faith.
This distinction became particularly apparent after Humanity gained undeniable knowledge of the supernatural. [[Andrew Walter]] personally knew that Gods existed, had met numerous Divine Beings and understood their abilities, yet he found it difficult to possess traditional religious Faith because direct knowledge of those Gods also allowed him to judge their actions critically.[7]
[[Frederick Winter]] encountered a similar problem with [[Victor Elderblood]]. Fred knew perfectly well that Victor was a God-Emperor capable of absurd supernatural feats, but psychologically continued viewing him primarily as his childhood friend. That familiarity prevented him from producing the type of Faith required to properly use Victor as the Divine source of his [[Onmyoujutsu]]. Victor deliberately showed Fred a fraction of his true existence, allowing Fred to finally reconcile the person he knew with the Divine Being Victor had become. Immediately afterward, Fred successfully invoked Victor's power.[7]
Faith therefore requires genuine internal acceptance rather than factual acknowledgment.

#### Faith as Energy
Faith eventually manifests as genuine Energy rather than remaining purely psychological. [[Rhea]] explicitly identified Faith as a form of Energy while explaining the artificial religious infrastructure discovered by [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction. Faith was described as unusually free and difficult to store properly, but still capable of being contained under the correct conditions.[4]
When Victor inspected a specialized Divine Item created to gather Faith, he perceived the stored power as an extremely small quantity of golden-and-white liquid-like Energy concentrated within the object.[4]
Faith can therefore exist in at least two interconnected states:
Faith as conviction — the believer's spiritual and psychological certainty.
Faith as Energy — the supernatural power produced or accumulated through that belief.
The two should not be treated as completely separate phenomena. The latter originates from the former.

### Generation of Faith

#### Individual Faith
A single individual can produce enough Faith to access supernatural abilities. Maria, Leonardo, Mizuki, Fred and other practitioners demonstrate that Faith does not require a massive congregation before it becomes useful.[1][8][7]
The quality of that Faith matters considerably. Leonardo's conviction was sufficiently developed that he described his Faith as coming directly from his heart and could employ powerful Faith-based abilities without depending upon the lengthy external rituals associated with less experienced religious practitioners.[8]

#### Collective Faith
When large populations direct their belief toward the same figure, the resulting collective Faith can have effects far beyond those of an individual practitioner. This is particularly important to Pantheons, Divine identity and the formation of Gods.[3][6]
Collective Faith is strengthened when many believers share a consistent image of the object being worshipped. Stories, symbols, miracles, titles, temples and public demonstrations can therefore shape not only a religion's culture but the supernatural properties associated with its deity.

#### Faith Through Dependence and Fear
Faith does not need to develop under benevolent circumstances. The [[New Dawn]] settlement investigated by [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction demonstrated that the environment surrounding believers can be deliberately manipulated to increase Faith.
The population was periodically subjected to attacks by supposed monsters, after which the God worshipped by the community would appear to save them. This repeated cycle of danger and rescue increased fear, insecurity and dependence upon the deity, consequently increasing the population's Faith.[4]
The method was an artificial recreation of conditions that historically produced myths. Rather than waiting for unexplained events to generate stories and belief naturally, those controlling the settlement deliberately manufactured "miracles" and crises in order to cultivate Faith.[3][4]
Faith itself is therefore not inherently benevolent or malicious. Its moral character depends upon the beliefs, methods and individuals involved.

### Collection and Storage
Faith can be gathered through supernatural infrastructure.
The settlement examined by [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] contained shrines throughout its homes and a specially constructed Divine Item designed to gather the Faith produced by the inhabitants. [[Rhea]] explained that this object did not create Faith by itself; instead, the surrounding religious structure captured and directed the Energy toward a centralized storage medium.[4]
Ancient religions had already produced similar effects naturally. The Temple of [[Athena]] was cited as an example of a place where Faith from the surrounding city accumulated. The primary difference was efficiency: ancient worship produced and gathered Faith organically, whereas [[New Dawn]] deliberately engineered its religious infrastructure to collect the Energy as efficiently as possible.[4]
The process can broadly be represented as:
Believer
↓
Belief / Conviction
↓
Faith
↓
Shrine / Temple / Religious Structure
↓
Collection and Direction
↓
Divine Item or Central Repository
↓
Stored Faith Energy
Because Faith is unusually free in nature, simply placing it inside an ordinary container is insufficient. Specialized conditions or Divine mechanisms are required to retain it effectively.[4]

### Faith and Gods

#### Gods Do Not Require Faith to Exist
One of the most important distinctions concerning Faith is that Gods do not universally depend upon worship in order to exist or use their innate powers.
When [[New Dawn]]'s Faith-producing settlement was discovered, [[Scathach Elderblood Scarlett]] directly questioned why Gods would require Faith. The answer confirmed that Gods do not need Faith simply to use their powers. Faith remained extremely important to Pantheons for different reasons, particularly its connection to the creation and development of powerful Divine Beings.[3]
Therefore:
No Faith ≠ Automatic loss of Godhood
No Worship ≠ Automatic loss of innate Divine power
Specific Divinities may interact with followers differently, but this is not a universal dependency shared by every God.

#### Birth of Gods
Faith is deeply intertwined with the natural formation of powerful Gods.
In ancient times, mortals frequently witnessed phenomena they could not explain. Those events became stories, and repeated retellings transformed those stories into legends and myths. As generations attached names, abilities, personalities and symbolic meanings to those myths, collective Faith accumulated around an increasingly defined Divine identity.[3]
An example given during the explanation involves people witnessing lightning strike a giant serpent. Without a scientific explanation, witnesses might interpret the event as a supernatural battle. The story could evolve into a tale of a Thunder God defeating a serpent; further generations could expand the myth until a stable Divine identity existed around it.[3]
The broad process is:
Unexplained Event
↓
Story
↓
Legend
↓
Myth
↓
Defined Divine Identity
↓
Collective Faith
↓
Conditions for a Powerful God
Gods born from myths, imagination and Human Faith were stated to possess greater potential than ordinary Gods simply born from two Divine parents.[3]
This does not mean that every God is a fictional idea made real by Humans. Gods possess multiple origins, including natural Divine birth and ascension. Faith-born Gods represent one particular method through which Divine existence can arise.

#### Myth as a Framework
Faith alone is not the entire process of forming a mythological God. Myth gives collective belief structure.
A population possessing vague belief in an unnamed supernatural force does not produce the same defined identity as generations repeatedly telling stories about a particular deity with a recognizable name, Domain, personality, symbols and accomplishments. Myth determines what people believe in, while Faith supplies metaphysical weight to that belief.[3]
This is why legends and public perception can become important supernatural resources rather than merely cultural phenomena.

#### Decline of Natural God Formation
The natural birth of powerful mythological Gods became dramatically less common in modern times. Scientific advancement provided explanations for many events that ancient Humans would have interpreted as Divine phenomena, while modern information systems made it considerably harder for isolated myths to evolve in the same uncontrolled manner they had throughout antiquity.[3]
[[New Dawn]]'s experiment attempted to recreate the ancient conditions artificially by isolating a population, controlling its information and deliberately generating supernatural events capable of reinforcing a chosen mythology.[3][4]

### Faith and Divinity
Main article: Divinity
Faith and Divinity are closely related but are not the same thing.
A Divinity represents a God's connection and Authority over a Concept. Faith is belief-derived metaphysical power that can influence the connection between a God and a Concept. It does not itself create the universal Concept being accessed.[6]

#### Divinity Development
[[Aphrodite]] Elderblood explained that titles given to Gods should not be dismissed as meaningless simply because the God does not currently possess the corresponding Divine Concept. When thousands of mortals gather and pray to a Being while consistently associating that Being with a particular Concept, the resulting Faith can contribute toward the God awakening that Divinity.[6]
This makes public Divine identity metaphysically important. If a Goddess is consistently regarded by enormous populations as a Goddess of Art, Knowledge or Culture, those associations can eventually contribute toward genuine Divine development rather than remaining purely honorary titles.[6]
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] deliberately exploited this mechanism within the [[Elderblood Empire]]. Public appearances, miracles and the roles performed by members of his family could be arranged in ways that encouraged the population to associate particular Concepts with particular Goddesses. Jeanne's public healing of the Empire, for example, was useful not merely as humanitarian action but as part of the long-term cultivation of her Divine identity.[6]

#### Victor's Divinities
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] later stated that many of his Divinities developed partially because of the Faith of his subordinates and followers. The Concepts he discussed included Blood, War, Demons, Vampires, Fear, Despair, Murder, Strength and Destruction.[7]
This does not mean that Faith was the sole source of every Divinity Victor possessed. Victor gained Divine Concepts through several different mechanisms, including personal development, Divine ascension and the absorption of other Gods. His statement establishes that collective Faith can actively contribute to the development of Divinities already becoming associated with a God's identity.[7]

#### Follower-Dependent Divinities
Some Divine Concepts possess a more direct relationship with followers than others.
[[Tyche]] is a notable example. Her Divinity of Luck does not operate according to the same straightforward relationship between raw power and output that many other Divine abilities follow. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] deliberately encouraged Tyche to accumulate greater numbers of followers because doing so caused her Power of Luck to become increasingly "dense".[9]
This should not be generalized into a universal rule that every God automatically becomes proportionally stronger with each worshipper. Tyche's Luck is specifically described as an unusual Divinity whose operation depends strongly upon affinity and its own special characteristics.

### Faith and the Soul
Faith can also be directed inward rather than toward an external God.
[[Hassan-i-Sabbah]] identified a lack of Faith as one of the principal spiritual weaknesses affecting several of [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s family members. In this context, he was not speaking about religious devotion. Hassan described Faith as conviction in oneself and in one's own existence—a thought capable of nourishing the Soul and increasing the individual's metaphysical worth within existence.[5]
For Hassan, the path toward Divinity required hardship, enlightenment, understanding oneself and understanding one's place in the world. He had himself attained Divinity through Faith and taught the Philosophy of Self-Discovery as a method designed to help others understand their true identities and strengthen their spiritual foundations.[5]
Faith was subsequently confirmed to be Hassan's strongest Divine Concept, alongside his lesser Divinities of Military and Truth.[10]
This establishes that Faith possesses significance at three different levels simultaneously:
- as a personal conviction;
- as usable supernatural Energy;
- and as an actual Divine Concept capable of being embodied and mastered by a God.

### Faith-Based Supernatural Systems

#### The Inquisition
The Inquisition historically developed one of the most prominent combat systems based upon Faith.
Individuals with suitable potential could access supernatural power associated with the Church, while exceptional Faith could compensate for limitations in natural aptitude. Carlos possessed unusual natural potential for the system, whereas Maria lacked the same aptitude but gained access through her overwhelming Faith in Carlos.[1]
Inquisitors consequently draw strength through belief rather than conventional Mana-based spellcasting. Their Faith can be expressed through physical reinforcement, Energy constructs, sacred weapons, barriers and other supernatural techniques depending upon the practitioner.[2][8]
Leonardo demonstrated one of the most advanced early examples. His heartfelt Faith allowed him to create golden wings, swords, shields and armor-like protection, produce explosive Energy, create duplicates and rapidly regenerate severe injuries without relying upon the ordinary incantations expected of less developed practitioners.[8]
These individual abilities should not be interpreted as powers automatically granted to every Faith user. They are manifestations produced through Leonardo's particular Faith-based combat system.

#### Onmyoujutsu
Main article: [[Onmyoujutsu]]
The Divine branch of Onmyoujutsu operates on a similar fundamental principle to Inquisition techniques. Onmyōji use Faith to establish a connection to a Divine source and then employ talismans, invocations and ritual techniques to express a portion of that source's characteristics.[2]
Traditional Japanese Onmyōji largely directed their Faith toward Gods belonging to the [[Shinto Pantheon]], but this association is cultural and historical rather than an absolute metaphysical requirement. Fred later successfully reconstructed his Faith around [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] and invoked Victor instead of relying exclusively upon Japanese Gods.[7]
Onmyoujutsu itself is broader than Faith. Faith-based Divine techniques represent only one branch of the system, while other traditions—such as the Dark/Yōkai Arts developed by [[Ashiya Doman]]—operate through different methods.

#### Borrowing Divine Power
Faith can form the bridge through which a practitioner temporarily accesses a portion of a God's power.
Fred explained that basic [[Onmyoujutsu]] normally used only a fraction of a percent of the invoked God's power. More advanced spells could access larger fractions, but the result depended upon both the practitioner's Faith and the permission of the God being invoked.[7]
The process can broadly be represented as:
Practitioner
↓
Faith
↓
Divine Connection
↓
Invocation / Technique
↓
God's Permission
↓
Fraction of Divine Power
↓
Supernatural Effect
When Fred invoked [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] specifically as the God of Fear, he temporarily expressed a tiny portion of Victor's Fear-related power as concentrated crimson Negativity. Fred himself did not acquire the Divinity of Fear; he was channeling power belonging to Victor through the Faith-based connection.[7]

#### Divine Permission
Faith does not automatically give a worshipper unrestricted ownership over the abilities of the Being they worship.
Advanced [[Onmyoujutsu]] remains dependent upon the invoked deity allowing its power to be used. A practitioner can therefore possess genuine and powerful Faith while still being incapable of taking unlimited [[Divine Energy]] from an unwilling God.[7]
Faith establishes the connection; Divine permission determines what can be transmitted through that connection.

#### Direct Divine Intervention
A prayer or invocation can also attract the personal attention of the deity rather than merely channel an impersonal fraction of their power.
During the later [[Elderblood Empire]], Fred and Andrew jointly invoked [[Violet Elderblood Snow]] and her Concept of Order. Their prayer attracted Violet herself, who manifested a projection before them and personally imposed Order upon the planet they were operating on, pacifying its hostile creatures and stabilizing its environment.[11][12]
This demonstrates a distinction between borrowing Divine power and receiving direct intervention from the God being worshipped. Faith can facilitate both, but the latter remains an action consciously performed by the deity.

### The Elderblood Empire
By the mature [[Elderblood Empire]], Faith had become part of a large institutionalized supernatural system rather than remaining restricted to isolated Church [[Hunters of Artemis|Hunters]] or traditional Onmyōji.
The Empire possessed both Onmyō warriors and a reorganized Imperial Inquisition. [[Frederick Winter]] became one of the foremost figures among the Onmyō practitioners, while [[Andrew Walter]] occupied a comparable position within the Imperial Inquisition. Both ultimately existed under the religious authority of [[Valeria Alekerth]], High Priestess of the Empire's official religion.[11]
The two systems remained distinct. Imperial Onmyō warriors retained the versatility to invoke different Gods when sufficient Faith and Divine permission existed. The Imperial Inquisition drew power more directly through [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] and the [[Dragons Nest Pantheon]], allowing its practitioners to function as Divine instruments of the God-Emperor and the Gods directly belonging to his Pantheon.[11]
Political submission to Victor did not automatically make every God within the Empire a member of Dragon's Nest. Gods belonging to subordinate Pantheons could remain outside the ordinary invocation structure of the Imperial Inquisition unless they had personally entered Victor's Divine family or Pantheon.[11]
Ordinary practitioners still required genuine Faith, training and Divine permission. Fred and Andrew were exceptional because their personal relationship with Victor, their immense supernatural development and Victor's direct Blessings reduced many of the restrictions that would apply to ordinary worshippers.[11]

### Powers and Abilities
The following abilities describe properties and applications directly associated with Faith itself. Individual Faith users do not automatically possess every ability listed, and powers belonging specifically to an invoked God should not be mistaken for innate abilities of Faith.
- Faith/Belief Empowerment: Faith converts sufficiently powerful belief and conviction into genuine supernatural influence. The stronger and more deeply rooted the practitioner's certainty becomes, the greater their ability to access Faith-based supernatural systems can become.[2][7]
- Reality Warping: [[Abe-no-Seimei]] explicitly explains that when unwavering confidence and belief are cultivated to extreme levels, they can create phenomena capable of bending Reality and accessing what he calls the "source".[2]
- Energy Generation: Genuine belief can produce Faith as a measurable supernatural Energy. Large communities can generate enough Faith for dedicated religious infrastructure to continuously accumulate it.[4]
- Energy Manipulation: Faith Energy can be gathered, directed and transferred through supernatural systems. [[New Dawn]] constructed an entire religious settlement around directing the Faith produced by its inhabitants into a centralized Divine Item.[4]
- Energy Storage: Although Faith is extremely free and difficult to contain, specialized Divine Items and temples can store accumulated Faith under the correct conditions.[4]
- Power Augmentation: Faith can increase the effectiveness of supernatural techniques using it as their source. The strength of Divine [[Onmyoujutsu]] in particular depends in part upon the practitioner's genuine Faith.[7]
- Power Borrowing: Faith can establish the spiritual connection necessary for a practitioner to temporarily borrow fractions of the power or characteristics belonging to a God. This does not permanently grant the practitioner the invoked Divinity.[7]
- Power Bestowal: Through a Faith-based connection, a Divine Being can permit a worshipper or practitioner to channel part of their power. The bestowed ability remains fundamentally derived from the deity rather than becoming the user's own Divine Authority.[7]
- Divine Power Channeling: Faith-based supernatural systems can channel Divine characteristics into spells, enchantments, weapons or transformations. Fred's invocation of [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s Fear and Andrew's later use of Victor as a Divine source demonstrate this principle.[7][11]
- Invocation: Faith can be focused through prayer, talismans or religious techniques to call upon a Divine Being. The result may range from borrowing a minute fraction of the deity's power to attracting the deity's direct attention.[7][11]
- Soul Empowerment: Faith directed toward oneself can nourish the Soul and strengthen the individual's metaphysical sense of existence. Hassan considered this form of self-conviction important to spiritual development and the path toward Divinity.[5]
- Deity Creation: Collective Faith combined with mythology can contribute to the natural formation of powerful Gods. Stories and legends establish a Divine identity while accumulated belief gives that identity supernatural weight.[3]
- Divinity Development: Collective Faith can contribute toward an existing God awakening or developing a Divine Concept corresponding to the identity through which mortals repeatedly worship them.[6]
- Conceptual Empowerment: Faith can strengthen the relationship between a God and a Concept associated with their Divine identity. It does not create or rewrite the fundamental Concept itself; rather, it assists the God's connection to it.[6]
- Collective Empowerment: Particular Divinities can become stronger or denser through larger numbers of followers. [[Tyche]]'s Luck is a confirmed example, although this effect should not be assumed to apply identically to every Divine Concept.[9]
- Spiritual Connection: Faith establishes a metaphysical relationship between believer and object of worship capable of functioning across supernatural systems. This connection can be strong enough for the believer to channel power or for the deity to notice and personally answer an invocation.[7][11]

#### Faith-Based Manifestations
The following abilities have been demonstrated through techniques powered by Faith, but are not universal innate abilities of Faith itself. Their exact form depends upon the practitioner, Divine source and supernatural system being used.
- Energy Projection: Faith-based practitioners can release supernatural Energy offensively. Leonardo repeatedly manifested golden Energy in direct combat, while Fred projected borrowed Fear as a massive sphere of Negativity.[8][7]
- Weapon Creation: Faith-based power can manifest supernatural weapons. Leonardo produced golden swords, while Onmyōji can invoke Divine weapons through the powers they borrow from Gods.[8]
- Force-Field Generation: Leonardo created golden shields and barriers through his Faith-based power.[8]
- Flight: Leonardo manifested golden wings and used his supernatural Energy for aerial movement.[8]
- Regeneration: Leonardo rapidly restored severe injuries, including regenerating a severed arm during his battle with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]].[8]
- Duplication: Leonardo produced independent golden duplicates capable of acting as decoys and continuing combat.[8]
- Armor Generation: Faith-based Energy can be condensed around the user as supernatural armor, demonstrated by Leonardo when he covered his body in golden protection.[8]
- Elemental Manipulation: Practitioners using Faith to borrow Divine characteristics can temporarily express the elemental or conceptual properties of the invoked deity, such as storm-related power through Shinto Gods or Fear through Victor.[7]
- Statistics Amplification: Faith-based transformations and enchantments can enhance a practitioner's physical combat ability. The degree of amplification depends upon the technique, user and supernatural source.[7]
- Healing: Faith-based Divine systems can produce healing effects when the invoked power or practitioner's technique supports such an application.
- Power Replication: Faith-based invocation can temporarily reproduce a limited expression of another Being's supernatural characteristic without permanently transferring ownership of that power to the practitioner.[7]

### Faith and Holy Power
Faith should not automatically be treated as another name for [[Holy Energy]].
The Inquisition frequently combines Faith with sacred techniques, blessed equipment and powers particularly effective against Vampires, creating a strong historical association between Faith and holiness. However, the underlying phenomenon is much broader. Maria generated Faith through Carlos rather than the [[Heavenly Father|Biblical God]]; Onmyōji use Faith toward Shinto Gods; Hassan developed Faith in himself; and Fred used Faith toward [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] to manifest the negative Concept of Fear.[1][2][5][7]
Faith is therefore better understood as the metaphysical principle enabling belief-based power, while Holy Energy represents a particular supernatural alignment or form of power that may be accessed through certain Faith-based traditions.

### Faith and Blessings
Faith and Blessings are likewise related but distinct.
A Blessing is a supernatural benefit deliberately granted by another Being. Faith may establish or reinforce the relationship between worshipper and deity, but belief alone does not automatically constitute a Blessing. Hassan, for example, had both extraordinary Faith and an actual Divine Blessing, with the two being treated as separate aspects of his development.[10]
Similarly, Fred and Andrew possessed exceptional advantages within the Imperial religious systems because [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] had personally Blessed them in addition to their own Faith and training.[11]

### Artificial Manipulation of Faith
Because Faith is produced through genuine belief, manipulating what people believe can indirectly manipulate Faith itself.
[[New Dawn]]'s experiment demonstrates that the believer does not need to know the truth behind the events generating their conviction. The inhabitants genuinely believed that their God protected them even though the dangers reinforcing that belief were deliberately manufactured by the same organization studying them.[4]
This creates a distinction between:
Genuine Faith
and
Genuine information
Faith can be sincere even when the worldview producing it is based upon false information.
Consequently, organizations capable of controlling information, manufacturing miracles and isolating populations can artificially accelerate the creation of Faith without directly controlling the believer's mind.

### Limitations
- Genuine Conviction: Faith cannot be reliably reduced to empty words or ritual behavior. The practitioner must possess genuine internal belief. Fred's inability to initially use [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] as his Divine source despite knowing Victor was a God demonstrates this limitation.[7]
- Faith Quality: Not all belief possesses the same supernatural weight. Deeply rooted conviction such as Leonardo's can produce much greater results than superficial religious observance.[8]
- Practitioner Capacity: Faith does not completely replace the user's own training, Soul or physical vessel. Fred noted that his [[Onmyoujutsu]] became considerably stronger after he ceased being an ordinary Human because his new body could express the technique more effectively.[7]
- Divine Permission: Borrowing greater amounts of a God's power requires the God's permission. Faith does not allow a mortal to freely steal unlimited Divine Authority.[7]
- Source Dependency: Techniques based on Divine invocation depend heavily upon the nature and power of the invoked Being. A practitioner borrowing from a vastly stronger God has access to greater potential than one connected to a much weaker source, assuming their body, Faith and technique can support it.[7]
- Storage Difficulty: Faith Energy is naturally difficult to contain because of its free nature. Specialized Divine objects or equivalent supernatural infrastructure are required to store it efficiently.[4]
- Conceptual Direction: Collective Faith influencing Divinity requires a sufficiently consistent conceptual identity. Random worship without a coherent Domain does not necessarily produce the same result as thousands of worshippers repeatedly associating a God with a particular Concept.[6]
- Not Universal God Fuel: Gods do not universally require Faith to maintain their existence or use their innate powers.[3]
- Different Divinities React Differently: The direct benefit provided by followers varies. [[Tyche]]'s Luck becomes denser through additional followers, but the novel does not establish an identical numerical strengthening mechanism for every God.[9]
- Faith Does Not Grant Automatic Mastery: [[Awakening]] or strengthening a Divinity through Faith does not automatically provide complete understanding or mastery over that Concept. Divine progression still depends upon comprehension, affinity and development.
- Resistance and Power Difference: Faith-based attacks do not automatically bypass the defenses of stronger supernatural Beings. Their effectiveness remains dependent upon the power involved, the source being invoked and the resistances of the target.

Practitioners capable of invoking different Divine sources through Faith, technique and the permission of the relevant Gods.[7][11]

### Relationship with Other Powers
Power / System
Relationship
Divinity
Divinity is a God's connection to a Concept. Faith can contribute to the awakening or development of that connection but is not synonymous with Divinity.[6]
[[Divine Energy]]
Faith can be used to establish a connection through which Divine power is borrowed or channeled. The practitioner's Faith and the God's own Divine power remain distinct parts of the process.[7]
[[Holy Energy]]
Frequently associated with Church Faith techniques but not identical to Faith itself. Faith can be directed toward non-holy and even negative Divine sources.[1][7]
Blessings
Blessings are powers or benefits granted directly by another Being. Faith can accompany a Blessing but does not automatically constitute one.[10]
[[Onmyoujutsu]]
Its traditional Divine branch uses Faith as the connection through which practitioners borrow characteristics from Gods. Other Onmyoujutsu branches can function through different mechanisms.[2][7]
Prayer
A method of focusing Faith toward a deity. Prayer can invoke a Divine source or attract the God's attention but is not itself identical to Faith.
Myth
Provides structure and identity to collective belief. Faith supplies metaphysical weight while myth defines the developing deity's name, history, Domain and characteristics.[3]
Soul
Self-directed Faith can nourish the Soul and assist the individual's understanding and metaphysical development.[5]
Concepts
Faith cannot simply rewrite Concepts, but collective belief can influence the connection between a God and a Concept, contributing to Divinity development.[6]

### Notes
- The "source" mentioned by [[Abe-no-Seimei]] in his explanation of Faith has never been conclusively identified with Attributeless Energy, [[Primordial Chaos]], the [[Akashic Records]] or any later cosmological structure. Until directly confirmed, it should remain described only by the terminology used in Chapter 153.[2]
- Faith can refer to the believer's conviction, the supernatural Energy produced by that conviction and the Divine Concept of Faith. Context is therefore necessary when interpreting the term.
- Faith is not restricted to conventional religion. Maria's belief in Carlos and Hassan's Faith in his own existence are explicit examples.[1][5]
- The existence of Gods being objectively proven does not automatically increase Faith. Direct knowledge can actually make conventional worship more difficult when mortals judge a God's behavior and find them unworthy of devotion.[7]
- Faith-generated Gods are not the only type of Gods in existence. Divine Beings can also be naturally born from Divine parents or ascend through other methods.[3]
- Gods do not universally require Faith to use their powers. Faith's major importance lies instead in Divine formation, conceptual development, invocation and particular Divinities that interact directly with followers.[3][6]
- The golden appearance of many Inquisition techniques should not be treated as the universal appearance of Faith. Fred's invocation of [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s Fear produced crimson Negativity, demonstrating that the manifestation can inherit characteristics from the power being invoked.[7]
- Religious institutions can deliberately engineer environments that increase Faith by controlling information, producing miracles or creating dependence, as demonstrated by [[New Dawn]]'s experimental settlement.[4]
- Large-scale Faith can possess political as well as supernatural value. Victor's later civilization uses Divine intervention, rewards and visible interaction between Gods and citizens to maintain an unusually direct relationship between worshippers and the Pantheon.[7][11]
