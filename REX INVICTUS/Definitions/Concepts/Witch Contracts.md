Witch Contracts are magically binding agreements created through the [[Magic]] of Witches. Unlike ordinary legal documents, a Witch Contract transforms agreed-upon clauses into supernatural restrictions capable of recognizing whether the individuals bound by the agreement have fulfilled or violated its terms. Depending on the conditions written into the contract, violation can automatically trigger supernatural consequences, including death.[1][2]
Witch Contracts exist in several grades represented by the color of the contract itself. The confirmed hierarchy progresses from White Contracts, which can be produced by comparatively inexperienced Witches, to Golden Contracts created by more experienced practitioners, and finally Black Contracts, the highest confirmed conventional Witch Contract and a form of agreement associated with the authority of the Witch Queen.[2]
The strength of a Witch Contract depends heavily upon the magical authority of its creator. Breaking or overriding a contract generally requires a Witch with sufficient ability relative to the one who created it, although the legitimate owner of a contract can also terminate it under the appropriate circumstances. Because of this, high-grade Witch Contracts are widely useful for political agreements, secrecy, service, espionage, supernatural employment and the formal establishment of positions such as the [[Vampire Counts]] of [[Nightingale]].[2][3][4]

This page specifically covers the contract system developed and administered through Witch Magic.

### Nature
A Witch Contract is a form of supernatural Binding [[Magic]].
Once the participants agree to the terms and the contract is established, its clauses become magically enforceable conditions rather than ordinary promises. The contract can recognize whether the people bound by it have violated specific restrictions and impose the consequences written into the agreement.[1]
This means that enforcement does not necessarily require the Witch who created the contract to remain physically present.
Known contractual conditions have included:
- prohibition against betrayal;
- secrecy requirements;
- restrictions against revealing information;
- mandatory service;
- loyalty conditions;
- espionage obligations;
- limitations associated with supernatural political offices;
- and death as punishment for violating specific clauses.[1][2][3]
The precise limitations of the system depend upon the grade of the contract, the skill of its creator and the wording of the clauses.

#### White Contracts
White Contracts are the lowest confirmed class of Witch Contract.
They can be created by comparatively inexperienced Witches and can likewise be broken by another Witch possessing sufficient ability within the same general level of Witchcraft.[2]
Very few important White Contracts have been shown directly because the principal characters generally operate within political and supernatural circles where stronger contracts are readily available.
Their existence nevertheless demonstrates that contract [[Magic]] is a standard part of Witchcraft rather than a secret art restricted exclusively to the Witch Queen.

#### Golden Contracts
Golden Contracts are significantly stronger.
They are created by experienced Witches and require a sufficiently capable Witch to break or interfere with them.[2]
Golden Contracts are useful when the agreement involves serious supernatural consequences but does not require the exceptional authority of the Witch Queen.
One of the clearest examples occurred when [[Ruby Elderblood Scarlett]] established contracts with captured [[Hunters of Artemis|Hunters]]. Their agreement contained clauses governing secrecy, betrayal and their work as intelligence assets.[1]
Golden Contracts can therefore function as:
- employment agreements;
- confidentiality agreements;
- servant contracts;
- intelligence contracts;
- loyalty agreements;
- and supernatural safeguards against betrayal.

#### Black Contracts
Black Contracts are the highest confirmed class of conventional Witch Contract.
They are associated with the Witch Queen and possess substantially greater binding authority than lower-grade contracts.[2]
The most important known examples are the Black Contracts governing the position of the [[Vampire Counts]] of [[Nightingale]].
[[Vlad III Tepes|Vlad Tepes]] maintained agreements with:
- [[Scathach Elderblood Scarlett]];
- Agnes Elderblood Snow;
- [[Annasthashia Elderblood Fulger]];
- and later [[Victor Elderblood]].[3][4]
When Victor formally became the Fifth Vampire Count, a black parchment was used to establish the obligations and privileges associated with the position.[3]
Black Contracts are normally beyond the ability of ordinary Witches to break.
However, later events revealed an important distinction: a Black Contract can be annulled by the Witch Queen, while the legitimate owner of the contract can also terminate the agreement under the appropriate circumstances.[4]
Therefore, the Witch Queen is not necessarily the only Being in existence capable of ending a Black Contract. Rather, she possesses the magical authority required to interfere with and annul such contracts externally.

### Creation
The complete ritual for creating every grade of Witch Contract has not been revealed, but several elements have been directly demonstrated.

#### Contract Medium
Witch Contracts normally appear as magically produced paper or parchment.
The color of the material corresponds to the contract's grade:
- white;
- gold;
- black.[2]
These are supernatural objects rather than ordinary pieces of paper used merely to record legal language.
The contract medium itself participates in the Binding [[Magic]].

#### Establishing Clauses
The terms of a contract can be established verbally.
When Ruby created a Golden Contract, she placed her Blood upon the contract and declared the conditions aloud. The magical parchment responded by recording the clauses.[1]
This demonstrates that the contract can translate a magically recognized declaration into written supernatural law.

#### Blood
Blood can be used during the creation or activation of a Witch Contract.
Ruby used her Blood while creating the Golden Contract for the captured [[Hunters of Artemis|Hunters]].[1]
However, the novel has not established that every Witch Contract universally requires Blood.
Blood should therefore be treated as a demonstrated method rather than an absolute requirement for all contractual [[Magic]].

### Enforcement
The defining characteristic of Witch Contracts is automatic supernatural enforcement.
Once a contract is properly established, violating a clause can trigger the punishment specified within the agreement.

#### Death Clauses
A contract can be written so that violating a particular condition results in death.[1]
This makes Witch Contracts especially useful when dealing with individuals who would otherwise be difficult to trust or supervise.
A participant cannot simply rely upon distance, secrecy or physical strength to ignore the agreement once its [[Magic]] has been properly established.

#### Secrecy
Contracts can forbid the disclosure of specific information.
Ruby used this property to ensure that the [[Hunters of Artemis|Hunters]] placed under her authority could not reveal sensitive information concerning her faction.[1]
Such clauses make Witch Contracts valuable for:
- espionage;
- political negotiations;
- secret organizations;
- confidential employment;
- and supernatural operations involving information that cannot be allowed to spread.

#### Betrayal Restrictions
Contracts can define betrayal as a violation and automatically punish the contracted individual if they attempt to act against the interests of the contract owner.[1]
The exact scope of what qualifies as betrayal depends upon the wording of the agreement.

#### Service Obligations
Witch Contracts can require the bound individual to perform a particular service.
This may include:
- working for another individual;
- acting as a spy;
- obeying specific orders;
- maintaining confidentiality;
- or fulfilling political responsibilities.

### Breaking a Contract
Witch Contracts are not universally unbreakable.
Their resistance to interference depends primarily upon:
- the grade of the contract;
- the authority of its creator;
- the authority of the person attempting to remove it;
- and whether the individual attempting termination possesses legitimate ownership over the agreement.

#### Magical Superiority
A stronger Witch can break a contract created by a weaker Witch.
Kaguya explained this principle while describing the contract hierarchy.[2]
For example:
- inexperienced Witches can create White Contracts;
- sufficiently capable Witches can break White Contracts;
- experienced Witches are required for Golden Contracts;
- Black Contracts normally require the Witch Queen's level of authority.[2]
This creates a direct relationship between contract security and the supernatural standing of its creator.

#### Witch Queen Authority
The Witch Queen possesses the highest confirmed authority over conventional Witch Contracts.
Her position gives her the ability to annul Black Contracts that would normally be beyond the reach of ordinary Witches.[2][4]
This ability became politically important when [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] sought to free the three existing Vampire Countesses from their agreements with Vlad.

#### Contract Ownership
Chapter 633 establishes that the legitimate owner of a contract can also void it.[4]
This is particularly important because it prevents the contract system from becoming a mechanism that permanently traps even the individual who originally possessed authority over the agreement.

### Vampire Count Contracts
The most politically important known Witch Contracts are those used to bind the [[Vampire Counts]] of [[Nightingale]].
When Vlad created the system of Vampire Counts, the position was formalized through Black Contracts.[3]
These contracts established the relationship between the King and the Countesses and formed part of the political structure that governed Nightingale.
Known contracted Counts included:
- [[Scathach Elderblood Scarlett]];
- [[Agnes Elderblood Snow]];
- [[Annasthashia Elderblood Fulger]];
- and [[Victor Elderblood]].[3][4]

#### Victor's Appointment
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s elevation to Fifth Vampire Count was formalized through a Black Contract.[3]
The agreement was not merely ceremonial.
By accepting it, Victor entered the same supernatural political structure that governed the older Countesses.
The contract represented both:
- recognition of his position;
- and magical enforcement of the conditions associated with it.

#### Liberation of the Countesses
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] later opposed the continued contractual control Vlad possessed over [[Scathach]], Agnes and Natashia.
Through his relationship and negotiations with the Witch Queen, Victor obtained a means of having their Black Contracts annulled.[4]
When Vlad discovered that the agreements had been voided, the event represented a major political loss.
The incident demonstrated that even the strongest Witch Contracts are not absolutely permanent when someone with sufficient magical or ownership authority intervenes.

### Use in Espionage
Witch Contracts are particularly effective for covert operations.
Ruby's agreement with the [[Hunters of Artemis|Hunters]] provides one of the clearest examples.[1]
Individuals who might otherwise betray their employer can be forced to:
- keep secrets;
- provide information;
- avoid hostile actions;
- and remain bound by predefined consequences.
Because enforcement is supernatural, the employer does not need to continuously observe the contracted agent.
This makes Witch Contracts highly valuable to organizations operating through:
- spies;
- infiltrators;
- mercenaries;
- intermediaries;
- and politically unreliable allies.

### Use in Supernatural Politics
The contract system gives Witches enormous political influence.
A powerful faction can possess enormous military strength but still require Witch expertise to establish agreements that can reliably bind other supernatural Beings.
This creates demand for Witch services among:
- Vampires;
- [[Werewolves]];
- [[Hunters of Artemis|Hunters]];
- rulers;
- supernatural organizations;
- and other political powers.
The Witch Queen's ability to create and annul the highest grade of contracts further increases the political importance of her position.
Black Contracts in particular can directly influence the balance of power between rulers and their subordinates.

### Relationship with Witch Society
Contract [[Magic]] forms part of the broader service economy of Witch society.
Witches commonly provide supernatural services to other races in exchange for:
- money;
- magical resources;
- political favors;
- information;
- and other forms of compensation.
Contracts complement other Witch products and services such as:
- Full Moon Talismans;
- magical artifacts;
- investigation Magic;
- teleportation;
- dimensional storage;
- and supernatural information networks.
Their ability to create enforceable agreements gives Witches a form of power that does not depend primarily upon direct combat.

### Limitations

#### Dependent on Creator
The strength of a Witch Contract depends heavily upon the Witch who created it.[2]
A contract produced by an inexperienced Witch does not possess the same resistance to interference as one produced by the Witch Queen.

#### Can Be Broken
Witch Contracts are not absolutely unbreakable.
A sufficiently capable Witch can remove weaker contracts, while the Witch Queen can interfere with Black Contracts.[2]

#### Owner Authority
The legitimate owner of a contract can also terminate it under the appropriate circumstances.[4]

#### Wording Matters
The contract enforces its established clauses.
Consequently, the exact wording and conditions used during creation are important.
The story has not established that a Witch Contract automatically interprets every possible hidden intention of its participants beyond the terms actually placed into the agreement.

#### Not Universal Reality Manipulation
Even Black Contracts remain a form of Witch [[Magic]].
They should not automatically be treated as irresistible against every Being in Creation regardless of power, authority, resistance or conceptual status.
No chapter establishes conventional Witch Contracts as absolute laws overriding every higher supernatural authority.

### Relationship with Other Contract Systems
Witch Contracts represent only one form of supernatural binding in the setting.
Other systems later include:
- Soul-based contracts;
- Demon-related agreements;
- Divine oaths;
- Ruler Authority;
- and bindings created directly through superior supernatural authority.
These systems can resemble Witch Contracts in purpose while operating according to completely different principles.
A Witch Contract should therefore be classified by its origin in Witch [[Magic]] rather than simply by the fact that it binds two parties.

### Strategic Importance
The importance of Witch Contracts comes from their ability to convert trust into an enforceable supernatural mechanism.
Supernatural society contains countless individuals capable of:
- disguising themselves;
- escaping across Dimensions;
- resisting ordinary punishment;
- surviving conventional execution;
- manipulating memories;
- and betraying employers from enormous distances.
A conventional legal agreement offers little protection against such Beings.
Witch Contracts compensate for this problem by attaching enforcement directly to the supernatural participants.
As a result, they became useful for:
- governments;
- intelligence operations;
- supernatural employment;
- political alliances;
- secrecy;
- military service;
- and high-level agreements between factions.

### Notes
- Witch Contracts are a standard branch of Witch [[Magic]] rather than a single unique artifact.[2]
- The known grade hierarchy is White → Golden → Black.[2]
- Contract color corresponds to magical grade rather than simple decoration.
- White Contracts can be created by comparatively inexperienced Witches.[2]
- Golden Contracts require more accomplished Witchcraft and are suitable for serious restrictive agreements.[2]
- Black Contracts are the highest confirmed conventional Witch Contract and are associated with the Witch Queen.[2]
- The political contracts of [[Nightingale]]'s [[Vampire Counts]] are Black Contracts.[3][4]
- Violating a Witch Contract can result in death if such a consequence is written into its clauses.[1]
- Ruby demonstrated that Blood and spoken conditions can be used during contract creation.[1]
- The Witch Queen can annul Black Contracts.[4]
- Chapter 633 also establishes that a contract's legitimate owner can void it.[4]
- Witch Contracts are not the same thing as every supernatural contract later used by [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] or other high-level Beings.
- No chapter establishes Witch Contracts as absolutely impossible to resist or override through sufficiently superior supernatural authority.
