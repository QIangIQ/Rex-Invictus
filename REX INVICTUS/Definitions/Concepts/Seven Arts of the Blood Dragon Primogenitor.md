### Users
[[Victor Elderblood]]
The Seven Arts of the Blood Dragon Primogenitor are an advanced progressive martial system created by Victor Elderblood after his evolution into the Blood Dragon Primogenitor. Rather than representing a single stance, weapon school or supernatural technique, the Seven Arts organize Victor's accumulated martial knowledge into a structured progression that gradually incorporates the practitioner's physical body, control over Reality, Energy circulation, Soul and weaponry into one unified method of combat.[1][2]
Victor created the system from everything he had experienced as a martial artist: the foundation originally taught to him by [[Scathach Elderblood Scarlett]], the numerous Human and supernatural martial systems he studied, his training with multiple weapons, thousands of years of practical experience and the enormous archive of combat memories inherited from beings whose Souls and experiences he absorbed.[1] Its purpose was not merely to provide another collection of powerful attacks. Victor wanted a martial art that completely matched what his body had become and could exploit the abnormal characteristics of a Blood Dragon without abandoning the technical discipline upon which his martial development had been built.[2]
Although the system is called the Seven Arts, its curriculum was already beginning to expand when Victor first presented it. The original version possessed seven levels divided into three major phases. After explaining those levels, Victor recognized that Level 1 assumed far too much prior knowledge to function as a genuine beginning and conceived a separate Level 0 to prepare future practitioners. The Seven Arts should therefore be understood as an evolving martial framework rather than eight independent Arts.[2]
Victor created the Seven Arts before obtaining his personal Greatsword Grandmaster Enlightenment, while [[Scathach]] nevertheless judged the martial system itself capable of rivaling the status of Grandmaster-level [[Martial Arts]].[1][3]

### Creation and Development
The Seven Arts did not originate from one pre-existing fighting style. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] describes the system as the result of everything he had learned since his mortal life, including martial arts practiced personally and those encountered through the memories of absorbed beings.[1] By the time of its creation, Victor possessed an unusually broad martial archive. He had trained under [[Scathach]], studied numerous Human and supernatural systems, become a Master of several forms of combat, developed extensive weapon proficiency and spent enormous subjective periods training, fighting and teaching others.
The approximately two-thousand-year period surrounding Victor's later martial development was especially important. Scathach noted that Victor used that time more completely than almost anyone else because he continuously trained and taught. Teaching itself became another form of advancement: explaining a technique to another practitioner forced Victor to examine why it worked, how another body interpreted it and whether its structure could be improved rather than merely repeated.[1]

Victor also states that the art did not appear instantaneously. It took several years merely to properly visualize what he wanted the system to become.[2] This distinction is important because Victor's unusual talent can make his creations appear effortless from the outside. The Seven Arts were instead the result of accumulated experimentation, enormous practical experience and repeated revision.

#### From Adaptation to Creation
Earlier in his martial life, [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] had already developed a method of preserving one broad foundation while accommodating principles from different martial arts. The Seven Arts represent a later stage of that philosophy. Instead of asking how an existing martial system could be altered to suit him, Victor began constructing a system from the beginning around the exact properties of his current existence.

### Nature and Design
The Seven Arts blur the distinction between conventional martial technique and supernatural power because the two are intentionally inseparable within the system. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] nevertheless considers the result [[Martial Arts]] because the supernatural effects are generated through the execution of structured martial principles rather than merely activating a Divine or racial power separately.[1][2]
A punch remains a punch, but Reality can be altered at the exact instant of impact. A stance remains a stance, but the "ground" supporting it may be a Reality-defined surface created in empty space. Energy still reinforces the practitioner, but instead of repeatedly releasing and replacing it, the Energy becomes part of a continuously circulating internal system. At the most advanced levels, properties of the practitioner's Soul are incorporated into that circulation and transferred back into the body or a compatible weapon.

#### Efficiency as a Central Principle
One of the most important themes of the Seven Arts is efficiency. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] repeatedly demonstrates that he is already capable of producing greater destruction through brute power than several techniques shown during his explanation. The reason to use [[Martial Arts]] is therefore not simply to produce a larger explosion. Proper technique allows him to generate extreme effects while spending substantially less Energy and applying his power more precisely.[2]
This is especially clear with Infinite Punch. Victor can cause even greater destruction by simply releasing more of his natural power, but Infinite Punch manipulates Reality so that the strike receives an enormously amplified effect while requiring comparatively little Energy from the practitioner.[2] The Seven Arts therefore transform Victor's immense reserves from a source of brute force into a resource that can be applied with progressively greater efficiency.

### Level 0 — Preparatory Foundation
Level 0 was not one of the original Seven Arts. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] conceived it only after explaining the seven-level system and realizing that the First Level was an unreasonable place for another practitioner to begin.[2] Level 1 already assumed advanced bodily control and a sufficiently intimate relationship with Reality to create functional surfaces where none naturally existed. Those are not genuine beginner requirements.
Level 0 was therefore envisioned as the educational foundation of the art. Its purpose is to teach a compatible Dragon how to properly move their body, establish foundational martial coordination and improve their control over Reality until the practitioner possesses the minimum framework necessary for Level 1.[2]

### First Phase — Body and Reality
The first three Arts transform the practitioner's body into the central weapon of the system. Their defining supernatural mechanic is increasingly precise Reality Distortion. The practitioner first establishes an environment in which correct martial movement is always possible, then learns to distort Reality during close combat and finally amplifies those effects until apparently ordinary strikes can tear open the surrounding structure of space and Reality itself.[2]

#### Level 1 — Gravity Control and Reality-Based Footing
The First Level teaches the practitioner to control gravity and maintain correct footing regardless of the environment.[2] [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s demonstration initially resembles standing or moving in the air, but the mechanism is more sophisticated than conventional flight. Victor distorts local Reality so that empty space behaves as though a solid surface exists beneath him.

The distinction is important to martial mechanics. A ground-based strike normally depends upon leverage transmitted through the feet and the surface beneath the fighter. Level 1 allows the practitioner to create an equivalent foundation wherever necessary, preserving stance, balance and force transfer even while fighting in empty air or other environments without conventional ground.[2]

#### Level 2 — Reality-Assisted Close Combat
Level 2 integrates Reality Distortion directly into close combat. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] performs punches and kicks with enough physical strength that ordinary movement should violently affect the surrounding atmosphere, yet the expected environmental reaction does not occur. Instead, Reality immediately surrounding his movements repeatedly shatters and reconstructs itself.[2]

The objective is not simply to throw a stronger punch. Reality manipulation must occur simultaneously with physical movement until the supernatural process becomes part of the martial action rather than a separate ability activated before or afterward.
To advance, the practitioner must become capable of manipulating Reality on increasingly small scales without relying upon spoken commands.[2] The fighter must eventually move, defend, attack and alter Reality simultaneously without losing martial coordination.

#### Reality-Based Attack Redirection
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] had already demonstrated an unusual application of this principle during his battle against [[Kali Elderblood]]. Kali launched concentrated Destruction toward him, and Victor responded by casually punching the air. Reality distorted around the movement and the incoming attack was returned toward Kali.[1]
The chapter does not assign an official name to the application or fully define whether the effect should technically be considered reflection, redirection or a localized restructuring of Reality. What is confirmed is that Victor accomplished it while demonstrating his martial system and triggered the phenomenon through a physical punch.[1]

[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s punches and kicks begin creating small ruptures in Reality with each movement, transforming conventional close-combat actions into attacks capable of damaging the surrounding structure of Reality rather than merely striking physical matter.[2]

##### Infinite Punch
Infinite Punch is the clearest named application of Level 3. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] based the technique partially upon principles associated with the [[Fulger Clan]]'s martial arts, but reconstructed the effect through Reality Distortion.[2]
At the instant of impact, Victor manipulates Reality so that Reality itself interprets the strike as though the same location had been hit with effectively infinite mass. The visible result is not simply a conventional explosion. Reality shatters forward from the attack, producing an enormous violet rupture extending outward into space.[2]

The technique is significant precisely because Victor does not need it to obtain raw destructive power. He openly states that he can create worse destruction through brute force alone. Infinite Punch instead represents economy: martial technique and Reality manipulation produce an extreme result while consuming substantially less Energy than overwhelming the target through raw output.[2]

### Second Phase — Energy
Levels 4 and 5 shift the system away from Reality amplification as the primary lesson and begin restructuring how the practitioner's own Energy behaves during combat. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] compares the process to Ouroboros: Energy continuously travels through the body in a loop rather than being constantly released, lost and replaced.[2]

#### Level 4 — Cyclic Energy Loop
At Level 4, the practitioner establishes continuous Energy circulation throughout the body. The loop dramatically reduces unnecessary expenditure and can maintain bodily activity for an extremely long period, with the physical condition of the body becoming a greater limitation than ordinary Energy depletion.[2]

Level 4 is intentionally imperfect. A small quantity of Energy is permitted to escape because the practitioner is not yet expected to perfectly control every difficult process at the same time. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] compares this deliberate leakage with allowing part of the body to function automatically while conscious attention is directed elsewhere.[2]

[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] repeatedly describes the process as an infinite or endless loop. The demonstrated mechanism does not create limitless new Energy from nothing. "Infinite" describes the continuous cyclic pattern: Energy already possessed by the practitioner is repeatedly circulated and reused while unnecessary losses are minimized.[2]

### Third Phase — Soul
The final phase transforms the Seven Arts from a system using the body, Reality and Energy into one that modifies the quality of Energy and the body through properties derived from the practitioner's Soul. These levels are consequently the most dangerous portion of the original curriculum.[1][2]

#### Level 6 — Soul-Reinforced Energy
Once the practitioner has obtained the required Energy control, the circulating loop is carefully directed toward the deepest portion of the Soul. Small remnants or properties of the Soul begin merging with the Energy traveling through the cycle, causing that Energy to become considerably more robust.[2]
06
SIXTH ART
Soul-Reinforced Energy
Perfect Energy loop • Controlled Soul contact • Soul-derived properties • Reinforced Energy
This stage requires extreme caution. The practitioner is no longer manipulating a replaceable quantity of ordinary external or internal Energy alone; they are deliberately allowing that Energy to interact with the core structure of their existence. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] emphasizes that the process must therefore be performed slowly, carefully and with extraordinary control.[2]

#### Level 7 — Body-Soul Reinforcement
Level 7 completes the original Seven Arts by carrying the reinforced properties developed through Level 6 back into the practitioner's physical combat structure. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] describes the result as partially merging the robust properties derived from the Soul with the body rather than simply placing the entire exposed Soul outside the body as armor.[1][2]

Victor further explains that penetrating this external reinforcement does not necessarily mean directly damaging the complete Soul. The technique exploits the robust defensive properties developed through the Soul while attempting to keep the Soul itself protected deeper within the practitioner's existence.[2]

### Weapon Integration
The Seven Arts are not restricted to unarmed combat. [[Rose Adrasteia Elderblood]] recognizes that the principles can be expressed through weapons, while [[Otsuki Elderblood Haruna]] concludes that [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] has effectively created a martial system capable of remaining compatible with almost everything a Blood Dragon learns.[2] This versatility reflects one of the defining properties associated with Dragons themselves: extremely broad compatibility.
Victor demonstrates the principle by creating a Greatsword and applying the advanced stages of the Seven Arts through the weapon. Its properties change as the reinforcing Energy and Soul-derived characteristics enter it, after which Victor performs a slash capable of opening a spatial rupture approximately fifty times larger than the earlier rupture created during his unarmed demonstration.[2]

#### True Dragons and Other Races
The Seven Arts are commonly described as a Dragon or Blood Dragon martial art because [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] explicitly uses [[True Dragons]] as the model for a race naturally capable of learning it. His actual restriction is more functional: the practitioner must belong to a race capable of inherently distorting Reality like True Dragons.[2]
True Dragon → Naturally possesses the required type of Reality interaction
This does not necessarily establish that the biological designation "True Dragon" is the only possible route. Another existence possessing genuinely equivalent inherent Reality control would satisfy the stated mechanical requirement unless later canon explicitly establishes otherwise.

The greatest dangers of the Seven Arts are not necessarily its visible punches, kicks or weapon movements. The true risk lies in simultaneously manipulating Reality, Energy and the Soul while subjecting the practitioner's body to extreme physical and supernatural strain.[2]

#### Soul Damage
The Soul phase is the most dangerous portion of the system. [[Scathach]] and the other observers recognize that a mistake while manipulating one's own Soul can result in catastrophic self-mutilation or death.[2] Unlike ordinary physical injuries, these consequences cannot safely be treated as damage that every practitioner can simply regenerate away.
This is the primary reason [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] or another properly qualified Grandmaster is required to supervise advanced training.

#### Excessive Physical Strain
Morgana's criticism causes [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] to recognize another major design flaw. He created the Seven Arts around a body so abnormally durable that his personal tolerance distorted his assumptions about what should be considered acceptable strain for another practitioner.[2]
Victor concludes that the system requires a better equilibrium between:
PHYSICAL BODY↔ENERGY↔SOUL
He even recognizes that portions of the demonstrated version could kill another practitioner even if that individual were a Dragon God.[2] The Seven Arts shown in Chapters 1035–1036 should therefore be understood as extraordinarily powerful but still imperfect.

#### Highest-Level Family Secret
Because of both the system's power and its mortality risk, the Elderblood Family places the Seven Arts at its highest level of secrecy.[2] The restriction exists for two separate reasons: enemies cannot be permitted easy access to the art, and reckless family members cannot be permitted to destroy their own Souls attempting techniques beyond their ability.

The Seven Arts were explicitly unfinished when [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] first presented them. Victor's response was not to abandon the system but to begin redesigning it.

The Seven Arts ultimately represent [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s attempt to answer a problem created by his own evolution. Traditional martial arts were created around bodies and limitations that no longer properly described him. Rather than abandoning martial technique in favor of raw cosmic power, Victor constructed a system capable of treating Reality Distortion, immense Energy reserves and the Soul itself as extensions of disciplined combat. Its most extreme applications—returning supernatural attacks by punching Reality, tearing enormous holes through space, simulating Big Bang-like events through efficiently amplified strikes and reinforcing weapons with Soul-derived properties—are not replacements for martial skill, but examples of what martial skill becomes when practiced by an existence capable of directly interacting with those layers of Reality.[1][2]

### Notes and Clarifications
- The Seven Arts originally consisted of seven levels. Level 0 was conceived afterward as a preparatory stage.[2]
- [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] created the Seven Arts before personally becoming a Grandmaster.[1]
- [[Scathach]] describes the system itself as capable of rivaling Grandmaster-level [[Martial Arts]]. This is an assessment of the martial art, not Victor's personal rank at the time.[1]
- The first three Arts primarily develop the body and Reality manipulation.[2]
- Reality Distortion is a fundamental prerequisite rather than an optional supernatural power added afterward.[2]
- Infinite Punch manipulates Reality so the strike is interpreted as possessing effectively infinite mass.[2]
- Level 3 is explicitly capable of creating Reality ruptures compared to simulating a Big Bang. The principal advantage is producing such an effect with very little Energy compared with brute-force alternatives.[2]
- The Big Bang comparison should not be rewritten as ordinary explosive Energy generation. The demonstrated mechanism is Reality amplification through martial technique.
- Levels 4–5 do not demonstrate the creation of literal infinite Energy. They establish an increasingly lossless cyclic Energy system.[2]
- Energy Barrier combines cyclic Energy with a thin Soul-derived reinforcement.[1]
- The spiderweb-like restraint used against Kali is canonically unnamed in Chapter 1035.[1]
- Advanced Soul manipulation is potentially fatal. Improper practice can damage or mutilate the Soul itself.[2]
- Victor's initial version is excessively demanding even for other Dragon Gods. This is one of the flaws he immediately begins correcting.[2]
- Weapon applications are confirmed. Ordinary weapons tested by Victor were destroyed, while [[Junketsu]] survived the complete technique.[2]
- Victor recommends weapons possessing both a Blood and Soul connection to the practitioner.[2]
- The weapon demonstration creates a spatial rupture approximately fifty times larger than the previous example.[2]
- The Seven Arts' internal levels are separate from Martial Art Mastery.
- Victor later becomes a Grandmaster specifically through Greatswordsmanship in Chapter 1039 rather than by unlocking another Seven Arts level.[3]
- Later combat abilities should only be added when the novel identifies them as developments or applications of the Seven Arts, or when the connection is sufficiently explicit to distinguish them from Victor's independent supernatural powers.
