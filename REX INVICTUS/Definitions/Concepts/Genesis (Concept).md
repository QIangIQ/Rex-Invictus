### Ownership

#### Owner
[[Lilith Elderblood]]
Genesis is the personal God-Slaying sword of Lilith Elderblood, the Progenitor and Goddess of Demons. It is one of the comparatively rare weapons explicitly identified as possessing anti-God properties and has been Lilith's primary weapon since before her liberation from [[Diablo]].[1][2]
Genesis was initially referenced without being named when [[Morgana Elderblood]] explained that Lilith was the only being she personally knew who possessed a God Slayer as her primary weapon.[1] Its name and special nature were directly revealed during the later Demonic War, when Lilith was freed from Diablo's control and recalled the sword to herself from a distant location. The narration explicitly identified the weapon as possessing anti-God properties and called it a God-Slayer before revealing its name as Genesis.[2]
Lilith subsequently wielded Genesis in her battle against Diablo, where the sword directly intercepted the [[Spears|Spear]] of Longinus without being destroyed.[3] Genesis remained in Lilith's possession long after the Demonic War and was still available for her immediate use in Chapter 828.[4]

### Description
Genesis is an ominous sword belonging personally to Lilith.[2] The novel has not yet provided a sufficiently detailed description of its exact dimensions, material, forging process or creator to justify specifying them.
Its defining characteristic is its classification as a God-Slayer. Chapter 362 establishes that God-Slaying weapons differ from ordinary supernatural weapons because they are capable of producing the special form of damage required to truly threaten Gods.[1]
Genesis is later explicitly described as a weapon possessing anti-God properties.[2]

### History

#### Lilith's God-Slayer
Genesis is strongly associated with Lilith and appears to have served as her primary weapon long before its name was revealed. In Chapter 362, Morgana explained to [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] that she had once been close to a woman whose primary weapon was a God Slayer. Victor immediately identified the woman as Lilith, which Morgana confirmed.[1]
The discussion occurred after [[Scathach Elderblood Scarlett]] wounded the Goddess Inari. Although [[Scathach]]'s spear could injure a God, it did not cause the special Royal Blood associated with damage from an actual God-Slayer. This prompted the explanation that a special weapon was necessary to produce that level of injury and established Lilith's sword as an exceptional weapon even among supernatural armaments.[1]

#### Diablo's control over Lilith
During [[Diablo]]'s plan against [[Lucifer]] Morningstar, Lilith was being controlled and used as his subordinate. She was already carrying a sword during the confrontation, and Diablo later specifically noted that his subordinate possessed a sword capable of killing a God.[5][6]
The sword is not directly named Genesis during that scene, so the article should avoid claiming details about its appearance there that the chapter does not explicitly establish. However, the later identification of Lilith's primary God-Slaying sword as Genesis provides the relevant context for her established equipment.

#### Lilith's liberation
After [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] broke [[Diablo]]'s control over Lilith in Chapter 685, Lilith immediately demanded the return of what belonged to her. She extended her hand and the sound of impacts could be heard from a distant location before an ominous sword flew into her hand.[2]
The narration then explicitly identified the weapon as possessing anti-God properties, described it as a God-Slayer, and revealed its name:
Genesis.[2]
The event demonstrates that Genesis is personally bonded or otherwise responsive enough to Lilith that she can recall it from a considerable distance.

#### Battle against Diablo
Lilith wielded Genesis throughout her battle with [[Diablo]] following her liberation.[3] She combined the sword with her Miasma and Divine Concepts while attempting to overcome Diablo's extreme durability and regeneration.
During the fight, Diablo revealed the [[Spears|Spear]] of Longinus and threw it at Lilith. Lilith intercepted the Spear directly with Genesis. The two weapons collided hard enough to produce an explosion of power, but Genesis remained intact.[3]
Lilith then caught Longinus in her free hand and threw it back at Diablo.[3]
This encounter provides one of Genesis' clearest physical feats: it could directly withstand a collision with another highly specialized supernatural weapon without being destroyed.

#### Later ownership
Genesis remained in Lilith's possession after the Demonic War. In Chapter 828, Lilith became irritated enough during a conversation that power fluctuated in her hand as if she were about to summon Genesis, with the narration again explicitly referring to it as a God-Slayer weapon.[4]
The weapon is therefore still active and belongs to Lilith.

### God-Slayer Classification
Genesis is one of the clearest named examples of a God-Slayer weapon in the story. Chapter 362 distinguishes between simply possessing a weapon capable of wounding a God and possessing a true God Slayer. [[Scathach]]'s spear could make Inari bleed Divine Blood but did not produce the special Royal Blood associated with a proper God-Slaying weapon.[1]
Morgana then identifies Lilith as the only person she knew who possessed such a weapon as her primary armament.[1]
This means Genesis should not be described merely as a strong Divine sword. Its primary established specialization is killing Gods.

### Relationship with Lilith
Genesis is strongly associated with Lilith personally. She refers to the weapon as something belonging to her and is capable of calling it directly into her hand from a distance.[2]
The novel has not yet explicitly explained whether this relationship is produced by a Soul bond, ownership authority, magical enchantment or another mechanism. The page should therefore describe the demonstrated remote recall without speculating about the underlying method.

### Limitations
Genesis is a God-Slayer, but the novel has not established that simply touching a God with the weapon guarantees instantaneous death. The effectiveness of supernatural weapons remains dependent upon the attack, wielder, target and comparative power involved.
Genesis also did not allow Lilith to immediately kill [[Diablo]] during their battle, although Diablo is not a normal God and possessed exceptional durability and regenerative characteristics.[3] Lilith's inability to immediately finish Diablo should therefore not be interpreted as proof that Genesis' God-Slaying property failed against Gods.
The exact limits of its anti-God effect against [[Primordial Gods]], [[God-Kings]], [[Primordial Entities]] or beings who have transcended standard God physiology have not been directly defined.
