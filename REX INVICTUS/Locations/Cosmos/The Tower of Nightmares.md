The Tower of Nightmare, also known as the Tower of Nightmares and Nightmare Tower, is an artificial Dimensional training realm and Growth Artifact created principally by [[Victor Elderblood]]. Its original purpose was to overcome one of the few weaknesses that could not be solved simply by giving Victor's family more Energy or stronger bodies: experience. [[Violet Elderblood Snow]], [[Ruby Elderblood Scarlett]] and [[Sasha Elderblood Fulger]] had reached extraordinary levels of raw Power within only a few decades, but they lacked the centuries or millennia of battle experience possessed by warriors such as [[Scathach Elderblood Scarlett]]. The Tower therefore places its users into genuine life-or-death situations where mistakes cause real pain and death, while restoration, accelerated Time and Soul nourishment allow those experiences to accumulate without permanently losing the trainee.[1][2]
The Family Tower originally contained 777 floors and was supplied with approximately 500 million Souls from the immense collection accumulated by Victor. Their [[Races (Flamel)|Races]], abilities, memories, combat characteristics and other information became material from which the Tower could construct enemies and scenarios tailored to whoever entered. Its environments are not static dungeon rooms: the system watches the challenger, identifies habits and weaknesses, changes terrain, mixes abilities that ordinarily would not belong together, introduces counters to repeatedly used Powers and allows unexpected solutions when a trainee displays genuine creativity. Even apparently safe rest areas can contain hidden rewards, traps and mimic creatures.[2][3][4][5]
Its most important mechanism is not merely repeated fighting. Victor built the Tower around the moment immediately surrounding death, when a Soul exposes a greater portion of its potential and becomes unusually receptive to interference. While the user approaches death, dies and is reconstructed, extremely small traces derived from the Souls incorporated into the Tower can be used to nourish the trainee's own Soul. The process improves the quality and latent potential of the Soul over repeated cycles, allowing the user's eventual limits to rise alongside the experience accumulated through battle.[3] A later Divine Artifact implementation of the Tower refined these systems into an Eternal Loop of combat, death, Soul nourishment and rebirth, contained within a planet-like internal Dimension and supported by several Divine Concepts. Its sophistication was great enough to shock the Owner of Limbo, who recognized principles comparable to mechanisms normally associated with [[The Limbo Realm]].[6]

### Purpose and Philosophy
The Tower was not created because Violet, Ruby and Sasha lacked talent. Their problem was the opposite: their growth had been so fast that their Power had advanced considerably faster than their practical experience. They wanted to continue fighting beside [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] as his enemies became increasingly dangerous, yet women such as [[Scathach]], [[Jeanne Elderblood]] and [[Aphrodite]] Elderblood possessed instincts shaped by centuries or millennia of conflict. No amount of theoretical knowledge could immediately reproduce the mentality produced by personally surviving war.[1]
Victor could have transferred memories taken from experienced warriors into his wives, but rejected that as an adequate substitute. Receiving another person's memories would provide information about what happened without necessarily reproducing the psychological and instinctive development of personally being wounded, surprised, cornered, terrified, forced to improvise and made to choose correctly while death was seconds away. The Tower instead creates circumstances where those lessons belong to the challenger herself.[3]
Its training philosophy can therefore be summarized through several connected principles: Power without experience creates exploitable weaknesses; predictable specialists can be countered; knowing the traditional abilities of a Race does not mean an individual follows those expectations; apparently safe environments should still be assessed; creativity is more valuable than blindly following an intended solution; and genuine warrior instincts are created through repeated decisions under pressure rather than through passive study. Victor expected users to keep their senses active, evaluate terrain and opponents, prepare for betrayal or ambush and remain capable of immediately fighting whenever circumstances changed.[3][4][5]
The Tower does not attempt to make every warrior identical. A specialist is not punished simply for possessing a preferred Power. Instead, excessive dependence upon that Power causes the Tower to create situations where the user must demonstrate that she remains functional without it. Violet was repeatedly denied the freedom to solve everything with overwhelming Fire, while Sasha's unusual speed and Lightning would have caused the system to introduce progressively more effective anti-speed and sensory counters if she relied upon them without adaptation.[4][5]

### History

#### Creation
The Tower originated after Violet, Ruby and Sasha openly acknowledged that their current strength was insufficient for the future they wanted. They did not merely want greater reserves of Energy; they wanted the mindset and experience necessary to stand at the front with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] instead of remaining dependent upon stronger or more experienced family members whenever an unfamiliar threat appeared.[1]
Victor responded by constructing a training environment severe enough to reproduce the experiences they were missing. [[Roxanne Elderblood]] assisted with the Soul component of the project, while [[Zaladrac Zeovnur Elderblood]], [[Gaia]] Elderblood and Nyx Elderblood were among those involved in creating, supplying or administering elements of the Tower. Nyx later managed mimic-type soulless creatures used in its challenges, while Gaia participated in observation and development of the system.[2][4]
The initial Tower was supplied with approximately 500 million Souls. Victor considered this only a small portion of the Souls already contained within him. These Souls had originated from the enormous variety of beings he had killed or absorbed throughout his life, including Humans, Vampires, [[Werewolves]], Demons, Monsters, Youkai and Gods. Rather than simply reviving five hundred million people as independent permanent inhabitants, their accumulated information became the foundation from which the Tower could reproduce, alter and combine opponents for training.[2]
The structure contained 777 floors. Difficulty rose as users advanced, with Floor 700 onward reserved for enemies at least within the High-Level God-Class. The final stages could use information derived from Primordial God-level existences Victor had absorbed, making the upper Tower relevant even to challengers who had long since surpassed ordinary supernatural combatants.[2]

#### First Training Cycle
Violet, Ruby and Sasha became the first challengers. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] deliberately stripped away the advantage of their highest-quality personal equipment and supplied comparatively ordinary leather armor, iron or steel weapons, Blood and basic living facilities. The purpose was to improve the warriors themselves rather than allow Divine weapons or other extraordinary possessions to hide fundamental mistakes.[2]
Their first mandatory cycle lasted approximately six internal months. Through the Time Principles Victor had taken from [[Kronos]], those six months corresponded to only about three days outside the Tower.[2] The women were not allowed simply to leave when the training became painful. They had asked for rapid growth with full knowledge that Victor considered their determination genuine, and the Tower was constructed to make that determination meaningful.
The early sessions demonstrated the system's cruelty almost immediately. Ruby was killed before she could properly react to what appeared to be an ordinary low-level Ice Demon; Violet repeatedly died because environments or opponents neutralized her preferred Fire; and Sasha entered scenarios designed to ensure that neither Lightning-enhanced perception nor extreme speed became something she trusted absolutely.[2][4][7][5]

#### Expansion of Access
The visible improvement of the original three quickly attracted the rest of [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s family. [[Siena Scarlett Elderblood]], [[Lacus Scarlett Elderblood]] and [[Pepper Scarlett Elderblood]] were sent into the Tower during the original observation period, while other later participants included [[Rose Adrasteia Elderblood]], [[Annasthashia Elderblood Fulger]], [[Agnes Elderblood Snow]], [[Lilith Elderblood]], Demon Generals and additional members of the wider faction.[5][8]
Access nevertheless remained controlled. Victor refused to allow someone into the Family Tower simply because the person wanted an easy path to greater Power. The experience includes repeated realistic deaths and continual psychological pressure; entering without sufficient determination could damage a person's mentality rather than strengthen it. The Tower was consequently treated as a dangerous Growth resource rather than an amusement or ordinary practice room.[4]

#### Natashia's Incident
The Tower's interaction with Soul, Blood and ongoing racial evolution was demonstrated particularly clearly through Natashia. While [[Gaia]] and Nyx were supervising the Tower, Natashia advanced to another floor, entered a rest period and drank [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s Blood. Soon afterward she collapsed into violent convulsions, forcing Victor and the others to intervene.[9]

#### Growth of Violet, Ruby and Sasha
The original training achieved exactly what [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] intended. When Violet, Ruby and Sasha later emerged after another significant period within the Tower, their effective combat level had risen from approximately Minor Combat God standards to High-Level Combat-Focused God standards. In their Vampire Count forms, Victor considered them capable of fighting among the strongest combat Gods, including figures such as [[Thor]] and [[Ares]].[10]
Their advancement helped confirm Victor's growing understanding that a Being's Soul heavily influences the heights its Power can reach. The Tower did not merely teach techniques; by repeatedly nourishing and refining the Soul while forcing the user to turn potential into actual experience, it addressed both sides of that limitation.[3][10]

#### Divine Artifact Development
The Tower design continued evolving after its original creation. By the negotiations surrounding the remnants of the [[Norse Pantheon]], Dragon's Nest possessed a compact Tower of Nightmares Growth Artifact created through the combined craftsmanship of [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] and [[Hephaestus]], with [[Thanatos]], [[Fenrir]] and [[Typhon]] also contributing to its systems.[6]
Externally, the Artifact could appear as a small tower-like object despite containing an immense independent Dimension. Divine observers detected an extraordinary mixture of authorities within it, including Soul, Death, End, Beginnings, Life, along with traces of War, Destruction and Time. It was regarded as a Divine Artifact of exceptionally high quality.[6]
Its internal structure had refined the Tower's original principles into a stable Eternal Loop. Death did not remove the challenger permanently; the user was restored, the Soul could be nourished during the death process, and the cycle could begin again. The Artifact could provide combat experience, Soul development and conditions capable of assisting a user toward Enlightenment regarding Divinity.[6]
The Time differential had also increased enormously. The later form could provide approximately 1,000 internal years for every seven external days.[6] Whether every later copy or alternate Imperial version possesses precisely the same specifications has not been established.

#### Imperial Expansion
The Tower eventually became important enough that alternate versions were produced for the military of the [[Elderblood Empire]]. These Imperial Nightmare Towers are distinct from the restricted Family Tower. The Family version remained the superior resource reserved for selected members of [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s household and individuals whom he personally authorized, while ordinary Imperial forces trained within alternate Towers created for wider use.[11]
The distinction became explicit when the Seven Original Emperor's Soldiers began their education. They normally would have used the Imperial version, but Victor ordered that they receive the best available resources. [[Scathach Elderblood Scarlett]] therefore took them into the Family Tower and immediately subjected them to a civilization-scale war simulation rather than beginning with conventional lessons.[11]

#### 777 Floors
The original Family Tower contains 777 floors, but they should not be interpreted as 777 permanently fixed rooms with one unchanging monster assigned to each number. The Tower dynamically reacts to the challenger and can alter environments, opponents and difficulty according to observed behavior. Even when a numerical floor is known, a later attempt may not reproduce the exact same environment experienced previously.[2][4]

#### Rest Areas
Each challenger can begin from a personal room containing basic supplies. Ruby's initial room included multiple iron weapons, a bed, a bathroom and refrigerated Blood. Her clothing had also been changed into simple leather armor appropriate for training rather than her highest-level personal equipment.[2]
The apparent rest area is deliberately not an absolute sanctuary. Violet's decision to inspect the room revealed a hidden chamber containing equipment superior to the standard weapons. When she attempted to take one of the steel swords, it transformed into a mimic-like tentacled creature. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] described the Tower as rewarding curiosity while simultaneously emphasizing that nowhere inside should be accepted as safe merely because it appears harmless.[4]
This creates a deliberate contradiction central to the Tower's design: exploration can produce superior resources, but the act of seeking those resources also creates additional danger.

#### Dynamic Environments
Doorways do not necessarily lead to the same location after death. Ruby initially entered a narrow dark cave; after being killed and returning to her room, she opened the same door and instead encountered an open mansion filled with genuine sunlight.[2] Violet similarly moved through completely different environments after successive deaths, including an ice cavern, a snow-covered region, a mansion contaminated by explosive green liquid and a forest where careless use of Fire could cause catastrophe.[4][7]
Known environmental forms include caves, tunnels, mansions, forests, snowfields, regions containing natural sunlight, chemically dangerous structures, battlefields and full civilization-scale war zones. The environment itself frequently functions as part of the enemy rather than merely as scenery.

#### Adaptive Intelligence
The Tower demonstrates an autonomous capacity to monitor challengers and choose circumstances according to their behavior. When Haruna questioned whether it was sentient after the Tower deliberately moved Violet to a higher difficulty, [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] described the answer as essentially something like that, but also different.[4]
It is therefore more accurate to describe the Tower as possessing a highly sophisticated adaptive will or autonomous system than to treat it as a conventionally independent person. Its demonstrated functions include identifying frequently used Powers, detecting tactical habits, escalating counters, choosing environments that deny favored abilities, altering creatures through combinations of Soul information, rewarding exploration, punishing complacency and permitting creative solutions that Victor himself did not necessarily predetermine.[4][5]

#### Parallel Instances
The Family Tower can place different users into separate versions of the same general scenario. The seven Original Emperor's Soldiers were told that they were experiencing the same training exercise without occupying the same place, allowing each recruit to confront the scenario independently rather than simply following another Soldier's solution.[11]
This makes the Tower suitable for evaluating individual leadership, judgment and personality even when several trainees begin the same training program.

### Demonstrated Trials

#### Opening Cave
Ruby's first trial began in an extremely narrow cave. She initially chose a spear from her rest room but immediately recognized that the weapon was unsuitable for the restricted terrain, discarded it and created short Ice daggers instead. The decision showed correct environmental adaptation, but her confidence collapsed when she encountered what appeared to be a low-level white wolf-like Ice Demon.[2]
Ruby assumed she already understood the creature because of her knowledge of Ice Demons. The wolf instead moved through Lightning and killed her before she could mount an effective defense. The encounter established one of the Tower's fundamental rules: appearance and Race cannot safely be used as a complete prediction of an opponent's abilities.[2]

#### Sunlight Mansion
After revival, Ruby returned through the same doorway expecting the cave and instead entered an open mansion exposed to genuine sunlight. She used Ice to block the sunlight and remove one of her racial disadvantages, but the large-scale manipulation alerted nearby enemies. Three Ice Wolves capable of Lightning movement attacked her; this time she remained prepared and killed them.[2]
Ruby relaxed immediately after defeating the obvious opponents. A shadow creature pierced her heart from behind, followed by hundreds more manifestations that overwhelmed and killed her. The sequence directly attacked her tendency to consider a confrontation finished once the visible enemy had been defeated.[2]

#### Mixed-Ability Monster Trials
Ruby's later opening-floor encounters further abandoned conventional racial assumptions. Spider-like Youkai and other creatures could display Powers unrelated to what their bodies suggested, including Fire and plant-based abilities, while the number and variety of enemies forced her to rely increasingly upon her [[Martial Arts]] foundations rather than solving every encounter through laboratory knowledge or elemental superiority.[3]
Her performance also became the clearest early measurement of the Tower's Soul-growth mechanism. After Ruby killed hundreds of creatures during one period, approximately 0.2% of their Soul material had been incorporated into the nourishment of her Soul.[3] The percentage belonged to that specific observed accumulation and is not established as a fixed amount transferred by every individual kill.

#### Hidden Equipment and Mimic
Violet approached the starting room differently from Ruby. Instead of immediately leaving, she searched walls and corners and discovered a hidden equipment chamber. The Tower rewarded that curiosity with access to superior weapons but simultaneously turned one of those weapons into a mimic-like monster when she touched it.[4]
The trial established that the Tower does not distinguish neatly between "reward room" and "trap room". A useful discovery can be genuine while still containing an additional threat, forcing challengers to preserve suspicion even when the environment appears to be rewarding them.

#### Violet's Anti-Fire Trial
Violet's aggressive tendency to use maximum Fire immediately caused the adaptive system to send her to Floor 21, substantially above Ruby's opening challenge. After escaping an ice cavern, Violet emerged into a vast snowstorm and encountered several enormous humanoid-reptilian creatures created by combining [[Nightingale]]-native Soul information, Dragon characteristics and other monstrous traits.[4]
Their construction specifically undermined her normal fighting style. Their bodies possessed extreme durability and significant resistance to flames, allowing them to survive attacks Violet normally expected to overwhelm an opponent. Her steel weapon shattered against one creature and her Fire could not solve the encounter before she was frozen to death.[4]
The sequence was not simply a stronger-enemy test. Violet possessed enough raw firepower to threaten the creatures, but the Tower had constructed opponents whose particular traits exploited her lack of versatility.

#### Explosive Mansion
A later reset placed Violet inside a mansion coated with foul-smelling green liquid. Her first instinct was to burn the substance away, but a warning from her instincts made her hesitate. Even after suppressing the immediate impulse, Fire coating her sword eventually touched the liquid and triggered an explosion powerful enough to kill her.[4]
The environment demonstrated that an ability can remain enormously powerful while becoming tactically unusable. The objective was not to make Violet's Fire weaker; it was to teach her that using the correct Power at the wrong moment can be indistinguishable from making a fatal mistake.

#### Sixth Attempt and the Forest
After repeatedly failing, Violet deliberately changed her approach on her sixth attempt. She entered a green forest where the vegetation appeared so combustible that even a small spark could potentially set off the surrounding environment. Rather than using Fire, she relied upon ordinary Vampire movement, swordsmanship, shapeshifting and her special Eyes.[7]
Violet moved through the forest using Vampire agility and the terrain itself, leaping through trees and using brief Future Vision to perceive approximately five seconds ahead. She fought plant-covered wolf-like enemies without relying on indiscriminate elemental destruction and eventually recognized an attempted ambush near the exit before it could catch her unaware.[7]
The sequence represents one of the clearest examples of successful adaptation inside the Tower. Violet did not lose Fire as a specialty; she gained the ability to function effectively when Fire was the wrong answer.

#### Mimic Corridor
Sasha entered a narrow corridor containing steel weapons similar to those encountered by Violet. Her Lightning-generated sensory field allowed her to detect that some of the weapons were dangerous, but instead of simply abandoning them she discovered that the mimics activated when physically touched. Sasha used Lightning to manipulate and carry the weapons remotely, converting potential traps into resources without triggering them.[5]
Nyx questioned whether this represented an unintended flaw. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] rejected that interpretation: the Tower exists to create problems, not demand one predetermined solution. If a challenger discovers a legitimate method of exploiting the scenario through observation and creativity, that success is itself part of the training.[5]

#### Anti-Lightning and Sensory Counters
The same corridor contained creatures capable of emerging through the surrounding surfaces. Sasha discovered that ordinary Lightning was not necessarily the correct answer and killed them using thrown physical weapons instead. The system was prepared to escalate further if she became dependent upon speed or Lightning, potentially introducing highly durable enemies, opponents capable of suppressing movement or information derived from beings such as [[Nocturnus]].[5]
Near an apparent exit, Sasha's static sensory field failed to detect a fish-like creature containing a minute trace of Void-related power. Rather than remain and fight an unknown enemy in a disadvantageous position, she immediately used the portal and escaped. The lesson targeted a different form of overconfidence: even an excellent detection system can have something capable of bypassing it.[5]

#### War of Two Empires
The training of the Seven Original Emperor's Soldiers demonstrated that the Family Tower eventually developed far beyond monster-filled dungeon environments. Each Soldier was placed into a separate instance of a large-scale war scenario containing two existing Empires already fighting one another when an interdimensional invasion began.[11]
One Empire emphasized technology and intelligence while possessing comparatively weak bodies; the other emphasized extreme physical strength while being intellectually inferior. Hostile interdimensional creatures attacked in the middle of the existing conflict. The Soldier's objective was not merely to kill monsters but to pacify both Empires and eliminate the invading threat.[11]
The exercise incorporated ruined cities, armies, civilians, aircraft, combat drones, monsters, battlefield logistics and competing political powers. Each Soldier possessed current Imperial equipment, including adaptable armor, a solar-recharging plasma weapon and a Hell-metal melee weapon containing dangerous Miasma, but no one was given a simple script explaining how to win.[11]
[[Scathach]] deliberately refused to begin their education with ordinary controlled lessons. The Soldiers were expected to experience the chaos of war, learn what responsibility toward civilians and allies meant, understand injury and near-death pressure and reveal how they naturally approached command. The Tower could therefore train not merely individual fighting skill but strategy, leadership, investigation, politics, diplomacy, conquest, judgment and independent decision-making.[11]

### Adaptive Training

#### Counter-Specialization
The Tower identifies the techniques upon which a challenger most heavily depends and creates conditions capable of punishing mindless repetition. Violet's Fire produced resistant enemies and explosive terrain; Sasha's Lightning invited sensory blind spots, non-conductive or inconvenient targets and the possibility of increasingly extreme anti-speed counters.[4][5]
This does not mean the Tower suppresses specialization itself. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]], [[Scathach]] and many of the greatest warriors in the setting possess exceptionally developed specialties. The system instead seeks to prevent the user's strongest ability from becoming the only answer the user knows how to produce.

#### Unpredictable Ability Combinations
Enemy templates can possess abilities that their Race would not normally suggest. Ice Demon-like creatures moved through Lightning; spider-like enemies could employ Fire and plant-based effects; modified [[Nightingale]] beings could possess Draconic characteristics and inherited durability; and Void-derived properties could be attached to creatures specifically to bypass ordinary detection.[2][3][4][5]
The Tower can achieve this because its enemy construction is based upon the information contained within [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s enormous Soul collection rather than being limited to exact historical replicas of the dead.

#### Environmental Counters
Terrain is another adaptive weapon. Narrow passages can make long weapons inconvenient, sunlight can exploit Vampire biology, chemical substances can punish Fire, forests can discourage area destruction and unfamiliar architecture can conceal enemies or traps. The user is expected to analyze the environment before treating it as neutral ground.[2][4][7]

#### Creativity
The Tower accepts unexpected solutions. Sasha carrying mimic weapons through Lightning instead of touching them was not considered a failure of the system; it demonstrated exactly the kind of practical creativity [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] wanted to develop.[5] The same principle applies to choosing appropriate weapons for terrain, creating alternate routes, exploiting environmental information and abandoning an unfavorable battle rather than fighting merely for pride.

### Death and Restoration

#### Realistic Death
Death inside the Family Tower is temporary in outcome but genuine in experience. Users feel wounds, fear, mutilation, suffocation, panic and the sensation of dying. Memories of the death remain after restoration.[2][3]
Removing the pain and psychological realism would undermine the point of the exercise. A challenger who subconsciously knows that nothing can hurt her would gradually stop responding to danger with the same seriousness required in a genuine war. The Tower consequently allows the trainee to know intellectually that resurrection exists while ensuring the body and mind still experience failure as something real.

#### Restoration
After death, the user is reconstructed and returned to continue training. Ruby and Violet repeatedly awakened in their personal starting rooms after fatal encounters, retaining complete awareness of how they had failed.[2][4]
The process allows a mistake that would normally end a warrior's development permanently to become material for the next attempt.

### Soul Nourishment
The Tower's greatest Growth effect operates directly upon the Soul. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] discovered that a Soul approaching death becomes more exposed and displays a greater portion of its latent potential. He exploits this brief state to add extremely small compatible traces of Soul material into the challenger.[3]
This operation is normally extraordinarily difficult because foreign Soul material cannot simply be forced into another Being without consequences. Victor could perform the process on a limited scale because the Souls stored inside him had already passed through and become connected to his own Essence. Even then, the procedure required careful supervision.[3]
Victor personally perceived what the women were experiencing during their final moments while administering the process. Watching and feeling repeated deaths from the people he loved was psychologically painful for him, but he continued because he considered their determination and future survival more important than protecting himself from that discomfort.[3]

#### Ruby's 0.2%
During one early period, Ruby killed hundreds of Tower creatures and approximately 0.2% of their accumulated Soul material entered and nourished her Soul.[3] The value represents the observed result of that particular training period rather than a universal formula stating that every defeated creature permanently transfers exactly 0.2%.
The importance lies in accumulation. An individually tiny improvement can become enormous when repeated across hundreds or thousands of battles, deaths and increasingly difficult floors. Soul quality rises, existing Powers gain a better foundation and latent potential that might otherwise require centuries to develop can emerge considerably faster.

### Divine Concepts and Enlightenment
The later Tower Artifact incorporates an exceptional mixture of Divine Concepts. Divine observers identified Soul, Death, End, Beginnings and Life, together with traces of War, Destruction and Time.[6]
These Concepts correspond closely to its functions: Death and End participate in the fatal stage of its loop; Life and Beginnings contribute to continuation and restoration; Soul governs the principal Growth mechanism; War and Destruction support the training environment; and Time allows immense subjective periods to pass internally.
The Tower can also help provide conditions for Enlightenment toward Divinity.[6] This does not mean entering the Tower automatically grants a Divinity. Divine development remains connected to Soul, identity, understanding and comprehension of a Concept. The Tower instead supplies enormous quantities of experience, pressure and Time within which that understanding can develop.

### Limitations and Unknowns
Despite the amount of information available about its mechanics, the majority of the 777 floors have never been individually shown. There is no confirmed canonical list assigning a fixed theme, environment or boss to every floor. The Tower is explicitly adaptive and randomized, so a scenario experienced by one user does not necessarily define what another user will encounter at the same stage.[2][4]
It has likewise never been established that every floor is physically larger than the previous floor, that every floor contains a complete independent Universe, or that every generated civilization possesses a literally pre-existing cosmological past and future. What is demonstrated is that the Tower can create enormous independent environments, parallel instances and sophisticated civilization-scale simulations.[11]
The autonomous system is highly intelligent in behavior but has not been established as an ordinary independent sentient person. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s own description deliberately treated its nature as similar to sentience while still being something different.[4]
The specifications of the Imperial Nightmare Towers are also incomplete. Their floor counts, maximum enemies, resurrection mechanics, Soul-growth systems and Time ratios should not be copied from the Family Tower unless later material establishes that they share those properties.

The Tower of Challenges, commonly called Babylon, is a 999-floor Imperial Growth Tower created by Victor Elderblood as one of the foundational institutions of the [[Elderblood Empire]]. Unlike the private Tower of Nightmare, which exists primarily to refine members of Victor's family and selected elites through extreme adaptive training, Babylon was created as a public path of advancement for the population of the Empire. A citizen without an exceptional Bloodline, Divine parentage or pre-existing supernatural connection could enter through the Imperial educational system, fight increasingly dangerous enemies, acquire Power and potentially rise far beyond the limitations of their birth.[1]
The original Babylon stands in the center of the Great Imperial City on [[Yggdrasil]]. Its physical Tower is enormous enough to be visible from several kilometers away, while its actual challenge system contains 999 progressively more difficult floors. The lower progression is capable of taking ordinary Humans into the Superhuman realm, while the Tower as a whole extends from encounters with Monsters to battles against Gods. Victor established the theoretical end-point at Floor 999: someone capable of reaching that stage should possess enough Power to fight a Primordial God.[1]
Babylon was never intended to produce only soldiers. Victor considered a civilization made entirely of fighters fundamentally incomplete and explicitly built the institution around the creation of both warriors and scholars. Scientists, doctors, engineers, researchers and other specialists were considered just as necessary for Imperial development as military personnel, which is why access to the original Tower was tied to the Imperial Academy and its broader system of Imperial service.[1]
As the Empire expanded beyond Yggdrasil, Babylon ceased being a single planetary Tower. Copies were constructed on newly developed Imperial worlds and linked into a unified Dimensional network. A challenger entering a Tower on Yggdrasil and another entering from a distant Solar System can reach the same corresponding floor, turning Babylon into shared interplanetary infrastructure. Individual floors within this mature network dynamically alter their dimensions according to the challengers using them and can reach spatial scales ranging from approximately the size of old Earth to the size of a mega-planet.[2]

#### Development

##### Origins
The conceptual foundation of Babylon predates the [[Elderblood Empire]] itself. After centuries of fighting increasingly powerful civilizations, [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] concluded that protecting his family required more than accumulating personal strength. His civilization needed methods through which its ordinary population could continually improve rather than permanently depending upon Gods, exceptional Bloodlines or a small number of elite warriors.
His plans combined technologies and supernatural disciplines that had previously developed separately. The original Tower of Nightmare provided the foundation for artificial training Realities, Velnorah contributed the technological framework of an advanced extraterrestrial civilization, the Elderblood faction's understanding of Runes supplied increasingly sophisticated metaphysical engineering, and Divine craftsmen such as [[Hephaestus]] made it possible to construct artifacts on a scale that ordinary Mortal engineering could not reproduce.[3]
Victor had spent approximately two thousand subjective years considering the larger plans that eventually became foundations of his Empire. Babylon therefore was not an isolated structure invented on the day the Empire was proclaimed; it was the public implementation of ideas developed through the Tower of Nightmare and the wider civilizational program surrounding Victor's future wars.[3]

##### Public Trial System
The intended function became more explicit as Velnorah and [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s family discussed transforming the principles of the Nightmare Tower into a place of trials for Mortals. The objective was to allow ordinary Humans to cease being helpless members of one of the physically weakest [[Races (Flamel)|Races]] and obtain a genuine opportunity to become something greater.[4]
The proposed system was not built upon contempt for Humanity. [[Scathach]]'s own [[Martial Arts]] were heavily influenced by techniques created by Humans who had been forced to compensate for weak bodies through intelligence, innovation and efficiency. Victor and Velnorah instead wanted to exploit precisely those characteristics—adaptability and creativity—while providing the population with a framework capable of overcoming the biological disadvantages that prevented ordinary Humans from competing with naturally supernatural Races.[4]
The Tower was also intended to identify exceptional individuals rather than assuming that talent could be predicted entirely from ancestry. The integrated population would provide the enormous numerical base necessary for future wars, while individuals whose talent, intelligence, adaptability or strength distinguished them could rise into increasingly important positions. Victor's civilization would therefore have a method of discovering exceptional people who otherwise might have lived and died without ever receiving an opportunity to develop their potential.[4]
This system also solved a practical military problem. Velnorah considered producing billions of specially engineered clones unnecessarily inefficient. The Empire would already possess enormous populations on Earth and on civilizations incorporated later; those populations could be trained and developed instead of being left strategically useless. Clone armies and engineered elites could then occupy more specialized positions rather than replacing the civilization's entire population.[4]

##### Planned Rewards
The developmental version of the public trial system included more than simple increases in raw Power. Race transformation was explicitly discussed as one possible reward. An ordinary Human could potentially obtain the opportunity to become a stronger supernatural Race while retaining the mentality and adaptability developed as a Human.[4]
Changing Race was never intended to be mandatory. A Human capable of progressing without transformation could remain Human, and a racial upgrade by itself would not automatically create an elite warrior. The new body still needed to be understood, trained and integrated into the challenger's fighting style. This preserved the central principle behind the project: Babylon was supposed to cultivate capable individuals rather than distribute Power without effort.[4]
Weaker Divine Artifacts usable by Mortals were also proposed as Tower rewards during development.[4] The operational Babylon shown after the foundation of the Empire is directly confirmed to grant Power through progression and the defeat of Monsters, but the exact completed reward table has never been exhaustively revealed. Race changes and Mortal-usable Divine Artifacts should therefore be treated as confirmed parts of the developmental reward design rather than assuming that every such reward necessarily appears at a specific currently known floor.

#### Foundation of Babylon
Babylon was formally established on the first day of the [[Imperial Calendar]], immediately after [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] unified Earth beneath the [[Elderblood Empire]]. The old continental structure was reshaped into one great continent, national borders ceased functioning as independent political boundaries, an Imperial Language was established throughout the population and Earth itself was renamed [[Yggdrasil]] in honor of its World Tree.[1]
Victor simultaneously created the Great Imperial City and erected Babylon at its center. The Tower rose high enough above the capital to be visible from several kilometers away and became one of the most recognizable structures of the newly unified civilization.[1]
Its formal name was:
Tower of Challenges, Babylon.[1]
The institution immediately created something that Earth's previous supernatural order had never provided on a comparable scale: a standardized public route from ordinary Mortal weakness toward genuine supernatural Power.

#### Purpose

##### Universal Advancement
Babylon's most important social function is to provide a path upward for people who were not fortunate enough to be born into powerful families. Before the Empire, extraordinary supernatural potential depended heavily upon circumstances of birth. A person might be born a Human without [[Magic]] while another entered existence as a Vampire, Werewolf, Demon, Dragon, Spirit, [[Demigod]] or descendant of an ancient supernatural Clan.
Babylon makes those starting conditions less absolute. Even a mediocre Human can enter the progression system, defeat Monsters, become stronger and gradually obtain Superhuman Power.[1] The Tower does not guarantee that every challenger will become exceptional, but it ensures that lack of supernatural ancestry is no longer an absolute barrier preventing someone from attempting to rise.

##### Warriors and Scholars
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] deliberately rejected the idea that strengthening civilization meant producing nothing except fighters. The Tower was established to help create powerful warriors and scholars, while the Academy system surrounding it trained the wider range of specialists required by an advanced civilization.[1]
The Empire still required doctors to preserve Life, engineers to construct infrastructure, researchers to create new technologies, scientists to understand newly encountered phenomena, administrators to govern expanding territories and countless other professions that could not be replaced simply by producing another powerful warrior. Babylon therefore belongs to a broader Imperial educational system rather than existing only as a military dungeon.
The novel has not yet described specialized scholarly floors in sufficient detail to assign individual subjects or curricula to them. The confirmed point is that the institution was created as part of an Academy intended to develop both military and non-military contributors to the Empire.[1]

##### Selection of Talent
Babylon naturally acts as a talent-selection mechanism. Individuals who adapt successfully to progressively harder floors separate themselves from those who rely only upon favorable circumstances. This principle was already part of the original design, where Violet summarized the project as a way for genuinely talented people to distinguish themselves and potentially rise into the Empire's elite classes.[4]
The system therefore combines social mobility with practical selection. Status inherited at birth can provide an advantage, but progress through the Tower requires the challenger to actually use what they possess.

#### Imperial Academy
The original Babylon was accessed through the Imperial Academy. Citizens who entered the Academy as cadets could pursue military or scholarly careers and obtain access to the Tower as part of their advancement.[1]
This relationship is important because Babylon was not originally presented as an unrestricted amusement facility where anyone could wander inside without responsibility. Entering the Tower was connected to participation within the institutions of the Empire. A person seeking Power gained an opportunity to develop, while the civilization gained warriors, researchers and specialists capable of contributing that development back to society.
The enormous desire for Power among people who had previously lived without supernatural advantages caused immediate interest. Young Humans familiar with games and fantasy stories recognized the Tower's progression structure almost instantly and enrolled in the Academy in large numbers. The possibility that an ordinary person could enter a supernatural Tower and emerge with powers previously reserved for hidden [[Races (Flamel)|Races]] or Divine champions was enough to transform Imperial recruitment.[1]
The precise admission requirements of the later interplanetary Babylon network have not been individually described. The original [[Yggdrasil]] Tower explicitly required Imperial Academy enrollment, while later planetary copies are described more broadly as institutions placed throughout Imperial territory to encourage citizens to surpass their limits.[1][2]

The Tower is described as suitable for encounters ranging from Monsters to fighting Gods.[1] The exact numerical floor at which each broad supernatural tier begins has never been given, and no canonical table currently identifies specific level ranges for Vampire-, Demon-, God-, God-King- or other classifications. #### Growth System

##### Monster-Based Advancement
Babylon converts combat progression into genuine supernatural development. An ordinary Human can kill Monsters within the Tower and become stronger, eventually obtaining Superhuman Power despite beginning without a connection to Gods or powerful supernatural families.[1]
This differs fundamentally from an ordinary training facility. The challenger is not merely improving technique through repetition; successful Tower progression can alter the practical level of Power the individual possesses.
The precise conversion system between defeated enemies and acquired Power has not yet been fully explained. No universal experience-point formula, fixed statistical interface or numerical reward table has been shown. The effect should therefore be described through the canonical result—defeating Tower enemies allows challengers to grow—without inventing game mechanics not provided by the story.

##### Adaptability
Adaptability was one of the qualities [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] and Velnorah specifically intended the public trial system to cultivate. Humans historically survived despite their weak natural bodies because they compensated with tools, tactics, cooperation and innovation. The Tower was intended to place challengers under sufficient pressure for those characteristics to remain relevant even after the individual obtained a stronger Race or supernatural abilities.[4]
A Race Change therefore was not viewed as the end of development. Someone who became a Vampire, Werewolf or another supernatural species would still need to learn how to use the new body effectively and integrate those capabilities with the mentality developed before the transformation.

##### Creativity
Creativity was treated as equally important. [[Scathach]]'s [[Martial Arts]] themselves demonstrated what Human ingenuity could achieve when weak bodies forced generations of warriors to find methods capable of threatening physically superior opponents. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] wanted this problem-solving instinct preserved rather than producing a population that relied entirely upon newly gained raw Power.[4]
Babylon's larger purpose was consequently evolutionary in both the biological and social sense: provide difficult circumstances, give people a reason to surpass themselves, identify those capable of adapting and then allow successful individuals to become increasingly valuable members of the Empire.

#### Rewards

##### Power Growth
The most consistently demonstrated reward is direct progression in Power. Ordinary Humans who survived and advanced through Babylon became Superhuman, proving that the Tower could elevate individuals who previously possessed no meaningful supernatural advantage.[1]

##### Race Change
Race transformation formed a major part of the developmental design inherited from the earlier public-Tower project. Humans could potentially receive opportunities to leave their original biological limitations and enter stronger supernatural [[Races (Flamel)|Races]].[4]
Transformation remained optional. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction did not consider Humanity worthless, and challengers capable of progressing as Humans were not required to abandon their Race. The reward existed because natural Human physiology possessed severe limitations compared with species such as Vampires, [[Werewolves]] and Dragons.

##### Divine Artifacts
Weak Divine Artifacts usable by Mortals were also planned as possible rewards for the public trial system.[4] These were not intended to be artifacts comparable to the highest treasures wielded by [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s family, but equipment that could allow successful Mortals to continue progressing beyond what ordinary mundane weapons permitted.
The exact completed distribution system has not been revealed. There is no canonical list identifying which Babylon floor awards a Race Change, which floor distributes a Divine Artifact or how often such rewards appear. Those developmental rewards should therefore not be assigned to specific floors without additional evidence.

#### Death

##### Permanent Consequences
The most important difference between Babylon and the Family Tower of Nightmare is its treatment of death. Ordinary challengers who die inside Babylon are not automatically restored by the Tower.[1]
The decision was deliberate. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] gave three principal reasons for refusing to provide unrestricted resurrection to the public Tower.
First, the possibility of genuine death creates consequences. A person who knows that every reckless mistake will simply be erased can begin treating danger carelessly, while a challenger who understands that failure can end their current life has a powerful incentive to think, prepare and avoid unnecessary errors.[1]
Second, automatic resurrection is treated as an exceptional Imperial privilege rather than a basic entitlement. Victor was willing to provide such protection to elite personnel sent into missions with extreme mortality rates, where those people risked their lives directly for the Empire. A citizen voluntarily entering Babylon in pursuit of personal growth did not automatically receive the same privilege.[1]
Third, Babylon existed alongside Victor's developing System of Life and Death. Physical death within the Empire was not intended to represent abandonment. Souls would enter the afterlife systems controlled by the God-Emperor and could eventually proceed toward reincarnation. Victor wanted death to become part of an organized metaphysical cycle rather than something the population regarded solely as meaningless annihilation.[1]

##### Babylon and Reincarnation
A challenger who dies in Babylon may eventually participate in the Empire's broader [[Reincarnation System]], but this should not be confused with resurrection inside the Tower. Babylon itself does not reset the dead challenger at the beginning of a floor in the manner of the Family Nightmare Tower.

During the Great Acceleration, the [[Elderblood Empire]] spread across its mother galaxy and established populations upon increasing numbers of worlds. Babylon expanded with the civilization. Each newly developed planet received a Tower of Challenges modeled upon the original structure.[2]
These Towers were not isolated copies running unrelated progression systems. They became nodes in one enormous interconnected network.

##### Shared Floors
A challenger entering Babylon on [[Yggdrasil]] and another entering a Tower located in a distant Solar System can encounter one another upon the same corresponding floor.[2] The physical entrance used by the challenger therefore does not determine an isolated copy of the internal trial environment.

Shared access created another engineering problem: Imperial citizens no longer possessed roughly comparable physical dimensions. Only a small number of numerical Babylon floors have received explicit canonical significance. The beginning of Babylon is accessible to ordinary Mortals. Its early challenge structure includes Monsters whose defeat allows a previously mediocre Human to obtain greater strength and eventually manifest Superhuman Power.[1]
The precise first-floor environment, first boss, reward table and number of floors required to become Superhuman have never been stated.

During the accelerated Imperial era, the first warrior in the Empire successfully passed Floor 500. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] and his Empresses regarded the achievement as important enough to be personally present through their avatars when it occurred.[2]
The warrior's identity, Race, exact Power and the challenge contained on Floor 500 were not revealed. Its importance is historical: an institution created on [[Yggdrasil]] for recently unified Humans had eventually produced a citizen capable of advancing beyond half of a 999-floor progression whose final level was intended for Primordial-God-class combatants.

Floor 999 is Babylon's highest known benchmark. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] stated that a person capable of reaching it should theoretically possess the Power necessary to fight a Primordial God.[1]
This does not establish that the floor itself contains one particular named Primordial God, that completing it grants Primordial status, or that every challenger who reaches it becomes equal to every member of that category. [[Primordial Gods]] vary enormously in actual strength. The statement establishes the intended general combat ceiling of Babylon's progression.

#### Cultural Impact

##### Rise of a Warrior Culture
Babylon dramatically altered the culture of the [[Elderblood Empire]]. Once Power ceased being something reserved almost entirely for fortunate Bloodlines, Gods and hidden supernatural families, ordinary citizens gained a visible path through which personal determination could produce genuine supernatural advancement.
During the Empire's Galactic expansion, the widespread Tower network helped transform Imperial society into a culture of warriors.[2] Citizens were surrounded by institutions encouraging them to challenge limits, improve their bodies and abilities and regard stagnation as something that could be actively overcome.
This warrior culture was not identical to societies that treated combatants as the only people of value. Imperial civilization continued emphasizing that researchers, scientists, engineers, administrators, artisans and other specialists all had roles that contributed to the Emperor's civilization.[2] Strength was respected, but contribution remained broader than combat alone.

##### Social Mobility
Babylon reduces the importance of birth as the sole determinant of supernatural status. A person without Divine ancestry may enter the Academy, challenge the Tower, acquire Power and reveal enough talent to attract greater opportunities.
The system does not erase natural advantages. A child born to Gods, Dragons or ancient supernatural families can possess vastly greater initial potential than an ordinary Human. Babylon instead prevents those advantages from becoming the only available route toward relevance.

##### Competition
The successes of early challengers encouraged others to continue. Humans who saw ordinary people emerge from Babylon with Superhuman Power became increasingly motivated by ambition, greed, envy and the desire not to remain weak.[1] [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] did not regard those emotions as inherently useless. Properly directed competition could become another force encouraging development.

##### Imperial Identity
As Babylon spread onto new worlds, it became more than a building belonging to [[Yggdrasil]]. Every Tower connected distant populations to the same internal challenge network, meaning citizens living in separate planetary cultures still participated within one shared Imperial institution.[2]
A warrior from the cradle world and an explorer from a distant Solar System could literally encounter one another inside Babylon. The Tower therefore helped reinforce the concept that Imperial identity extended beyond planet, Race or local origin.

#### Relationship with the Tower of Nightmare
Babylon and the Tower of Nightmare share technological and philosophical ancestry but should not be treated as two names for the same Artifact. The Family Tower was built to subject selected users to repeated death, resurrection, Soul nourishment and adaptive scenarios under tightly controlled conditions. Babylon was built for civilization-scale development and deliberately preserves genuine mortality.

#### Relationship with the Imperial Nightmare Tower
Babylon should also be distinguished from the Imperial Nightmare Tower referenced during the training of the Original Emperor's Soldiers. The distinction is especially important because the Nightmare systems are associated with controlled resurrection and specialized simulation training, while Babylon intentionally allows genuine death. A statement concerning one should not automatically be used as evidence for the other.

#### Civilizational Significance
Babylon is one of the clearest expressions of [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s approach to Empire-building. He does not consider a civilization strong merely because its ruler is overwhelmingly powerful. If the population beneath that ruler remains helpless, then the civilization's apparent strength disappears the moment its supreme protector is absent.
The Tower attacks that weakness directly. Ordinary people can become supernatural, talented people can reveal themselves through performance rather than ancestry, newly integrated populations can be cultivated into useful citizens and military personnel, and planetary colonies can participate in the same Growth system regardless of astronomical distance.[4][1][2]
Its expansion also mirrors the development of the Empire itself. Babylon began as one gigantic Tower standing at the center of a newly unified planetary civilization. Tens of thousands of years later, copies existed throughout the mother galaxy and were connected through shared dimensional floors large enough to contain planetary-scale environments.[2]
The achievement of the first warrior to pass Floor 500 demonstrated that the project had succeeded beyond simply giving weak Humans basic Superhuman abilities. Imperial citizens were climbing into regions of the Tower ultimately designed to culminate at a level where even [[Primordial Gods]] became relevant opponents.

#### Trivia
- Babylon was formally erected on Imperial Year 1, Day 1, the same day Earth was unified beneath [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] and renamed [[Yggdrasil]].[1]
- The original Tower stands at the center of the Great Imperial City and can be seen from several kilometers away.[1]
- Babylon possesses 999 floors.[1]
- Its challenge range was described as extending from Monsters to fighting Gods.[1]
- A challenger reaching Floor 999 should theoretically possess enough Power to fight a Primordial God.[1]
- Ordinary Humans can obtain Superhuman Power by successfully progressing through the Tower.[1]
- Many early challengers died, but the visible success of survivors encouraged more citizens to enter.[1]
- Babylon does not automatically resurrect ordinary challengers because Victor intentionally preserved consequences for failure.[1]
- Victor reserved automatic resurrection privileges primarily for elite personnel serving in missions with exceptionally high mortality rates.[1]
- The public-Tower project originally considered optional Race Changes and weaker Divine Artifacts suitable for Mortal users as rewards.[4]
- Race Change was intended to be optional rather than mandatory; Humans could remain Human if they wished.[4]
- The developmental philosophy emphasized Human adaptability and creativity rather than treating Humanity as inherently worthless.[4]
- The first warrior to pass Floor 500 became an important Imperial historical milestone witnessed by Victor and his Empresses.[2]
- The identity of that warrior has not yet been revealed.
- Copies of Babylon were eventually placed on newly developed Imperial planets and connected into a single shared network.[2]
- Challengers entering from separate Solar Systems can meet on the same corresponding floor.[2]
- Individual floors can expand from approximately old-Earth size to mega-planet scale.[2]
- Babylon became one of the institutions responsible for turning the mature [[Elderblood Empire]] into a widespread warrior culture without eliminating respect for scientists, researchers, craftsmen and other non-combat professions.[2]
- Babylon, the Family Tower of Nightmare and the Imperial Nightmare Towers share technological ancestry but are separate systems with different purposes and confirmed mechanics.
