The Core of the Afterlife is a generalized term for the metaphysical Core or administrative node through which an individual afterlife Realm interacts with the greater system governing Souls, judgment, Heaven, Hell and reincarnation. The clearest directly observed example is the Core of the [[Greek Underworld]], an enormous sphere located at the heart of Greek Hell through which countless Souls continuously flow.[1]
The Core itself should not be confused with the entire afterlife system. Heaven, Paradise, Hell, Underworlds, Soul judgment and reincarnation belong to a much larger Primordial infrastructure. Chapter 629 establishes that both Heaven and Hell exist fundamentally to process and recycle Souls, while Chapter 674 explains that [[The Judges of The Abyss]] and [[The Universal Tree]] cooperate in maintaining the larger machinery of Souls, Life and reincarnation.[2][3]
The Greek Core is therefore best understood as a local interface into a universal system rather than the source of that system. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] explicitly described it as a "door" through which deeper layers of the Soul System could be accessed when the proper Authorities were present.[1]
Although Heaven participates in the same overall Soul cycle, the novel has not directly shown or named an equivalent spherical Core of Heaven. The term Core of the Afterlife on this page is therefore an encyclopedic umbrella term: the physically demonstrated Core belongs to Underworld/Hell infrastructure, while the article documents the complete Heaven–Hell–judgment–reincarnation system to which that Core is connected.

### Overview
The afterlife of the native Universe is not simply a collection of unrelated religious destinations. Beneath the cultural forms of different Pantheons exists a larger metaphysical system responsible for moving Souls from Life through death, judgment, afterlife processing and eventually reincarnation.
At the broadest level, the system involves:
- [[The Judges of The Abyss]], who govern Souls, judgment and the administration of Hell and Paradise;
- The Universal Tree, who governs Life, reincarnation and the continuation of the cycle;
- local Heavens, Paradises, Hells and Underworlds;
- legitimate Rulers who administer individual Soul Zones;
- the [[Reincarnation System]];
- and local interfaces such as the Core of the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]].[2][4][3][1]

### Nature
The Core is a piece of metaphysical infrastructure rather than a conventional Artifact, Energy generator or naturally occurring celestial object.
When [[Victor Elderblood]] examined the [[Greek Underworld]]'s Core through his evolved perception, he recognized that it had been created by the Primordials so that the Soul System could function.[1] He also recognized something fundamentally similar within his own Hell, demonstrating that the Greek Core was not a completely unique phenomenon.
The physical and cultural appearance of individual afterlives can differ enormously, but the underlying system is more standardized than those differences suggest. Greek Hell, [[Biblical Hell]] and other Underworld traditions can possess different landscapes, punishments, Gods, hierarchies and religious symbolism while still performing equivalent functions within the greater cycle of Souls.[2][1]
The Core is therefore best classified as an operational node between a local Soul Zone and the deeper Primordial system.

### Origin
The novel has not identified one specific Primordial Being as the sole creator of every Afterlife Core.
Chapter 811 states more broadly that the Core was created by the Primordials for the Soul System to function.[1] Chapter 674 provides greater context for the overall system, identifying [[The Judges of The Abyss]] and [[The Universal Tree]] as the two Primordial powers most directly involved with Souls, judgment, Life and reincarnation.[3]
The Judges are responsible for Souls and the administration of Hell and Paradise, while the Universal Tree is responsible for Life and reincarnation. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] describes them as two cosmic administrators whose systems work together to maintain the larger cycle.[3]

### The Primordial Soul System

#### The Judges of the Abyss
Main article: [[The Judges of The Abyss]]
The Judges of The Abyss are the Primordial Entity most directly associated with Souls. Their responsibilities extend considerably beyond simply deciding whether somebody was morally good or evil.
Chapter 629 explains that much of their ordinary work is more accurately described as separating and routing Souls rather than personally conducting a courtroom-style judgment for every deceased Being.[2]
Chapter 632 further reveals that the Judges are also involved in the creation of Souls. They explain that the force of Life creates the living form while they provide that form with its Soul.[4]
Their responsibilities therefore include:
- Soul creation;
- Soul judgment;
- separation of Soul components;
- routing Souls toward appropriate afterlives;
- administration of Hell and Paradise;
- supervision of powerful supernatural Souls;
- and cooperation with Life and reincarnation to keep the greater cycle functioning.[2][4][3]

#### The Universal Tree
Main article: [[The Universal Tree]]
The Universal Tree represents the complementary Life-side of the system.
Where the Judges administer Souls and afterlife processing, the Universal Tree is responsible for Life, reincarnation and the continuation of the cycle after Soul processing is complete.[2][3]
The two systems cannot be treated as completely independent. Souls must eventually return toward Life, while new Life requires Souls.

#### Local Rulers
Local Kings, Queens and Divine administrators do not create the universal Soul System.
Their purpose is to administer particular portions of it.
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] explains in Chapter 674 that Rulers of Hell and [[God-Kings]] effectively operate beneath the greater Soul/Life administration while retaining considerable autonomy so long as their Realm continues performing its required function.[3]
If an afterlife Realm ceases functioning properly for too long, the consequences are therefore more serious than the collapse of an ordinary kingdom. Victor states that the Universal Tree and Judges can destroy a failed Hell rather than allow a broken Soul Zone to continue disrupting the larger cycle.[3]

### Heaven and Hell

#### Shared Purpose
One of the most important revelations concerning the afterlife occurs when Nyx explains the system to [[Persephone]].
Hell and Heaven are not metaphysical opposites whose only purposes are punishment and reward. Their deeper function is the same:
Both exist to recycle Souls.[2]
Their methods differ, but both eventually return processed Souls toward reincarnation.

#### Heaven and Paradise
A Heaven or Paradise receives the positive portion of a Soul, or in the case of some stronger Beings, the complete Soul judged appropriate for that destination.[2]
The experience inside Heaven is not necessarily permanent. Nyx explains that when the Soul has become "satisfied" with its personal Heaven, it eventually leaves that Realm and returns to the reincarnation cycle.[2]
Different Pantheons implement this function differently.
The Norse system provides one example. Beings who die with honor may enter Valhalla. If they later die again there, they can proceed directly toward reincarnation rather than undergoing the same process as ordinary dead.[2]
The term Paradise is therefore a broad category rather than one single universal dimension.

#### Hell and Underworld
Hell receives negative Soul components, sinners and other Souls assigned to infernal processing.[2]
A Soul entering Hell may undergo punishment, purification, recycling or another process appropriate to the rules of that specific [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]].
Non-sentient Souls can also pass through Hell. Chapter 629 explicitly states that even plants and mountains possess Souls and that these non-sentient Souls can enter the infernal system before being automatically recycled back into the cycle of Life.[2]
Individual Hells can possess dramatically different rules, but their cosmological work remains fundamentally similar.

### Universality of Souls
The Soul System is not limited to Humans.
Chapter 629 establishes that essentially everything within Creation can possess a Soul, including:
- Humans;
- supernatural Beings;
- animals;
- plants;
- mountains;
- and other non-sentient existences.[2]
This means the afterlife infrastructure is not simply a religious institution constructed for intelligent species.
It is part of the ecological and metaphysical circulation of existence itself.
For ordinary non-sentient Souls, most processing occurs automatically. A King of Hell is not expected to personally judge every dead tree, animal or mountain fragment entering the system.[2]

#### Powerful Supernatural Souls
Powerful supernatural Souls are treated differently.
Nyx explains that strong supernatural Beings may be sent directly to Heaven or Hell rather than being divided in the same manner as ordinary Mortals.[2]
She theorizes that sufficiently powerful Souls possess such strong [[Will Solace|Will]] and structural integrity that dividing them carelessly could damage the Soul beyond what the reincarnation cycle can properly use. This explanation is explicitly presented as Nyx's theory rather than a fully confirmed universal law.[2]

#### Gods and Higher Existences
Gods can receive even more direct Primordial judgment.
For a sufficiently powerful deceased God, the Judges may determine that the Being:
- ceases existing in its previous form;
- returns directly toward reincarnation;
- or receives another fate according to the judgment.[2]
Their treatment is therefore not identical to that of an ordinary Mortal Soul.

#### Non-Sentient Souls
Non-sentient Souls generally enter an automated process.
Plants, animals, mountains and similar existences do not require individualized moral judgment. Their Souls can pass through Hell and re-enter the reincarnation cycle automatically.[2]

### The Core Itself

#### Appearance
The Core of the [[Greek Underworld]] appears as an enormous sphere positioned deep within the heart of the Realm.[1]
Countless Souls move continuously through and around the structure, forming immense streams of spiritual traffic.
Its appearance reflects its function: it is not an inactive object stored inside the Underworld but an operational component through which vast quantities of Soul activity constantly pass.
Even [[Aphrodite]] Elderblood had never personally seen the Core before [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s visit despite her status as an ancient and influential Greek Goddess.[1]

#### Restricted Location
The region containing the Greek Core was normally accessible only to the legitimate King and Queen of the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]].[1]
This restriction demonstrates that the Core is not merely another location inside Hell.
Access is tied to the Authority required to administer the Realm itself.

#### Metaphysical Composition
The exact material composition of the Core has never been identified.
Nothing suggests it is built from ordinary matter or functions through conventional machinery.
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s perception identifies it through its relationship with the greater system rather than through a physical material, making metaphysical infrastructure the most accurate classification presently available.

### Functions

#### Local Soul Flow
The most directly observable function of the Core is its relationship with the massive Soul traffic moving through the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]].[1]
It serves as one of the principal local points through which the Realm's Soul activity is organized.

#### Soul-System Integration
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] states that the Core was created specifically so the greater Soul System could function.[1]
Its function therefore extends beyond simply storing deceased Souls.
It participates in:
- local Soul circulation;
- afterlife administration;
- the connection between legitimate Rulers and their Realm;
- Soul recycling;
- and interaction with deeper layers of the greater Soul System.[2][1]

#### Ruler Interface
A legitimate Ruler possesses a privileged relationship with their afterlife Realm.
When [[Persephone]] sought to become the true Ruler of the [[Greek Underworld]], merely possessing political influence was insufficient. [[Hades]]' Helm contained the Authority necessary to Rule Greek Hell, and accepting that Authority forced her to undergo a test administered by the Soul System.[2][4]
Passing that test connected Persephone to the system managing the Underworld's Souls.
The Core later became the location through which that relationship could be actively used.[1]

#### Gateway to the Greater System
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s most important statement concerning the Core is that the sphere was not merely the Core of Hell.
It was also a door into the entire system.[1]
This does not mean that touching the Core automatically grants unrestricted control over Souls, reincarnation or universal information.
Victor required several compatible Authorities simultaneously before he could exploit the connection safely.

[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] explains in Chapter 811 that a structure analogous to the Akashic Records exists within Creation and contains the records of events occurring throughout the Universe.[1]

### Adonis Snow's Echo
The most extraordinary demonstrated use of the Greek Core occurred during [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s attempt to allow his family to interact with [[Adonis Snow]] again.
Adonis' original Soul had already merged with Victor, meaning simply extracting or resurrecting that Soul would have required damaging Victor's own existence.[1]
Victor therefore used another method.
Through the Core and the surrounding system, he accessed Adonis' historical Record from before the merger and temporarily projected that information back into Reality.[1]
Victor explicitly stated that this was not Time Travel.
The result could be described as:
- a memory;
- a trace;
- an echo of the past;
- a recording;
- or, most accurately, a living and sentient recording.[1]
The manifested Adonis could interact with others, but he was not the original deceased Soul being conventionally returned from death.
This event demonstrates two separate functions:
1. the Core can connect authorized users to deeper Soul-System infrastructure;
2. that infrastructure intersects with universal records containing historical information about deceased individuals.

### Death and Identity
The Soul System establishes that physical death has consequences even in a setting where resurrection abilities exist.
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] summarizes this principle while speaking with Agnes:
"When you die, you always lose something."[1]
Once the greater Soul System begins processing an individual, the original identity can be altered through:
- Soul separation;
- punishment;
- purification;
- memory removal;
- information removal;
- recycling;
- reincarnation;
- or complete destruction of the previous identity.[2][1][5]
A future Being produced from the same Soul substrate is therefore not automatically the same person who originally died.

#### Agnes' Example
Agnes argued that dying should not matter because [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] ruled Hell and could simply return her afterward.
Victor rejected this assumption.[1]
As a powerful supernatural Being, Agnes would fall under the jurisdiction of the greater Soul System rather than simply becoming property of Victor's local Hell.
If her Soul were processed and divided, the negative portion eventually reaching Victor's Hell would no longer necessarily be Agnes as an intact individual. It could become a new existence constructed from that portion of her Soul.[1]
His rule over Hell therefore did not guarantee perfect resurrection.

### Reincarnation
Main article: [[Reincarnation System]]
After Souls complete the relevant afterlife processes, they eventually return toward Life through the Reincarnation System.

#### Identity Erasure
Chapter 1021 reveals that reincarnation normally involves removing the previous individual's information from the Soul.[5]
This can include:
- memories;
- identity;
- experiences;
- and other information defining the former individual.
The Primordials deliberately prevent reincarnated Beings from normally retaining complete memories of previous lives.
Powerful supernatural Souls receive particularly high priority during this process because reincarnating an extremely powerful Being with its entire former personality, knowledge and experience intact could create major complications.[5]

#### Soul Substrate and Personal Identity
The fate of [[Vanessa Alekerth]] demonstrates that the Soul itself and the information defining a person are related but distinct.
When [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] negotiated with the Judges for Vanessa's Soul, most of the information that made the Soul Vanessa Alekerth had already been erased in preparation for reincarnation.[5]
The Soul still existed, but reincarnating it normally would have produced a new person.
The distinction can be simplified as:

Victor circumvented this process by using the remaining connection between the Soul and Vanessa's historical Record to restore the erased information through his access to the Records.[5]
This was an extraordinary intervention and not the normal function of the reincarnation cycle.

#### Reincarnation Conditions
Chapter 1021 also reveals that the [[Reincarnation System]] does not simply insert Souls anywhere at random.
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] states that ordinary reincarnation depends upon there being enough Beings available for a Soul to reincarnate on the same planet associated with its previous life.[5]
Large demographic collapses can therefore produce queues of Souls waiting for appropriate reincarnation opportunities.
This is one reason the system requires large-scale administration rather than functioning as a simple instantaneous resurrection mechanism.

### Rulers of the Afterlife

#### Authority to Rule
Legitimate rulership of an [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] is metaphysical, not merely political.
A conqueror can occupy an afterlife dimension without necessarily possessing the Authority required to administer its Soul functions.
[[Persephone]]'s succession illustrates this distinction. [[Hades]]' Helm carried the Authority to Rule Greek Hell, and Persephone had to pass the Judges' test before gaining complete recognition from the system.[2][4]

#### The Ruler's Test
The test required [[Persephone]] to demonstrate that she could:
- abandon simplistic Mortal prejudices;
- evaluate context;
- judge impartially;
- avoid favoritism;
- make decisions involving people emotionally important to her;
- and understand that administering Souls required standards different from ordinary Mortal morality.[2][4]
The test was not measuring raw power.
It was determining whether Persephone possessed the judgment necessary to become an authorized administrator of a system affecting innumerable Souls.

#### Rulers Are Administrators, Not Owners
Even a legitimate Ruler does not possess unrestricted ownership of every Soul entering their Realm.
The greater Primordial system retains authority over powerful Souls and over the larger reincarnation cycle.[2][1]
A local Ruler therefore functions more like an administrator with privileged access than the absolute creator of the afterlife system.

### Reorganization of Earth's Hells
The afterlife infrastructure is capable of being reorganized when its administrators consider existing Soul Zones inefficient.
By Chapter 805, the Judges were already discussing reforming Earth's infernal dimensions, including the planned consolidation of Greek and [[Biblical Hell]].[7]
[[The Universal Tree]] and Judges later reorganized Earth's Hells rather than preserving every historical infernal dimension as a separate system.[8]
Greek Hell was merged with Biblical Hell as part of this restructuring, while other Hells were consolidated into a smaller number of major Soul Zones.
This demonstrates that individual afterlife Realms are not cosmologically sacred in their original cultural forms.
What matters to the Primordial administrators is that the Soul-processing function continues to operate correctly.
A Realm can therefore be:
- merged;
- expanded;
- reassigned;
- restructured;
- or, if it becomes useless or dangerous to the greater cycle, potentially destroyed.[3][7][8]

### Victor's Afterlife System
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] eventually developed one of the most unusual local implementations of the Soul System.

#### Merged Hell
After the restructuring of the infernal Realms, [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s Hell incorporated the former Biblical and Greek systems into a much larger unified [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]].[8]
The previous individual rulership positions were consequently no longer sufficient by themselves. Victor later reinforced Aline and [[Persephone]] as Rulers of the newly merged Hell rather than merely Rulers of the former Biblical and Greek systems.[9]

#### Elvenorah and Hell
By Chapter 1100, [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s afterlife had evolved beyond a simple Hell.
Elvenorah and Hell, together with the Cities of Hell, functioned as a connected system for the Souls of Beings who worshipped the Gods of the [[Dragons Nest Pantheon]].[6]
Elvenorah could function as the Pantheon's Divine or Heaven-facing territory, while Hell handled the infernal side.
Victor's private family world was treated separately as his personal Paradise and was not intended to function as an unrestricted public afterlife.[6]

Violet consequently considered complete independence from Primordial access an unresolved problem.
The novel had not yet established at that point that Victor had successfully removed the entire public afterlife system from Primordial jurisdiction.

### Imperial Afterlife Expansion
The greatest practical expansion of [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s afterlife infrastructure occurred during the accelerated development of the [[Elderblood Empire]].
The Empire eventually contained hundreds of billions and later vastly greater populations, making the older Soul-processing infrastructure inadequate. The Volume 9 chronology identifies this as one of the first serious administrative problems created by Victor's enormous temporal-development ritual. Hell, reincarnation and the existing [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] cities were no longer large or efficient enough to process the number of citizens dying throughout the expanding civilization.[10]
[[Persephone Elderblood]] and [[Lilith Elderblood]] reported that:
- the reincarnation cycle lacked sufficient capacity;
- the Underworld cities were becoming overcrowded;
- Souls were remaining within the Underworld too long;
- and the Empire required a substantially more efficient system.[10]
The problem did not arise because the Empire was constantly at war.
Deaths still occurred through:
- natural aging;
- accidents;
- combat training;
- the Tower of Challenges;
- interpersonal violence;
- and the ordinary dangers accompanying a civilization of enormous size.[10]
Victor and his family could theoretically resurrect many citizens, but deliberately rejected universal immortality.
The reason was structural: Life needed to retain a beginning, continuation and end.[10]
A civilization in which everyone remained permanently alive would eventually stagnate, while the cycle of birth, death and reincarnation allowed new generations, new experiences and continued social development.

#### New Afterlife Regions
The Imperial family therefore expanded the afterlife beyond a single overcrowded Hell.
The later system included separate functions and regions for:
- sinners and Demonic Souls;
- Soul evaluation;
- worthy dead;
- temporary Soul residence;
- reincarnation preparation;
- and the processing of exceptional Souls.[10]
Creating additional dimensions was relatively easy for Beings at [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s level.
The difficult part was properly controlling Soul ownership, routing and reincarnation without disrupting the deeper cosmic system.
Victor consequently became directly involved because his Authority over Souls exceeded that of most of the Gods responsible for the local afterlife administration.[10]

By Chapter 1139, death and reincarnation had become integrated directly into Elderblood Imperial administration.[11]

### Relationship with World Trees
Main article: [[World Trees]]
World Trees are closely associated with planetary Life and the spiritual infrastructure of developed civilizations.
Their broader relationship with afterlife dimensions helps explain why the Universal Tree naturally cooperates with the Judges of the Abyss: Life, death, Soul processing and reincarnation are not isolated systems but consecutive stages of the same cosmic circulation.
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s later Imperial infrastructure increasingly used World Trees as major anchors for civilization-wide systems, while his Soul and associated World Trees provided him with the capacity to support enormous numbers of worlds and Souls.[10]
The World Trees should nevertheless not be conflated with the Core itself.
A World Tree maintains Life and can support the dimensions and systems surrounding a civilization; an Afterlife Core is a specific Soul-System node belonging to an afterlife Realm.

### Limitations

#### Not the Entire Soul System
The Core is a local interface, not the full universal Soul infrastructure.
Destroying or controlling one Core does not automatically grant ownership of every Soul in Creation.

#### Not a Resurrection Machine
The Core cannot simply reverse every death.
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s Adonis experiment required an extraordinary combination of Authorities and produced a historical echo rather than conventional resurrection.[1]

#### Authority-Gated
The Greek Core was normally restricted to legitimate [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] Rulers.[1]
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] required [[Persephone]] and [[Thanatos]] precisely because their positions were recognized components of the system.

#### No Confirmed Identical Heaven Core
Heaven possesses a confirmed Soul-processing and recycling function, but the novel has not explicitly shown an identical physical sphere inside Heaven.
This article therefore does not assume that every Paradise contains the same visible Core architecture.

#### Rulers Do Not Own Every Soul
Local Kings and Queens possess administrative Authority over their Realm, not absolute ownership of every Soul connected to it.
Powerful supernatural Souls can still fall directly under Primordial jurisdiction.[2][1]

#### Reincarnation Does Not Preserve Identity by Default
A reincarnated Soul is not automatically the original deceased person.
Memories and identity can be stripped during processing, producing a genuinely new individual from the same Soul substrate.[5]

#### Different Afterlives Use Different Rules
The overall purpose is shared, but individual Pantheons can process Souls differently.
Valhalla, Greek Hell, [[Biblical Hell]] and other afterlives should therefore not be assumed to operate through identical cultural or administrative procedures.[2]

### Strategic and Cosmological Importance
The Core reveals that Heaven, Hell and reincarnation are not merely religious territories.
They are pieces of functional cosmic infrastructure.
A Hell that stops processing Souls creates a problem for the cycle of Life itself.[3]
A Heaven that indefinitely traps Souls rather than allowing them to complete their cycle would likewise conflict with the deeper purpose described by Nyx.[2]
This is why afterlife rulership requires more than military control.
The individual governing the Realm must maintain the metaphysical system supporting Soul circulation.
The importance of this function only becomes greater as civilizations expand. The [[Elderblood Empire]] eventually grew so large that its original afterlife architecture physically could not process Souls quickly enough, forcing [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s family to redesign the system on a Galactic scale.[10][11]
The Core of the Afterlife is therefore best understood as the local operational heart and access point of an afterlife Soul Zone, embedded within a much larger Heaven–Hell–judgment–reincarnation cycle.

### Known Facts
- The only directly observed Afterlife Core is the enormous sphere within the [[Greek Underworld]].[1]
- Countless Souls continually flow through and around the Greek Core.[1]
- Access to its location was normally restricted to the King and Queen of the Underworld.[1]
- Even [[Aphrodite]] Elderblood had never previously seen it.[1]
- [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s Hell contains a fundamentally similar mechanism.[1]
- The Core was created by unspecified Primordial powers for the Soul System to function.[1]
- The Judges of the Abyss and Universal Tree administer the larger Soul/Life system together.[3]
- Both Heaven and Hell ultimately exist to recycle Souls.[2]
- The novel has not directly shown a physically identical Heaven Core.
- The Core can serve as a gateway into deeper Soul-System infrastructure when the appropriate Authorities are present.[1]
- Victor used this connection to retrieve Adonis' historical Record and manifest a sentient echo rather than literally resurrecting the original Adonis.[1]
- Reincarnation normally removes much of a deceased Being's previous identity information.[5]
- Victor's later Hell and Elvenorah formed a connected afterlife system for the [[Dragons Nest Pantheon]].[6]
- By the accelerated Imperial era, the Elderblood afterlife required a major redesign because its reincarnation cycle and Underworld cities could no longer process the enormous number of Souls efficiently.[10]
- The mature Empire deliberately preserved birth, Life, death and reincarnation rather than granting universal immortality to its population.[10][11]

### Notes
- Core of the Afterlife is a generalized encyclopedia title rather than the only explicit in-universe name for the Greek structure.
- Core of the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] and Core of Hell are better terms when specifically discussing the sphere shown in Chapter 811.
- Heaven belongs to the same greater system, but an identical Core of Heaven has not yet been demonstrated.
- The [[Akashic Records]], [[Reincarnation System]], System, Afterlife Core and an individual's Soul Core are separate structures even though they can interact.
- "Recycling" should not be interpreted as ordinary resurrection. The previous identity may be separated, erased or replaced before a Soul returns to Life.
- The later Elderblood afterlife should be treated as an evolution of the same Soul-processing principles rather than evidence that the original Greek Core alone controlled every Heaven and Hell.
