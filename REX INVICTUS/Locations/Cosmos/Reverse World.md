### Story Information

#### First Appearance
Chapter 345
The Reverse World is an artificial spatial world and supernatural technique primarily associated with Yōkai. It is created and sustained through Yōuki or other sufficiently compatible supernatural Energy and functions as a separated battlefield corresponding to a location in the ordinary Human World. Jeanne described the Reverse World as a Yōkai technique that used Yōuki and compared the Tokyo instance to a scattered dimension of Tokyo itself, explaining that Yōkai normally used such spaces to fight and resolve their conflicts without directly involving the Human World.[1]
Although sometimes described by the narration as a mirrored world or inverse world, the recurring terminology used by characters is Reverse World. The known examples do not appear to be naturally existing parallel universes or alternate timelines. Instead, the novel consistently presents them as artificial spatial environments which can be activated, supplied with Energy, reinforced, damaged and eventually dismissed.[1][2]
Reverse Worlds are particularly important to Yōkai society because they allow supernatural combat to occur on a scale that would otherwise devastate populated Human territory. However, their separation from ordinary reality is not absolute. Extremely powerful combatants can damage the spatial boundary itself, revealing the corresponding Human World through cracks in the dimension, while sufficiently capable spatial users can bypass the Reverse World entirely. The world also possesses finite structural durability and requires a continuing supply of Energy to remain stable.[3][4][5]

### Nature

#### Artificial Spatial World
A Reverse World is fundamentally a constructed spatial environment. Jeanne explicitly identifies it as a technique belonging to Yōkai and powered through Yōuki rather than as a naturally occurring dimension. The Tokyo Reverse World was described as being similar to a scattered dimension of Tokyo itself, indicating that its environment corresponded to an existing region of the Human World while remaining spatially separated from it.[1]
The later Reverse World created for [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] and Haruna reinforces this interpretation. When Kuroka left the arena, dark Energy covered the battlefield before Victor and Haruna found themselves within an inverse world created using enormous amounts of Yōuki.[2] Unlike naturally existing worlds such as [[Nightingale]], Samar or a Sector, the Reverse World required active supernatural expenditure to exist and could disappear once that support ceased.[3][6]

The exact metaphysical process through which the corresponding environment is reproduced has never been explained. It is therefore unclear whether every Reverse World creates a completely new pocket dimension from nothing, rearranges an existing spatial layer or temporarily separates a portion of reality into an isolated dimension. What is confirmed is that Yōkai can deliberately establish the space, that it corresponds to the ordinary world and that it requires Energy to remain maintained.[1][2]

#### Relationship with the Human World
Reverse Worlds occupy a close spatial relationship with the ordinary Human World rather than existing as completely unrelated distant Realms.
This became unmistakable during the battle between [[Scathach]] and [[Inari-Omikami]]. Their weapons struck each other with enough force to crack the surrounding space. As those fractures widened, openings appeared through which the occupants of the Reverse World could see the real Human World outside, including ordinary people moving through Shibuya Crossing. The breaches were then rapidly repaired by the forces maintaining the dimension.[5]
The event establishes several important properties:
- the Reverse World and its corresponding Human location can exist simultaneously;
- the spatial boundary between them can be physically damaged;
- the Human World remains present beyond that boundary;
- damage to the Reverse World does not automatically reproduce itself in the ordinary location;
- and the boundary can be actively repaired while the Reverse World remains operational.[5]
This spatial separation is the principal reason the technique can function as a supernatural battlefield. Combatants can devastate the artificial environment without immediately reproducing every attack within the densely populated Human World.

#### Internal Environment
A Reverse World is capable of containing a substantial three-dimensional environment rather than functioning as a featureless barrier or simple illusion.
The Tokyo instance reproduced enough of the surrounding environment for supernatural groups to move and fight throughout it.[1] During [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] and Haruna's duel, the Reverse World possessed terrain, a visible sky, clouds and sufficient internal distance for clouds several kilometres away to be affected by Victor's attacks. Haruna was also capable of reproducing her artificial moon technique within its sky.[7]
The precise physical dimensions of a Reverse World are unknown and appear likely to vary according to the purpose, Energy invested and region being isolated. Nothing in the novel confirms that every Reverse World possesses the same size or that any demonstrated example encompasses the entirety of Earth.

### Creation
Reverse Worlds are primarily associated with Yōkai techniques.
Jeanne directly identifies the Reverse World as a Yōkai technique using Yōuki. Yōkai traditionally use the space to conduct battles and settle internal supernatural disputes without unnecessarily involving the ordinary Human population.[1]
The clearest on-screen creation occurs before [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] and Haruna's duel. [[Yotsuba Kuroka]] announced that the battle would begin once she left the arena. Immediately after she crossed outside the ring, dark Energy covered the battlefield and Victor and Haruna appeared inside an inverse world, which the narration explicitly described as a place created using massive quantities of Yōuki.[2]
The demonstrated process can therefore be represented approximately as:

The novel has not revealed whether there is a fixed ritual, minimum number of practitioners or standardized formation necessary to create every Reverse World. Kuroka could initiate one for the duel, while a far larger Reverse World in Tokyo was being maintained through the combined involvement of major Japanese Gods.[3][2]

### Energy and Maintenance

#### Continuous Energy Requirement
A Reverse World does not remain active indefinitely without support. [[Scathach]] specifically questioned why the Reverse World surrounding her group was still standing and concluded that someone had to be maintaining it.[1]
In the following chapter, Scathach forced Anna to recognize the important limitation herself. Anna stated that the Reverse World required Energy to remain maintained, after which Scathach revealed that the Major Gods of Japan were watching events while simultaneously supplying the Energy necessary to keep the world reversed.[3]
This means that the continued existence of a Reverse World is directly connected to the resources of its creators or maintainers.

#### Reinforcement
Additional Energy can be supplied to strengthen a Reverse World when the existing structure is threatened.
During [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] and Haruna's duel, Victor's One Hit, One Million Punches produced enough force that Kuroka believed the attack had nearly destroyed their Reverse World. She immediately ordered the supporting Yōkai to channel additional Yōuki into it.[7]
Victor later warned Kuroka directly that the world would be destroyed by his next display unless substantially more Yōuki was supplied. Kuroka responded by ordering [[Hashimoto Genji]] and additional Oni to reinforce the structure.[6]

#### Exhaustion
Maintaining a Reverse World can consume enormous quantities of Energy.
At the conclusion of [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] and Haruna's duel, the artificial world began shaking before disappearing completely. Haruna returned to the arena and observed that Kuroka, Genji and the Oni who had reinforced the structure had collapsed unconscious from Yōuki exhaustion.[6]
This demonstrates that even experienced supernatural beings can exhaust themselves sustaining a Reverse World against forces significantly beyond its initial design.

### Functions

#### Battlefield Isolation
The primary traditional purpose of a Reverse World is to provide a controlled environment where Yōkai can fight and resolve disputes. Jeanne explicitly explains that this is how the spaces are ordinarily used.[1]
This makes Reverse Worlds particularly valuable in densely populated Human regions. Supernatural combatants frequently possess enough destructive power to devastate buildings, neighbourhoods or substantially larger areas. Moving those confrontations into an isolated spatial counterpart dramatically reduces the immediate risk posed to uninvolved Humans.
The technique was used in this manner during both of its principal appearances:
- supernatural conflict in Tokyo and Shibuya during the Japanese incident;[1][5]
- and the formal duel between [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] and Haruna.[2][7][6]

#### Containment
Reverse Worlds can also be deliberately used as containment spaces.
During the Japanese incident, the Shinto Gods deliberately attempted to keep [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s group trapped inside the Reverse World while weaker Gods throughout Japan were evacuated. [[Tsukuyomi]] later openly acknowledged that the Gods had attempted to trap the group there specifically to buy time.[4]
The plan failed because Victor's faction possessed several individuals capable of circumventing the dimensional boundary. Nevertheless, the attempt confirms that a Reverse World can be maintained specifically to prevent its occupants from freely returning to ordinary reality.

#### Protection of Civilians
The Shibuya confrontation illustrates the technique's protective value most clearly. [[Scathach]] and Inari were fighting with enough force that even their weapon clashes shattered portions of the surrounding space. Despite this, the Humans visible through the resulting dimensional fractures continued moving normally in Shibuya Crossing, separated from the immediate battlefield by the Reverse World.[5]
When those fractures appeared, the damaged dimensional boundary was rapidly repaired, further reducing the possibility that the supernatural conflict would spill directly into Human territory.[5]

### Durability
Reverse Worlds possess finite structural durability.
They are not invulnerable dimensions capable of containing unlimited power. Their stability depends on both the strength of the spatial construct and the amount of Energy being used to maintain it.

#### Spatial Cracking
During [[Scathach]]'s battle with Inari, their ordinary exchanges of martial attacks were sufficient to crack and shatter the surrounding space. The fractures resembled breaking glass and eventually exposed the Human World beyond the Reverse World's boundary.[5]
The world did not immediately collapse because the breaches were being repaired by whoever was sustaining it.[5]

#### Near Destruction
During [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s duel with Haruna, Victor performed One Hit, One Million Punches. The resulting blast erased Haruna's thousands of incoming Energy attacks and dispersed clouds several kilometres away within the artificial reality. Kuroka immediately warned her subordinates that the attack had nearly destroyed the Reverse World and ordered greater Yōuki input.[7]

#### Overwhelming Power
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] later intended to show Haruna the difference between their levels by releasing a greater portion of his raw power. Before doing so, he looked directly toward Kuroka despite her being beyond the Reverse World and warned her that she needed to reinforce the world or it would be destroyed. Kuroka immediately summoned Genji and the Oni to assist with its stabilization.[6]
These examples demonstrate that Reverse World durability is not an absolute value. A sufficiently reinforced Reverse World can survive attacks that would have destroyed a weaker version, but doing so dramatically increases the Energy burden placed upon its maintainers.

### Entry and Exit

#### Normal Transfer
The exact standard procedure for entering a Reverse World has not been fully explained. Known examples demonstrate that a practitioner can apparently transfer combatants into one during activation.
When Kuroka created the Reverse World for [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] and Haruna, the arena was covered by dark Energy and the two combatants subsequently found themselves inside the artificial environment.[2]

#### Natural Dissolution
When a Reverse World is deliberately released or can no longer remain maintained, the artificial environment can disappear and return its occupants to the corresponding ordinary location. After [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] and Haruna's duel concluded, the world shook and dissolved, leaving Haruna back in the arena while the exhausted Yōkai maintainers lay outside.[6]

#### Spatial Escape
A Reverse World can be escaped without waiting for its creator to release it.
The Shinto Gods attempted to confine [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s group within one, but [[Clan Alioth]] possessed spatial abilities capable of allowing the group to escape. [[Tsukuyomi]] acknowledged that the Gods' containment attempt had failed because of the Alioth Clan's abilities.[4]

#### Forced Dimensional Breach
Sufficiently powerful beings can also break through the Reverse World's boundary directly.
[[Scathach]] casually swung her spear and opened a breach in the sky of the Reverse World, demonstrating that she did not require the normal Yōkai method or the Alioth Clan's specialized spatial ability to leave. [[Tsukuyomi]] later specifically acknowledged that even without [[Clan Alioth]], Scathach herself could open a rift returning to the Mortal World.[1][4]
Known escape methods therefore include:
- conventional release by the creator or maintainer;
- spatial abilities such as those possessed by Clan Alioth;
- and overwhelming spatial force capable of tearing open a dimensional breach.[1][4]

### Perception Across the Boundary
The Reverse World's separation can also interfere with ordinary perception between the interior and exterior.
During [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s duel with Haruna, Kuroka was observing the battle from outside the Reverse World. Victor nevertheless turned directly toward her and focused his gaze upon her. Kuroka was visibly disturbed and internally questioned how Victor could see beyond the Reverse World.[6]
Her reaction indicates that directly perceiving someone beyond the dimensional boundary is not normally expected from a combatant inside the artificial world.
The phenomenon also reinforces that the outside arena continued to exist independently while Victor and Haruna fought within the Reverse World. Kuroka and the supporting Yōkai remained outside and supplied Energy into the construct rather than physically occupying the same battlefield.[6]

### Known Users and Maintainers

#### Yōkai
Reverse Worlds are explicitly identified as a technique belonging to Yōkai. They traditionally use the dimension to conduct battles and settle disputes away from ordinary Humans.[1]

#### Yotsuba Kuroka
[[Yotsuba Kuroka]] is the clearest individually demonstrated user. She activated the Reverse World used for [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] and Haruna's duel through a large expenditure of Yōuki and remained responsible for monitoring and stabilizing it throughout the battle.[2][7][6]

#### Hashimoto Genji and the Oni
[[Hashimoto Genji]] and several Oni were summoned to reinforce Kuroka's Reverse World after [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] warned that his next demonstration would destroy it. They supplied additional Yōuki to the spatial construct until the end of the duel and ultimately collapsed from exhaustion.[6]
They are therefore confirmed as capable of supporting and maintaining an existing Reverse World, although the cited scene does not independently demonstrate Genji or the Oni creating one from the beginning.

#### Japanese Major Gods
[[The Major]] Gods of Japan were capable of supplying Energy to maintain the large Reverse World surrounding [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s group during the Japanese incident. [[Scathach]] detected their observation and realized they were simultaneously keeping the world reversed.[3]
Their exact method was not explained, and the technique itself is still explicitly identified as originating from Yōkai rather than the Gods.[1]

### Limitations
Despite their usefulness, Reverse Worlds possess several clearly demonstrated limitations.
- Energy Dependency: A Reverse World requires continuing Energy to remain operational.[3]
- Finite Durability: Powerful attacks can damage or completely threaten the spatial structure.[5][7]
- Maintenance Burden: Reinforcing the dimension against extreme power can exhaust its maintainers.[6]
- Spatial Vulnerability: Specialized spatial abilities can bypass the boundary.[4]
- Brute-Force Escape: Extremely powerful individuals such as [[Scathach]] can tear open passages to the Mortal World.[1][4]
- Boundary Damage: Sufficient force can create cracks exposing ordinary reality beyond the artificial world.[5]
- Perception Is Not Absolute: Exceptional beings such as [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] can perceive individuals beyond the Reverse World's boundary.[6]
- Temporary Existence: Known examples can disappear once maintenance ends or the battlefield is released.[6]

### Cosmological Classification
Reverse Worlds should be distinguished from the larger naturally or independently existing worlds and Realms present within the setting.
They are never identified as Sectors, complete alternate universes, independent timelines or naturally existing cosmic Realities. Instead, the clearest direct explanation calls the Reverse World a technique of Yōkai powered by Yōuki.[1]
Their known characteristics are consequently much closer to an artificial pocket dimension or temporary spatial sub-world than an independent cosmological universe:
GREATER COSMOLOGY
│
├── Sectors / Local Cosmic Realities
│
├── Natural Worlds
│
├── Divine / Supernatural Realms
│
└── Artificial Spatial Realms
│
└── REVERSE WORLD
│
├── Created through supernatural Energy

This classification does not mean the Reverse World is merely an illusion. Characters physically fight within it, its terrain and sky respond to supernatural attacks, its space can crack, the dimensional barrier can be torn open and its maintainers must supply genuine Energy to prevent structural destruction.[1][5][7]

### History

#### Japanese Incident
The first detailed explanation of a Reverse World occurred during [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s campaign through Japan following the Crimson Nightmare. [[Scathach]] noticed that the Reverse World surrounding the group continued to remain active despite the immediate conflict and correctly concluded that someone was intentionally maintaining the structure.[1]
When Scathach casually tore open its sky, Jeanne explained to Ruby that the Reverse World was a Yōkai technique using Yōuki and that Yōkai normally used such spaces to fight and settle their conflicts. She characterized the existing Reverse World as resembling a scattered dimension of Tokyo itself.[1]
The group soon discovered why the world had not been released. Scathach revealed that Japan's Major Gods were observing them while providing the Energy necessary to maintain the dimension. The Shinto Gods were attempting to delay Victor's faction long enough for the weaker Gods scattered across Japan to evacuate.[3]
The plan ultimately failed. [[Clan Alioth]]'s spatial powers allowed the group to bypass the Reverse World's confinement, and [[Tsukuyomi]] acknowledged that even if the Alioth Clan had not been present, Scathach possessed enough power to forcibly tear open a rift back into the Mortal World.[4]
The Reverse World's durability became relevant again when Scathach fought Inari. Their exchanges cracked and shattered the surrounding space, with breaches temporarily exposing the ordinary Human World and the population of Shibuya Crossing. Those openings were rapidly repaired, preventing the supernatural battle from directly spilling into Human territory.[5]

#### Victor vs. Haruna
The technique appeared again centuries later in story progression when [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] agreed to duel Otsuki Haruna. Kuroka established a Reverse World specifically for their fight. As she exited the arena, dark Energy enveloped the battlefield and Victor and Haruna entered an inverse world created through massive amounts of Yōuki.[2]
The world initially contained their battle without serious difficulty, but Victor's raw physical and supernatural output rapidly exceeded what Kuroka had prepared for. When Victor used One Hit, One Million Punches, the resulting blast destroyed Haruna's Energy barrage and affected clouds several kilometres away. Kuroka immediately warned that the attack had almost destroyed the Reverse World and ordered her subordinates to increase their Yōuki output.[7]
After Haruna exhausted much of her own Yōuki, Victor decided to show her how far she remained from his level. He looked directly toward Kuroka despite the spatial boundary separating them and warned her to reinforce the world before he released his power. Kuroka summoned Genji and multiple Oni to contribute their Yōuki, allowing the artificial space to survive Victor's demonstration.[6]
Once the duel ended, the Reverse World began shaking and disappeared. Haruna returned to the ordinary arena and found Kuroka, Genji and the Oni unconscious from the enormous amount of Yōuki they had expended keeping the spatial world intact.[6]

### Notes
- Reverse World is the most consistently used in-universe term, while mirrored world and inverse world are alternate descriptions of the same demonstrated phenomenon.[1][5][2]
- Reverse Worlds are not confirmed alternate timelines. Their direct correspondence with the ordinary Human World, dependence upon external Energy and description as a Yōkai technique instead support their treatment as artificial spatial dimensions.[1][3]
- A Reverse World is not automatically indestructible simply because it is a separate dimension. Both [[Scathach]] and [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] demonstrated enough power to seriously compromise its structure.[5][7][6]
- Spatial cracks within a Reverse World can expose the Human World beyond it, but no demonstrated instance has shown complete destruction of a Reverse World simultaneously destroying its corresponding Human location.[5]
- [[Clan Alioth]]'s ability to escape the Japanese Reverse World demonstrates that dimensional separation can be bypassed through sufficiently advanced Space-related powers.[4]
- Scathach's escape method did not require a specialized Yōkai technique; she simply opened a dimensional breach through overwhelming spatial force.[1][4]
- Victor's ability to directly perceive Kuroka from within the Reverse World surprised her, indicating that normal occupants are not expected to freely see through its dimensional boundary.[6]
- The amount of Yōuki necessary to maintain a Reverse World increases drastically when the structure is subjected to forces approaching or exceeding its normal durability.[7][6]
- The exact maximum size, duration and Energy requirement of a Reverse World remain unknown.
