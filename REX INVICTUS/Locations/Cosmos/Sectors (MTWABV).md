A Sector is a cosmological subdivision of the greater Universe, organized through the regulations of the Primordial Entities. It is not synonymous with a planet, an individual Galaxy, a Divine Realm or a civilization. The known Sectors encompass multiple galaxies and their associated astronomical structures, while their boundaries separate regions whose development and supernatural inhabitants differ enormously in age and capability. The explicitly established classifications are Inhospitable, Low-Level, Medium-Level and High-Level.[1][2]
The divisions protect not only intelligent civilizations but the development of galaxies themselves. Young Sectors possess comparatively immature astronomical structures, while mature regions contain older and more developed Galactic environments. Their classification is consequently distinct from the technological capabilities of their civilizations or the personal strength of exceptional beings residing within them. A sufficiently powerful individual may surpass the expected capabilities of their home Sector without automatically changing its developmental classification.[3][4]

### Overview
Sectors form the principal developmental divisions of the known Universe. The [[Primordial Entities]] maintain a cosmic system that gives younger regions an opportunity to develop without being immediately overwhelmed by civilizations possessing millions or billions of years of accumulated power. The protective distinction applies to both the intelligent beings inhabiting those regions and the astronomical structures surrounding them. Their galaxies possess their own developmental cycles, gradually advancing from young formations into mature structures that can eventually exist within the greater environment of the Higher Sectors.[1][4]
The distinction between a developing Sector and a mature one is therefore more fundamental than the existence of powerful inhabitants. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s original Middle Sector produced exceptional beings such as Victor Elderblood, [[Kali Elderblood]], [[Diablo]], the Apocalyptic Beasts and other supernatural anomalies despite remaining classified as a developing region. Similarly, the presence of a technologically advanced civilization does not automatically transform the surrounding astronomical environment into a High-Level Sector. The maturity of a cosmic region, its civilization's capabilities and the strength of its inhabitants represent separate characteristics that can develop at radically different rates.[1][3]
The younger Sectors associated with [[Nightingale]], Samar and Arcane provide an important comparison. These regions contain relatively few galaxies, with many still possessing extremely young astronomical structures. In contrast, Victor's central Middle Sector contains a vastly larger collection of mature galaxies and approaches the end of its protected development. The comparison resembles the difference between newborns and an adolescent approaching adulthood, emphasizing that Sector classification concerns the age and development of the surrounding cosmic environment rather than the political history of any individual planet.[4]

### Nature of a Sector

#### Part of the Greater Universe
Sectors are subdivisions of the greater Universe rather than independent cosmologies existing outside its structure. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] describes them as the means by which the Primordials divide the Universe, creating invisible boundaries between Galactic regions possessing different levels of development. The Universe itself exists within a state of continuous expansion associated with Infinity, whose primordial function causes Creation to expand simply through his continued existence.[2][6][4]

The continuous expansion of the Universe does not establish that every individual Sector possesses infinite dimensions or an unrestricted astronomical extent. Victor's Middle Sector possesses a recognizable boundary capable of stopping the outward expansion of his Cosmic Consciousness, while younger Sectors contain comparatively small collections of galaxies. Individual Sectors consequently represent distinguishable regions within a greater and vastly more extensive cosmic structure.[3][4]
The existence of foreign Universes is a separate matter. [[Azathoth Elderblood]] originates from beyond Victor's native Universe, while the original [[Primordial Chaos]] is associated with a much broader existential history than the ordinary developmental hierarchy of Sectors. These circumstances establish that the cosmology encompasses phenomena beyond the known Sector system, but do not make individual Sectors equivalent to separate Multiverses or complete cosmologies.[7][4]

#### Multi-Galactic Structure
A Sector is not synonymous with one Galaxy. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s original Sector is described as a group of countless galaxies, and his Cosmic Consciousness eventually becomes capable of surveying the astronomical structures contained within its boundaries. The same principle applies to young Sectors, since [[Nightingale]], Samar and Arcane occupy separate developing regions containing several galaxies rather than existing as isolated planets within otherwise empty cosmic spaces.[8][3][4]
The number and scale of galaxies vary with development. Young Sectors contain relatively few Galactic structures, many of which have not completed the astronomical processes necessary to become mature. Victor's Middle Sector, by comparison, possesses an enormous population of established galaxies, numerous inhabited worlds and millions of World Tree connections. The regions governed by civilizations occupying the Higher Sectors may possess galaxies vastly larger than the Milky Way.[3][9][10]
The galaxies of the Upper Sectors can reach sizes approximately 10,000 times greater than the Milky Way, demonstrating the astronomical disparity between some mature regions and the familiar Galactic structures of the lower cosmic environment. These enormous galaxies can contain trillions of living beings, and their destruction can therefore produce catastrophic population losses extending far beyond a single planetary civilization. This extreme scale does not establish that every Higher-Sector Galaxy possesses identical dimensions, since the comparison concerns the upper extent of the examples described.[10]

#### Sector, Reality, Universe and Cosmos
The terms Universe, Reality and Cosmos can describe different scales depending upon the supernatural environment being discussed. Some isolated dimensional systems are experienced by their inhabitants as complete Universes, while the greater cosmology identifies them as parts of larger Sectoral or dimensional structures. Their contextual use does not automatically establish that every region referred to as a Universe is identical in scale or existential status to the overarching Universe containing the entire Sector system.
The [[Magic]] World provides an example. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] encountered a foreign Reality connected through Arcane's portal, while the Primordial authorities evaluated the resulting disturbance through the regulations governing Sector development. Natalia likewise perceived an extraordinary cosmic environment associated with [[Nightingale]], despite the world belonging to a comparatively young Low-Level Sector. These descriptions distinguish the experience of observing a complete local Reality from the larger framework used by the Primordials to organize Creation.[11][12][1][4]

#### Cosmic Life and Renewal
The primordial function of Life encompasses far more than intelligent beings or biological organisms. The Universal Tree regards Creation itself as participating in a living system extending from minute matter to planets, stars and enormous Galactic structures. These entities possess developmental cycles and eventually encounter Death when their time reaches its natural conclusion. Their dissolution returns matter and Energy to the wider cosmic cycle, supporting the emergence of new astronomical structures and living beings.[4]
The death of a supergalaxy within the Higher Sectors can release enough Energy to contribute to the creation of hundreds of smaller galaxies within younger regions. This process connects the decline of mature astronomical environments to the formation of new ones, allowing the Universe to continue renewing itself while maintaining an enormous range of different developmental stages.[4]

### Developmental Cycle
Sector classification concerns the maturation of a cosmic region rather than a permanent designation assigned independently of development. Its galaxies age, new Life emerges, [[World Trees]] nurture suitable worlds, civilizations develop supernatural and technological systems, and the barriers separating the region from older environments eventually change. The result is a progression from comparatively young protected regions toward mature cosmic environments exposed to the greater powers of the Higher Sectors.[1][4]

#### Astronomical Development
The development of galaxies is an essential component of Sector maturation. The Sectors associated with [[Nightingale]], Samar and Arcane contain relatively few galaxies whose astronomical structures remain young, while [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s central Middle Sector contains a considerably greater number of mature systems. The difference is comparable to the developmental stages of living beings, with the younger regions resembling newborns and the Middle Sector resembling an adolescent approaching adulthood.[4]
The aging of an entire Galactic environment operates on astronomical timescales that differ considerably from the political development of its civilizations. A society may advance through extraordinary technological progress or supernatural Time manipulation without causing every galaxy in its Sector to complete the equivalent amount of natural development.

#### Civilizational Development
Intelligent civilizations participate in Sector development by organizing Life-bearing worlds, creating political structures, advancing technology and [[Magic]], and developing methods of utilizing increasingly large portions of their astronomical environments. These processes influence the ability of the inhabitants to survive once the Sector enters the greater cosmic system, but do not independently define every property of the surrounding region.
Multiple civilizations possessing radically different levels of advancement can coexist within the same Sector. One society may possess interstellar technology while another remains confined to a single planet, just as powerful Gods can originate from the same environment as comparatively ordinary mortal populations.

#### Natural Timescale
An ordinary Middle Sector may require billions of years to reach the point at which it naturally enters the Higher-Sector environment. Such periods encompass the gradual development of galaxies and their inhabitants, making the process fundamentally different from the ordinary expansion of a political empire.[8]
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s Sector is an exceptional example because its civilization underwent extraordinary artificial acceleration. The [[Elderblood Empire]] combined supernatural evolution, advanced technology, political unification, World Tree integration and immense manipulation of Time to develop at a rate that conventional civilizations could not naturally reproduce.

#### Development and Exceptional Individuals
Sector classification remains regional even when an individual exceeds the capacity of the surrounding environment. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s existential development eventually reached the point at which his presence became disproportionately large compared with the Middle Sector containing him. The situation was compared to placing a fully grown whale inside a small pond, emphasizing that the cosmic region had not matured at the same rate as the Being residing within it.[3]
Victor possessed enough power to force the Sector to expand simply to accommodate his existence, but deliberately refrained from doing so merely for personal comfort. The possibility demonstrates an exceptional interaction between an overwhelmingly powerful individual and the Reality surrounding him rather than the ordinary mechanism through which Sectors naturally mature.[3]

### Sector Classification
The Primordial classification system recognizes four principal developmental levels: Inhospitable, Low, Medium and High. The later terms Low-Level, Mid-Level, Middle Sector, Upper Sector and Higher Sectors describe corresponding portions of the same broader hierarchy.[1]

#### Inhospitable Sectors
An Inhospitable Sector is a region in which Life has either not emerged or has not developed sufficiently to produce established intelligent civilizations. Such regions may contain chaotic astronomical environments and incomplete biological development, representing the earliest explicitly recognized stage of the Sector system.[1]
The designation refers to the broader developmental condition of the cosmic region rather than the habitability of an individual planet. A barren world existing inside a mature Sector does not cause the entire region to become Inhospitable, just as the discovery of a single hostile planetary environment does not alter the classification of its surrounding galaxies.

#### Low-Level Sectors
Low-Level Sectors contain planets supporting intelligent Life, including civilizations possessing distinct cultures, supernatural species and developing political structures. Their surrounding galaxies remain comparatively young, making them particularly vulnerable to the influence of civilizations whose development began millions or billions of years earlier.[1][4]
The worlds [[Nightingale]] and Samar originally belonged to separate Low-Level Sectors. Their inhabitants included Noble Vampires, [[Werewolves]] and other supernatural species whose development was influenced by interactions with foreign powers. The Primordial authorities maintain protection over such regions, particularly through the Universal Tree, to prevent older civilizations from prematurely destroying their developmental potential.[1]
The protection is not merely intended to preserve intelligent populations. Young galaxies themselves require time to mature, and their premature exposure to powerful foreign civilizations could disrupt astronomical development on scales far exceeding ordinary planetary conflicts.[4]

#### Mid-Level Sectors
Mid-Level Sectors are comparatively mature but still developing cosmic regions positioned between young protected Sectors and the open Higher-Sector environment. They can contain enormous groups of galaxies, established supernatural systems, powerful civilizations and numerous World Tree-bearing worlds.
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s native Sector is the principal example. Its astronomical environment encompasses a vast number of galaxies rather than merely [[Yggdrasil]] or the Milky Way, and millions of [[World Trees]] eventually become connected to the Emperor through his expanding supernatural network. Its civilizations possess enough development to begin contemplating the Higher Sectors, while its protective boundary continues weakening as the region approaches its natural transition.[8][3][9]
The [[Elderblood Empire]]'s influence extends across planets distributed throughout countless galaxies, supported by advanced infrastructure, supernatural transportation, communication systems and the personal authority of its ruler. Nevertheless, the region's classification is not determined solely by the Empire's military supremacy or the capabilities of Victor himself. Even after he becomes an Overlord and obtains extraordinary cosmic powers, the surrounding region remains a Middle Sector undergoing its own developmental process.[13][3]

#### High-Level Sectors
High-Level Sectors represent the mature environment into which developing regions enter after their previous isolation ends. Such regions contain ancient civilizations, enormous galaxies, exceptionally powerful Gods and political factions whose territories may encompass hundreds of galaxies. Their inhabitants possess greater opportunities to develop advanced supernatural techniques and technological systems because they are no longer limited to the isolated conditions of young protected Sectors.[1][7]
The civilization once ruled by [[Velnorah Xyphora Elderblood Thaloria II]] provides a direct example of the consequences of this transition. Her original Sector evolved from Medium-Level to High-Level through Convergence, removing the previous separation between her civilization and older Galactic powers. Although Velnorah and [[Thaloria Xyphora]] possessed exceptional individual strength, their civilization was unable to withstand the enormous pressure created by neighboring powers that had developed for considerably longer periods.[14]
Gods operating within the Higher Sectors are also accustomed to training their Divinities and combat capabilities over extended periods. This distinguishes many of them from the traditionally stagnant Divine establishments originating from Earth, whose supernatural authorities frequently developed through inherited or naturally assigned Concepts rather than sustained combat experience.[15]
High-Level classification does not mean that every civilization within the environment possesses identical technology, military strength or political influence. The Higher Sectors contain multiple factions, independent powers and civilizations whose development varies enormously despite occupying the same broad stage of cosmic maturity.

### Sector Barriers

#### Purpose
Sector barriers are cosmological boundaries associated with the Primordials' regulation of developing portions of Creation. Their principal purpose is to prevent overwhelmingly mature powers from freely interfering with younger regions, preserving the opportunity for emerging Life, civilizations and galaxies to complete their natural development. These boundaries function as more than ordinary physical walls, separating environments whose inhabitants possess radically different levels of supernatural and technological advancement.[1][2][4]
YOUNGER REGIONProtected DevelopmentGalaxies, Life and civilizations mature with restricted external interference.

MATURE REGIONGreater ExposureAncient Galactic civilizations and powerful factions operate beyond the protected environment.
The intervention of mature powers within young Sectors can violate the regulations protecting their development. Vlad's invasion of [[Nightingale]] demonstrates the potential consequences of hostile activity within a weaker cosmic region, while the disturbance caused by the Arcane portal attracts the attention of the Primordial authorities when abnormal temporal effects cross between environments whose development differs substantially.[1]

#### Restriction of Perception
Sector barriers can restrict supernatural perception as well as physical travel. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s Cosmic Consciousness eventually expands throughout his Middle Sector, allowing him to perceive its galaxies and construct an extensive mental map of the surrounding astronomical environment. However, his awareness encounters the Sector boundary and cannot naturally extend beyond it, demonstrating that even extraordinary cosmic perception remains subject to the separation established between developing regions.[3]
The routes toward stronger and weaker regions are not equally accessible. Victor can identify the path toward the Upper Sectors more readily than the direction leading toward the protected Lower Sectors, reflecting the increased difficulty imposed upon mature beings attempting to enter environments whose inhabitants remain comparatively weak.[3]

#### Weakening During Maturation
The boundary separating a Middle Sector from the Higher Sectors gradually weakens as the region approaches the end of its protected development. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] discovers that his Upper-facing barrier has become thin enough for his Non-Attributed Energy to interact with it directly, allowing him to contemplate opening a passage into the mature cosmic environment.[3]
The weakening becomes more severe as the Empire continues developing. Its barrier eventually reaches a condition in which creating additional breaches could trigger premature collapse, making ordinary reconnaissance increasingly dangerous. Victor recognizes that the continued protection of the Sector is essential to the Empire's preparations, particularly while its military infrastructure and supernatural systems are still undergoing accelerated development.[16]

#### Directional Asymmetry
Sector barriers do not necessarily possess identical resistance when approached from opposing directions. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s Middle Sector eventually develops a boundary that is considerably weaker from his own side while remaining substantially stronger from the Upper-Sector side. This asymmetry allows the inhabitants of a maturing region to approach their eventual transition without automatically permitting ancient foreign powers to invade at the same moment.[16]
IMPORTANTA Sector barrier is not an ordinary physical wall.
Its accessibility can differ according to developmental maturity, the direction of approach and the supernatural characteristics of the Being attempting to cross it.

#### Artificial Breaches
Extremely powerful Beings may possess methods of crossing or manipulating Sector boundaries that ordinary inhabitants cannot employ. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s Non-Attributed Energy allows him to interact with his weakening Upper-facing barrier, while his connection to the [[World Trees]] and extraordinary Draconic capabilities provide additional means of sustaining and influencing the surrounding Reality.[3]
The Emperor eventually opens a passage into the Upper Sectors for War, allowing his Herald to enter Black Eclipse's territory before the protective boundary has naturally disappeared. This crossing demonstrates that an exceptional Being can establish a controlled route through a barrier under suitable conditions, but does not prove that every developing Sector possesses equally vulnerable boundaries.[17]

#### Hostile Pressure and Infiltration
Sector barriers can become targets of foreign supernatural operations. As [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s original region approaches Convergence, powers originating from the Upper Sectors attempt to exploit the weakening separation by distributing fragments of Divine Concepts into Imperial territory. These fragments can attach themselves to suitable hosts and establish connections through which foreign Gods may gain influence without physically entering the protected region.[18]
The [[Black Eclipse Pantheon]] employs this strategy while preparing to acquire Victor's Sector. Its fragments are repeatedly neutralized by the Empire, prompting the faction to increase its attempts at establishing supernatural footholds. Victor responds by maintaining the barrier against the pressure originating from the opposite side, expending extraordinary amounts of Energy to prevent the region from being opened before his civilization is ready for war.[17]
The resulting confrontation demonstrates that a weakened boundary does not necessarily collapse immediately when attacked. Victor continues supporting the separation through his personal power and the Empire's supernatural infrastructure, while the hostile faction must rely upon indirect infiltration and attempts to exploit the developing instability.

### Convergence and the Higher Sectors

#### Convergence
Convergence is the developmental transition through which an isolated Middle Sector enters the mature Higher-Sector environment. Its previous protective separation ends, allowing civilizations that once existed within relatively independent cosmic regions to encounter older Galactic powers whose military and technological development may vastly exceed their own.[14][8]

Velnorah's former civilization provides one of the clearest examples. Before Convergence, its political and military strength was sufficient to dominate the protected region, allowing Thaloria and later Velnorah to exercise extraordinary authority over the surrounding civilization. Once the Sector entered the High-Level environment, Galactic Emperors and other older powers gained access to territory previously protected by the Primordial barriers. The resulting conflicts destroyed the former political supremacy of the civilization despite the personal strength of its rulers.[14]
The same process threatens [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s original Sector. Its galaxies are approaching the mature environment, and the weakening barrier provides increasingly direct contact with foreign civilizations. Once the transition is complete, the group of galaxies composing the original Middle Sector is expected to become part of a much larger Galactic cluster within the Higher-Sector environment.[8]

#### Loss of Isolation
The central consequence of Convergence is the disappearance of developmental protection. Civilizations that had previously governed independent cosmic regions can suddenly encounter opponents possessing vastly older military establishments, unfamiliar Divine Concepts and technologies capable of operating at extraordinary astronomical scales.
Convergence does not automatically grant a civilization superior technology or the military strength necessary to resist the surrounding powers. Velnorah's experience demonstrates that individual strength may allow a ruler to confront powerful opponents while remaining insufficient to preserve an entire civilization against overwhelming numerical and technological superiority.[14]

#### Lower and Higher Existential Environments
Differences between developmental regions can also become apparent to extraordinarily powerful beings. An Outer God accustomed to a greater cosmic environment experiences a lower region as comparatively cramped, containing extensive astronomical structures but relatively few existences capable of attracting its attention. Such descriptions reflect the disparity between the scale and existential capacity of environments rather than establishing a universal mathematical ratio between every Low-, Middle- and High-Level Sector.[5]
The distinction becomes even more pronounced in the Higher Sectors, where individual galaxies can possess dimensions many thousands of times greater than the Milky Way. Their enormous populations and astronomical structures create environments capable of sustaining civilizations and supernatural conflicts on scales vastly exceeding those familiar to younger Galactic regions.[10]

#### Sector Expansion vs. Sector Ascension
The enlargement of an existing Sector and its transition into a mature Higher-Sector environment are different processes. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] possesses enough power to expand the capacity of his Middle Sector simply to accommodate his extraordinary existential size, but such expansion would not independently complete the natural Convergence process.[3]

### Higher-Sector Influence

#### Manipulation of Developing Sectors
The mature civilizations of the Higher Sectors possess methods of influencing younger regions long before their natural developmental isolation ends. Exceptionally powerful beings can distribute fragments of their own supernatural power into developing Sectors, allowing those fragments to influence the eventual destination of the regions as they mature.[19][4]
The fragments gradually affect the cosmic relationship between the developing Sector and the greater environment, steering the region toward the sphere of influence selected by the sponsoring Higher-Sector power. This strategy allows ancient factions to position themselves near newly mature civilizations before those civilizations possess the experience or military infrastructure necessary to resist immediate territorial acquisition.

The method is particularly effective because ancient civilizations possess an enormous developmental advantage over newly emerged powers. A younger civilization may possess formidable individuals or advanced technology, but its overall institutions and military reserves frequently remain small compared with those accumulated by factions that have existed for millions of years.[4]

#### Success Rate and Overlords
The Higher-Sector strategy succeeds in approximately 99% of ordinary cases. Developing regions without a sufficiently powerful unified sovereign can be influenced toward foreign territories and subsequently brought beneath the authority of older civilizations with comparatively little resistance.[4]
The principal exceptions are Sectors that produce individuals capable of completely controlling their original region and becoming Overlords before entering the mature environment. Such rulers possess an already unified civilization, reducing the opportunity for older factions to exploit fragmented political systems or immature leadership. Nevertheless, Overlord status does not make a civilization invincible, particularly when confronting enemies possessing vastly greater military populations, resources and historical experience.[4]

#### Victor's Adaptation
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] obtains information concerning the Higher-Sector method while investigating an enemy attempting to establish influence within his own territory. Rather than allowing the foreign faction to determine the eventual position of his Sector, he adopts the underlying principle for the protection and expansion of his own civilization.[19]
The Emperor produces small fragments of his own supernatural existence and distributes them within the younger Sectors associated with [[Nightingale]], Samar and Arcane. These fragments establish an influence intended to guide the developing regions toward his own sphere rather than allowing them to emerge near unrelated Higher-Sector civilizations.[19][4]
Because the smaller Sectors possess relatively few galaxies, their complete astronomical structures are considerably easier for Victor to influence than a mature Middle Sector. His personal authority and exceptional understanding of Reality also allow him to go beyond the passive methods employed by ordinary Higher-Sector factions.

#### Artificial Sector Integration
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] eventually performs an extraordinary operation by physically drawing the smaller Sectors associated with [[Nightingale]], Samar and Arcane into his own Middle Sector. The process is fundamentally different from ordinary Convergence, which involves a mature developing region leaving its protective isolation and entering the broader Higher-Sector environment.[4]
[[The Universal Tree]] compares normal Convergence to a small enclosed region being pulled into an open sea, while Victor's operation resembles drawing several smaller enclosed regions into another protected enclosure. The distinction makes the process exceptionally complicated because the movement of entire Sectors risks disturbing neighboring developing regions or unintentionally drawing unrelated territories into the operation.[4]
Victor assumes his enormous Draconic form and uses the connections established through his fragments to pull the three Sectors toward his own. The fragments distributed among the smaller regions activate, and their inhabitants experience extraordinary changes in the surrounding Reality. The Universal Tree personally assists by managing the neighboring regions, preventing accidental collisions or interference involving other developing Sectors.[4]
As the operation proceeds, Victor's boundary expands enormously and entire galaxies begin appearing within his sphere of influence. The younger Sectors are drawn completely into the region governed by the Emperor, while the Universal Tree ensures that the extraordinary movement does not destabilize unrelated portions of Creation.[4]
EXCEPTIONAL COSMIC EVENTThree younger Sectors were deliberately integrated into Victor's Middle Sector.
The merger required Victor's extraordinary Reality Manipulation and direct assistance from the Universal Tree. It was not an automatic consequence of becoming an Overlord or an ordinary example of natural Convergence.

#### Post-Merger Galactic Reorganization
The integration of the three Sectors required additional work after their boundaries had been drawn into [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s domain. Their galaxies were considerably younger than those of the original Middle Sector, and many remained in early developmental stages. Allowing them to remain scattered without supervision could expose their astronomical structures to unnecessary danger, prompting Victor to reorganize the newly incorporated Galactic environments.[20]
Victor expands his Draconic body beyond the dimensions of entire galaxies and begins moving their astronomical structures through direct Reality Manipulation. He envelops individual spiral galaxies within his power and relocates billions of stars together with their associated systems, preserving their relative positions and preventing disruption to their orbital structures. The movement occurs without damaging the stars or destabilizing the Solar Systems contained within the affected galaxies.[20]
The Emperor subsequently separates groups of young galaxies and assigns them locations where their astronomical development can proceed with reduced interference. Rather than treating the new territories merely as resources for immediate Imperial expansion, he deliberately preserves their developmental potential and establishes conditions intended to protect them throughout their continued growth.[20]
The placement of the three principal worlds requires additional consideration. [[Nightingale]] and Samar possess unusual planetary characteristics that make it necessary to preserve their original Solar Systems, while Arcane can be moved more freely. Victor consequently relocates an inactive Solar System and places Nightingale's system within the new arrangement, positioning Samar's system only a few hundred light-years away.[20]
The newly positioned worlds occupy a protected central region of the Empire. Reaching them from outside Imperial territory requires passing through numerous defensive checkpoints, making their relocation part of the civilization's wider security strategy rather than a purely astronomical reorganization.[20]
[[The Universal Tree]] approves the arrangement and agrees to monitor the younger galaxies while they continue developing. Because the Sectors were integrated far more quickly than they would have matured naturally, their astronomical structures require additional attention. Victor also intends to assign Imperial personnel to supervise the affected regions, combining the Universal Tree's developmental assistance with the Empire's political and military protections.[20]

#### Strategic Consequences
The successful merger changes the political meaning of Sector protection within the [[Elderblood Empire]]. [[Nightingale]], Samar and Arcane are no longer isolated regions whose eventual emergence can be freely influenced by an unrelated Higher-Sector faction. Their astronomical structures have been incorporated into [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s sphere, and their principal worlds now occupy deliberately protected locations within the Empire's central territories.[4][20]
The operation also demonstrates that sufficiently advanced Reality Manipulation can alter the geographical arrangement of entire Galactic environments without requiring the natural destruction or maturation of their original barriers. Such intervention remains exceptional and should not be treated as a capability possessed by every Overlord or even every powerful God.

#### The Four Great Higher-Sector Factions
The greater political structure of the Higher Sectors contains four exceptionally powerful factions, traditionally associated with some of the highest-ranking Authorities recognized beneath the original Primordial Administrators. The rulers identified within the System's Authority Ranking include <Light>, <Darkness>, <Emperor> and <Law>. Their civilizations possess enormous territories, advanced technologies and Divine military establishments capable of threatening entire galaxies.[7]
However, the fourth major faction possesses a fundamentally different purpose from the other three. Its leader is a Herald of Balance, and the organization exists primarily to carry out the primordial regulator's orders rather than compete for territorial expansion in the same manner as the other powers. The faction remains comparatively inactive, and the rulers of the other major civilizations generally avoid provoking it.[4]
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] consequently recognizes that the political arrangement consists more accurately of three major competing powers and a fourth faction serving a regulatory or mediating function. The existence of this distinction does not establish which of the remaining named Authority titles corresponds to the Herald-led organization, and the complete identities and internal structures of the four factions have not all been revealed.[4]
The regulatory role of the fourth faction reflects the wider responsibilities of the [[Primordial Entities]]. Balance supervises disturbances capable of threatening the stability of Creation, while the other primordial authorities maintain functions associated with Infinity, Life, Death, Souls and the broader cosmic cycle. The existence of a powerful organization acting on Balance's behalf allows the enforcement of primordial instructions without requiring direct intervention by the regulator during every dispute between civilizations.[4]

#### Primordial Intervention and Galactic Warfare
The destructive potential of Higher-Sector warfare can attract the attention of the original [[Primordial Entities]] when powerful combatants threaten the stability of enormous astronomical regions. The confrontation involving War and [[Azrakar]] demonstrates this danger when their attacks destroy numerous Solar Systems and produce destructive effects reaching distant galaxies.[21][10]
The galaxies of the Higher Sectors can possess dimensions reaching approximately 10,000 times those of the Milky Way and contain trillions of living beings. Their destruction consequently threatens populations and astronomical structures far larger than those associated with ordinary Galactic conflicts in developing Sectors.[10]
When the conflict begins endangering additional galaxies, Equilibrium and a Judge of the Abyss consider intervention. The [[Vharzath|Great Darkness]] personally assumes responsibility for containing the destruction within his territory, imposing his Authority upon the surrounding Solar Systems and galaxies and forcibly arresting their movements. He also agrees to surrender the Souls of those killed during the battle to Soul, preventing direct involvement by the observing Primordial Entities.[10]
This incident demonstrates that the autonomy of a Higher-Sector ruler does not extend to unrestricted destruction of the greater cosmic system. Even exceptionally powerful Overlords must account for the regulations maintained by the primordial authorities when their actions threaten fundamental cosmic stability.

The material structure of a Sector consists of galaxies containing stars, black holes, Solar Systems, planets and other celestial bodies. The expansion of the [[Elderblood Empire]] demonstrates that a mature Middle Sector can encompass enormous numbers of populated and unpopulated worlds. Sectors can contain supernatural dimensions that do not occupy the same kind of astronomical space as physical planets and stars. The Abyss is not an ordinary mandatory layer contained inside every functioning Sector. It is associated with the deep Universe outside the predetermined Sector divisions and with astronomical regions that have reached their true End.[5]

Its existence reflects the broader cycle through which Creation undergoes decay and eventual renewal, rather than representing another inhabited region that every Sector must possess.
The distinction between the Abyss and functioning Sectors also emphasizes that not every astronomical environment completes a successful developmental transition. Some regions are destroyed or reach the end of their existence, contributing to the larger cosmic cycle governed by Death and the other primordial authorities.

### World Trees and Universal Life
[[World Trees]] are fundamental supernatural structures associated with the development of Life-bearing worlds. Their importance extends beyond ordinary planetary ecosystems, as they participate in a much larger Universal network connected to the Universal Tree, whose primordial function encompasses the development and preservation of Life throughout Creation.

#### Planetary Function
[[World Trees]] nurture suitable worlds and their developing Life, supporting both physical and supernatural systems. Their presence contributes to the maturation of planetary environments and provides a foundation through which intelligent beings, supernatural species and the greater cycles of Life can develop.[8]
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] recognizes the importance of this function when considering the creation of living environments upon barren planets. Conventional terraforming can alter the physical characteristics of a planet, but establishing a self-sustaining supernatural ecosystem normally requires additional Life-related support. Victor possesses the ability to provide such functions directly through his own supernatural authority, allowing him to bypass limitations that ordinary civilizations cannot overcome through technology alone.[3]
The relationship between World Trees and planetary Life does not establish that every planet contains one. Barren worlds, unsuitable environments and ordinary astronomical bodies may exist without established World Tree structures.

#### Universal Network
[[The Universal Tree]] regards individual [[World Trees]] as nodes extending his influence throughout Creation. These nodes contribute to a vast network connecting living environments, allowing Life-related power and information to be distributed through innumerable worlds.[19]

[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] eventually establishes connections with millions of [[World Trees]] distributed throughout his Middle Sector. World Tree integration supports transportation, supernatural communication, environmental protection and the ability to coordinate large numbers of planets without depending entirely upon conventional interstellar transit.[9]

#### Mars and the Imperial Network
The Empire's development of Mars establishes a more advanced application of the World Tree system. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] transforms the formerly barren planet into a central Imperial world and establishes a connection with the [[World Trees]] belonging to his supernatural network. Rather than relying upon a conventional independent World Tree that would be overwhelmed by the resulting information, he uses his own Life-related authority to provide the necessary function and create a suitable central structure.[9]
The World Trees connected to Victor participate in the formation of an extraordinary planetary core, linking the newly established center with the broader network of Imperial worlds. This arrangement substantially improves communication between distant galaxies, allowing information to move across the connected infrastructure essentially immediately rather than requiring the conventional transmission periods previously associated with astronomical distances.[9]
The system is connected to the Emperor's supernatural existence and protected through his authority rather than depending exclusively upon the continued survival of Mars. The World Trees themselves remain associated with Victor's personal domain, allowing the network to retain a foundation beyond the ordinary physical structure of the planetary installation.[9]

#### Universal Proportion
Despite the enormous number of [[World Trees]] connected to [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]], their collective population remains an extremely small portion of the Universal network. [[The Universal Tree]] estimates that Victor possesses less than one percent of the total World Trees distributed throughout Creation.[19]
The comparison demonstrates the extraordinary scale of the wider Life network. Even a civilization extending across countless galaxies and maintaining millions of World Tree connections controls only a small fraction of the nodes associated with the Universal Tree's overall primordial function.
Victor also describes his own Sector as one among hundreds of average Sectors while discussing the enormous difference between his personal network and the universal total. The statement emphasizes the scale of Creation without establishing an exact census of every Sector in existence.[19]

#### Protection of Younger Galaxies
The World Tree system also contributes to the protection of newly incorporated Sectors. Following the integration of [[Nightingale]], Samar and Arcane, the Universal Tree agrees to assist the development of their young galaxies. Because those regions were moved far more rapidly than natural maturation would permit, their astronomical structures require additional attention to preserve suitable conditions for future development.[20]
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] combines this primordial assistance with the Empire's political and military infrastructure, repositioning the younger galaxies and assigning protected locations to their principal worlds. The resulting arrangement demonstrates cooperation between a primordial administrator of Life and an Overlord possessing direct authority over the region into which the young Sectors have been incorporated.[20]

The concept of Overlord sovereignty is associated with individuals capable of exercising extraordinary authority over entire civilizations. The recognition of such characteristics does not mean that the individual has already fulfilled the political conditions necessary to become an Overlord. #### Victor Elderblood
[[Victor Elderblood]] becomes an Overlord after the [[Elderblood Empire]] defeats, absorbs or subordinates the principal independent powers operating within his original Sector. His civilization establishes an overwhelming position, leaving no meaningful native power capable of preventing the continued expansion of Imperial authority.[13]
Victor is explicitly recognized as the Overlord of his Sector and its strongest force during the earliest period of the Imperial Era. Although not every planet has been individually settled or administratively processed, the Empire possesses sufficient supremacy for further annexation to become effectively inevitable.[13]
The development of Victor's personal supernatural authority subsequently extends far beyond the ordinary capabilities associated with a Sectoral ruler. #### Thaloria and Velnorah
[[Thaloria Xyphora]] developed [[Eldoria]] into an advanced interstellar civilization and obtained Overlord status through the political and military supremacy of her dynasty. Her daughter [[Velnorah Xyphora Elderblood Thaloria II]] eventually defeated her, inherited control of the civilization and became the [[Anna Elderblood|Second Empress]] and Overlord of the region.[14]
Velnorah subsequently expanded her influence across additional territories and became known as the Ancient Overlord of Eldoria. However, the Convergence of her original Sector exposed the civilization to older Galactic powers whose resources and technological development exceeded its capacity to defend itself. The resulting conflicts ended the supremacy previously supporting her position.[26][14][27]
Her history demonstrates that Overlord status is contextual. A sovereign who completely controls a protected Sector may lose that supremacy when the region enters a larger political environment containing civilizations capable of defeating or absorbing the former ruler's territory.

#### Overlords and Sector Maturation
Overlord status does not automatically transform a Middle Sector into a High-Level Sector. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] possesses complete political authority over his original region while its protective barrier continues weakening, demonstrating that Sectoral sovereignty and astronomical maturation remain separate processes.[13][3][16]
Political unification may nevertheless improve a civilization's readiness for Convergence by allowing its military, technological and supernatural resources to be coordinated under one authority.

#### Why Overlords Matter to Higher Factions
Overlord-controlled Sectors present special difficulties for ancient Higher-Sector factions attempting to absorb newly mature civilizations. Instead of encountering fragmented governments and inexperienced local authorities, the invading civilization must confront a unified political structure capable of mobilizing the resources of its entire original Sector.[4]
This distinction contributes to the failure of ordinary Higher-Sector influence in a small percentage of cases, since powerful Overlords may resist attempts to redirect or annex their regions. Nevertheless, even a united Sector may face enormous dangers from civilizations possessing military establishments developed across millions of years.

#### Upper-Sector Overlords
The title can also apply to rulers possessing sovereignty over immense territories within the mature environment. The [[Vharzath|Great Darkness]] governs the [[Black Eclipse Pantheon]], a civilization whose territories encompass hundreds of galaxies, while other principal authorities occupy similarly influential positions within the Upper Sectors.[28]
Their extraordinary personal abilities should not be confused with the title itself. An Overlord may possess immense combat strength, advanced Divine Authority and technological resources, but the defining condition remains complete sovereignty over the territory associated with the title.

#### Type 0 — Developing Planetary Civilization
A Type 0 Civilization can exploit portions of its home planet's available Energy without possessing complete mastery over its planetary environment. Such a civilization may develop advanced industries, weapons and scientific institutions while remaining unable to utilize the full range of resources provided by its world.[7]
The distinction concerns the extent of planetary Energy control rather than the mere existence of advanced technology. A technologically sophisticated civilization may remain Type 0 if its infrastructure does not permit comprehensive exploitation of the Energy available throughout its planet.

#### Type I — Planetary Civilization
A Type I Civilization can utilize essentially the entire Energy potential of its home planet. Its capabilities encompass comprehensive exploitation of solar, wind, kinetic, geothermal and other planetary Energy sources, together with the ability to manage major environmental systems such as climate and natural disasters.[7]
The achievement ordinarily requires either global political unity or exceptionally effective cooperation between the civilization's governing powers. Pre-Imperial Humanity had not reached this classification, despite its extensive industrial development and global technological infrastructure.

#### Type II — Stellar Civilization
A Type II Civilization expands beyond planetary mastery and treats its native star and surrounding Solar System as resources capable of being comprehensively engineered. Its capabilities include extensive use of stellar Energy, manipulation of planetary orbits, construction of enormous megastructures and advanced colonization of the surrounding astronomical environment.[7]
The development of Dyson-style systems and other methods of exploiting stellar power distinguishes this stage from civilizations whose principal infrastructure remains limited to planetary surfaces.

#### Type III — Galactic Civilization
A Type III Civilization possesses the technological and organizational capability to exploit the Energy of an entire Galaxy. Its infrastructure can encompass enormous numbers of star systems, while its engineering capacity extends to the manipulation of stars, black holes and other major Galactic structures.[7]
Possible applications include relocating Solar Systems, creating or destroying stars, manipulating extreme astronomical phenomena and constructing computing or Energy systems operating across Galactic distances.

#### Type IV — Universal Civilization
A Type IV Civilization possesses capabilities associated with the utilization of Energy on the scale of an entire Universe. Its development extends beyond astronomical engineering into advanced manipulation of Space-Time, physical laws, entropy and alternate Realities.[7]
The category includes the theoretical capacity to create Bubble Universes or comparably complete artificial environments, together with extraordinary freedom of movement and manipulation across distant Galactic regions.
The Empire of Light provides the principal assessed example. After examining captured information concerning its technology and military capabilities, Velnorah places the civilization within the upper range of Type IV. This assessment concerns one exceptionally advanced Higher-Sector faction and does not establish Type IV as the ordinary developmental standard of every civilization existing within the mature cosmic environment.[7]

#### Type V — Multi-Universal Civilization
A Type V Civilization is associated with the utilization of Energy extending across multiple Universes and the ability to manipulate increasingly fundamental aspects of Reality. Its theoretical capabilities encompass changes to the laws governing Space, Time, Logic and Causality, together with the manipulation of Universe-scale creation and destruction.[7]
At this level, the distinction between technological capability and supernatural authority becomes increasingly difficult to maintain because the civilization's power extends to phenomena ordinarily associated with the highest forms of Divinity.

#### Type VI — Transcendent Civilization
A Type VI Civilization represents the highest named theoretical classification within the expanded framework. It is associated with existence beyond conventional Space-Time and extraordinary power over the creation and destruction of Universes.[7]
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] compares his mysterious Outer Self with a Type VI existence while contemplating Beings whose capabilities exceed the ordinary limitations of the Universe. This comparison is an analogy applied to an individual rather than a formal classification of the [[Elderblood Empire]] as a complete civilization.[7]

#### Source Numbering Discrepancy
The expanded framework is described as containing six theoretical categories while naming the classifications from Type 0 through Type VI. Counting Type 0 separately produces seven written labels. The discrepancy belongs to the classification's original presentation and does not establish an additional hidden Civilization Type or justify removing one of the named levels.[7]

### Exceptions and Abnormalities

#### Existentially Oversized Beings
An individual may possess supernatural characteristics far exceeding the ordinary capacity of the surrounding Sector. Extreme [[Outer Gods]] can experience developing regions as existentially cramped, while [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] reaches a level at which his presence places considerable pressure upon the boundaries of his own Middle Sector.[5][3]
Such circumstances demonstrate the distinction between individual supernatural development and the maturity of the surrounding environment. The existence of an exceptional Being does not automatically transform the classification of an entire Sector.

#### Premature Cross-Sector Contact
Connections between cosmic regions at radically different developmental stages can create dangerous interactions. The Arcane portal demonstrates this problem when extreme temporal effects cross through a connection between regions whose natural development differs substantially, attracting the attention of the Primordial authorities responsible for maintaining cosmic stability.[1]

#### Forced Development
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s civilization possesses extraordinary methods of accelerating development through Time manipulation, technological advancement, political unification and supernatural evolution. These methods allow the [[Elderblood Empire]] to achieve capabilities normally requiring incomparably longer periods, making it an unreliable example of ordinary civilizational maturation.
The surrounding astronomical region nevertheless possesses its own developmental history. Accelerating the progress of its inhabitants does not automatically cause all galaxies to complete billions of years of natural maturation.

#### Artificial Sector Integration
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s merger of [[Nightingale]], Samar and Arcane's younger Sectors represents an exceptional departure from the normal progression of the cosmic system. Rather than permitting the regions to develop independently until their eventual Convergence, he directly incorporates them into his own protected Middle Sector with assistance from the Universal Tree.[4]
The subsequent relocation of entire young galaxies and Solar Systems demonstrates an additional departure from normal astronomical development. Victor preserves their structures during movement and establishes protected locations where their maturation can continue under Imperial supervision.[20]

#### Artificial Influence by Higher-Sector Factions
Ancient Higher-Sector powers can influence the eventual emergence of developing regions by distributing fragments of supernatural power into them. The strategy allows those factions to position newly mature Sectors near their territories, increasing the likelihood that the arriving civilizations will become subordinate.[4]
The method's high ordinary success rate does not establish that every developing Sector can be controlled in this manner. Overlord-governed regions and exceptionally powerful individuals may resist the influence, while the precise limits of the technique depend upon the abilities of the parties involved.

#### Hostile Barrier Manipulation
The weakening of a Sector barrier can allow foreign powers to project limited supernatural influence into a protected region before unrestricted military travel becomes possible. Black Eclipse attempts to exploit this vulnerability by sending Divine fragments into [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s Sector, while the Emperor maintains the boundary through his personal power and the broader Imperial infrastructure.[18][17]
The resulting conflict demonstrates that the loss of complete isolation may occur gradually, allowing hostile infiltration and controlled crossings before the natural transition has been completed.

#### Sector Destruction and Dead Regions
Not every astronomical region survives long enough to complete its developmental ascent. Galaxies can be destroyed, exhausted or brought to their true End, contributing to the dead regions associated with the Abyss and the wider cosmic cycle governed by Death.[5][4]
The destruction of a civilization is also distinct from the destruction of its complete Sector. A political empire may collapse while the surrounding astronomical structures remain intact, just as individual galaxies may cease functioning without automatically proving the annihilation of the entire greater Universe.

#### The Limits of Upper-Sector Sovereignty
Overlords governing mature Galactic environments possess extraordinary authority, but they remain subject to the fundamental regulations maintained by the [[Primordial Entities]]. The [[Vharzath|Great Darkness]] can impose control upon Solar Systems and galaxies within his dominion, yet the destruction caused by his subordinate Gods attracts the attention of Equilibrium and the Judge of the Abyss.[10]
His decision to stabilize the affected region and surrender the dead Souls to Soul demonstrates that territorial sovereignty does not establish unrestricted permission to endanger the greater cosmic order.

A Sector is one planet.

A Sector is one Galaxy.
False. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s original Sector contains an enormous group of galaxies, and even the smaller developing Sectors contain several.[8][4]
Every Sector of the same classification contains an identical number of galaxies.
No universal fixed number is established. Young Sectors contain relatively few galaxies, while mature developing regions may encompass enormous astronomical populations.[4]

False. The approximately 300 seedlings identified during the Milky Way survey represent a local observation rather than a universal rule.[8]
Every Solar System contains a World Tree.
Unsupported. World Trees are associated with suitable living worlds, while Natalia's survey identified them in only part of the examined Solar Systems.[24]
Every Sector is infinite because the Universe continually expands.
Unsupported. The greater Universe's expansion does not establish that each individual protected Sector possesses infinite dimensions. Victor's Middle Sector has a recognizable boundary.[3]

Every Higher-Sector civilization is Type IV.
Unsupported. The [[Veillon Empire]] is a specific upper-Type-IV example rather than a universal standard for every mature civilization.[7]

Sector expansion and Convergence are identical.

False. The Abyss is associated with dead astronomical regions outside the predetermined Sector divisions.[5]

Not necessarily. Galaxies are components within larger cosmic regions, and their individual destruction does not automatically establish the disappearance of every surrounding astronomical structure.

### Notes
01
The four explicitly named Sector classifications are Inhospitable, Low, Medium and High. "Mid-Level", "Middle Sector", "Upper Sector" and "Higher Sectors" are terms associated with the broader developmental system.[1]
02
Developing galaxies are also depicted as an early astronomical stage, but they are not independently established as a fifth formal Sector classification.[5]
03
Sectors are subdivisions of the greater Universe. Even relatively young regions can contain several galaxies, while mature Sectors may encompass enormously greater astronomical populations.[2][4]
04
No universal fixed number of galaxies has been established for all Low-, Middle- or High-Level Sectors.
05
Sector development includes the age and formation of galaxies themselves rather than only the technological and supernatural development of intelligent civilizations.[4]
06
The smaller Sectors associated with [[Nightingale]], Samar and Arcane are compared to newborns, while [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s Middle Sector is compared to an adolescent approaching adulthood.[4]
07
An ordinary Middle Sector may require billions of years to complete its natural approach toward Convergence.[8]
08
Sector barriers protect both developing civilizations and young astronomical structures from premature interference by mature powers.[1][4]
09
Sector barriers can possess directional differences in strength. Victor's Upper-facing boundary becomes comparatively weak from his side while remaining stronger from the opposite direction.[16]
10
Convergence removes a developing Sector's isolation and exposes its civilizations to the greater Higher-Sector environment.[14][8]
11
Higher-Sector powers can influence the eventual position of developing regions by distributing fragments of supernatural power into them. The method succeeds in approximately 99% of ordinary cases.[4]
12
Overlords are individuals possessing complete control over their Sectors, but the title does not represent a distinct personal Power Level.[4]
13
Victor's integration of Nightingale, Samar and Arcane represents an artificial merger rather than normal Convergence. [[The Universal Tree]] assists by protecting unrelated younger regions from interference.[4]
14
Following the merger, Victor rearranges the newly incorporated galaxies while preserving their Solar Systems and astronomical structures.[20]
15
Nightingale and Samar retain their respective Solar Systems, which are repositioned a few hundred light-years apart within a central protected region of the Empire.[20]
16
The Universal Tree agrees to support the development of the newly integrated galaxies, whose natural maturation has not progressed as rapidly as their artificial relocation.[20]
17
[[World Trees]] function as nodes within the Universal Tree's larger Life network. Their existence supports the development of suitable living worlds rather than constituting a mandatory structure within every ordinary planet.[19]
18
The approximately 300 seedlings identified within the Milky Way and Natalia's later local survey are separate observations, not universal statistical rules.[8][24]
19
Victor's World Tree network extends across planets in countless galaxies and possesses an extraordinary communication infrastructure associated with the Imperial center on Mars.[9]
20
The World Trees connected to Victor constitute less than one percent of the total network associated with the Universal Tree.[19]
21
The Universal Tree describes cosmic Life as encompassing astronomical structures, including planets, stars and galaxies. Their eventual deaths contribute to the creation of new structures.[4]
22
The Judges of the Abyss, [[Akashic Records]], Life and Death participate in complementary portions of the wider cosmic cycle through which spiritual information and Energy are processed and recycled.[4]
23
One of the four principal Higher-Sector factions is led by a Herald of Balance and exists primarily to fulfill the regulator's instructions, distinguishing it from the three principal competing powers.[4]
24
Gods possessing equivalent ordinary Divine Concepts may develop independently within isolated Sectors without immediately entering direct competition.[15]
25

26
The Empire of Light is an advanced Higher-Sector civilization assessed within the upper range of Type IV, not a universal standard for every civilization in the mature cosmic environment.[7]
27
The [[Black Eclipse Pantheon]] controls hundreds of galaxies and possesses an ancient Divine hierarchy beneath the [[Vharzath|Great Darkness]], including the [[Seven Black Thrones]] and Ten Chief Gods.[23][10]
28
Upper-Sector galaxies can possess dimensions approximately 10,000 times greater than the Milky Way and contain trillions of living beings, making Galactic-scale warfare especially destructive.[10]
29
The Great Darkness can impose his Authority upon the astronomical environment within his territory, stabilizing surrounding Solar Systems and galaxies after a devastating Divine confrontation.[10]
30
Pantheons, Heavens, Hells and other supernatural dimensions should not be counted as physical galaxies unless their astronomical placement is independently established.
