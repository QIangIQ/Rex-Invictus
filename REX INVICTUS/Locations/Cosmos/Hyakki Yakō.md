Hyakki Yakō (百鬼夜行, also rendered Hyakki Yagyō) is an ancient Youkai tradition in which a sufficiently powerful leader gathers Youkai belonging to multiple clans and species beneath a single banner and publicly marches with them as proof of their authority. Although outwardly resembling a supernatural parade, the Hyakki Yakō is far more important than a ceremonial gathering: it is a declaration of rulership, a demonstration of military strength, a method of recruiting other factions and, when invoked against another established commander, a formal challenge through which one Hyakki Yakō can absorb another.[1][2]
The most prominent Hyakki Yakō shown in the story is the one assembled by Haruna, leader of the [[Otsuki Clan]]. Haruna used the tradition as the foundation of her campaign to unite the fragmented Youkai of Japan beneath one authority, eventually challenging [[Hashimoto Genji]], the leader of Japan's Kitsune and servant of [[Inari-Omikami]]. Her victory and subsequent expansion transformed her Hyakki Yakō from a single procession into the military and political foundation of the unified Japanese Youkai faction.[2][3]

At a Glance
A Hyakki Yakō represents more than the number of supernatural creatures following a ruler. It demonstrates the commander's ability to attract, organize and protect many different Youkai factions. When two recognized commanders contest one another through the tradition, victory can transfer the defeated leader's followers to the victor and radically alter the political structure of Youkai society.[1][2]
In Youkai society, possessing overwhelming personal strength is not sufficient to form a Hyakki Yakō. A prospective commander must first accomplish the considerably more difficult task of placing numerous Youkai from different clans and species beneath their banner. Once this has been achieved, the commander earns the right to publicly manifest a Hyakki Yakō. The resulting procession is therefore evidence that the leader has accumulated not only personal power, but enough reputation, authority and leadership to convince otherwise independent supernatural creatures to follow them.[1]
A Hyakki Yakō consequently functions as a visible representation of a ruler's total power. Every subordinate marching behind the commander represents another clan, species, warrior or faction that has accepted that commander's leadership. The larger and more varied the procession, the greater the political message being delivered to surrounding Youkai. Haruna's first shown procession contained thousands of supernatural creatures from numerous backgrounds, immediately demonstrating that the supposedly isolated leader of the Black Kitsune had quietly built an enormous coalition before revealing herself publicly.[1]
The term is also used for the body of followers under a successful commander. A leader does not merely perform a Hyakki Yakō; their assembled followers can collectively be referred to as their Hyakki Yakō. This becomes particularly important when two qualified commanders challenge one another, because the defeated commander's entire Hyakki Yakō can be incorporated beneath the victor.[2][4]

A Youkai must gather several Youkai belonging to different clans or species beneath their banner before they are considered qualified to manifest a Hyakki Yakō.[1]

Conducting the procession openly announces that its commander possesses sufficient strength and influence to be recognized as a ruler by other Youkai.[1]
Challenge
A commander possessing a Hyakki Yakō can challenge another commander who possesses one of their own. Haruna deliberately invoked this right when she demanded Hashimoto Genji and his followers join her forces.[2]

Victory is not determined solely by a duel between leaders. The challenger's followers must prove themselves against the opposing Hyakki Yakō, demonstrating that the challenger can actually command and support the stronger force.[4]

After establishing the superiority of their forces, the challenging commander must personally prove their strength against the opposing commander. This confirms both military leadership and individual supremacy.[4]
Absorption
Once the necessary conditions are fulfilled, the defeated Hyakki Yakō is absorbed into the victor's forces. The defeated commander and their followers consequently recognize the victor as their new supreme commander.[2][4]
The purpose of these customs is not simply to determine who can kill the most enemies. As [[Shuten Douji]] explained during Haruna's war against Genji, the confrontation resembles a violent recruitment ceremony. The victorious leader must demonstrate both that they are stronger and that they are capable of commanding the people they intend to acquire. Establishing superiority through an accepted Youkai tradition greatly reduces the likelihood that the absorbed factions will immediately reject or betray their new commander afterward.[4]
For the same reason, complete annihilation of the opposing Hyakki Yakō is counterproductive. The challenger is fighting to obtain the opposing forces, not destroy them. Haruna planned her battle with Genji around this principle and deliberately employed techniques capable of incapacitating the opposing army without unnecessarily killing the people she already considered her future subordinates.[3][5]
Haruna's Hyakki Yakō
Rise of the Supreme Commander

#### Formation
Haruna's Hyakki Yakō was the product of years spent bringing independent Youkai beneath the authority of the [[Otsuki Clan]]. Her objective extended far beyond increasing her own prestige. Japan's supernatural society was severely fragmented, with different Youkai clans fighting one another while powerful sections of the race remained subordinate to the [[Shinto Pantheon]]. Haruna eventually concluded that the endless cycle of conflict would continue unless one ruler became powerful enough to impose order upon everyone else.[6]
Her philosophy was intentionally authoritarian. Rather than attempt to create equality among dozens of mutually hostile clans through endless negotiations, Haruna intended to become powerful enough that no faction could ignore her commands. She believed that a single overwhelmingly dominant leader could prevent the weak from continuously being exploited and could stop generations of revenge between competing Youkai groups. Building a Hyakki Yakō therefore became both her method of unification and the visible proof that her plan was succeeding.[6]
By the time Haruna finally revealed her forces, thousands of Youkai from different clans and species were already following her. [[Yotsuba Kuroka]], a powerful Nekomata, served as Haruna's principal commander and right hand. Haruna's military also included supernatural beasts and Oriental Dragons, giving her procession a scale large enough to attract practically every important independent Youkai leader in the surrounding region.[1][7]

#### The Shibuya Procession
The declaration
Haruna did not summon thousands of Youkai simply to celebrate. The procession publicly announced that she had already accumulated enough followers to stand as a ruler and was prepared to challenge the existing order of Japan's supernatural society.
Haruna publicly revealed her Hyakki Yakō in Tokyo. Two small Youkai carrying a black banner emerged first from a gigantic supernatural portal before Haruna herself appeared wearing a black kimono, her nine black fox tails openly displayed. Behind her came thousands of Youkai representing different clans and species. The size of the procession immediately made clear that Haruna was no longer merely the leader of the Black Kitsune; she had already become the commander of a considerable portion of Japan's independent supernatural population.[1]
The event attracted prominent Youkai leaders such as [[Shuten Douji]], [[Ibaraki Douji]] and [[Gyuki]], all of whom understood the political meaning of what Haruna was displaying. Such a large Hyakki Yakō was rare enough that major faction leaders gathered simply to witness it. [[Nero]] and Ophis, who were attempting to remain hidden in Japan at the time, also witnessed the procession. Ophis reacted particularly strongly to Haruna and instinctively addressed her as "Mother", foreshadowing the later revelation that Haruna was the younger sister of Ophis' deceased biological mother, Otsuki Hana.[1][2]
Haruna's parade was not performed merely for spectacle. Its real target was [[Hashimoto Genji]], the nine-tailed White Kitsune who controlled Japan's Kitsune population and directly served [[Inari-Omikami]]. When Genji appeared, he initially believed Haruna's increasingly aggressive rise might be motivated by a desire to obtain Divinity. Haruna rejected the idea completely, openly dismissing the Gods and declaring that she had no desire to spend eternity subordinate to them.[2]
Haruna's Objective
Genji himself — and every Youkai marching beneath his banner.
Haruna publicly demanded that Genji enter her Hyakki Yakō. Because both possessed forces recognized as Hyakki Yakō, the declaration constituted a formal challenge. If Haruna proved herself victorious, Genji and the Hyakki Yakō beneath him would be obligated by their own traditions to join her side. Haruna thus turned what appeared to be a parade into an open declaration of war against one of the strongest existing Youkai authorities in Japan.[2]

#### Challenge to Genji
Haruna gave Genji approximately one week before their confrontation. The ultimatum placed the wider Youkai community in an unusual position: the battle was not simply a private dispute between two Kitsune. Its outcome could determine who controlled a significant portion of Japan's supernatural population and whether the Youkai would continue existing as numerous competing factions or begin consolidating beneath a single supreme commander.[2][8]
Genji represented the older order. He was disciplined, politically experienced and closely connected to Inari and the Gods. Haruna represented an increasingly independent Youkai faction that rejected Divine ownership and wanted the race to govern itself. Their Hyakki Yakō confrontation therefore carried both military and ideological significance.[2]

The two armies eventually confronted one another in Tokyo. Haruna and Genji summoned their respective followers through enormous supernatural gates before beginning their own duel. Haruna's side was commanded beneath her by Yotsuba Kuroka, while Genji's son, Hashimoto Gin, coordinated much of the opposing army.[7]
The scale of the confrontation immediately surpassed an ordinary clan battle. Haruna's three Oriental Dragons confronted the enormous white Oriental Dragon belonging to Genji's side while large numbers of different Youkai fought throughout the streets, buildings and sky. Haruna and Genji simultaneously battled one another in the centre of the conflict. Haruna fought through the flowing swordsmanship associated with the [[Otsuki Clan]], moving as though performing a dance, while Genji relied upon an extremely refined but much more rigid traditional style developed through centuries of experience.[7]
The armies reflected their commanders. Genji's forces were highly disciplined and operated according to a recognizable command structure, but were less adaptable when circumstances changed. Haruna's forces initially appeared much less orderly, yet possessed greater individual freedom and unpredictability. [[Victor Elderblood]], who observed the battle, noted the shortcomings of both approaches and recognized that the ideal military would require the discipline demonstrated by Genji's side combined with the flexibility possessed by Haruna's followers.[4]
Haruna had also taken extraordinary precautions to prevent the recruitment battle from becoming a massacre. She connected her Yōuki with members of her army, reinforcing them and lowering their chances of dying while causing injuries inflicted upon those subordinates to be transmitted back to her as pain. This allowed Haruna to personally shoulder part of the cost of the battlefield while continuing to command the engagement.[3]
During the battle, Victor briefly interfered when the four Dragons began creating excessive destruction around the area. Rather than kill them and directly decide the conflict, he physically threw the Dragons away from the main battlefield so that their fight could continue elsewhere. Haruna immediately confronted him for interfering, and Victor insisted that he had deliberately avoided killing either side because he understood that the confrontation belonged to her.[7][3]

#### Mugetsu and the Collapse of Genji's Forces
Mugetsu
Rather than slaughtering Genji's followers, Haruna employed an Otsuki technique capable of removing an entire portion of the enemy army from combat without physically killing them, perfectly matching the recruitment-oriented nature of the Hyakki Yakō challenge.
As the fighting continued, Haruna determined that she had demonstrated enough of her army's capabilities. She ordered Kuroka to withdraw their forces and personally moved against the remaining opposing Youkai. Genji realized what she was preparing and attempted to command his followers to retreat, but could not do so quickly enough.[4]
Haruna then used Mugetsu, an Otsuki technique that appeared at first to cut every enemy within its range into pieces. The affected Youkai soon discovered that their bodies had been separated without actually being physically injured. Dark energy occupied the divisions instead of blood, leaving the targets alive but effectively removed from combat. Haruna explicitly refused to kill them because she already regarded the opposing Youkai as future subordinates.[5]
The technique perfectly demonstrated the underlying purpose of a Hyakki Yakō challenge. Haruna had the power necessary to slaughter the defeated forces, but doing so would have destroyed the very army she intended to acquire. Instead she established battlefield superiority while preserving the people who would become part of the unified faction afterward.[5]
Before the final confrontation with Genji could proceed normally, [[Inari-Omikami]] personally intervened. Using a spatial opening, the Goddess attempted to strike Haruna from outside the normal battlefield. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] removed Haruna from the attack and confronted Inari, while [[Scathach Elderblood Scarlett]] subsequently entered the conflict. The intervention transformed the conclusion of the Hyakki Yakō battle into a much larger confrontation involving Inari, [[Scathach]] and Victor.[5][9]
Despite this disruption, Haruna's campaign ultimately succeeded. Genji's former position beneath Inari ended and he later served under Haruna as one of her generals. Haruna's Hyakki Yakō became the dominant Youkai coalition in Japan, and Haruna was subsequently recognized as the Supreme Commander of the Youkai and the commander of the strongest Hyakki Yakō.[10][11]
Political Significance
The success of Haruna's Hyakki Yakō fundamentally changed the political organization of Japan's Youkai. Before her campaign, the race was divided among numerous clans, local rulers and groups influenced or controlled by the Shinto Gods. Haruna brought the majority of these independent forces beneath a common banner and created a faction capable of negotiating with Vampires, Gods and other major supernatural civilizations as a unified political entity.[10][11]
Her accomplishment was eventually recognized outside Japan. When the Meeting of Supernatural Beings was organized, Haruna attended as an official representative of the Youkai and was introduced through her position as Supreme Commander of the Hyakki Yakō. What began as a culturally recognized procession and recruitment challenge had effectively become the foundation of an internationally recognized supernatural faction.[11]
The creation of this unified faction also advanced Haruna's original objective of separating the Youkai from unconditional dependence upon the Shinto Gods. While certain groups remained associated with the Pantheon, Haruna's growing coalition allowed large portions of the race to negotiate and defend themselves through their own leadership rather than existing merely as servants, resources or subordinate species of Divine factions.[6]
Legacy
Haruna's greatest achievement was not simply defeating Genji. By weaponizing an existing cultural institution rather than abandoning Youkai traditions, she created a mechanism through which previously independent clans could recognize a common ruler without erasing their identities. The Hyakki Yakō became the bridge between the fragmented Japanese Youkai community and the organized supernatural power that later represented the race internationally.[11][6]

Trivia
- The novel initially describes Hyakki Yakō as a "parade of supernatural creatures", but establishes almost immediately that the term represents a much larger political and military institution rather than a simple parade.[1]
- The name comes from the Japanese Hyakki Yagyō (百鬼夜行), traditionally translated as the Night Parade of One Hundred Demons. The novel uses variations of the romanization.
- Haruna did not create the tradition itself. The Hyakki Yakō was already an established part of Youkai culture, which is why independent leaders immediately understood the consequences when she challenged Genji.[2]
- Winning a Hyakki Yakō challenge requires proving both personal strength and leadership. Defeating the opposing commander alone does not satisfy the tradition if the challenger's army cannot demonstrate superiority.[4]
- The conflict is fundamentally a recruitment battle rather than a war of extermination. Haruna deliberately designed her strategy to minimize deaths among both armies because the defeated Youkai were intended to become her future followers.[3][5]
- Haruna's accomplishment eventually became inseparable from her identity, with her being recognized outside Japan as the Supreme Commander of the Hyakki Yakō.[11]
