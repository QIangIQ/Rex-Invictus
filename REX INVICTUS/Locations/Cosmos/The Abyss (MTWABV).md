**[[The Abyss (MTWABV)]]** Abyss is principally inhabited by the Creatures of the Abyss, Eldritch beings adapted to conditions in which ordinary life should be impossible. (Lore adapted from *My Three Wives Are Beautiful Vampires*.)

> **Rex Invictus canon.** This material is adapted into the Rex Invictus world at the lowest priority: Rex Invictus wins any conflict, then the Flamel material, then MTWABV. Events of the MTWABV timeline (Victor's conquests, the fall of the pantheons, the [[Elderblood Empire]]) are distant background or rumor unless a chapter puts them on the page. #### Inhabitants
[[Creatures of the Abyss]]
The Abyss, also called the Abyss Realm and later described more generally as the Dead Sectors, is a collective cosmological region within the deepest parts of The Universe where galaxies have reached their true End. It lies beyond the predetermined Sectors organized by the Primordials and consists of dead galactic environments in which the normal foundations of reality have already ceased to function. Unlike ordinary empty intergalactic space or the [[Dimensional Void]], a fully ended region of the Abyss lacks conventional Life, Time, Space and even the Concept of Void, leaving behind what [[Hela Elderblood]] described as True Darkness surrounding supermassive black holes.[1][2]
The Abyss is principally inhabited by the Creatures of the Abyss, Eldritch beings adapted to conditions in which ordinary life should be impossible. Many of these beings were formed under the indirect influence of [[Azathoth Elderblood]], whose fragments of Eldritch Power continued affecting Creation even while she was imprisoned. This influence concerns the creatures, however, not the origin of the Abyss itself; Azathoth is never established as its creator. The Abyss exists because regions of the Universe have reached their End, while the creatures subsequently developed inside those dead regions and were eventually accepted by The System as native components of Creation.[2]
After the Primordials discovered what had developed inside the abandoned dead galaxies, the region was placed under the jurisdiction of [[True Death]]. Death watches the Abyss, restrains its inhabitants, controls their access to the Energy required for their growth and destroys them if they become an unacceptable danger to living Creation. This does not make Death the creator or absolute owner of the Abyss. He explicitly did not create it through his Powers and therefore lacks the automatic domain-wide awareness possessed by entities such as The Owner of Limbo inside realms they personally created.[2][3]

### Cosmological Position
The Abyss exists inside the Universe, not beyond it. When Velnorah first explained the creatures summoned by Hela during Ragnarök, she produced a representation of the greater Universe containing developing galaxies, Lower-Level Sectors, Middle-Level Sectors and Higher-Level Sectors. She then indicated a dark region outside the Primordials' predetermined Sectors, associated with the dead portions of the Universe where stars had disappeared and only darkness and black holes remained. The position shown in Velnorah's hologram was diagrammatic rather than a literal claim that the Abyss possesses a conventional vertical location "below" the rest of Creation.[1]
Its existence is tied to the larger lifecycle of the Universe. Infinity continuously causes Creation to expand and new cosmic structures to emerge, yet the Universe is not static or immortal in the sense that every individual structure lasts forever. [[True Death]] and [[The Universal Tree]] participate in the opposite side of that cycle, allowing old Creation to end while new Creation continues appearing. Velnorah consequently described the Universe as simultaneously living and dying: new galaxies, worlds and civilizations arise while sufficiently exhausted or completely ended regions become part of the dead side of Creation associated with the Abyss.[1]
The Abyss should therefore be understood less as an independently manufactured Dimension and more as a cosmological condition and collection of regions produced by the terminal stage of Creation. Canon refers to it as a place, territory and Domain for practical purposes, but its known structure is fundamentally based upon dead galaxies rather than an artificial realm built by a particular God, Primordial or Outer God.[1][2][3]

### Formation and Nature
The Abyss is produced when a galaxy reaches its true End, which is explicitly distinguished from ordinary astronomical destruction. A conventionally dying or disrupted galaxy can still leave enormous quantities of existing structure behind: stars may survive, others may explode as supernovae and disperse heavy elements capable of contributing to future celestial formation, while the supermassive black hole at the galactic core may remain active and continue attracting matter or releasing radiation. Such a galaxy has been catastrophically damaged or destroyed, but Creation has not necessarily reached a complete conceptual End there.[2]
A galaxy that experiences its true End is fundamentally different. Hela's explanation showed that what remains is a dark environment centered around supermassive black holes which may or may not still display activity, while the ordinary framework through which a living galaxy exists has disappeared. The story specifically states that Life does not exist, Time does not exist, Space does not exist and even the Concept of Void does not exist in such a region. In mundane language, Hela compared what remains to the Universe's discarded waste, while the supernatural beings that developed there were described as creatures born from True Darkness.[2]

This is more than the optical darkness produced by an absence of stars. It describes the condition left after the supporting framework of the galaxy has truly ended and is the environment in which Abyssal creatures naturally thrive.
The existence of black holes inside these regions should not be interpreted as proof that the statement concerning the absence of Space is false. The novel itself uses ordinary astronomical and spatial vocabulary to make an otherwise abnormal state intelligible to the characters and reader. What is directly established is that a true End produces a condition qualitatively different from normal interstellar emptiness, conventional galactic destruction or an ordinary Void.[2]

#### Relationship with End
The Abyss is closely connected to the Concept and Energy of End. During [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s earlier investigation, he recalled Hela's explanation that the beings of the Abyss inhabit galaxies whose existence has already ended. They are not necessarily Gods of End themselves; rather, they can appear as consequences of a galaxy reaching that state, including cases in which a sufficiently powerful End-aligned being causes a galaxy to be erased until only its dead darkness remains.[4]
This relationship is also why End Energy can serve as a means of locating and opening a path toward the Abyss. It does not mean that [[True Death]] personally manufactures every Abyssal region, nor does it establish that every destroyed celestial body automatically becomes part of the Abyss. Chapter 1059 deliberately differentiates ordinary destruction from a complete true End.[2]

### Role in the Universal Cycle
The Abyss represents the terminal side of the greater cycle through which Creation develops, decays and makes way for subsequent Creation. The Primordials originally divided the greater Universe into Sectors so powerful civilizations and beings would not freely prey upon weaker regions, but they paid comparatively little attention to galaxies that had already died. From their perspective there appeared to be little reason to regulate an environment containing no conventional life and little besides darkness and black holes. That assumption proved incorrect when organisms capable of thriving specifically under those conditions appeared.[1][2]
A simplified representation of the process is:
Continuing Creation
Development
Terminal State
New galaxies and celestial structures emerge as the Universe continues expanding.
Galaxies develop worlds, civilizations and eventually organized Sectors.
A galaxy may decline, be destroyed or eventually approach its End.
Infinity is associated with the continuous expansion of the Universe.
Creation remains active and its conventional Concepts continue functioning.
If the galaxy reaches its true End, its surviving dead condition becomes part of the environment associated with The Abyss.
The Abyss is therefore not the "opposite universe" or a separate foreign cosmology. It is a consequence of the lifecycle occurring inside [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s native Creation and exists alongside the still-active portions of the same Universe.[1]

### Creatures of the Abyss
Main article: [[Creatures of the Abyss]]
The known native inhabitants of the Abyss are collectively called the Creatures of the Abyss. They are Eldritch beings whose forms, instincts and biology do not follow the expectations of conventional living species. Their bodies can appear as unstable masses of liquid darkness covered by independently moving eyes, tentacles and numerous mouths filled with sharp teeth. Some specimens possess viscous or semi-translucent surfaces with an iridescent appearance resembling oil across water, while their anatomy can continually shift rather than maintaining a single fixed structure.[2]
Their mere presence is dangerous to ordinary minds. When Hela opened a connection during [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s later investigation, enormous numbers of distorted eyes became visible beyond the opening and their presence was capable of driving insufficiently protected beings mad. Even Hela, despite her familiarity with the Abyss and resistance to End, required protection when exposed to the far more extreme Eldritch phenomena produced during Victor's interaction with them. Chapter 1060 further establishes that even beings from Higher Sectors are not necessarily completely unaffected by Abyssal creatures inside their own territory; only the strongest entities of High-Level Sectors were specifically considered capable of remaining comparatively unaffected.[2][3]

#### Azathoth's Influence
The creatures' Eldritch characteristics originate partly from [[Azathoth Elderblood]]. Even while imprisoned, Azathoth could interfere indirectly with Creation, and beings seeking Power could enter her sphere of influence. Fragments of her Power contributed to the formation of abnormal Eldritch entities capable of thriving within dead galaxies. When [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] examined them, he immediately recognized an ancient familiarity in their existence and concluded that they had been born under Azathoth's influence.[2]
This does not mean that Azathoth created the Abyss. The sequence established by the story is that galaxies reached their true End, producing dead regions; abnormal beings influenced by Azathoth then developed within those regions. The Abyssal creatures are also fundamentally different from true [[Outer Gods]] originating outside Victor's cosmological system. Because the creatures themselves were born inside Creation, they were incorporated into The System rather than treated as foreign existences invading from another Cosmos.[2]
The older an Abyssal creature becomes, the more strongly it can develop characteristics associated with Victor and Azathoth's type of Eldritch existence. This does not place ordinary Abyssal creatures on the same hierarchy as Victor, Azathoth or their direct Eldritch descendants; Amara and Roxanne explicitly treated such a comparison as an enormous difference in existential hierarchy.[2]

#### Natural Function
Despite their danger, the [[Creatures of the Abyss]] unintentionally perform an important function for Creation. Even after a galaxy has died, aged or residual Energy can remain. Abyssal organisms consume that Energy rather than allowing it to spread uncontrollably from dead regions into active Sectors, effectively turning the Abyss into a natural disposal system for exhausted Creation. Amara compared the result to a cosmic trash depot, and this useful role was one reason the Primordials did not simply exterminate every creature after discovering them.[2]
Their presence therefore became integrated into the larger cosmic system: dead regions provide an environment in which they can exist, the creatures remove Energy that would otherwise remain within those regions, and [[True Death]] prevents the resulting population from expanding uncontrollably into living galaxies. Their usefulness does not make them harmless. Hela specifically explained that Death was expected to eliminate specimens if they became dangerous enough to threaten active Creation.[2]

#### Hierarchy and Population
The Abyss is inhabited by an enormous and highly unequal population rather than a single uniform class of creature. When [[Victor Elderblood]] personally entered and extended his senses through the region, vast numbers of Abyssal beings surrounded him. His wider perception identified millions of extremely Low-Level Eldritch beings, several hundred specimens roughly comparable to the contractor Hela had previously summoned and approximately seven beings sufficiently powerful to genuinely attract his attention.[2]
Hela's Ragnarök contractor was itself nowhere near the top of this ecosystem. Victor later recalled that the creature was not even considered a fully mature representative and existed near the lower end of the Abyssal food chain, despite possessing lesser minions beneath it. The beings consequently display enormous differences in maturity, capability and individual supernatural characteristics.[4]
Age is important but does not guarantee power. One creature encountered by Victor was among the oldest present yet simultaneously one of the weakest because it repeatedly failed to obtain enough of the specific Energy necessary for its growth. Competition for feeding opportunities consequently plays a major role in the hierarchy of Abyssal life.[3]

#### Growth
Abyssal creatures cannot reach unlimited strength merely by devouring arbitrary matter or large quantities of normal Energy. Their meaningful development requires a specific form of Energy associated with celestial structures approaching death. Known viable sources include stars, planets and entire galaxies that are about to die.[3]
This feeding mechanism is one of the principal reasons [[True Death]] keeps them restrained. Allowing the creatures unrestricted movement would enable them to seek dying or potentially vulnerable celestial structures throughout Creation. Death and the other Primordials instead control which beings receive the Energy released by dead galaxies, while Death personally filters a large portion of that Energy as part of his cosmic function. Access to the energy of dying stars and planets is likewise restricted, making natural competition for the available nourishment extremely severe.[3]
Individual creatures can also possess their own supernatural abilities. Chapter 1060 establishes that some develop unique characteristics while others can possess similar or overlapping powers. The specific catalogue of these abilities belongs primarily to the [[Creatures of the Abyss]] article rather than the location itself.[3]

### True Death's Jurisdiction
Main article: [[True Death]]
After the Primordials discovered the Abyssal ecosystem, the whole region was placed under the jurisdiction of True Death. In this context the Abyss becoming Death's "Domain" refers primarily to responsibility and authority, not literal authorship. Death watches the creatures, restricts their feeding, prevents them from freely entering living galaxies, filters Energy associated with dead celestial structures and possesses the authority to end Abyssal organisms that become unacceptable threats. He also occasionally permits lesser creatures to make contact with individuals who have been touched by End.[2][3]
Death explicitly did not create the Abyss with his own Powers. Chapter 1060 directly contrasts his situation with The Owner of Limbo: because the Owner created his own realm, he possesses an intrinsic awareness of what occurs throughout it, whereas Death must actively observe events in the Abyss. Death himself considers the native beings the true occupants of the region while his role is essentially to keep them "on a leash".[3]
Consequently, describing True Death as the Abyss's ruler is only accurate in an administrative sense. He is its supervising Primordial and the highest known authority responsible for controlling it, but he is neither its creator nor automatically omniscient throughout every part of it.[3]

### Access
The abnormal condition of the Abyss makes ordinary travel methods unreliable, but [[Hela Elderblood]] discovered a method based upon End Energy. Because the Abyss is composed of regions that have already reached their End and subsequently fell under Death's jurisdiction, End can be used as a metaphysical guide toward those dead areas. Hela is particularly capable of applying the method because, unlike her brothers whose bodies are intrinsically saturated with End, she primarily channels that Energy through her staff. This gives her considerably more flexibility in how the Power is shaped and applied.[4][2]
During [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s investigation, Hela directed End Energy through her staff and opened a connection to the dead galaxies. The process immediately attracted the creatures on the other side. When Victor addressed them through the language shared by Eldritch beings and commanded them to show him their home, hundreds and eventually thousands of appendages seized the opening and forcibly enlarged it until Victor could physically cross through.[2]
Using End in this manner is not inconspicuous. Victor later compared Hela's method to making an obvious announcement to the Primordial responsible for the region: it works, but the use of End immediately risks alerting [[True Death]]. This became one reason the creature Victor eventually selected was valuable. Despite being weak, the ancient specimen possessed an unusual direct connection to the Abyss which even the creature itself apparently did not fully understand. Victor considered that connection a potential future key through which the Abyss could be reached without depending upon Hela's much more conspicuous method.[3]

### History

#### Ragnarök
The Abyss first became directly relevant during Ragnarök when [[Hela Elderblood]] summoned an Abyssal creature against the forces of [[Odin]]. Its arrival tore through the surrounding Space and manifested an enormous Eldritch presence characterized by monstrous claws and a gigantic blood-red eye with black sclera, shocking observers who had never encountered that type of being. Velnorah identified it as a Creature of the Abyss and used the event to explain for the first time that such beings inhabited the dead regions beyond the Primordials' predetermined Sectors.[1][5]
Although the summoned creature was sufficiently abnormal to become a major weapon in the war, [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] later learned that it represented only a relatively low member of the actual Abyssal hierarchy. Hela had been able to contact it because of her connection to End, and the encounter left Victor with an unusual sense of familiarity. His developing Eldritch existence instinctively resonated with the Abyssal beings in a manner similar to recognizing something distantly related to himself, creating his first serious desire to investigate their origin.[4]

#### Victor's Expedition
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] eventually questioned Hela directly about where her contractor originated. Hela distinguished the ordinary destruction of a galaxy from a true End, explained the ecology that had developed among the dead galactic regions and demonstrated the method through which her staff could use End Energy to establish a passage. Victor recognized that the inhabitants carried Azathoth's influence and used his own Eldritch nature to communicate with them rather than treating them simply as hostile monsters.[2]
After commanding the creatures to reveal their home, Victor crossed through the enlarged opening and entered the Abyss himself. Enormous numbers of creatures immediately surrounded him, but their presence produced none of the incapacitating reaction expected from an ordinary visitor because Victor's own existence belonged to a vastly greater Eldritch hierarchy. Instead, the environment felt strangely familiar to him. He described it as reminiscent of home, although not remotely as comfortable as his actual home or personal existence.[2]
Victor used the visit to survey the population and then deliberately selected one of the oldest yet weakest creatures rather than taking one of the most powerful specimens. [[True Death]], who had been observing the situation, initially considered the choice unimportant because the creature had repeatedly lost feeding opportunities and seemed incapable of catching up with stronger competitors. Victor's actual interest was not immediate combat strength: the being possessed a direct connection to the Abyss and a mentality he considered useful for future plans. He removed it from the region and eventually incorporated the "Little One" into his wider Eldritch household.[3]

#### Later Influence
Contact with the Abyss did not remain without consequences for Hela. Much later, the Little One demonstrated enough attachment to seek her out, and [[Violet Elderblood Snow]] discovered that Hela's repeated interactions with beings of the Dead Sectors had gradually produced the potential for Hela herself to develop toward an Eldritch existence. This did not mean that Hela automatically became a Creature of the Abyss; rather, prolonged exposure to their type of existence had changed her future possibilities sufficiently for Violet to recognize the potential of an Eldritch Goddess developing from Hela's existing powers over Death, Souls and End.[6]

### Relationship with Other Cosmological Structures
The terminology surrounding Void, End, Nothingness and external cosmologies can make the Abyss easy to confuse with unrelated structures. The defining distinction is that the Abyss remains a product of the lifecycle of [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s native Universe: it is composed of dead regions of Creation, not a generic name for every place lacking conventional matter.
Structure
Relationship to the Abyss
[[Dimensional Void]]
The Dimensional Void is an existing Void associated with the separation and connection of Dimensions. The Abyss cannot simply be another section of it because Chapter 1059 explicitly states that even the Concept of Void no longer exists within a galaxy that has reached its true End.[2]
Nothingness
Nothingness concerns regions outside the stable influence of ordinary Creation and the coherent operation of its Concepts. The Abyss is different: its dead regions originated inside the Universe and its inhabitants were born within Creation strongly enough to be recognized by The System.[1][2]
[[Void Between Cosmoses]]
The Void Between Cosmoses separates distinct cosmological systems. Entering the Abyss does not mean leaving Victor's Cosmos; Velnorah explicitly places it in the deep Universe among that Universe's dead regions.[1]
[[Edge of the Cosmos]]
The Edge of the Cosmos is associated with the frontier of continuing universal expansion and the emergence of new Creation, whereas the Abyss is associated with regions whose Creation has already reached its terminal state. They represent different portions of the Universe's lifecycle rather than alternate names for one location.[1]
Domains personally created by Primordials
The Abyss is not equivalent to an artificial personal realm such as that of The Owner of Limbo. [[True Death]] possesses jurisdiction over the Abyss but did not create it and therefore lacks the same intrinsic awareness there.[3]

### Scale and Canon Limits
The full size and topology of the Abyss have never been numerically established. Canon confirms that it encompasses dead galactic regions in the deep Universe and contains millions of Eldritch organisms, but no chapter states a precise diameter, volume or number of galaxies belonging to it. Because conventional Space itself is absent inside regions that have reached their true End, ordinary measurements may not even describe its internal condition correctly.[1][2]
The Universe's continuous expansion through Infinity also does not by itself prove that the Abyss is spatially infinite. New regions of Creation can emerge while other regions die, but no chapter establishing the Abyss states that its total extent is infinite. Similarly, the use of singular terms such as "the Abyss", "that place" or Death's "Domain" does not establish whether every dead galactic area forms one conventionally contiguous spatial volume; the confirmed point is that these true-dead regions collectively constitute the environment associated with the Abyss.[1][2]
The Abyss should also not be described as a universal warehouse containing everything ever erased from existence. Chapter 1059 metaphorically compares its dead regions to the Universe's trash because aged and residual Energy remains there and is consumed by the native organisms, but it never states that every erased person, object, Soul, Concept or phenomenon is automatically transported into the Abyss.[2]
Likewise, neither Azathoth nor [[True Death]] should be listed as its creator. Azathoth influenced the formation of its Eldritch inhabitants, while True Death later received jurisdiction over the resulting ecosystem. The physical/cosmological environment itself originates from galaxies reaching their true End.[2][3]

### Trivia
- The Primordials originally neglected dead galaxies while organizing the Universe into Sectors because they believed there was little reason for powerful living civilizations to travel into regions containing only darkness and black holes. The emergence of the [[Creatures of the Abyss]] proved that assumption incorrect.[2]
- The distinction between an ordinarily destroyed galaxy and a galaxy that has experienced its true End is essential to the Abyss. Ordinary galactic destruction can leave matter capable of participating in future Creation, while an Abyssal region has lost even fundamental components such as Life, Time, Space and Void.[2]
- The native creatures are Eldritch but are not classified in the same manner as foreign [[Outer Gods]], since they were born inside Creation and became recognized by The System.[2]
- The Abyssal ecosystem performs a useful cosmic function by consuming aged residual Energy from dead Creation, preventing that Energy from freely spreading toward living Sectors.[2]
- [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] detected millions of Low-Level Abyssal organisms, several hundred beings comparable to Hela's former contractor and roughly seven whose strength was significant enough to catch his attention.[2]
- An Abyssal creature's age does not automatically determine its strength. The Little One was among the oldest beings Victor encountered yet remained one of the weakest because stronger competitors repeatedly denied it the Energy necessary for growth.[3]
- [[True Death]] controls access to the Energy of dying celestial objects because Abyssal creatures specifically develop by consuming Energy associated with stars, planets or galaxies approaching death.[3]
- Hela's End-based portal is effective but conspicuous enough that using it can alert True Death, which motivated Victor to investigate less obvious methods of reaching the Abyss.[3]
- The Little One taken by Victor possessed a natural direct connection to the Abyss, making the apparently weak creature potentially more strategically valuable than far stronger inhabitants.[3]
- Hela's repeated contact with beings of the Dead Sectors eventually gave her the potential to develop Eldritch characteristics of her own.[6]
