Nordic Hell, most commonly known as Helheim, is the principal Underworld and Soul Zone associated with the [[Norse Pantheon]]. It is a vast frozen realm governed by Hela Elderblood, the Norse Goddess of Death and Underworld, and historically served as the primary destination for Norse Souls who did not enter celestial afterlives such as Valhalla or Fólkvangr.[2][3][4]
Helheim is one of Earth's major Hell systems and participates in the universal process through which Souls are judged, processed and eventually recycled toward Reincarnation. Its function therefore extends beyond simply punishing the dead: it is part of the greater Soul infrastructure administered by [[The Judges of The Abyss]], while Earth's [[World Trees]] provide the planetary cosmological structure connecting its Heavens, Hells and other supernatural dimensions.[3][5] By Chapter 947, following the restructuring of Earth's afterlife dimensions, only three massive Heaven/Hell systems remained sufficiently important that the collapse of the Norse system could produce consequences across the entire Sector.[6]

### Description
Helheim is an overwhelmingly cold and desolate realm characterized by permanent darkness, snow, ice and violent freezing winds. Chapter 939 describes an endless night illuminated only by faint lunar light, with frozen valleys populated by suffering Souls and grotesque [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] creatures that prey upon or torment the dead.[4] Its atmosphere produces an intense sense of oppression and melancholy, and even Nyx Elderblood, whose original home was itself an exceptionally dark Underworld region, considered Helheim substantially more desolate than the Hell governed by [[Victor Elderblood]].[4]
The realm possesses a particularly abnormal relationship with Fire. Chapter 555 states that ordinary fire cannot burn within Helheim and that surviving its environment requires both an inherently powerful Soul and sufficient authority or affinity over Fire.[2] This makes the Underworld naturally hostile even to supernatural intruders whose bodies would ordinarily withstand extreme cold.
Despite its surface appearance, Helheim is more than a frozen wasteland. Nyx Elderblood's enhanced perception revealed that the dimension possessed a functioning Soul/Ruler system beneath its physical environment, although the greater structure of the Kingdom had deteriorated considerably by Chapter 939. Nyx described the realm as appearing to be barely holding its structure together even though the fundamental Soul-processing mechanism remained operational.[4]

### Soul System
Helheim functions as the primary Hell for ordinary deceased members of the Norse supernatural system. Chapter 629 establishes that the Norse afterlife differs substantially from systems in which the positive and negative portions of an ordinary Soul are simply divided between Heaven and Hell. Norse Souls who die with sufficient martial honor may be taken to Valhalla, while other selected Souls can reach Fólkvangr, but those who die ordinary deaths generally enter Helheim regardless of whether they were morally good or evil.[3][4]
A peaceful Norse civilian who dies naturally therefore does not automatically receive a conventional Paradise. Chapter 954 explicitly states that ordinary civilians are sent directly to Hela's Hell, where they are judged by the Ruler or processed through the automated System.[7] Helheim should consequently not be described merely as a prison reserved for evil people; entry depends heavily upon the Norse cultural and afterlife structure.
The Souls sent there do not remain forever in the literal sense. Like other legitimate Hell systems, Helheim participates in Soul recycling. After the appropriate processing is completed, the Soul eventually returns toward the universal Cycle of Reincarnation maintained through [[The Universal Tree]].[3] The imagery of Souls wandering Helheim "eternally" in Chapter 939 therefore describes the apparent experience and atmosphere of the realm rather than establishing that every Soul is permanently excluded from reincarnation.

### Hela's Rulership
Main article: [[Hela Elderblood]]
Hela Elderblood is the legitimate Goddess and Ruler associated with Helheim. Her authority extends beyond conventional political kingship because the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] is a Soul Zone whose Ruler possesses administrative control over the dead assigned to it. Hela can perceive, command and utilize Souls belonging to her realm, and during Ragnarok she converted this authority into one of the [[Norse Pantheon]]'s most dangerous military advantages.[8][7]
Her position also gives her an authority over Death comparable in administrative scope to other principal Death Gods of Earth's major Pantheons. Chapter 954 specifically compares Hela with [[Thanatos]] of the Greek system and Azrael of the Biblical system, stating that the major Death authorities were deliberately maintained at equivalent levels to prevent one Pantheon's Death God from casually stealing Souls belonging to another afterlife.[7]
Hela's Ruler authority should be distinguished from political ownership of the greater Norse Pantheon. She later sold the key and political ownership of the Norse Pantheon to [[Shiva]] in Chapter 972, but the transaction is not stated to transfer her Ruler office, Soul authority or status as Goddess of Helheim.[9] Unless later canon explicitly establishes otherwise, Hela should therefore remain listed as Helheim's Ruler even though the Pantheon surrounding it changed political owners.

### Norse Afterlife and Odin's Interference
The natural Norse Soul system was heavily distorted by [[Odin]]. Warriors who failed to qualify for Valhalla but were nevertheless considered useful were retained rather than immediately entering the ordinary afterlife process. Odin stored large numbers of these Souls and transformed them into automated metal warriors intended for the next Ragnarok.[7] By Chapter 943, more than 100,000 such black-armored Soul-powered soldiers could already be observed among Odin's forces.[10]
This practice effectively created a hierarchy in which martial Souls were treated as military resources while ordinary civilians were sent directly to Helheim. Chapter 954 describes the resulting Norse system as being in considerable disarray, although it remained functional because Odin understood enough about the System to avoid pushing his interference beyond the threshold that would force Primordial intervention. He periodically returned large numbers of stored Souls to the proper process so that the underlying Soul cycle continued operating.[7]

### Army of Darkness
Hela possessed direct access to an enormous army composed of Souls belonging to Helheim. During Ragnarok, [[Odin]] understood that destroying these soldiers through ordinary physical means would accomplish little because their Souls would simply return to Hela's authority and be manifested again.[8]
Chapter 954 demonstrates the process directly. When members of Hela's Army of Darkness were destroyed, tainted black Souls emerged from their bodies and returned to Hela's Staff. Hela then recreated new bodies around those Souls, effectively allowing the army to fight as a renewable or functionally immortal military force so long as the underlying Souls and her authority remained intact.[7]
Odin considered using an Artifact capable of destroying Souls permanently, but doing so risked provoking [[The Judges of The Abyss]], whose authority governs the integrity of the universal Soul system.[8] Hela therefore possessed a major strategic advantage: conventional killing merely recycled her soldiers back into her command structure, while permanently destroying them risked intervention from entities vastly beyond the ordinary Norse Gods.

### Ragnarok
Helheim became one of the principal centers of Hela's campaign against [[Odin]]. Hela had been isolated there because of Odin's fear of the Ragnarok prophecy and spent thousands of years preparing revenge against [[Asgard]]. Her closest allies included her brothers [[Fenrir]] and [[Jormungandr]] and the ancient END Dragon Nidhoggr.[4][11]
Hela used Nidhoggr's power to destroy the [[Bifrost]] during the Demonic War, severing one of the principal routes into the [[Norse Pantheon]] and causing [[Diablo]] to lose access to Helheim.[12] With the Bifrost destroyed, conventional entry into the Norse structure became extremely difficult because the remaining routes passed through the branches and roots of the World Tree, whose Ego could detect and expel unauthorized intruders.
During the later Ragnarok conflict, Hela mobilized the Souls under her authority as an Army of Darkness while Fenrir, Jormungandr and Nidhoggr served as major apocalyptic combatants. Her actions were directed primarily toward Odin and the Æsir responsible for the persecution of herself and her siblings rather than toward the complete destruction of every Race within the Norse Pantheon.

### Soul Overcrowding Incident
Ragnarok produced an unusual anomaly involving the Souls of [[Odin]]'s metal golems. Under normal circumstances, destroyed golems containing rejected warrior Souls should have continued toward Helheim after death. Instead, their Souls were unexpectedly diverted into [[Victor Elderblood]]'s Hell.[7]
The same diversion did not occur to the Souls belonging to Hela's Army of Darkness; those continued returning directly to Hela's Staff and being recreated. This disproved the initial theory that Victor's earlier interaction with Hela had simply established a connection redirecting all Norse Souls toward his domain.[7]
Even [[Aline Elderblood Valefar]], a legitimate Ruler possessing extensive knowledge of the Hell system, could not immediately explain the phenomenon. The group considered possibilities involving the comparative Authority of Death Gods and Victor's authority over Negativity, but neither explanation satisfactorily accounted for why only Odin's golem Souls were affected. The immediate practical consequence was severe overcrowding in Victor's Hell, forcing his Rulers to modify portions of their automated Soul-processing system temporarily.[7]
The exact mechanism responsible for this particular diversion should therefore not be presented as conclusively known based solely upon Chapter 954.

### Nidhoggr and the World Tree
Main article: Nidhoggr
One of the most important locations associated with Helheim and the Norse cosmology was the separate dimension containing Nidhoggr and the roots of Earth's World Tree. Nidhoggr had spent his entire existence imprisoned around these roots, constantly gnawing upon them while feeding upon the Positive and Negative Energies they supplied.[5]
This location should not simply be described as an ordinary internal region of Helheim. When [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] investigated it in Chapter 946, his perception crossed the abyss between dimensions before locating the isolated space, demonstrating that it existed as its own dimensional pocket connected to the Norse World-Tree structure.[5]

### Collapse of the Root Dimension
The equilibrium began collapsing after Hela opened a rift and allowed Nidhoggr to leave. Creating even that small passage cost approximately 99% of Hela's vitality, demonstrating the extraordinary resilience of the dimension.[5][6] Once Nidhoggr departed, the balance that had existed around his presence disappeared. The World Tree subsequently abandoned the damaged roots and the larger trunk connected to them because it could not simply sever one isolated piece without affecting the dimensions attached to the same structure.[5]
The abandoned dimension began shrinking and slipping outside the normal influence of the [[Akashic Records]] and System. Positive, Negative, END, Nature/Draconic, Death and Hela's Time-related Energy mixed together, creating a state in which Life and Non-Life could coexist simultaneously.[5] Hela's sacrificed Time Energy acted as a temporary stabilizer preventing END from simply deleting the remaining Positive and Negative Energies.
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] eventually discovered something even more dangerous within the collapsing realm: a fragment of a new Soul was developing and feeding upon the entire dimension and abandoned World-Tree branch. The chaotic environment had begun reproducing conditions analogous to the primordial beginning of Creation and was in the process of giving birth to a new Primordial Norse God, likely an apocalyptic Being capable of controlling END strongly enough to preserve the unstable environment while feeding upon it.[5]

### Primordial Intervention
The anomaly was particularly dangerous because it had slipped outside the System's normal detection. When [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] contacted the Administrator registered as <Soul>, [[The Judges of The Abyss]] were shocked to discover a dimensional bug existing beyond the System's awareness.[5] They determined that allowing it to continue could disrupt the entire Soul and Reincarnation cycle of the Sector and potentially produce a chain reaction affecting connected Sectors.
The Judges could not repair the anomaly alone because the abandoned roots belonged to [[The Universal Tree]]'s domain. They therefore summoned the Universal Tree and additionally called [[True Death]] to deal with the excess END Energy.[6] The incident consequently required cooperation among the Primordial authorities governing Soul, Life and Death.
Victor explained that if the anomaly had been allowed to mature unchecked, the emerging Primordial God would have consumed the surrounding Norse dimensional structure to complete its birth. Because Earth's afterlife structure had already been consolidated into only a small number of massive Heavens and Hells, destruction on this scale could have produced severe consequences throughout the Sector.[6]
The event demonstrates that Hell dimensions are not cosmologically isolated prisons. Their stability is tied directly into the planetary [[World Trees]], Soul-processing infrastructure and greater dimensional network of the Pantheon.

### Relationship with the World Trees
Main article: [[Yggdrasil]]
Main article: [[Qliphoth]]
Helheim exists within Earth's larger supernatural structure, which is supported by the planetary [[World Trees]] Yggdrasil and Qliphoth. The roots and branches of the World Trees connect Earth's Pantheon dimensions and supply the supernatural foundation required for those realms to remain stable.[5]
The root-dimension incident demonstrates the consequences of damaging that structure. When Yggdrasil abandoned the roots associated with Nidhoggr's prison, she was forced to abandon the larger connected trunk as well, causing some regions of [[Asgard]] to lose nourishment and placing the entire Norse dimensional system at risk.[5]
The World Trees should therefore be described as cosmological supports of Helheim rather than simply its confirmed personal creators.

### Political History
Helheim was historically part of the independent [[Norse Pantheon]] while remaining internally governed by Hela as its [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] Goddess. After Hela defeated [[Odin]] during Ragnarok and dismantled the former Æsir leadership, she chose not to retain the greater Pantheon as a political possession.
In Chapter 972, Hela negotiated the sale of the entire Nordic Pantheon and handed its key to [[Shiva]] in exchange for several valuable Essences.[9] The contract is explicitly described as a purchase-and-sale agreement, transferring the Pantheon's political ownership to Shiva and the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]]. This transaction should not automatically be interpreted as Hela surrendering her independent Ruler authority over Helheim, because no transfer of the Underworld's Soul office is described.
The later formation of the [[Elderblood Empire]] changed the greater hierarchy again. ### Current Status
Helheim remains an active Norse Soul Zone and [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]]. Its greater political affiliation has changed repeatedly, but the dimension itself was not merged into [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s Hell in the manner of the old Greek Underworld and [[Biblical Hell]]. Hela remains associated with Helheim as its Ruler, while the wider [[Norse Pantheon]] was first sold to [[Shiva]] and later incorporated into the political structure of the [[Elderblood Empire]].[9][13]
The catastrophic World-Tree-root anomaly associated with Nidhoggr was addressed through the intervention of [[The Judges of The Abyss]], [[The Universal Tree]] and [[True Death]], preventing the emerging Primordial God from consuming the Norse dimensional structure.[6]
