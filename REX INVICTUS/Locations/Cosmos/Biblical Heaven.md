Biblical Heaven, commonly referred to as Heaven or the Seven Heavens, is the celestial dimensional structure belonging to the [[Biblical Pantheon]]. It is governed by the Heavenly Father, inhabited primarily by Angels, and contains seven principal Heavenly regions whose individual names and functions have only partially been revealed.[1][2]
Biblical Heaven also participates in the greater positive afterlife structure described on Paradise. Detailed information concerning the universal processing of positive Soul portions, satisfaction within Paradise and eventual return to Reincarnation belongs on that page rather than being duplicated here. This article instead concerns the Biblical Heaven's geography, numbered structure, government, access and known internal locations.

### Structure
Biblical Heaven contains seven major Heavens. Chapter 904 confirms that all seven possess individual names, although only the Seventh Heaven has been identified by name.[2] The Heavens are not portrayed as seven rooms stacked immediately above one another; travelling from the First toward the Seventh requires crossing enormous stretches of celestial space, and Ariel describes the Seventh as one of the farthest locations.[3] The exact total scale of the dimension has not been quantified, so descriptions of seemingly endless travel should not automatically be converted into literal infinite size.

No chapter currently establishes that the seven numbered Heavens correspond individually to the Seven Heavenly Virtues, so the two systems should remain separate unless later canon explicitly connects them.

### First Heaven
The First Heaven is the first numbered region directly shown. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]], Ophis, [[Nero]] and [[Metis]] entered it after Victor forcibly broke through the surrounding dimensional boundary, immediately alerting nearby Angels who interpreted the intrusion as a potential attack until Ariel intervened.[1] The region is exceptionally bright and dominated by white architecture decorated with gold. Buildings can exist directly upon clouds, and Metis remarks upon the nearly universal white-and-gold visual style while Ophis complains about the overwhelming brightness.
The First Heaven therefore demonstrates that Biblical Heaven contains an organized celestial civilization rather than consisting solely of abstract afterlife space. Angelic patrols actively monitor unusual disturbances and respond to unauthorized dimensional entry.

### Second to Fifth Heavens
The Second, Third, Fourth and Fifth Heavens are confirmed parts of the sevenfold structure, but their canonical names, geography and specialized functions have not yet been revealed. Additional characteristics should not be assigned to them merely by extrapolating from theological traditions outside the novel.

### Sixth Heaven
The Sixth Heaven is the formal governmental center of the [[Biblical Pantheon]].[4] Although the [[Heavenly Father]] personally resides within the Seventh Heaven, his throne and official seat of rulership are located in the Sixth, making it effectively the administrative capital from which he exercises his duties as King of Heaven and God-King of the Biblical Pantheon.
This distinction is important because earlier descriptions can easily create the impression that the Seventh Heaven is simultaneously both his private residence and governmental capital. Chapter 1045 explicitly separates those functions.

### Seventh Heaven
The Seventh Heaven, explicitly named the Heaven of Humility, is the [[Heavenly Father]]'s personal sanctuary.[2] Its appearance contrasts strongly with the ornate white-and-gold architecture of the First Heaven. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s group reaches it through an unexpectedly ordinary wooden door within a temple-like structure, discovering a peaceful landscape of green grass, rolling hills and bright blue sky. The Heavenly Father himself is shown relaxing on a simple chair beneath a parasol rather than occupying an elaborate palace.[3]
This simplicity reflects the concept of Humility. The Heavenly Father explains that even Beings possessing overwhelming Power require humility to recognize their own shortcomings and prevent arrogance from dominating their decisions.[2] Entry is highly restricted; Ariel initially worries that Victor's chaotic nature could contaminate the realm because only sufficiently "pure" Beings normally enter. The Heavenly Father dismisses that concern and personally authorizes Victor, indicating that the relevant purity is more complex than conventional moral goodness.[1]

### Garden of Eden
The Garden of Eden is a heavily protected Paradise region somewhere within the greater Biblical Heaven. Its exact numbered Heaven has not been explicitly identified. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] discovers it with his enhanced perception while travelling toward the Seventh Heaven and observes an extensive natural paradise containing lush vegetation, rivers, the Tree of Life and the Tree of Knowledge of Good and Evil.[3]
The Garden is protected by the [[Heavenly Father]]'s Power, but Victor's perception is sufficiently advanced to observe through those defenses. The two major Trees emit powerful supernatural qualities, and their fruits possess an unusual temptation effect capable of stimulating the observer's desire to take them. Detailed mythology and history concerning Eden itself should remain on its dedicated page.

### Angels and Administration
Main article: Angels
Angels are the primary native celestial population and perform the military, administrative and religious functions necessary to maintain the Seven Heavens. The [[Heavenly Father]] created the earliest Angels with complete Souls, while most later generations were formed from purified Soul portions and therefore possess incomplete Souls compared with natural living Beings.[5] Original Seraphim consequently possess far greater natural potential than ordinary modern Angels.
The Heavenly Father holds supreme internal authority over the dimension. High-ranking Angels serve as generals, administrators, messengers and guardians, while the Sixth Heaven functions as the central governmental region. The Seventh Heaven remains far more private and restricted.

### Access and Defenses
Ordinary mortals reach the Biblical Paradise through the afterlife processes described on Paradise, while Angels naturally possess access to their home realm. Authorized supernatural visitors can enter with the [[Heavenly Father]]'s permission, as demonstrated by [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s group, whereas extraordinarily powerful Beings can potentially force dimensional entry. Victor's first arrival involved directly breaking through the boundary of the First Heaven, immediately triggering a defensive response.[1]
Known security mechanisms include Angelic patrols, dimensional separation, restrictions upon higher Heavens and direct protections established by the Heavenly Father around locations such as Eden. The full defensive structure has never been exhaustively explained.

### Current Status
The Seven Heavens remain active under the [[Heavenly Father]]'s internal rulership. Unlike [[Biblical Hell]], which was transferred into [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s Soul and incorporated into Dragons Nest, Biblical Heaven remained in its original cosmological position. Following the formation of the [[Elderblood Empire]], the [[Biblical Pantheon]] became part of Victor's greater political order while the Heavenly Father continued governing the Heavens internally.[6]
