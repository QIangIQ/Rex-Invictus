World of Unconsciousness is a phrase occasionally used in [[MTWABV Series|My Three Wives Are Beautiful Vampires]] when describing a character losing consciousness. Despite the wording, the story has never confirmed the existence of a universal dimension or metaphysical realm formally known by this name. In most appearances, expressions such as "falling into the world of unconsciousness" or "falling into the realm of unconsciousness" simply describe a character fainting, sleeping or otherwise becoming unconscious.[1][2][3][4]
The term became associated with a possible supernatural mental space because of [[Carlos Reiss]]' experience in Chapter 213. After losing consciousness, Carlos awakened within an apparently infinite dark void in which his voice repeatedly echoed, memories from his life manifested around him, and a gigantic formless entity confronted him regarding the consequences of his actions.[5] However, the chapter never identifies this environment as an independent dimension. Carlos returned to ordinary consciousness immediately afterward, the entity he perceived possessed characteristics strongly resembling [[Victor Elderblood]], and Victor referred to Carlos' experience as a "dream". Consequently, whether Carlos entered a genuine spiritual space, experienced a supernatural dream, or merely perceived a manifestation generated by his unconscious mind remains unconfirmed.[5]

### Nature
There is currently insufficient evidence to classify the World of Unconsciousness as a physical, spiritual or cosmological dimension. The phrase itself is repeatedly used figuratively throughout the story for ordinary unconsciousness, while the unusual experiences associated with Carlos and several other characters differ substantially in origin and mechanics.
The novel contains numerous genuine mental, Soul and spiritual spaces, but these should not automatically be interpreted as manifestations of one universal unconscious realm. When such spaces possess an actual supernatural mechanism, the narrative normally explains their origin. Examples include [[Persephone Elderblood]]'s shared Soul domain, the internal Soul Cores of various Beings, [[Victor Elderblood]]'s Personal Universe, and temporary manifestations produced through the [[Akashic Records]]. None of these have been canonically identified as the World of Unconsciousness.

### Carlos Reiss
The clearest use of the phrase occurs in Chapter 213, when [[Carlos Reiss]] loses consciousness after being captured and tortured. The narration states that Carlos fell into the "world of unconsciousness" before he opened his eyes within a dark environment completely devoid of recognizable objects.[5]
Carlos could perceive only apparently infinite darkness, while his voice echoed repeatedly throughout the surrounding void. Memories from his life subsequently appeared around him, including moments in which he helped Human children and occasions in which he killed Vampires who had done nothing to harm him. A majestic voice used those memories to confront Carlos with the consequences of his choices before an enormous formless creature possessing blood-red eyes and a gigantic mouth appeared before him.[5]
The creature's appearance and behavior strongly resembled the monstrous image Carlos associated with [[Victor Elderblood]]. When Carlos awakened, Victor stood directly in front of him, and Victor's laughter was identical to the laughter Carlos remembered hearing during the experience. Carlos consequently became uncertain whether he was still dreaming, while Victor remarked that he appeared to have experienced a "good dream".[5]
The chapter does not explain whether Victor consciously caused the experience. It likewise does not state that Carlos' consciousness travelled outside his body or entered a shared cosmic realm. The event should therefore be described as an unexplained dreamlike or mental phenomenon rather than confirmed dimensional travel.

### Other Unconscious States
The expression "world of unconsciousness" is used elsewhere without any corresponding supernatural realm. Characters may simply close their eyes, faint or fall asleep before the narration describes them as falling into the world or realm of unconsciousness.[6][7][8][9]
These repeated uses strongly indicate that the phrase itself is primarily narrative language for becoming unconscious rather than the established proper name of a location.

### Natashia's Mental Space
Natashia experienced a superficially similar phenomenon in Chapter 230 after being incapacitated during her battle against Niklaus. She found herself within a completely dark world where the only immediately visible person was herself before encountering Natasha, the alternate personality produced through her past trauma.[10]
The environment served as the setting for an internal confrontation involving Natashia's memories, emotions, desires and relationship with her alternate personality. Nothing in the chapter identifies this space as the same location experienced by Carlos, nor does either character recognize a shared geography or supernatural structure. Later revelations concerning Natashia's Soul further establish that distinct manifestations can exist within her own internal spiritual structure.
Natashia's experience should therefore be treated as a mental or Soul-related internal space rather than evidence for a universal World of Unconsciousness.

### Persephone's Shared Soul Space
See also: [[Persephone Elderblood]]/Abilities
Chapter 297 demonstrates a genuine supernatural spiritual domain that can superficially resemble the dark environments associated with unconsciousness. After inheriting [[Adonis Snow]]' curse, [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] was pulled into a dark space connected to Persephone Elderblood.[11]
Unlike the World of Unconsciousness theory, the chapter explicitly explains this realm. It is a shared place created from the meeting of [[Persephone]]'s Soul and the Soul of the person affected by her curse. Persephone used it to interact with cursed individuals and encourage them to willingly surrender their Souls. Ordinary victims were generally unconscious inside this domain and perceived the experience like a dream, while beings possessing sufficiently powerful Souls could remain aware.[11]
This realm is therefore a confirmed spiritual domain, but it is a product of Persephone's ability and should not be identified with Carlos' unexplained Chapter 213 experience.

### Adonis Snow
[[Adonis Snow]]' temporary reconstruction in Chapter 812 has also occasionally been associated with the World of Unconsciousness, but the chapter provides no evidence for this connection.
While [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] reconstructed an Akashic Echo of Adonis, Adonis initially felt as though he were floating in a space completely separated from existence. The narration immediately questioned whether this was actually an external space or simply Adonis' consciousness rebuilding itself.[12] His senses gradually returned as Victor completed the reconstruction, after which Adonis awakened as a temporary manifestation of his past Akashic Record.
The phenomenon was directly caused by Victor's attempt to temporarily reconstruct an historical Record through the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]]'s Soul infrastructure. It should therefore be classified as part of the Akashic reconstruction process rather than evidence for a universal World of Unconsciousness.

### Relationship with the Void
There is currently no canonical evidence that the World of Unconsciousness exists within the [[Dimensional Void]], Nothingness, The Abyss or any other major cosmological structure.
The use of words such as "void", "darkness" and "nothing" during Carlos' experience describes the appearance of his surroundings and should not automatically be interpreted as the cosmological Void.
Consequently, the World of Unconsciousness should not presently be positioned beneath the Void in diagrams or descriptions of The Universe.

### Current Interpretation
Based upon currently available chapters, the most conservative interpretation is that "World of Unconsciousness" is primarily a narrative expression describing unconsciousness rather than the canonical name of a universal realm. Chapter 213 contains an unusual dreamlike mental experience associated with Carlos, but insufficient evidence exists to demonstrate that his consciousness entered a persistent location accessible to other unconscious Beings.
Future chapters could potentially establish a greater metaphysical explanation for such experiences, but until that occurs, the concept should remain classified as unconfirmed rather than incorporated into the established cosmological hierarchy.

### Trivia
- Chapter 213 is the principal source responsible for the interpretation of the World of Unconsciousness as a location.[5]
- Carlos describes the surrounding darkness as apparently infinite, but this is his perception within an unexplained unconscious experience and is not sufficient evidence that a literal infinite dimension exists.
- The entity Carlos perceived strongly resembled [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] and possessed the same laughter Victor displayed after Carlos awakened.[5]
- Victor referred to Carlos' experience as a dream, although the chapter does not establish whether Victor knew its exact contents.[5]
- The phrase "world of unconsciousness" is repeatedly used elsewhere to mean ordinary unconsciousness or sleep.[18][19]
- The later phrase "realm of unconsciousness" is likewise used when characters simply fall asleep.[20][21]
- Natashia's dark mental environment in Chapter 230 has never been confirmed to be the same place Carlos experienced.[10]
- [[Persephone]]'s dark spiritual domain is explicitly a shared Soul space created through her curse and is therefore a different phenomenon.[11]
- Adonis' Chapter 812 experience occurred while Victor was reconstructing an Akashic Echo and should not be treated as evidence for the same realm.[12]
- No known Regulator, System explanation, [[Akashic Records]] exposition or later cosmological discussion identifies a universal World of Unconsciousness.
- The concept should not currently be categorized as a confirmed Dimension.
