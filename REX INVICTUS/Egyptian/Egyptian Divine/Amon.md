### Appearances

#### Debut
Chapter 795
Amon, also known as Amun, is an ancient Egyptian God and a Primordial God of the [[Egyptian Pantheon]]. He belongs to the extremely small class of Divine beings whose existence predates or fundamentally exceeds conventional Gods and was powerful and influential enough to interact directly with ancient [[Primordial Gods]] such as [[Erebus]] and [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]].[1]
Amon first appeared during the final conflict surrounding the [[Greek Pantheon]]. After [[Victor Elderblood]] nearly destroyed Erebus' Soul, an outside Primordial intervention prevented Victor from completing the kill and allowed Erebus to escape. Immediately afterward, Erebus was shown beside Amon and explicitly thanked him for the assistance, strongly indicating that Amon was responsible for the intervention that saved him.[1]
Amon quickly became fascinated and disturbed by Victor. Although he regarded considering a Mortal comparable to a God as practically blasphemous, he was forced to acknowledge that Victor could no longer be evaluated using ordinary mortal standards. Victor was simultaneously a Vampire Progenitor, capable of directly interacting with Souls, supported by the World Tree Roxanne and allied with the Ancient Dragon [[Zaladrac Zeovnur Elderblood]]. Amon consequently understood why Ra had already forbidden the more unruly Egyptian Gods from recklessly challenging Victor.[1]
His caution increased when Victor began permanently killing Greek Gods and immediately resurrecting their corpses as members of his Immortal Army. Amon recognized that the greatest danger surrounding Victor was not simply raw strength but the fact that his abilities repeatedly violated what ancient Gods considered possible. He therefore became increasingly reluctant to personally enter the battle and instead proposed summoning the Egyptian Primordial God Seth as reinforcement.[2]

### Physical Appearance
Amon's canonical physical appearance has not been described in sufficient detail to establish permanent traits such as hair color, eye color, height, clothing or Divine form.
He appears in a humanoid male form while interacting with [[Erebus]] and [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]], but the narration focuses upon the conversation between the [[Primordial Gods]] rather than providing a detailed description of Amon himself.[1]
Traditional Egyptian depictions and later mythological associations should not automatically be used as Amon's MTW appearance. The novel has not confirmed that he transforms into a ram, serpent, goose, frog, crocodile or any of the other forms associated with Amun in real-world Egyptian mythology.

### Personality
Amon is an extremely proud and elitist Divine being. His immediate reaction to [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] demonstrates the enormous social divide he perceives between Gods and Mortals. Even after personally observing that Victor could injure [[Primordial Gods]], Amon found the idea of recognizing a Mortal on the same level as Gods to be almost blasphemous.[1]
Unlike more blindly arrogant Gods, however, Amon is also highly pragmatic and observant. He does not allow his disdain for Mortals to completely override evidence. Once he examined Victor's circumstances—his Progenitor nature, Soul interaction, World Tree support and connection to an Ancient Dragon—Amon admitted that Victor's abnormal strength was understandable even if he personally disliked accepting it.[1]
This pragmatism became even more visible once Victor began massacring the combined Greek forces. Victor's ability to permanently kill Gods and immediately convert the fallen into Immortal Soldiers made Amon reconsider the entire confrontation. While [[Erebus]] and [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]] initially prepared to intervene more directly, Amon hesitated because he believed they were dealing with something fundamentally unpredictable.[2]
Amon summarized Victor's greatest danger through the idea of the unknown. In Amon's view, an anomaly was dangerous not merely because it possessed power but because established beings did not know what abilities it might reveal next. Victor had already repeatedly violated Divine common sense, and every new technique created another possibility that the Primordial Gods had failed to anticipate.[2]
His caution does not make him passive. When intervention still seemed necessary, Amon immediately searched for an alternative and proposed summoning Seth, another Primordial-level Egyptian God. Even after Erebus warned that Seth barely distinguished ally from enemy, Amon insisted that three Primordial Gods should be enough to deal with Victor.[2]
Amon is also extremely goal-oriented. His involvement in the Greek conflict was tied to a wider conspiracy concerning the future of the Pantheon rather than simple curiosity. When Erebus and Tartarus later considered abandoning the plan because the destruction of Greece had become too severe, Amon reacted angrily and accused them of turning against their original objective.[3]
This moment also demonstrates a flaw in his pragmatism. Amon was cautious when Victor represented a threat to himself, but he remained willing to continue a plan that was rapidly destroying another Pantheon. Erebus immediately pointed out the hypocrisy, stating that Amon would likely behave differently if the [[Egyptian Pantheon]] itself were being annihilated.[3]

### Synopsis

#### King Among Kings
Amon entered the story during the chaotic final stages of the Greek civil conflict. [[Victor Elderblood]] had confronted [[Erebus]] and managed to pierce the Primordial God's body while specifically targeting his Soul, intending to erase him completely. Erebus avoided permanent destruction only when an invisible supernatural intervention stopped Victor's hand and allowed his body to dissolve away from the battlefield.[1]
After escaping, Erebus examined his damaged Soul. Someone warned that the injury would require substantial time to recover and suggested that Ra might be capable of helping him. Erebus then turned toward Amon and directly thanked the Egyptian Primordial God for saving him, while Amon responded that Erebus now owed him a favor.[1]
The exchange establishes an important correction to older versions of Amon's article: Amon is not shown healing Erebus' damaged Soul. Erebus' Soul remained damaged after Amon's intervention, and Ra was specifically suggested as the God who might be capable of assisting with the recovery. Amon's confirmed feat was rescuing Erebus from Victor before the Soul-destroying attack could be completed.[1]
Amon was visibly shocked that Victor had been capable of seriously harming ancient [[Primordial Gods]] at all. He initially regarded Victor as a Mortal anomaly but quickly reviewed the factors surrounding him. Victor was the Progenitor of Vampires, possessed an ability capable of directly interacting with Souls, maintained a bond with the World Tree Roxanne and had the support of the Ancient Dragon Zaladrac.[1]
These advantages forced Amon to reconsider Victor's place within the supernatural hierarchy. Treating a Mortal like a God was deeply offensive to Amon's worldview, yet he acknowledged that Victor had demonstrated enough abnormalities for the comparison to become reasonable. The incident also allowed him to understand why Ra had already prevented more reckless Egyptian Gods from challenging Victor without cause.[1]
The situation became considerably worse once Victor attacked the combined Greek army. His assaults did not merely destroy Divine bodies temporarily; they severed Souls and killed Gods permanently. Even more disturbing to the observers, those same Gods immediately returned as members of Victor's Immortal Army.[2]
Amon, Erebus and [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]] watched as the situation rapidly exceeded anything anticipated by the conspiracy. Amon warned that if Victor continued at the same rate, their plans concerning the [[Greek Pantheon]] would collapse completely. Erebus and Tartarus considered entering the battle personally, but Amon suddenly hesitated.[2]
He explained that Victor had forced him to recognize something about anomalies: their greatest weapon was not simply strength, but the unknown. The Primordial Gods had repeatedly believed they were beginning to understand Victor only for him to reveal another ability that contradicted their assumptions. Victor was not even a God of Death, yet he could command the dead and convert fallen Gods into an army immediately after killing them.[2]
Amon compared dealing with Victor to opening a box without knowing what might emerge. Sending Erebus and Tartarus directly into a fight could therefore result in consequences none of them had predicted. Rather than immediately committing two more Primordial Gods, Amon proposed calling another reinforcement from Egypt: Seth.[2]
Erebus considered the suggestion insane because Seth was infamous for lacking meaningful distinction between allies and enemies once violence began. Amon nevertheless insisted that the plan was rational, arguing that even Victor should not be capable of handling three Primordial Gods simultaneously. Erebus reluctantly accepted the proposal.[2]
The conflict escalated further after [[Zeus]] destroyed [[Poseidon|the Master]] Bolt and transformed its accumulated authority into the Territory of [[Olympus]]. Amon was stunned by the decision because destroying one's own Symbol of Power was an act he considered extreme even by the standards of desperate Gods.[3]
By this point, Tartarus and Erebus had concluded that preserving the surviving Greek Pantheon was more important than completing the original conspiracy. Tartarus intended to speak with [[Gaia]] Elderblood and abandon the existing plan before Olympus became so damaged that neighboring Pantheons could exploit its collapse.[3]
Amon reacted angrily and accused them of betraying the operation. Erebus responded that there would be no point completing their plan if the Greek Pantheon was reduced to an empty ruin and reminded Amon that he would undoubtedly prioritize Egypt's survival if the same destruction were occurring there.[3]
After Erebus and Tartarus withdrew, Seth finally arrived in Amon's presence. Amon told him that he had found something interesting and directed his attention toward the battle unfolding around Victor. Seth immediately became interested, setting the stage for his own involvement with Victor and the Greek conflict.[3]
Amon himself did not directly descend into the battlefield afterward. His actions throughout the conflict remained those of an observer, conspirator and outside Primordial power rather than a frontline participant.

#### Dragon God of Chaos
Amon's home Pantheon eventually became entangled in a completely different conflict with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]. The relationship between the [[Egyptian Pantheon]] and Victor deteriorated dramatically after several Egyptian Gods, particularly Seth, became involved in hostile actions against the [[Blood God Religion]] and Victor's wider faction.
By the later [[Supernatural Gathering]], the Egyptian Pantheon had become one of the principal factions under suspicion for conspiring against Victor. Ra, Seth and [[Anubis]] were directly confronted over their activities and connections to forces hostile to Victor, eventually leading to open conflict between Victor's coalition and the Egyptian Divine establishment.
The Egyptian Pantheon was subsequently invaded and conquered by Victor's forces. Its Gods were overwhelmed, imprisoned or forced beneath the new political authority established by Victor's faction, while the Pantheon's former territory was eventually transformed into one of the principal bases of the civilization that would become the [[Elderblood Empire]].
Amon is not individually singled out during the later conquest. No verified chapter currently states that he was permanently killed, had his Soul erased or personally fought Victor during the invasion. His current status therefore remains alive unless future material explicitly establishes otherwise.

### Skills & Abilities
Amon is a genuine Primordial God and therefore exists on a fundamentally higher level than ordinary Gods. His direct combat abilities remain largely unexplored because he has never fought in an extended on-page battle, but his intervention against [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] and his behavior among [[Erebus]] and [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]] demonstrate that he possesses abilities and authority appropriate to the Primordial class.[1]

#### Primordial God Physiology
- Primordial God Physiology: Amon is explicitly identified as a Primordial God belonging to another Pantheon when he appears beside [[Erebus]].[1]
- Immense [[Divine Energy]]: As a Primordial God, Amon possesses reserves vastly exceeding ordinary Divine beings.
- Superhuman Physical Characteristics: His body possesses the supernatural strength, durability, speed and resilience expected from a Primordial-level God.
- Divine [[Immortality]]: Amon has lived for eons and is not subject to ordinary mortal aging.
- Divine Soul: His Soul exists at the extraordinarily developed level characteristic of ancient [[Primordial Gods]].
- Regeneration: Primordial Gods possess vastly superior recovery compared with ordinary Divine beings, although [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s ability to damage Souls demonstrates that such regeneration is not absolute.
- Divine Energy Manipulation: Amon possesses and controls Divine Energy as part of his existence.
The older article classified Amon specifically as a First Generation Primordial God. The presently verified Chapter 795 material explicitly confirms that he is a Primordial God but does not itself state his numbered generational classification. Unless another chapter or Auxiliary entry directly establishes "First Generation", this should not be treated as more certain than his confirmed Primordial status.

#### Primordial Intervention
- Remote Divine Intervention: Amon was able to interfere with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s attempt to permanently destroy [[Erebus]] despite not initially standing directly beside them.[1]
- Barrier Generation (Strongly Implied): Victor's hand was stopped by an invisible barrier at the exact moment he attempted to pierce Erebus' Soul. Immediately after Erebus escaped, he thanked Amon for helping him.[1]
- Protective Intervention: The intervention prevented Victor from completing a Soul-erasing attack that would otherwise have threatened Erebus with permanent death.[1]
- Extraction/Rescue (Strongly Implied): Erebus' body dissolved away from Victor after the barrier appeared, allowing him to escape the battlefield. The sequence and Erebus' gratitude identify Amon as the most likely source of the rescue, although the chapter does not give the technique a name.[1]

#### Soul Knowledge
- Soul Perception: Amon immediately understood that [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] had inflicted serious damage upon [[Erebus]]' Soul.[1]
- Advanced Soul Knowledge: He was knowledgeable enough to recognize that Erebus' injury would require significant recovery rather than ordinary physical healing.
- Knowledge of Soul Restoration: The conversation identified Ra as someone capable of potentially helping Erebus recover from the Soul damage.[1]
Amon should not currently be credited with Soul Healing based on this scene. Erebus remained injured after being rescued, and the chapter points toward Ra as the possible source of actual treatment.

#### Divinity
- Divinity Manipulation: As a Primordial God, Amon possesses highly developed Divine Concepts.
- Air Divinity: Amon has traditionally been indexed within the wiki as a Primordial God associated with Air.
- Sky Divinity: He has likewise been indexed as possessing authority associated with the Sky.
Neither Air nor Sky is directly demonstrated through a named technique during Chapters 795–797. Their exact applications, range and power should therefore remain unspecified until the novel directly shows Amon using them.

#### Divine Perception and Analysis
- Energy Perception: Amon could assess [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s abnormal existence and recognize that his power had reached a level dangerous even to [[Primordial Gods]].[1]
- Soul Awareness: He could identify that Victor possessed the ability to directly interact with and damage Souls.[1]
- Racial Analysis: Amon understood the significance of Victor being a Vampire Progenitor rather than an ordinary Vampire.[1]
- Supernatural Knowledge: He correctly recognized the strategic significance of Victor possessing the support of both a World Tree and an Ancient Dragon.[1]
- Anomaly Recognition: Amon realized earlier than several others that attempting to predict Victor solely through previously observed abilities was dangerous because Victor repeatedly revealed new powers that violated established supernatural expectations.[2]

#### Strategic Abilities
- Strategic Planning: Amon participated in the wider Primordial conspiracy surrounding the Greek civil conflict and understood the political consequences if the plan failed.[2][3]
- Threat Assessment: He reconsidered direct intervention once [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] revealed the ability to permanently kill Gods and immediately turn them into soldiers.[2]
- Adaptive Strategy: Rather than insisting that [[Erebus]] and [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]] charge directly into an unknown situation, Amon suggested bringing additional Primordial reinforcement.[2]
- Political Awareness: Amon understood that the collapse of one major Pantheon could alter the balance between supernatural civilizations, even if his concern initially focused more heavily upon preserving the conspiracy than preserving Greece itself.[3]

### Power and Scaling
Amon belongs to the Primordial God class and is therefore superior in existential quality to ordinary Low-, Mid- and High-Level Gods.
His inclusion in conversations alongside [[Erebus]] and [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]] demonstrates that he is treated as a relevant participant among ancient Primordial-level beings rather than as an ordinary Egyptian Major God.[1]
Amon nevertheless considered [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] dangerous enough to avoid immediately entering battle after witnessing the Demon King's abilities. This should not be interpreted as proof that Amon was individually weaker than Victor at that exact moment; Amon's hesitation was specifically based upon Victor's unpredictability and the possibility of unknown abilities rather than a straightforward comparison of raw [[Divine Energy]].[2]
Amon believed that Victor would be unable to defeat three [[Primordial Gods]] simultaneously and therefore proposed adding Seth to Erebus and Tartarus. The subsequent development of the battle demonstrated that Amon still underestimated how abnormal Victor had become, but his statement provides insight into how Primordial Gods themselves evaluated conventional numerical advantages at the time.[2]

### Relationships

#### Ra
Ra appears to hold considerable authority within Amon's political environment.
Amon specifically stated that he now understood why Ra had prohibited the more unruly Gods from attempting to challenge [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]. The remark shows that Ra had already recognized Victor as a dangerous anomaly before several other Egyptian Gods accepted the risk.[1]
Ra was also identified as someone who might be capable of helping [[Erebus]] recover from Victor's Soul damage.[1]
Amon's later actions indicate that he respected Ra's judgment even while independently participating in Primordial schemes outside Egypt.

#### Erebus
[[Erebus]] was one of Amon's principal allies during the Greek conflict.
Amon saved Erebus after [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] damaged his Soul and was immediately told that Erebus now owed him a debt.[1]
The two subsequently continued working together on the plan surrounding the [[Greek Pantheon]]. Their relationship became strained when Erebus abandoned the conspiracy in favor of preserving Greece from complete destruction. Amon angrily objected, while Erebus accused him of hypocrisy and argued that Amon would make the same choice if Egypt were being destroyed.[3]

#### Tartarus
[[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]] participated in the same conspiracy as Amon and [[Erebus]].
Amon initially questioned whether Tartarus and Erebus should personally attack [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] after witnessing how unpredictable the Demon King had become. His caution ultimately led him to propose summoning Seth instead of relying solely upon the two Greek Primordials.[2]
Tartarus later withdrew from the plan alongside Erebus when the destruction of the [[Greek Pantheon]] became too severe.[3]

#### Seth
Seth was the reinforcement Amon personally chose once [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s massacre made the original plan unstable.
Amon considered Seth dangerous enough that [[Erebus]] immediately questioned his sanity for suggesting him. Seth's inability or unwillingness to distinguish allies from enemies during battle was already notorious among the [[Primordial Gods]].[2]
Amon nevertheless believed Seth's destructive nature was exactly what the situation required and argued that three Primordial Gods should overwhelm Victor.[2]
Seth eventually appeared before Amon after Erebus and [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]] withdrew. Amon directed his attention toward Victor's battle and asked whether he was interested. Seth immediately accepted.[3]

#### Victor Elderblood
Amon's relationship with [[Victor Elderblood]] is defined primarily by fascination, disdain and increasing caution.
Amon initially struggled with the idea that someone still technically classified as a Mortal could reach a level where [[Primordial Gods]] needed to seriously evaluate him. He regarded such a comparison as almost insulting to the Divine hierarchy.[1]
His opinion changed after studying Victor's accumulated advantages. Victor's Progenitor physiology, Soul interaction, World Tree support and Ancient Dragon connection made his abnormal growth more understandable even to an ancient Primordial God.[1]
Amon's greatest concern emerged once Victor demonstrated abilities the Primordial Gods had not predicted. The immediate resurrection of permanently slain Gods as Immortal Soldiers convinced Amon that Victor's unknown capabilities were more dangerous than simply comparing raw strength.[2]
Despite recognizing that danger, Amon still underestimated Victor and believed three Primordial Gods should be sufficient to defeat him.[2]

### Current Status
Amon remains classified as alive because no verified chapter has shown his permanent death.
The [[Egyptian Pantheon]] later lost its political independence after [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s conflict with its leadership. Its Gods were brought beneath the authority of Victor's growing civilization, and the former Egyptian territory eventually became one of the principal bases used by his faction.
Amon has not been individually shown during this later restructuring. Consequently, his precise current duties, political rank and relationship with the [[Elderblood Empire]] remain unknown.
