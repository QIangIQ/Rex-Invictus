### Appearances

#### First Mention
Chapter 667: There Is No Rest For The Wicked.
Apep, also known as Apophis, was an ancient First Generation Primordial God, a God of Destruction, and the former Ruler of Egyptian Hell belonging to the [[Egyptian Pantheon]]. Whereas his brother Ra occupied the Pantheon's celestial position as God-King, Apep governed its infernal side, making the two brothers the principal authorities over opposite portions of the Egyptian Divine system.[1]
Apep's direct involvement in the story is extremely limited, but his position was significant enough that [[Diablo]] expected him to become an ally during the conflict surrounding the Demonic War. Those plans collapsed because Apep instead entered another conflict with Ra and was subdued by his brother.[1] Contrary to an older version of this article, Chapter 667 does not state that Ra killed Apep during this confrontation; the exact circumstances of Apep's eventual death have not been directly shown in the currently verified novel material.
Unlike the real-world mythological Apophis, whose conflict with Ra contains a large body of traditional mythology, relatively little of that material has been directly adapted or explained on-page in the novel. Claims regarding Apep's powers should therefore be based upon his established positions as a First Generation Primordial God, God of Destruction and [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] Ruler rather than automatically importing every attribute associated with the mythological serpent.

### Physical Appearance
Apep has not received a sufficiently detailed textual description in the currently verified chapters to establish his canonical height, facial features, clothing or exact Divine appearance. The novel material located for him is primarily indirect exposition rather than an on-screen character scene.
The wiki depicts both a normal appearance and a gigantic serpentine true form, reflecting Apep's traditional identity as the great serpent Apophis. However, without a direct chapter description, the exact details of these forms should be treated as visual representations rather than a fully documented canonical design.

### Personality
Apep's personality is known almost entirely through [[Diablo]]'s comments in Chapter 667. Diablo expected the Egyptian Ruler to cooperate with him but complained that Apep had instead started what he dismissively called another "childish" conflict with Ra, resulting in Apep being subdued.[1]
This establishes Apep as sufficiently combative and independent that his feud with his brother could take priority over a wider supernatural alliance. It also suggests that conflict between Apep and Ra was not an unprecedented occurrence from Diablo's perspective. Because the description comes from an irritated Diablo rather than Apep himself, more specific personality traits—such as cruelty, malice or a desire to destroy Creation—cannot presently be established from the novel alone.

### History
Apep was one of the ancient Gods associated with the original structure of the [[Egyptian Pantheon]]. He is indexed among the First Generation [[Primordial Gods]], placing his origin within the oldest Divine generation of his Pantheon rather than among the later descendants that appeared throughout subsequent Divine eras.
The Egyptian Pantheon possessed separate celestial and infernal authorities. Ra functioned as its God-King and principal heavenly authority, whereas Apep was its Ruler, governing Egyptian Hell and the negative/infernal side of the Pantheon. This made Apep's office structurally comparable to other [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] Rulers such as [[Hades]], Hela, [[Izanami-no-Mikoto]], Yama and [[Enma]].
A Ruler is not simply another title for a God-King. The position is tied specifically to a Pantheon's Hell and the administration and judgment of the Souls entering it. The greater system of the afterlife is overseen by [[The Judges of The Abyss]], while individual Rulers exercise delegated authority within their respective Hells.[2][1]
At some point before the main Demonic War reached its decisive stages, Apep entered another confrontation with Ra. The details, scale and cause of their battle are not shown. What is known is that Ra ultimately subdued him, leaving Apep unavailable when [[Diablo]] expected the Egyptian Ruler's assistance.[1]
Apep later ceased functioning as an active authority of the Egyptian Pantheon. The exact circumstances surrounding his eventual death are currently unknown. In particular, the statement formerly placed in his infobox claiming that he was killed by Ra is not supported by Chapter 667; that chapter establishes only that Ra subdued him.

### Synopsis

#### Demonic War
Apep was first mentioned while [[Diablo]] was assessing the external Divine factions he had expected to support his plans. Several potential allies had become unavailable because of internal crises occurring throughout Earth's supernatural world: the [[Greek Pantheon]] had become increasingly unreliable, Hela had disrupted the Norse system, and other Pantheons were dealing with their own problems.[1]
Among the figures Diablo had expected to cooperate with him was an Egyptian authority whom he contemptuously referred to while complaining about the failure of his planned alliances. Diablo explained that the Egyptian had chosen to engage in a "childish fight" with his brother instead and had subsequently been subdued. Within the Egyptian Divine structure, this refers to Apep and his brother Ra.[1]
Apep's absence was strategically relevant because he was not an ordinary Egyptian God. He occupied the Ruler position over the Pantheon's Hell and belonged to the First Generation Primordial class. Diablo had therefore expected cooperation from one of the [[Egyptian Pantheon]]'s highest-ranking infernal authorities, only to lose that support because of Apep's internal conflict with Ra.
Apep does not personally appear during the resulting Demonic War, and the novel does not reveal the techniques Ra used to subdue him or whether the confrontation left Apep imprisoned, incapacitated or otherwise prevented from participating.

#### Dragon God of Chaos
The [[Egyptian Pantheon]] later became one of the Divine civilizations caught in the expansion of [[Victor Elderblood]]'s power. By this period Apep was no longer shown functioning as an active defender or Ruler, while Ra and the surviving Egyptian Gods became the principal characters associated with the Pantheon.
Victor's campaign eventually dismantled the Egyptian Pantheon's independent political power.[3] Its former territories were subsequently incorporated into Victor's growing civilization, with the ancient Egyptian lands becoming a homeland for populations and factions aligned with him.[4]
Apep does not receive an on-screen battle during the Egyptian conflict. Consequently, the later fall of the Egyptian Pantheon cannot be used as a direct combat feat against him, and the novel has not yet provided a scene connecting his death specifically to Victor's conquest.

### Skills & Abilities
Apep has very few direct combat demonstrations. His confirmed capabilities are therefore best indexed through the Divine classifications and offices explicitly associated with him rather than through speculative abilities taken from Egyptian mythology.

#### First Generation Primordial God Physiology
First Generation Primordial God: Apep belonged to the oldest Primordial generation of the [[Egyptian Pantheon]]. First Generation [[Primordial Gods]] are ancient Divine existences fundamentally above ordinary Gods in origin and quality, although generation alone does not establish an exact combat level.
- Divine Physiology: As a God, Apep possessed a fully transformed Divine existence rather than mortal biology. Ascension into Godhood changes the Soul itself into a Divine form of existence.[5]
- Divine Soul: Apep possessed a Divine Soul capable of sustaining his Divinities and [[Divine Energy]].[5]
- Divine Energy Manipulation: As a Primordial God, Apep possessed and could manipulate Divine Energy. The novel has not shown the appearance, quantity or specific techniques associated with his personal Divine Energy.
- Agelessness and Longevity: Apep had existed for eons and belonged to an ancient Divine generation. Ordinary mortal aging was therefore irrelevant to his lifespan.
- Superhuman Physical Characteristics: His Divine existence naturally placed his physical capabilities beyond mortal standards, although no direct strength, speed or durability feat has currently been shown for him.
- Primordial-Level Existence: His classification places him among the Egyptian Pantheon's oldest and highest-order Gods rather than its ordinary later-generation Divine population. This should not be confused with being a Primordial Entity, which is a later and fundamentally higher state of existence.

#### Destruction Divinity
God of Destruction: Apep is classified among Earth's Gods of Destruction, placing Destruction among the central aspects of his Divine nature.
- Destruction Manipulation: Through his Destruction Divinity, Apep possessed Divine authority associated with destruction. The precise applications of this authority have not been demonstrated on-screen.
- Destruction Energy Manipulation: Gods aligned with Destruction can manifest their Divinity through destructive [[Divine Energy]]. Apep belongs to the same broad Divine category as other Destruction Gods, although no named Apep-specific Destruction technique has been revealed.
- Conceptual Authority — Destruction: Divinities function through the Concepts represented by their Gods. Apep's classification consequently gives him Divine authority over Destruction, but the degree of mastery he achieved over that Concept remains unknown.
- Enhanced Destructive Power: Destruction is inherently suited to offensive applications, making Apep a destructive Primordial-class existence by nature. No direct feat currently establishes the maximum scale of this power.
Apep should not automatically be given END merely because several other destructive beings possess it. Destruction and END are separate powers in the setting, and no verified chapter currently identifies Apep as an END Being.

#### Ruler Authority
Apep was the Ruler of Egyptian Hell, making him the principal infernal authority of the [[Egyptian Pantheon]] while Ra served as its celestial God-King.[1]
- [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] Lordship: Apep possessed administrative authority over the Egyptian Underworld and its supernatural system.
- Hell Authority: His status as Ruler granted authority specifically connected to Egyptian Hell rather than the celestial territories administered by Ra.
- Soul Judgment: Rulers are responsible for the judgment of Souls entering their respective Hells under the wider supervision of [[The Judges of The Abyss]].[2][1]
- Soul Interaction: Performing the functions of a Ruler requires the ability to interact with Souls and spiritual existences rather than being limited to material beings.
- Spiritual Administration: Apep's authority included the governance of an afterlife system populated by deceased Souls, placing him among the supernatural administrators responsible for maintaining Earth's post-mortem order.
- Infernal Authority: A Ruler occupies a position comparable in importance to a God-King within the infernal half of a developed Pantheon's structure, although their responsibilities and jurisdictions are different.[1]
These abilities represent functions inherent to Apep's confirmed office. The novel does not provide a dedicated scene of Apep personally judging a Soul, issuing an infernal decree or using the full authority of Egyptian Hell in battle.

#### Divine Authority
Divinity Manipulation: As an ancient Primordial God possessing established Divine Domains, Apep could wield Divinity as a supernatural authority. His only currently well-supported personal Divinity is Destruction; older versions of the page assigned several additional Domains without sufficient chapter evidence.
Conceptual Manipulation: Gods interact with the Concepts embodied by their Divinities. In Apep's case, this is safely applicable to his established Destruction Divinity, but there is presently insufficient evidence to give him unrestricted Concept Manipulation over unrelated phenomena.

#### Combat Capability
Apep receives no complete battle scene, preventing a detailed reconstruction of his fighting style. Nevertheless, several points can be established:
- He was an ancient First Generation Primordial God rather than an ordinary member of the [[Egyptian Pantheon]].
- He possessed an offensive Divinity centered upon Destruction.
- He governed one of Earth's major Pantheon Hells and was important enough for [[Diablo]] to have expected his participation as a major Divine ally.[1]
- Ra personally subdued Apep during their conflict, demonstrating that the confrontation involved the Egyptian God-King rather than being handled by an ordinary subordinate.[1]
The final point does not mean that Apep was weak. Ra himself belonged to the highest strata of the Egyptian Pantheon. The novel simply does not describe enough of their confrontation to determine how difficult the victory was, what abilities were used or whether outside circumstances influenced the result.

#### Unknown or Unverified Abilities
Older versions of Apep's article listed him as the God of Earthquakes, God of Thunder, God of Darkness, God of Storms and God of Death. No direct novel evidence located during the current source audit demonstrates these as Apep's Divinities or shows him using corresponding abilities.
They have therefore not been indexed as confirmed powers. His Egyptian mythological associations should not be treated as automatic evidence for abilities within the novel continuity.
Likewise, Apep's mythological association with primordial [[Chaos]] does not by itself grant him Chaos Manipulation, [[Primordial Chaos]], Reality Warping or the powers later associated with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s Chaos Authority.

#### Limitations
- Subdued by Ra: Whatever the circumstances of their confrontation, Ra successfully subdued Apep before Chapter 667.[1]
- No Demonstrated Combat Techniques: Apep has no presently verified on-screen battle, named attack, weapon or martial style.
- Unknown Conceptual Mastery: Although Apep was a God of Destruction, the story has not revealed how developed his mastery over Destruction was compared with figures such as [[Shiva]], Kali or later [[Victor Elderblood]].
- Unknown Death: The exact method and perpetrator of Apep's eventual death have not been directly established in the verified chapters. Ra defeating and subduing him should not be rewritten as Ra killing him without additional evidence.

### Relationships

#### Ra
Ra was Apep's brother and the principal celestial authority of the [[Egyptian Pantheon]]. Their positions created a fundamental division in the Pantheon's government: Ra was its God-King, while Apep governed its Hell.
The brothers had an openly hostile relationship. By Chapter 667, they had entered another conflict severe enough for Ra to subdue Apep completely and remove him from the wider supernatural situation. [[Diablo]]'s wording portrays the confrontation as part of an existing pattern rather than an entirely unprecedented dispute.[1]
The actual cause of the fight is never explained. Likewise, there is no verified statement that Ra killed Apep during this encounter. Their relationship should therefore be described as one of hostile Divine siblings rather than reproducing the complete Ra-versus-Apophis cycle from real-world Egyptian religion as though every mythological event occurred in the novel.

#### Diablo
[[Diablo]] considered Apep a potential ally during his preparations for the Demonic War. The alliance was never realized because Apep had already fought Ra and been subdued.[1]
Diablo's frustration is important because it indicates Apep's strategic relevance. He was not merely an obscure Egyptian deity; he was a Primordial God and [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] Ruler whose participation was valuable enough to factor into the Demon King's planning.
There is no evidence that Apep formally joined Diablo's faction before being subdued.

#### The Judges of The Abyss
As an [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] Ruler, Apep's office ultimately existed beneath the greater afterlife system administered by [[The Judges of The Abyss]]. The Judges supervise the movement and judgment of Souls, while individual Rulers administer the Hells belonging to developed Pantheons.[2]
No direct personal interaction between Apep and a Judge has presently been shown.

#### Egyptian Pantheon
Apep was one of the highest authorities of the [[Egyptian Pantheon]]. While Ra governed its celestial side, Apep governed its infernal domain. The Pantheon also contained Gods such as [[Amon]], Seth, [[Anubis]], [[Thoth]], [[Ptah]], [[Khons]], Kuk and [[Upuaut]].
His exact personal relationships with these Gods have not been explored.

### Mythological Basis
In real-world Ancient Egyptian mythology, Apep or Apophis is a gigantic serpent associated with [[Chaos]] and the principal enemy of the solar deity Ra. Traditional accounts frequently portray Apep attempting to obstruct Ra's solar journey and being repeatedly opposed or defeated by Ra and other Divine protectors.
The novel borrows Apep's identity, his antagonistic relationship with Ra and his association with destructive forces, but the complete mythology should not be assumed to have occurred identically within the story. In particular, the traditional serpent's associations are not evidence that the novel's Apep automatically possesses every power connected to darkness, storms, thunder, earthquakes, death or Chaos.

- Chapter 667 says that Apep was subdued by Ra. It does not state that Ra killed him.[1]
- The exact circumstances of Apep's eventual death remain unshown in the currently verified chapters.
- Older versions of the article incorrectly converted Apep's mythological attributes into confirmed novel Divinities, listing Earthquakes, Thunder, Darkness, Storms and Death despite no currently verified chapter demonstrating those Domains.
- Apep's status as a God of Destruction is supported by the series' Divine classification, but no named Destruction technique has been shown.
- Despite being associated with [[Chaos]] in real-world Egyptian mythology, Apep should not be confused with Chaos, [[Primordial Chaos]] or [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s later Chaos Authority.
- No verified chapter currently provides a detailed physical description of Apep's normal or true form.
- Apep does not participate on-screen when Victor Elderblood later dismantles the Egyptian Pantheon's independent power.[3][4]
