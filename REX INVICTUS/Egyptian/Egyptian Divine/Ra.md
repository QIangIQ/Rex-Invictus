Ra is the former Primordial God of the Sun, God of the Sky, God-King and former supreme leader of the [[Egyptian Pantheon]]. An ancient Primordial God whose authority extended over both his own Divinity and the Divine Dimension of his Pantheon, Ra was regarded as a genuine God-King rather than merely the strongest individual among the Egyptian Gods. His control over the Egyptian Pantheon was sufficiently complete that he could seal its Dimension almost entirely from outside interference.[1][2]
Ra became one of [[Victor Elderblood]]'s principal Divine enemies after members of the Egyptian Pantheon became involved in a conspiracy targeting Victor and his family. Although Victor eventually narrowed his declaration of war from the entire Pantheon to Ra, [[Anubis]], Seth and their associates, Ra remained one of the primary targets because Victor considered the God-King responsible for actions taken under his leadership.[3]
During Victor's invasion of the Egyptian Pantheon, Ra became the only Egyptian God present who resisted [[Aphrodite]] Elderblood's enhanced Divine Charm. He was nevertheless completely overwhelmed by Victor, who invaded Ra's Soul, forcibly extracted and consumed his Sun Divinity, restored the resulting Soul damage only enough to keep him alive and deliberately reduced the ancient Primordial God into an ordinary Mortal. Ra was left permanently incapacitated and unable to recover his former growth even if his Soul healed completely.[4][5]

### Physical Appearance
Ra's ordinary physical appearance has not been described in sufficient detail for a reliable account of his height, hair, eyes or skin color. During his first physical appearance at the [[Supernatural Gathering]], he arrived alongside the severely injured Seth and [[Anubis]], but the narration did not provide a detailed description of Ra himself.[6]
His Divine state is considerably better documented. When activating his Sun Divinity against [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]], Ra's body became enveloped in a brilliant golden glow and continuously increased the surrounding temperature. When he pushed his Divinity to its maximum, his entire body became completely golden as he effectively became one with the Sun itself. His exterior reached temperatures comparable to the surface of the Sun, while the inside of his Divine body was stated to be even hotter.[5]
Following the extraction of his Sun Divinity, the golden radiance disappeared immediately. Victor later restored Ra's heart and repaired part of the damage to his Soul, allowing him to awaken in an ordinary Mortal state without the Divine manifestation that had formerly defined his existence.[5]

### Personality
Ra was historically characterized as a considerably more cautious and pragmatic God-King than many other Divine rulers. Long before [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] formally became his enemy, [[Amon]] revealed that Ra had prohibited the more unruly Egyptian Gods from recklessly challenging Victor, demonstrating that Ra had already recognized the danger represented by the rapidly growing Vampire Progenitor.[7] [[Gaia]], who was familiar with Ra's behavior, similarly described him as someone who knew how to choose his battles and considered the idea of him recklessly provoking Victor strange enough to suspect that something unusual was influencing the [[Egyptian Pantheon]]'s decisions.[1]
This caution extended into his leadership. Once Victor and several Pantheons openly turned against him, Ra did not wait for an invasion to begin. He immediately isolated the Egyptian Pantheon and used his authority as God-King to seal its entire Divine Dimension. Unlike [[Zeus]], whose fractured authority over [[Olympus]] prevented complete isolation, Ra possessed enough influence over his unified Pantheon to almost completely close it, although the merger of multiple Paradises meant that tiny spatial cracks still remained.[2]
Ra's actions surrounding the conspiracy nevertheless contradicted the careful reputation others associated with him. When confronted directly, he attempted to shift responsibility toward Seth, telling Victor that Seth had been responsible for the situation. Victor rejected the distinction, arguing that Ra had still acted as the Leader whose Pantheon targeted his wives and daughters. Once it became obvious that his Divinity could not affect Victor, Ra's confidence rapidly collapsed into genuine terror.[5]
His greatest psychological collapse came after the loss of his Godhood. Ra considered existence as a powerless Mortal so intolerable that he immediately demanded death rather than continue living in that state. Victor's declaration that even suicide would not free him because he could simply resurrect Ra drove him into even greater despair. The shock and fear ultimately caused his newly Mortal heart to stop, temporarily killing him before Victor immediately revived him with [[Natural Energy]].[5]

### History
Little of Ra's ancient history is directly explored in the novel. He had already existed for eons before the main story and occupied the highest position within the positive or heavenly side of the Egyptian Divine structure as its God-King. By the period immediately preceding his direct involvement with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]], he possessed enough political and Divine authority that his decisions applied across the Pantheon rather than merely to his personal followers.[7][2]
Ra was also regarded as someone knowledgeable about Souls. When [[Erebus]] suffered serious Soul damage during his confrontation with Victor, [[Amon]] specifically suggested that Erebus visit Ra because Ra might be capable of helping him recover. The statement came from another Primordial God and establishes that Ra possessed, or was at least widely believed to possess, methods capable of treating damage to the Soul itself.[7]

### Synopsis

#### King Among Kings
Ra's earliest verified mention occurred during the conflict surrounding the [[Greek Pantheon]]. After [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] damaged [[Erebus]]' Soul, [[Amon]] recommended seeking Ra's assistance, suggesting that the Egyptian God-King might be capable of repairing the injury. During the same conversation, Amon revealed that Ra had previously forbidden unruly Egyptian Gods from recklessly challenging Victor, demonstrating that Ra had already been monitoring the increasingly dangerous anomaly Victor represented.[7]
Shortly before the Third [[Supernatural Gathering]], [[Gaia]] discussed the developing Egyptian crisis with Victor's family. She found the actions attributed to the Pantheon suspicious because the Ra she knew was a careful God-King who selected his battles rather than provoking an opponent without considering the consequences. She specifically identified him as the God of the Sky and God-King of the [[Egyptian Pantheon]]. The possibility that Ra would willingly confront Victor after Victor had become a Dragon Progenitor therefore appeared abnormal enough that Gaia considered either independent action by Seth or previously unknown Egyptian internal politics more plausible explanations.[1]
Ra physically debuted during the Gathering itself, arriving alongside [[Anubis]] and a badly injured Seth. His presence placed the leadership of the Egyptian Pantheon directly before Victor and the other major supernatural powers at a meeting that had effectively become an international reckoning over the conspiracies developing throughout the supernatural world.[6]
The confrontation ultimately ended with Victor revising his initial declaration of war. Rather than targeting every Egyptian God indiscriminately, Victor publicly declared that his enemies would specifically be Ra, Anubis, Seth and the associates responsible for the plan. The [[Heavenly Father]] and [[Shiva]] accepted this distinction, leaving the three Egyptian Gods aware that some of the strongest forces in the supernatural world had effectively agreed to hunt them once the Gathering ended.[3]
Ra responded quickly. Knowing that Victor's coalition would come for him, he completely sealed the Egyptian Pantheon's Dimension. His ability to do so demonstrated the difference between his position and that of [[Zeus]]: Ra's control and influence over his Pantheon were sufficiently complete for the narration to explicitly describe him as a true God-King. The defense was not perfect, however. Because several Divine Paradises had already been merged into a larger structure, microscopic cracks remained between them. Victor located one of those spatial imperfections and Rose Elderblood forcibly opened a route through it, allowing the coalition to enter Ra's supposedly isolated domain.[2]
The Egyptian Pantheon itself was unusually unified compared with the divided [[Olympus]] Victor had previously invaded. Victor consequently brought numerous elite combatants rather than assuming that the Gods could be defeated piecemeal. The expected battle never developed as planned because Velnorah demonstrated the overwhelming technological and supernatural advantages of an Overlord: she captured 76 of the 77 Gods designated on Victor's target list in a single operation, deliberately leaving Ra for Victor and his companions.[4]
[[Aphrodite]] then released the strengthened influence of her Divinity throughout the Dimension. Every Egyptian God affected by it succumbed to her Charm with the sole exception of Ra, demonstrating that the God-King possessed sufficient resistance to retain his will despite an ability capable of subjugating virtually the entire Pantheon simultaneously. With the remaining Gods neutralized, Victor proceeded toward Ra's golden palace.[4]
Ra waited within the palace on his throne and attempted to negotiate when Victor entered. Victor refused. His anger over the actions against his wives and daughters caused his body and powers to distort into an increasingly Eldritch appearance as he advanced toward the God-King. Ra activated his Sun Divinity, surrounding himself with golden light and increasing the temperature of the throne room before insisting that Seth had been responsible for the conspiracy. Victor dismissed the argument because Ra had nevertheless been the Leader who participated in the attack against his family.[4][5]
Ra then pushed his Sun Divinity to its maximum. His body reached temperatures comparable to the Sun's surface before becoming completely golden as he became one with the Sun itself. The transformation accomplished nothing against Victor, who held him directly despite the extreme heat. Victor pierced Ra's chest and began manipulating the Primordial God's Soul, forcibly extracting a major component of Ra's existence while the God screamed from the unprecedented damage.[5]
Victor eventually removed Ra's still-beating heart together with the golden Energy representing his Sun Divinity. After consuming the Divinity, Victor immediately acquired a direct connection to the Sun existing inside his Personal Universe, allowing him to control its solar power directly instead of reproducing similar effects through his Dragon Progenitor abilities.[5]
Rather than allow Ra to die from the procedure, Victor returned his still-beating heart to his body and used Nature and Negative Energy to repair the Soul damage. The restoration was intentionally incomplete at the existential level: Ra had been permanently incapacitated and would no longer be capable of becoming stronger even if the remaining damage to his Soul completely recovered. When Ra regained consciousness and demanded to know what had happened, Victor informed him that he had been transformed into a Mortal, making Ra the first Primordial God explicitly reduced to mortality in this manner.[5]
Ra reacted with horror and asked Victor to kill him rather than force him to live without his Godhood. Victor instead stated that Ra would not be permitted to escape through death and that he could simply resurrect him whenever necessary. The realization terrified Ra so severely that his new Mortal heart stopped, causing an immediate cardiac death. Victor casually restored him to life with [[Natural Energy]] moments later.[5]
Consuming Ra's Divinity had another consequence: Victor inherited access to the Egyptian Pantheon's Dimension. He transferred administrative responsibility to Aphrodite, ordered every Divine Artifact in the conquered territory catalogued and stored, and left to continue the hunt against his remaining enemies. Ra consequently lost not only his individual Divinity but the Divine territory over which he had ruled for eons.[5]
After Victor's subsequent ascension, the former Egyptian Pantheon became one of the principal territories of his expanding faction. Victor divided his holdings into three large organizational blocks, with the conquered Egyptian Dimension forming the second block and serving as a residence for particularly loyal non-Infernal followers and allied populations. Ra's former kingdom was therefore permanently absorbed into the political and supernatural infrastructure that would eventually become the [[Dragons Nest Pantheon]] and [[Elderblood Empire]].[8]

### Skills and Abilities
Before being stripped of his Godhood, Ra was an ancient Primordial God and genuine God-King whose authority extended beyond personal combat power. His demonstrated abilities were primarily centered around the Sun, his Divine Soul and the [[Egyptian Pantheon]]'s Dimension.

#### Former Abilities
- Primordial God Physiology: Ra formerly existed as a Primordial God rather than an ordinary physical life-form. His Divine Soul represented the fundamental foundation of his Godhood and housed the Divine power [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] later extracted. Even after Victor physically pierced his heart, the decisive method of permanently defeating him required manipulating and tearing apart the Divine structure embedded directly within his Soul.[5]
- Enhanced Physical Characteristics: As an ancient Primordial God, Ra possessed physical attributes vastly exceeding ordinary mortals and lesser supernatural beings.
- Divine Power: Ra possessed enormous quantities of [[Divine Energy]] and could release his Divinity strongly enough to transform his body and alter the environment around him. Despite this, Victor's power during their confrontation completely eclipsed Ra's maximum output.[5]
- Divine Soul: As a Primordial God, Ra possessed an exceptionally powerful and "heavy" Soul. Victor's attempt to actively edit it was noteworthy specifically because manipulating a God's Soul in this manner was unprecedented for him at the time.[5]
- [[Immortality]]: As a God, Ra formerly possessed the ageless existence and supernatural longevity inherent to Divine Beings.
- Regeneration: Ra's former Divine nature allowed him to survive damage that would be fatal to ordinary life-forms, although severe Soul damage remained capable of threatening his existence.
- Sun Divinity: Ra's primary demonstrated Divinity was the Sun. His Divine foundation contained golden Solar Energy that could be physically extracted from his Soul, and consuming it gave Victor a direct metaphysical connection with the Sun inside his Personal Universe.[5]
- Solar Manipulation: Ra could generate and wield the power of the Sun through his Divinity. When activating it, his body became covered in golden Solar Energy and the surrounding room rapidly increased in temperature.[5]
- Solar Mimicry / Solar Embodiment: By using his Divinity to its fullest, Ra could become one with the Sun itself. His entire body turned completely golden while assuming this state.[5]
- Heat Manipulation: Ra's body could become as hot as the surface of the Sun, with the interior of his Divine body becoming even hotter. The heat spread into his surroundings simply through the activation of his Sun Divinity.[5]
- Solar Energy Generation: Ra naturally generated and contained Solar Energy as part of his Divine existence. Once the relevant portion of his Soul was consumed, Victor immediately manifested golden Solar Energy without needing to reproduce the phenomenon through Creation-related powers.[5]
- Light Generation: Ra's activation of his Sun Divinity caused his body to emit intense golden radiance, allowing him to naturally produce Solar Light as an extension of his Divinity.[5]
- Sky Divinity / Authority: [[Gaia]] directly identifies Ra as the God of the Sky.[1] The story has not yet demonstrated a distinct Sky-based technique from Ra, so abilities such as unrestricted weather or atmospheric manipulation should not be attributed to him without additional chapter evidence.
- God-King Authority: Ra was not merely a high-ranking member of the [[Egyptian Pantheon]]; the narration explicitly describes him as a true God-King. His influence over his Pantheon was substantially greater than [[Zeus]]' authority over the divided [[Greek Pantheon]].[2]
- Dimensional Authority: Ra possessed authority over the Egyptian Pantheon's Divine Dimension. After Victor consumed Ra's Divinity, this authority transferred sufficiently for Victor to immediately gain access to the Dimension and distribute its "Keys" to [[Aphrodite]].[5]
- Dimensional Sealing: Through his authority, Ra could completely close the Egyptian Pantheon to outside entry. The only remaining weaknesses were extremely small spatial cracks created by the wider merger of several Paradises rather than an inability on Ra's part to seal his own domain.[2]
- Divine Leadership: Ra's authority extended politically across the Pantheon. Even before his conflict with Victor, he had sufficient command to prohibit troublesome Egyptian Gods from recklessly challenging Victor.[7]
- Soul Restoration (Attributed): After [[Erebus]] suffered severe Soul damage, [[Amon]] advised him to seek Ra's assistance because Ra might be capable of helping him recover. Although Ra is not directly shown performing the procedure on-screen, the recommendation came from another Primordial God familiar with his abilities and establishes that Ra was considered capable of treating serious Soul injuries.[7]
- Resistance to Mind Manipulation / Divine Charm: When Aphrodite expanded her empowered Divinity throughout the Egyptian Pantheon, every affected Egyptian God except Ra fell under her influence. Ra alone retained control of himself while the remainder of the Pantheon was gathered beneath her Charm.[4]

#### Current Condition
- Mortal Physiology: [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] permanently stripped Ra of his Divine nature and explicitly transformed him into a Mortal. The exact Mortal species Ra became has not been identified; the chapter does not establish that he specifically became Human.[5]
- Power Loss: Ra lost his Sun Divinity, Primordial God status, God-King authority and demonstrated Divine abilities after Victor extracted the Divine component of his Soul.[5]
- Permanent Incapacitation: Even after Victor repaired the immediate Soul damage, Ra was stated to be permanently incapacitated and incapable of becoming stronger even if his Soul eventually recovered completely.[5]
- Forced Resurrection: Ra does not possess a demonstrated resurrection ability of his own after becoming Mortal. Instead, Victor proved capable of restoring him to life with [[Natural Energy]] after his heart stopped and explicitly stated that death would not allow Ra to escape his punishment.[5]

### Former Territory and Possessions
- [[Egyptian Pantheon]] Dimension: Ra's most important former possession was effectively the Divine Dimension of the Egyptian Pantheon itself. His God-King authority allowed him to seal the realm almost completely. After his Sun Divinity was consumed, [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] acquired access to the Dimension and subsequently incorporated it into his own faction.[2][5][8]
- Golden Palace: Ra ruled from a large golden palace located inside the Egyptian Pantheon. His throne was situated within its central interior, where Victor eventually confronted him.[4]
- Egyptian Divine Artifacts: Numerous Divine Artifacts were stored throughout Ra's Pantheon. After taking control of the Dimension, Victor ordered [[Aphrodite]] to catalogue them and transfer them into secure storage. The verified chapters do not identify any of these artifacts specifically as Ra's personal weapon.[5]

### Relationships

#### Victor Elderblood
[[Victor Elderblood]] became Ra's most significant enemy after the Egyptian conspiracy targeted Victor's family. Ra had already been sufficiently wary of Victor long before their direct conflict to restrict Egyptian Gods from recklessly challenging him.[7] By the Third [[Supernatural Gathering]] their relationship had become openly hostile, with Victor naming Ra as one of the specific individuals he intended to hunt rather than condemning the entire [[Egyptian Pantheon]] indiscriminately.[3]
Ra attempted to avoid the confrontation by sealing his Pantheon and later attempted to negotiate once Victor entered his palace, but neither measure succeeded. Victor ultimately removed the Divine foundation that made Ra a Primordial God, transformed him into a Mortal and claimed the territory Ra once governed.[2][4][5]

#### Seth
Seth was one of the other principal Egyptian Gods involved in the plot that provoked [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]. When directly confronted, Ra attempted to place the primary responsibility on Seth, claiming that Seth had been responsible for the events. Victor rejected the excuse because Ra had nevertheless acted as the Pantheon's Leader and taken part in actions against his family.[5]

#### Anubis
[[Anubis]] appeared beside Ra and Seth during the Third [[Supernatural Gathering]] and was later included alongside them when [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] narrowed his declaration of war to the specific Egyptian Gods and associates responsible for the conspiracy.[6][3]

#### Amon
[[Amon]] and Ra are separate Gods within the novel. Amon was sufficiently familiar with Ra's capabilities to recommend him when [[Erebus]] required treatment for a damaged Soul and knew that Ra had prohibited reckless challenges against [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]].[7] Although Amun-Ra is a famous syncretic deity in Egyptian mythology, the story has not demonstrated Amon and Ra literally merging into one character.

### Trivia
- Ra is the first Primordial God explicitly transformed into a Mortal by [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]].[5]
- Ra temporarily died almost immediately after becoming Mortal because extreme fear caused his newly Mortal heart to stop. Victor resurrected him only moments later.[5]
- Ra was the only Egyptian God shown resisting [[Aphrodite]]'s Pantheon-wide Divine Charm during the invasion.[4]
- Velnorah deliberately captured 76 of the 77 Gods on Victor's target list and left Ra behind for Victor's group to confront.[4]
- Consuming Ra's Sun Divinity gave Victor a direct connection to the Sun inside his Personal Universe and allowed him to use its Solar Energy without relying upon his Dragon Progenitor's Creation abilities.[5]
- Ra's former Egyptian Dimension was later transformed from the seat of his Pantheon into one of the principal territorial blocks of Victor's faction.[8]
- The first currently verified novel reference to Ra occurs in Chapter 795, when [[Amon]] recommends seeking him for [[Erebus]]' damaged Soul and mentions Ra's prohibition against challenging Victor.[7] The previously listed Chapter 668 first mention could not be substantiated by the available chapter material.
- The novel explicitly calls Ra the God of the Sky in Chapter 861 and identifies his Solar Divinity directly during his confrontation with Victor.[1][5]
- The titles and powers sometimes attributed to Ra through real-world Egyptian mythology—such as broad authority over Law, Honor, Order, Day, weather, nuclear processes or every aspect historically associated with the deity—have not been demonstrated by the novel and are therefore not treated as canon abilities.
- Likewise, the Solar Barque and [[Aten]] are important concepts in Egyptian mythology and religion, but the verified novel chapters do not identify either as equipment personally wielded by Ra.
- Ra's former page contained an extensive genealogy largely derived from mythology. Since the novel has not directly established most of those familial relationships, they should not be presented in the infobox as confirmed canon unless future chapters explicitly verify them.
