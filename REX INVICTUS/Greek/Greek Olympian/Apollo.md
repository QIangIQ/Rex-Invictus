**[[Apollo]]** is the [[Olympians|Olympian]] god of the sun, light, music, poetry, prophecy, archery, healing and plague. He is the son of [[Zeus]] and [[Leto]], twin brother of [[Artemis]] and father of [[Asclepius]]. He keeps the same name in Rome. In the Empire he is the [[Divine Being 'Providence of Archers'|Providence of Archers]] and owns the [[Oracle of Delphi]].

## Greek History (IRL)

**Birth.** [[Hera]] forbade [[Leto]] to give birth on solid land. The floating island of Delos received her, and [[Artemis]] was born first and helped deliver Apollo.

**Delphi.** As a child he killed the serpent [[Python]] at Delphi and took over its oracle. The Pythia spoke his prophecies there, and the Pythian Games honored him.

**Myths.** He and Artemis killed the children of Niobe. He loved Daphne, who became a laurel tree, and Hyacinthus, killed by a discus. He flayed the satyr Marsyas for challenging him at music. [[Zeus]] killed his son [[Asclepius]] for raising the dead, and Apollo killed the [[Cyclopes]] in revenge and was sentenced to serve King Admetus as a mortal herdsman. He fought for Troy and guided the arrow that killed [[Achilles]].

**Domain.** Music (the lyre), prophecy, healing and plague, the sun (later identified with [[Helios]]). Symbols: lyre, bow, laurel, raven, swan.

**Primary sources:** *Homeric Hymn to Apollo*, Homer's *Iliad*, Ovid's *Metamorphoses*.

## PJO Canon

Apollo drives the sun chariot as a red sports car or bus. In [[The Titan's Curse]] he gives [[Percy Jackson|Percy]] and the [[Hunters of Artemis|Hunters]] a ride and gives [[Thalia Grace|Thalia]] a terrifying driving lesson. He is the father of Will Solace, Michael Yew and Lee Fletcher in [[Apollo's Cabin]] (Cabin 7) and of the Roman augur [[Octavian]] through his legacy. In [[The Heroes of Olympus]] he and [[Artemis]] are split and weakened. In [[The Trials of Apollo]] [[Zeus]] punishes him by making him a mortal teenager, Lester Papadopoulos, who must free the five great oracles from [[Nero]], Commodus and Caligula.

### Appearances

#### Debut
Chapter 623
Apollo was a Major God of the [[Greek Pantheon]], one of the Twelve Olympians and a son of [[Zeus]]. Within the novel he is principally identified as the God of the Sun, possessing Divine authority over the Sun and enough power to be treated as one of [[Olympus]]' important combatants.[1][2]
Unlike Gods such as [[Ares]], whose abilities were overwhelmingly focused on direct warfare, Apollo occupied the general middle range of powerful Divine Beings. The author's Divine ranking used Apollo alongside figures such as Susanoo, [[Tsukuyomi]] and [[Inari-Omikami]] as examples of Gods whom a sufficiently skilled [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] could contend with around the Chapter 630 period.[3]
Apollo was also extraordinarily famous among Greek mortals. Following the Demonic invasion of Earth, Zeus selected him as the Greek representative sent into the Human World to help reconstruct civilization and restore the population's faith in the Gods. The narration described Apollo as both powerful and highly recognizable to mortals, as well as being regarded as the most beautiful God of Olympus.[4]
He ultimately died during the destruction of the old Greek Pantheon. After participating in Zeus' forces against Victor Elderblood, Apollo became one of the remaining Olympians caught within [[Aphrodite]] Elderblood's increasingly powerful Divine Charm. Aphrodite directly ordered him to die; although [[Artemis]] attempted to interrupt the command, Apollo's subsequent status is consistently treated as deceased.[5]

### Physical Appearance
Apollo was an exceptionally handsome man with golden hair compared directly to the Sun. During the final battle of [[Olympus]], his appearance remained immediately recognizable even amid the enormous number of Gods fighting throughout the [[Greek Underworld]].[5]
His beauty was famous even outside the Pantheon. The narration referred to Apollo as the most beautiful God, making him one of the traditional standards by which male Divine beauty was measured.[4] Other characters likewise referred to him as particularly handsome, including [[Medusa]] Elderblood, who immediately recognized him during the war despite being primarily interested in killing him.[6]
Beyond his golden hair and exceptional Divine beauty, the novel has not provided confirmed details regarding Apollo's eye color, exact height or ordinary clothing.

### Personality and Traits
Apollo displayed a mixture of pragmatism and traditional Olympian arrogance. During the return of [[Typhon]], he was considerably more realistic than [[Poseidon]]. When Poseidon argued that [[Olympus]] had defeated Typhon before and could do so again, Apollo immediately rejected the comparison. He correctly pointed out that their previous victory occurred against a newborn and substantially weaker Typhon, while Fate, a unified Pantheon and capable Heroes had all favored the Gods. Even with those advantages, Olympus had suffered enormous casualties.[1]
His assessment demonstrated that Apollo was capable of evaluating threats without allowing pride to completely override his judgment. Alongside [[Athena]] and [[Ares]], he understood that fighting the adult Beast of the Apocalypse under their current circumstances was effectively suicidal.[1]
Apollo nevertheless possessed much of the arrogance common among the Olympians. When first confronted by [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s transformed presence during the final Greek war, Apollo attempted to insult him as a mere "lizard" and boasted that he had killed Dragons before. Victor did not respond verbally; merely turning his gaze toward Apollo caused the Sun God to become pale and instinctively retreat several steps as Victor's overwhelming Negative Energy bore down upon him.[2]
Despite his arrogance toward powerful enemies, Apollo was not shown to be completely indifferent toward his worshipers. After the Demonic invasion devastated Earth, he personally descended to the Human World with [[Zeus]]' permission and helped the surviving Greek population rebuild. His presence, like that of [[Thor]] among the Norse, became a symbol that restored hope and Faith among mortals whose civilization had nearly collapsed.[4]

### Synopsis

#### Demonic War
Apollo made his first direct appearance during the crisis caused by the return of [[Typhon]]. After Typhon's roar echoed throughout [[Olympus]], the ongoing conflict between [[Zeus]]' faction and the [[Titans]] temporarily became secondary, as both sides understood that an adult Beast of the Apocalypse represented a danger to the entire Pantheon.[1]
During Zeus' emergency meeting, [[Hermes]] advocated escaping while [[Poseidon]] insisted that Olympus should fight because the Gods had defeated Typhon once before. Apollo sided with the more cautious position. He explained that their previous victory had depended upon three exceptionally favorable circumstances: Typhon had only been a weak newborn, Fate had favored Olympus, and the Gods had been united with competent Heroes fighting beside them. Even under those circumstances, many Gods had died.[1]
Apollo consequently considered attempting to directly defeat the adult Typhon unrealistic. [[Athena]] and [[Ares]], despite rarely agreeing with one another, independently reached essentially the same conclusion.[1]
Following the enormous Demonic invasion of Earth and the collapse of normal Human society, the Greek and Norse Pantheons faced the problem of rebuilding the populations from which much of their Faith originated. Even though Olympus remained trapped in its internal civil war, individual Gods could still descend into the Human World under particular circumstances.[4]
Zeus chose Apollo to represent the [[Greek Pantheon]] among mortals. Apollo was powerful, widely recognized and sufficiently famous that his appearance carried enormous religious significance. His absence from Olympus was also less damaging because [[Helios]], the Titan God of the Sun, was aligned with Zeus at the time and could compensate for some of Apollo's normal Solar responsibilities.[4]
Apollo therefore descended to Earth and personally aided surviving Greek communities. Together with the activities of Gods such as [[Thor]] and the intervention of the Angels, his presence helped accelerate reconstruction and caused mortals to regain Faith in their respective Pantheons after the catastrophe.[4]

#### King Among Kings
Apollo later joined [[Zeus]]' forces during the final conflict in the [[Greek Underworld]]. When the assembled Olympians and [[Titans]] encountered [[Victor Elderblood]] in his increasingly Draconic state, most of the Gods immediately became cautious due to the oppressive nature of Victor's existence. Apollo was among the particularly arrogant exceptions.[2]
Apollo attempted to mock Victor and claimed that he had killed many Dragons before. Before he could finish speaking, Victor looked directly at him. Apollo immediately felt as though the totality of the world's Negativity were pressing upon him, causing his face to turn pale and forcing him to instinctively step backward. The response was severe enough that Zeus and several other major Gods immediately prepared for battle.[2]
[[Aphrodite]] Elderblood initially attempted to prevent the conflict by unleashing her Divine Charm over the battlefield. Apollo, alongside virtually every non-Primordial Greek God present, was successfully affected and temporarily fell beneath her influence. The Charm was subsequently dispelled by another party, allowing hostilities to resume.[2]
Victor then activated Blood Age, transforming the Greek Underworld into an enormous sea of supernatural Blood and suppressing the Olympians' normal environmental advantages. Apollo and [[Helios]], despite both possessing Solar authority, were explicitly unable to summon the Sun while trapped under those conditions. The Greek Underworld naturally favored Chthonic Gods rather than Olympian Solar Gods, and Victor's expanding power further disrupted their ability to exercise their Concepts normally.[2]
When Victor created a gigantic Dragon composed from Blood and the Souls contained within his existence, Zeus ordered Apollo, [[Poseidon]] and [[Ares]] to attack alongside him. The four major Gods combined their Divine Powers and successfully tore the Blood Dragon apart. The apparent victory immediately became a trap, as the creature exploded and covered all four Gods in highly corrosive Blood, severely burning them while Victor simply began generating additional Dragons.[2]
Apollo continued fighting throughout the collapsing Pantheon. He was later observed battling [[Diablo|Big Guy]], while [[Medusa]] Elderblood singled him out as another Olympian she personally wanted dead. Victor's faction simultaneously began slaughtering Gods permanently through attacks capable of severing their Souls and preventing normal Divine recovery.[6]
As [[Olympus]]' defeat became inevitable, Aphrodite stopped suppressing her Divinity and unleashed her Charm across the remaining battlefield. Gods of both sexes were overwhelmed and began killing one another at her command.[5]
Apollo attempted to approach Aphrodite in the form of golden light, but [[Morgana Elderblood]] emerged from Kaguya's shadow and struck him away before he could reach her. Aphrodite then focused her Charm directly upon Apollo and commanded him to die. Apollo initially attempted to resist the command before his resistance collapsed and he accepted it.[5]
[[Artemis]] intervened before the command could immediately be completed, shooting Apollo through the shoulder with an arrow surrounded by her silvery Divine Power. Apollo disappeared from the immediate battlefield in golden light following the attack. Despite Artemis' attempt to save her brother, subsequent continuity identifies Apollo as one of the deceased Olympians following the destruction of the old Greek order, with Aphrodite's Charm responsible for his death.[5]

### Skills & Abilities
Apollo was a Major Olympian God possessing considerable Divine strength, although he was not placed among [[Olympus]]' highest direct-combat specialists such as [[Ares]]. His abilities revolved primarily around his status as a Solar deity.
- God Physiology: As an ancient natural-born Olympian, Apollo possessed the supernatural body, [[Divine Energy]], longevity, regeneration and other fundamental characteristics associated with Gods.
- Divine Energy Manipulation: Apollo possessed the large reserves of Divine Energy expected from a Major Olympian and could channel his Divine Powers directly in combat. He combined his power with [[Zeus]], [[Poseidon]] and Ares strongly enough to tear apart one of [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s gigantic [[Blood Dragons]].[2]
- Sun Manipulation: Apollo's principal confirmed Divine authority was the Sun. He was directly identified throughout the novel as the Greek God of the Sun and possessed the ability to exercise supernatural control over Solar power.[1][4]
- Sun Summoning: Apollo was capable of directly summoning or manifesting the Sun through his Divine Concept. During Victor's Blood Age, both Apollo and [[Helios]] attempted to invoke their Solar authority but were unable to summon the Sun because they were fighting inside the [[Greek Underworld]], outside the environment that normally strengthened their powers, while Victor's Blood was suppressing the surrounding Divine Concepts.[2]
- Light Manipulation: Apollo's Solar power manifests through intense golden light. During the final battle he moved or disappeared through a burst of golden radiance after being struck by [[Artemis]].[5]
- Flight: Apollo was capable of freely remaining and fighting in the air. During the destruction of Olympus, [[Medusa]] observed him fighting [[Diablo|Big Guy]] while airborne.[6]
- Enhanced Strength: As a Mid-Level Divine Being, Apollo possessed physical strength enormously beyond conventional supernatural creatures. His power was considered relevant even among the major Olympians and he participated directly in attacks against Victor's creations.[2]
- Enhanced Durability: Apollo survived being struck by Victor's overwhelming pressure, exposure to Blood Age and the corrosive explosion produced when Victor's Blood Dragon was destroyed.[2]
- Enhanced Speed: Apollo possessed the supernatural speed expected of a Major God and could participate in large-scale Divine combat involving Gods such as Zeus, Ares, Poseidon and Victor.[2]
- Combat Proficiency: Although Apollo was not primarily classified as a combat-oriented God like Ares, he had enough combat experience to fight in Olympus' major wars and immediately cooperate with the other Olympians against high-level supernatural threats.[1][2]
- Supernatural Beauty: Apollo was regarded as the most beautiful male God known to the Greek population. His appearance and recognition were among the reasons Zeus selected him to publicly represent Olympus among surviving mortals after the Demonic invasion.[4]
- Faith Empowerment: Like other Pantheon Gods, Apollo possessed a relationship with mortal Faith. His direct appearance among Greek survivors restored worship and confidence in the Pantheon, strengthening the religious relationship between Gods and mortals.[4]

#### Power and Limitations
Apollo occupied approximately the Mid-Level God range within the author's early Divine hierarchy rather than the High-Level range attributed to Gods such as [[Ares]] and [[Thor]]. The author specifically used Apollo, Susanoo, [[Tsukuyomi]] and Inari as examples of the level a sufficiently skilled [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] could challenge around Chapter 630.[3]
His Solar Divinity was also environmentally dependent. Apollo was significantly disadvantaged inside the [[Greek Underworld]], a Realm naturally aligned with Chthonic powers and Negative Energy rather than [[Olympus]]. When Victor activated Blood Age there, Apollo and [[Helios]] were unable to properly summon the Sun despite their respective Solar authorities.[2]

### Relationships

#### Zeus
[[Zeus]] was Apollo's father and King. Apollo continued serving beneath Zeus throughout the instability of [[Olympus]], including participating in the emergency council concerning [[Typhon]] and later entering the final battle against [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] under Zeus' command.[1][2]
Zeus also selected Apollo to represent the [[Greek Pantheon]] on Earth following the Demonic invasion, demonstrating that he considered Apollo both powerful and publicly valuable to Olympus.[4]

#### Artemis
[[Artemis]] was Apollo's sister. During the collapse of the Pantheon, she attempted to save him after [[Aphrodite]]'s Charm forced Apollo to accept a command to die. Artemis shot him through the shoulder with an arrow infused with her Divine Power, interrupting Aphrodite's immediate control and causing Apollo to disappear from the battlefield in golden light.[5]
Her intervention ultimately failed to change Apollo's eventual fate.

#### Aphrodite Elderblood
Apollo was one of the many Greek Gods susceptible to [[Aphrodite]] Elderblood's increasingly powerful Divinity of Love and Beauty. Her large-scale Charm successfully affected him during the initial confrontation with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] and later overwhelmed him again when she stopped suppressing her power during [[Olympus]]' defeat.[2][5]
Aphrodite ultimately focused her Charm directly upon Apollo and commanded him to die. Although he initially resisted verbally and [[Artemis]] attempted to intervene, Apollo is subsequently treated as deceased, making Aphrodite directly responsible for his death.[5]

#### Victor Elderblood
Apollo's only direct interaction with [[Victor Elderblood]] began with hostility. Seeing Victor's Draconic appearance, Apollo attempted to ridicule him and boasted about having killed Dragons before. Victor's gaze alone immediately stripped away that confidence and forced Apollo backward beneath the pressure of his Negative Energy.[2]
Apollo later fought Victor's Blood Age creations beside [[Zeus]], [[Poseidon]] and [[Ares]]. Although the four Gods destroyed one of the [[Blood Dragons]], Victor had deliberately designed the creature to explode into corrosive Blood, injuring all four and immediately recreating it in greater numbers.[2]

#### Medusa Elderblood
[[Medusa]] Elderblood held enough resentment toward the Olympians that, upon recognizing Apollo during the final battle, she explicitly added him to the Gods she intended to kill. Apollo was at that moment occupied fighting [[Diablo|Big Guy]] and ultimately died before a dedicated confrontation between Apollo and Medusa could occur.[6]

### Death
Apollo's final known battle occurred during Chapter 800 as the old [[Greek Pantheon]] collapsed beneath simultaneous attacks from [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction, [[Persephone]]'s [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] forces and the internal devastation caused by [[Aphrodite]]'s Divine Charm.[5]
After Aphrodite commanded the remaining Gods to kill one another, Apollo appeared beside her in golden light. Morgana immediately attacked him and knocked him away, giving Aphrodite an unobstructed opportunity to target him directly. She asked Apollo to die. He initially rejected the order before the effects of her Charm overrode his resistance and forced him to agree.[5]
[[Artemis]] attempted to save him by striking his shoulder with an arrow empowered by her Divinity. Apollo reacted to the wound and disappeared in golden light. The chapter does not linger on the exact physical mechanism of his eventual death after this interruption; however, later continuity consistently identifies Apollo as deceased following the fall of [[Olympus]]. His death is therefore attributed to the command imposed upon him by Aphrodite's Charm.[5]

### Trivia
- Apollo and [[Helios]] were simultaneously active Solar Gods within the [[Greek Pantheon]]. This was possible because Divine authorities can overlap between different generations and Gods, even when one possesses a stronger or older claim to an aspect.[2]
- Helios' alliance with [[Zeus]] reduced the necessity for Apollo to remain permanently on [[Olympus]], allowing Zeus to send Apollo into the Human World after the Demonic invasion.[4]
- Apollo was regarded as the most beautiful God among the male Greek deities.[4]
- Despite being a Solar God, Apollo could not freely summon the Sun from every location. Fighting inside the [[Greek Underworld]] placed him at a significant environmental disadvantage.[2]
- Apollo initially displayed better judgment regarding [[Typhon]] than [[Poseidon]], correctly emphasizing that the circumstances surrounding Olympus' ancient victory against the Beast could not be reproduced against an adult Typhon.[1]
- His behavior toward [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] later demonstrated the opposite tendency: despite the overwhelming danger Apollo could already feel from Victor, he attempted to mock him before immediately being frightened into retreat by Victor's gaze.[2]
- [[Medusa]] intended to personally kill Apollo during the fall of Olympus, but his death ultimately resulted from [[Aphrodite]]'s Charm instead.[6][5]
- The former infobox classification of High-Level God is inconsistent with the author's ranking notes, which place Apollo around the general Mid-Level Divine range rather than alongside High-Level combatants such as [[Ares]] and [[Thor]].[3]
