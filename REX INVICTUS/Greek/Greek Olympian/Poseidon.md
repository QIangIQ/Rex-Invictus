**[[Poseidon]]** is the [[Olympians|Olympian]] god of the sea, earthquakes, storms and horses. He is a son of [[Kronos]] and [[Rhea]], one of the [[Big Three]], husband of [[Amphitrite]] and father of [[Triton]] and of [[Percy Jackson]]. His Roman counterpart is [[Neptune]]. In the Empire he is the [[Divine Being 'King of Atlantis'|King of Atlantis]] and [[Divine Being 'Storm-Wrath of the Deep'|Storm-Wrath of the Deep]], called "the Master" of [[New Atlantis]].

## Greek History (IRL)

**Origins.** Swallowed by [[Kronos]] and freed by [[Zeus]], Poseidon received the sea when the brothers drew lots. The Cyclopes forged his trident. He lived in a golden palace beneath the sea near Aegae and drove a chariot drawn by [[Hippocampi]].

**Myths.** He contested Athens with [[Athena]] and lost: his salt spring on the Acropolis was judged less useful than her olive tree. He and [[Apollo]] built the walls of Troy for King Laomedon, who refused to pay, so Poseidon sent a sea monster. He hounded Odysseus for blinding his son the Cyclops Polyphemus. He fathered [[Pegasus]] and [[Chrysaor]] with [[Medusa]], the horse [[Arion]] with [[Demeter]], and heroes such as Theseus, Bellerophon, Orion and Pelias.

**Domain and cult.** God of the sea, of earthquakes ("Earth-Shaker", Ennosigaios) and creator of horses (Hippios). Major sanctuaries at Isthmia (the Isthmian Games), Cape Sounion and Helike. Symbols: trident, horse, bull and dolphin.

**Primary sources:** Homer's *Odyssey* and *Iliad*, Hesiod, Herodotus, Pausanias.

## PJO Canon

Poseidon is [[Percy Jackson|Percy]]'s father. In [[The Lightning Thief]] he is accused of stealing [[Zeus]]'s bolt and claims Percy at camp. In [[The Sea of Monsters]] Percy meets his half-brother Tyson, a Cyclops. In [[The Last Olympian]] Poseidon's undersea kingdom is besieged by [[Oceanus]], and he chooses to leave it to fight [[Typhon]] when Percy asks. He is a relaxed father in Hawaiian shirts and fishing gear, proud of Percy but distant.

In [[The Heroes of Olympus]] his Roman form [[Neptune]] is mistrusted at Camp Jupiter. In [[The Son of Neptune]] Percy is the first child of [[Neptune]] at the camp in ages.

#### Personality
- **Boisterous, proud and embarrassing.** He bursts in on [[Percy Jackson|Percy]] and [[Artemis]]'s private moment: "Oh, this is priceless" ([[Ch19 - Rex Invictus XII|Ch19]]). He brags, "My son. Pulling a goddess. I told [[Zeus]] you had game!" He slings an arm round Percy and jokes that he "skipped like twelve tiers of emotional incompetence."
- **Dangerously calm when protective.** When [[Apollo]] calls Percy's blood "strange", he stands: "That is my son you are talking about" ([[Ch19 - Rex Invictus XII|Ch19]]). When Artemis says Percy is under her protection, "The grin fades. Just a little… 'And we are going to talk about that.'" The clash of their auras cracks marble.
- **Knows his son's tells.** "That's my 'I broke something important' face! It's always before [[Amphitrite]] blows a fuse at me" ([[Ch19 - Rex Invictus XII|Ch19]]).
- **Elemental and impersonal at full strength.** He calls the Imperial pegasi to him, and the horses come with "the joy of a child running to its father". Then he drowns 71 riders of the [[Peltast]] ala ([[Ch22 - Rex Invictus XV|Ch22]]; see 00a_CANON_RULINGS.md C8). He sinks the Titan by turning the field into ocean.
- **Capable of remorse.** He stands on the dock "with Keyes's blood still drying on his hands", counting the Pyrrhus soldiers he drowned. When Marien confronts him he answers simply: "I did." / "I did not know." / "I'm sorry" ([[Ch22 - Rex Invictus XV|Ch22]]).
- **A paternal disciplinarian, even to other gods.** He scolds [[Ares]]: "You were getting prayed to, nephew… Go and think about why" ([[Ch22 - Rex Invictus XV|Ch22]]).
- **Gentle with the defeated.** He carries the injured Titan pilot Keyes "the way a man lifts a wet kitten" to the infirmary.
- **Attentive to Percy above all.** In the Ch22 council he spends "the whole meeting watching Percy rather than the footage".
- **Pragmatic problem-solver.** He resolves Article IV by offering [[Triton]], not Percy, as [[Helmsman]].

#### Appearance
- He holds "his signature trident", glowing faintly. He stands in a throne hall with [[Amphitrite]] and the [[Nereids]] ([[Ch12 - Rex Invictus V|Ch12]]).
- He emerges "in a curtain of fog. Barefoot and frowning" ([[Ch15 - Rex Invictus VIII|Ch15]]). He leans on a forcefield wall "as if it were just another dock in Key West of old Earth".
- At [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]] he wears war plate, and "the salt tang of the sea god's presence" mixes with the ozone ([[Ch15 - Rex Invictus VIII|Ch15]]).
- On Orbital Platform One he stands with arms folded while "the currents thrashed around them" ([[Ch13 - Rex Invictus VI|Ch13]]).
- Old timeline: an "azure throne" ([[Ch01 - Rex Imperator I|Ch1]]).

#### Personality
- **Grief-stricken father.** His first reaction to the clone's face is hope: "[[Percy Jackson|Percy]]?" … "By the tide… you've returned." It turns to cold rage when he realises it is not his son: "You—you're wearing him." ([[Ch12 - Rex Invictus V|Ch12]])
- **Perceptive and hard to fool.** "That's not how he would've said it." "Your voice doesn't match the face." ([[Ch12 - Rex Invictus V|Ch12]]) "You're not who you say you are." He sees through [[Minerva (AI)|Minerva]]'s simulated nervousness, but chooses to work with her: "Just don't lie to me again, Minerva." ([[Ch15 - Rex Invictus VIII|Ch15]])
- **Weary and haunted.** "Just tired… Dreams. Too many of them. Too much bleeding through. So many Mortals." His "sigh was a tidal thing" ([[Ch15 - Rex Invictus VIII|Ch15]]).
- **Secretive.** He keeps the [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]] war "compartmentalized" and refuses Minerva's offer of [[Hunters of Artemis|Hunters]] ([[Ch15 - Rex Invictus VIII|Ch15]]). "We learned to watch from a distance. Power draws attention." (Ch15)
- **Glorifies war.** He rhapsodises about the [[War of Princes]]: "[[Olympus]], it had been beautiful. No better war had ever been fought." He relishes the memory of a "beautiful death", and finds the Tartarus grind "filthy" by comparison (Ch15).
- **Mischievous and competitive.** He gloats at [[Zeus]] with "regular mischievous smiles" after his descendant killed Zeus's daughter ([[Ch05 - Intermission I|Ch5]]).
- **Sheepish rule-breaker.** He slipped an entire battleship past the orbital works; when Varric asks how they got in, "Poseidon coughed. Suddenly the walls looked like a very interesting view." (Ch15)
- **Formally respectful of the doomed.** He acknowledges Varric's fate and sends him off: "Die well, nephew." (Ch15)
- **Ruthless sovereign.** His adjutants have a disloyal colonel "dealt with" and arrange an "accidental failure" of another's Repertoire infusers ([[Ch12 - Rex Invictus V|Ch12]]). The text implies but does not state that this was on his orders.
- **Inactive at the crucial moment.** When the Olympian Court crumbled, "Poseidon would not act" ([[Ch13 - Rex Invictus VI|Ch13]]). Minerva accuses him of "your abandonment many millennia ago" (Ch12).

#### Voice & Speech Patterns
His voice rumbles. He speaks in short, gruff lines, and grief turns them dangerous. He uses sea oaths ("By the tide"). He calls [[Minerva (AI)|Minerva]] "Impostor", "son" (by slip) and "Minnie", and calls Varric "nephew". When reminiscing he becomes expansive, even purple. Mild profanity is not shown on-page in the future.

#### Abilities, Powers & Equipment
- **Trident** (glowing). Command over currents ("the currents thrashed around them", [[Ch13 - Rex Invictus VI|Ch13]]). He can arrive and depart in fog.
- **Realm-level protection:** inside his realm "augurs could not peer into reality" ([[Ch12 - Rex Invictus V|Ch12]]).
- **[[New Atlantis]] / the Nereid Expanse:** a star system whose waters form "flat, circular disc" currents "like Saturn's rings around a star". It has towers of coral and metal exhaling steam and bioluminescent mist, and Oceanic Terraforming Stations in orbit around the ice moon Enceladus ([[Ch10 - Rex Invictus III|Ch10]]–11). An augur calls it "the Master's greatest creation since your conceiving" ([[Ch11 - Rex Invictus IV|Ch11]]).
- **Orbital assets:** orbital water towers (possibly undersupplied, [[Ch15 - Rex Invictus VIII|Ch15]]). Orbital Platform One is "larger than most gas giants" ([[Ch12 - Rex Invictus V|Ch12]]).
- **Forces:**
  - The New-[[Atlantis]] Navy.
  - The 32nd [[Scylla]]'s Sisters (about 300,000 troops) and the 121st ([[Ch12 - Rex Invictus V|Ch12]]).
  - The mechanical Titans of [[Stephanos Pyrrhus]]'s Honour Guard.
  - Adjutants such as Cyria (also "Cyrus" twice in Ch12; see Continuity Notes).
  - Augurs.
- **Bloodlines:** only 127 first-born descendants remain in his System-Dominion ([[Ch15 - Rex Invictus VIII|Ch15]]), plus legacies. Poseidon legacies are amputated and "integrated" as neural relays in AnnChase ships ([[Ch11 - Rex Invictus IV|Ch11]]). The Pyrrhus dynasty ("Waves of Poseidon") is taught to pray to him ([[Ch22 - Rex Invictus XV|Ch22]]).
- **His blood in Regen-ex:** [[Percy Jackson|Percy]]'s healing fluid contained "fragments of the Lord of Atlantis' blood" ([[Ch01 - Rex Imperator I|Ch1]]).
- **Genetic tie to [[Minerva (AI)|Minerva]]:** his data matches "sixty-seven percent of my embedded [[Ageis|Aegis]] matrix" (Ch12).

#### Backstory
- **Old timeline:** he laughed "triumphant" with "genuine pride" when [[Percy Jackson|Percy]]'s half-brother Rammael set foot on Cyrax. That moment seeded Percy's jealousy ([[Ch01 - Rex Imperator I|Ch1]]). At the Great Abandonment the gods "ascended to the bridge of The Spear of [[Zeus]]", and Percy bade "his father, and the rest of his Divine family, a farewell" ([[Ch01 - Rex Imperator I|Ch1]]). Four centuries after the Great Abandonment, [[Typhon]] destroyed his palace on [[Olympus Primaris]] ([[Ch01 - Rex Imperator I|Ch1]]).
- **[[The Great Expansion]]:** [[Minerva (AI)|Minerva]] says he abandoned his son "many millennia ago" with the beginning of the Great Expansion ([[Ch12 - Rex Invictus V|Ch12]]).
- **[[War of Princes]] (16212/13–16227 per the vault, a few years before the future-era scenes; Poseidon says "millennia ago", a slip; see Continuity Notes):** his sons tried to fight through to the last legions of [[Apollo]], but the Princes' ships split the armies. He watched the last Sun-Born, who "had screamed the Orchestras of Hate into space", die, and a blonde, imperial-gold-clad Legacy of Apollo keep the Roman standard upright while he was run through ([[Ch15 - Rex Invictus VIII|Ch15]]).
- **[[Caligo Vale]]:** when the Olympian Court crumbled he "would not act", which led to Percy's political marriage to [[Artemis]] ([[Ch13 - Rex Invictus VI|Ch13]]).
- **The near-monthly Zeus skirmishes:** Zeus blames his "damned direct descendant" for killing [[Eleni Theodosia]] ([[Ch05 - Intermission I|Ch5]]). She died at Fulguris to the [[Dionysus]]–Poseidon–[[Aphrodite]] coalition (Melina Callisto's ramming, [[Ch02 - Rex Imperator II|Ch2]]; [[Stephanos Pyrrhus]] was in the coalition, `G03_Stephanos_Pyrrhus.md`); her last memory curses Dionysus ([[Ch15 - Rex Invictus VIII|Ch15]]). The individual killer is unresolved.
- **Author's vault note (not yet in text):** at the Great Expansion he claimed the **Thalassus Belt** and created **[[Karcharonexus]]** and **[[New Atlantis]]** (dated 3045.06.3.5). New Atlantis is his chief residence with [[Amphitrite]], one of the UOE's main centres of authority, and one of only three entrances to the [[Forge of Hephaestus]]. Its residents include [[Benthesikyme]], [[Ceto]] (fleeing [[Pontus]]), [[Delphin]], [[Eidothea]], [[Galene]], [[Glaucus]], the [[Hippocampi]], [[Kymopoleia]], [[Leucothea]], [[Nereus]], the [[Nereids]], [[Phorcys]], Proteus, [[Rhode]] and [[Scylla]]. Helmsmen are lobotomised direct descendants of Poseidon interred in ships. His UOE Unified Rank is #27.

Poseidon, also known as [[Neptune]], was an ancient Olympian God, former ruler of [[Atlantis]] and one of the three exceptionally powerful brothers collectively known as the [[Big Three]] alongside [[Zeus]] and [[Hades]]. His principal and directly confirmed Divine Concept was the Seas, making him the Greek God of the Seas and one of the major [[God-Kings]] belonging to the old [[Greek Pantheon]].[3]
Poseidon spent most of his existence near the summit of the Olympian hierarchy, but his enormous power was accompanied by the same arrogance, entitlement and abuse common among many members of the old Greek order. His history with Medusa Elderblood became particularly important to his eventual fate. Poseidon had gravely wronged Medusa in the past, while Athena was likewise connected to the suffering that followed. When Victor later promised Medusa that the people responsible would eventually be placed completely at her mercy, Poseidon became one of the primary targets of that promise.[4]
Unlike Zeus and many other Olympians, Poseidon was deliberately kept alive after the fall of [[Olympus]]. Victor had no intention of granting him mercy; his continued survival existed specifically so Medusa could personally exact revenge. Poseidon spent years as a prisoner and object of torture until Medusa finally became satisfied and lost interest in continuing his punishment.[2]
Victor subsequently brought the broken God-King before him one final time. Their conversation became less about Poseidon's former status and more about the enormous difference between their worldviews. Poseidon had spent eons considering attachment to family, responsibility and mortal values unnecessary for beings possessing supreme power, whereas Victor had become vastly more powerful without abandoning those principles. Victor ultimately killed Poseidon completely, absorbed his Divine Concept of the Seas and immediately began putting the acquired Concept toward a new creative purpose.[5][1][2]

### Physical Appearance
The novel has never provided a complete canonical description of Poseidon's ordinary appearance. His exact hair color, eye color, height, clothing and other permanent identifying characteristics therefore remain unknown. Traditional depictions of Poseidon as a bearded man wielding a trident should not automatically be treated as his MTW appearance unless explicitly established by the novel.
Later chapters provide considerably more information about his physical condition after the destruction of [[Olympus]]. When Poseidon was brought before [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] in the [[Greek Underworld]], he was already severely wounded from the previous war. His body was covered in deep cuts and several fingers were missing from one hand. He, [[Athena]] and [[Artemis]] had been restrained with Divine shackles powerful enough to suppress their ability to meaningfully resist.[3]
Years of captivity and [[Medusa]]'s revenge left Poseidon even more broken. By the time Victor summoned him for their final conversation, the once-proud God-King had effectively lost the vitality and pride that had characterized him. His demeanor was lifeless, exhausted and resigned, with death itself having become preferable to the continuation of his punishment.[5][2]
Immediately before his final disappearance, Poseidon's remaining existence was transformed into pure Energy within Victor's hand as Victor completely ended the former Sea God's life and appropriated the Concept that had defined his Divine existence.[1][2]

### Personality
Poseidon was intensely arrogant, prideful, entitled and overconfident. His position as one of the [[Big Three]] and ruler of [[Atlantis]] gave him enormous confidence in the authority of the Greek Gods, and he frequently behaved as though an ancient Divine reputation was sufficient evidence that an opponent could eventually be overcome.
This mentality was displayed clearly when an adult [[Typhon]] returned. While [[Hermes]] immediately argued that the Olympians needed to flee, Poseidon rejected retreat and insisted that the Gods should fight. He cited [[Olympus]]' ancient victory against Typhon as proof that defeating the Beast was possible, forcing [[Apollo]] to explain how badly Poseidon was misrepresenting the circumstances: the previous Typhon had been much younger, Fate had favored Olympus, the Pantheon had been united and exceptional Heroes had assisted the Gods, yet the battle had still produced catastrophic casualties.[6]
Poseidon's treatment of women demonstrated an even uglier aspect of his entitlement. His sexual history extended across Goddesses, supernatural beings and mortals, and the consequences of his actions toward [[Medusa]] became severe enough that [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] personally remembered the offense long after first learning about it. Victor explicitly grouped Poseidon with [[Athena]] as people who had hurt Medusa and regarded delivering both of them to her as the fulfilment of an old promise.[4][5]
His ancient worldview also regarded power as something that should naturally free its owner from the restraints ordinary people accepted. Poseidon consequently struggled to understand Victor. By the time of their final conversation, Victor had surpassed nearly every God Poseidon had ever known and possessed the memories and knowledge of numerous Divine beings, yet he continued prioritizing his wives, children, friends and responsibilities rather than discarding them as weaknesses.[5][1]
Poseidon considered those attachments strange because they contradicted how many ancient Gods behaved after obtaining enormous power. The mentality reminded him of [[Hestia]], whose emphasis on family and responsibility he had historically found frustrating. Victor responded by arguing that Poseidon was the truly strange one. Poseidon had been born with enormous talent, Divine status, a vast lifespan and virtually unlimited opportunity to become something greater, yet had squandered those advantages while behaving like the worst examples of the old Gods.[5][1]
The criticism forced Poseidon into uncomfortable introspection. Victor had risen from an extremely fragile Human body into a being capable of dominating Pantheons without abandoning the principles that had mattered to him as a Human. Poseidon, despite beginning his life immeasurably more privileged, had failed to become the kind of ruler or example that his position theoretically allowed him to be.[5][1]
Poseidon's final years also demonstrated that his pride was not indestructible. The fall of Olympus, imprisonment and years of punishment under Medusa eventually broke him psychologically. By Chapter 1071, the former ruler of Atlantis displayed almost no desire to reclaim his title, fight Victor or protect his legacy. He wanted the ordeal to end and understood that Victor's decision was absolute.

### Synopsis

#### Discoveries, Changes, And War
Poseidon was first mentioned through Roberta, whose past remained tied to the Greek Gods responsible for her suffering. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] understood that [[Medusa]] still carried unresolved hatred toward the people who had wronged her and promised that he would eventually give her the opportunity to personally decide their fate.[4]
Victor specifically assured Medusa that the two individuals she hated most would one day be completely at her mercy. Those targets were Poseidon and [[Athena]]. The promise was made long before Victor possessed enough political or military power to dictate the fate of major Olympian Gods, but he treated the statement as something that would eventually be fulfilled rather than empty consolation.[4]
Poseidon's first narrative importance therefore did not come from being one of the [[Big Three]]. It came from the consequences of actions he had committed against someone Victor considered his own.

#### Demonic War
Poseidon made his physical debut during the emergency surrounding the return of [[Typhon]]. [[Olympus]] was already suffering from an increasingly severe civil conflict when the Beast's roar caused the smaller confrontations throughout the Pantheon to become temporarily irrelevant. [[Zeus]] gathered the surviving Olympian Gods to determine how Mount Olympus could respond.[6]
[[Hermes]] immediately recommended fleeing. Poseidon strongly rejected the suggestion, insisting that running was impossible and that the Gods needed to fight. When [[Apollo]] argued that an adult Beast of the Apocalypse was something one escaped rather than confronted directly, Poseidon pointed toward Olympus' previous defeat of Typhon as evidence that victory remained possible.[6]
Apollo dismantled the argument. The Typhon defeated in the ancient conflict had effectively been a far less developed version of the Beast; the Gods had possessed much stronger unity, Fate had favored them and powerful Heroes had participated. Even with those advantages, the Pantheon suffered enormous casualties. The current situation was drastically worse, particularly because the Greeks were already divided by internal warfare.[6]
Poseidon's refusal to retreat demonstrated the confidence created by his ancient victories and position among the [[Big Three]], but also the tendency to judge new threats according to achievements from an age when Olympus had operated under completely different circumstances.
The meeting deteriorated further when [[Dionysus]] arrived severely injured and revealed that [[Aphrodite]] Elderblood, [[Hestia]] Elderblood, [[Demeter]], [[Rhea]], Nike, [[Tyche]] and other Goddesses had robbed Mount Olympus' treasury and escaped. Zeus subsequently sealed the entire Dimension, trapping the remaining Olympians inside a Pantheon threatened by Typhon, [[Titans]], internal betrayal and the wider supernatural war.[6]
Poseidon remained associated with Olympus through the collapse rather than joining the defecting Goddesses.

#### King Among Kings
The Greek Civil War eventually developed into the destruction of the old Olympian order. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] entered the [[Greek Underworld]] after [[Zeus]], Kronus and the hostile Greek armies attempted to seize control of the realm. Victor's arrival transformed what the Gods expected to be another war between Divine factions into a massacre.
His attacks did not merely destroy Divine bodies. Victor severed and damaged Souls, bypassing the normal survival mechanisms upon which Gods relied. Fallen Gods could then be incorporated into his Immortal forces, meaning each casualty simultaneously reduced the Greek army and strengthened Victor's side.
Poseidon survived the initial annihilation while the majority of Zeus' forces were killed. Zeus and Kronus were eventually personally destroyed by Victor, permanently ending both of the rulers around whom the old Greek conflict had revolved.[7]
Victor did not allow Poseidon to share Zeus' immediate fate. Years earlier he had promised [[Medusa]] that the Gods responsible for her suffering would eventually belong to her, and Poseidon remained valuable alive precisely because killing him immediately would prevent that promise from being fulfilled.
When the defeated Gods were finally brought before Victor in the Underworld, Poseidon's fall was complete. The former ruler of [[Atlantis]] appeared covered in deep wounds with several fingers missing, restrained by shackles capable of suppressing his Divinity. Beside him were [[Athena]], [[Artemis]] and [[Persephone]], although the circumstances surrounding each Goddess were different.[3]
Victor no longer acknowledged Poseidon's former political status as meaningful. Poseidon was not treated as Lord of Atlantis, a [[Big Three]] God or one of the greatest rulers of ancient Greece. He was treated as a defeated enemy who remained alive solely because Victor's wife still had a use for him.
Medusa was given the right to determine what happened to Poseidon and Athena. Victor made the punishment intentionally personal: both would be placed completely beneath the authority of the woman whose life their actions had damaged, allowing Medusa to decide how long their suffering continued.
Poseidon's remaining pride initially caused him to resist his humiliation, but he possessed no ability to enforce that defiance. Victor's faction had already defeated [[Olympus]], his Divinity was restrained and the people judging him were vastly beyond the power available to the surviving Olympians.[3]
The following chapter completed the fulfilment of Victor's old promise. Medusa took Poseidon and Athena away so that her revenge could continue without interference from the surviving Greek political order.[8]
Poseidon consequently survived Olympus' destruction in the worst possible manner from his perspective. He remained alive but possessed no kingdom, authority, freedom or meaningful protection from death. His life had become the property of the people he once considered beneath him.

#### Dragon God of Chaos
Poseidon's captivity continued after the [[Greek Pantheon]] ceased existing as an independent power. The defeated Gods taken by [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] occupied a fundamentally different position from the Goddesses who had voluntarily fled [[Zeus]]. The latter had submitted to Victor's faction before the collapse of [[Olympus]], while the hostile Gods captured through war were treated as conquered property and prisoners rather than equal allies.
Poseidon remained beneath this system for years while [[Medusa]] continued her revenge. His previous status as one of the [[Big Three]] offered no protection. The political world in which that title had mattered was gone, and Victor had become powerful enough that Poseidon represented neither a military threat nor a diplomatically important hostage.
His survival throughout this period therefore should not be interpreted as mercy or successful rehabilitation. Victor was intentionally keeping the former Sea God alive because Medusa had not yet finished with him.

#### God Emperor Victor Elderblood
By the mature Imperial era, [[Medusa]] had spent years punishing Poseidon and had finally become bored with continuing. Nyx Elderblood explained that Medusa was already satisfied, making Poseidon's continued existence unnecessary. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] consequently brought the former God-King out for the final purpose he had reserved for him.[2]
Poseidon was almost completely psychologically broken by this point. The man who had once insisted [[Olympus]] confront [[Typhon]] rather than retreat now displayed little interest in resistance. His former titles had become irrelevant and the only meaningful question remaining was what Victor intended to do with him.[5]
Victor reminded Poseidon exactly why he had been preserved. Poseidon and [[Athena]] had hurt Medusa, and Victor had promised his wife that they would pay for it. Medusa had now received the revenge she wanted, meaning Victor no longer had a personal reason to keep Poseidon alive.[5]
Their conversation expanded beyond punishment and became a discussion concerning power, Humanity and responsibility. Poseidon struggled to understand Victor because Victor's behavior contradicted the pattern Poseidon had observed among ancient powerful beings. Victor possessed overwhelming strength, extraordinary authority and access to the memories and knowledge of numerous Gods, yet his fundamental priorities still revolved around family, responsibility and the people he considered his own.[5]
Poseidon considered those ideals strangely mortal. He associated them with figures such as [[Hestia]], whose worldview had often appeared unnecessarily restrictive to beings who could simply use their power to obtain what they wanted. Victor instead argued that power did not excuse responsibility and that possessing more power created even greater reason to become an example rather than an irresponsible tyrant.[5][1]
Victor also discussed his increasingly negative opinion of Humanity and intelligent life more generally. Power did not automatically transform good people into monsters; often it simply removed the restraints that had previously prevented people from revealing what they already wanted to do. A weak person could appear harmless simply because law, fear and superior forces prevented them from acting otherwise. Give the same person sufficient authority, and their true nature could become much clearer.[5]
Poseidon represented one of Victor's examples of this principle. Poseidon had been born with extraordinary power, status, lifespan and opportunity, yet much of what he had done with those advantages consisted of indulgence, arrogance and abuse. Victor regarded that outcome as a colossal waste of potential.
The criticism forced Poseidon to recognize the contrast between them. Victor had begun as a sick Human whose body could barely support ordinary life, yet continuously pursued improvement until reaching heights Poseidon had never approached. More importantly, the memories of countless Gods had not erased Victor's original identity. His Human experiences remained the core through which he interpreted everything he later became.[5][1]
Victor then revealed why Poseidon's continued existence still held one final use. Poseidon's Divine Concept of the Seas was exactly the kind of Concept Victor wanted for a new project involving marine life. Poseidon understood enough of Victor's proposed objective to regard it as madness, but there was nothing he could do to prevent it.[1]
Victor finally killed Poseidon completely and absorbed the Divine Concept that had defined him as the God of the Seas.[1]
Shortly afterward, [[Gaia]], Nyx and [[Amaterasu]] observed Victor with Poseidon and understood that Medusa's satisfaction meant the Sea God's life was finally ending. Poseidon's remaining existence was reduced into pure Energy within Victor's hand.[2]
Victor thanked him for the final conversation and turned immediately toward his next project. Using the knowledge, Conceptual resources and creative abilities available to him, Victor began working upon the World of Water and a new marine race related in part to his own existence.[2]
Poseidon's Divine Concept therefore outlived Poseidon himself. The God who had spent eons using the Seas as the foundation of his own authority died, while the Concept Victor harvested from him was redirected toward the creation of new life.

### Skills & Abilities
Poseidon was one of the most powerful conventional Olympians and belonged to the God-King class. As one of the [[Big Three]], he stood considerably above ordinary Olympian Gods in status, [[Divine Energy]] and accumulated authority.

#### God Physiology
- God Physiology: Poseidon possessed the supernatural physiology inherent to ancient Olympian Gods.
- Superhuman Strength: His Divine body possessed physical strength vastly exceeding mortal and conventional supernatural species.
- Superhuman Speed and Reflexes: Poseidon possessed the enhanced movement and reactions characteristic of high-ranking Gods.
- Superhuman Durability: His body could survive extensive Divine injuries. Following the fall of [[Olympus]] he remained alive despite being covered in deep wounds and missing several fingers.[3]
- [[Divine Energy]] Manipulation: Poseidon possessed enormous Divine Energy developed across his eons of existence.
- Divine Soul: As a God-King, Poseidon possessed a highly developed Divine Soul that supported his Divinity and immortal existence.
- [[Immortality]]: Poseidon could live indefinitely without aging naturally.
- Supernatural Regeneration: His Divine physiology allowed recovery from injuries that would be fatal to mortal races, although sufficiently advanced Soul and Conceptual attacks could permanently kill him.
- Enhanced Senses: Poseidon possessed the supernatural perception expected from an ancient major God.

#### God-King Physiology
- God-King Level Existence: Poseidon belonged to the exceptionally powerful group of Olympians known as the [[Big Three]] and possessed status beyond conventional Major Gods.
- Immense Divine Reserves: His long existence, Divine Concept and position over [[Atlantis]] gave him a far larger foundation than ordinary members of [[Olympus]].
- Ancient Combat Experience: Poseidon had existed through the old Divine era and participated in conflicts dating back to the earliest generations of the Olympian order.
- Rulership: He governed Atlantis and possessed the political and Divine authority associated with controlling a major supernatural territory.

#### Divinity of the Seas
Seas is Poseidon's primary and directly confirmed Divine Concept. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] specifically killed Poseidon in order to acquire the Concept, and the authority remained sufficiently valuable for Victor to incorporate it into a major later creation project.[1]
- Sea Manipulation: Poseidon possessed Divine authority over seas and oceanic phenomena.
- Water Manipulation: As the Divine ruler of the Seas, Poseidon's authority naturally encompassed enormous bodies of water associated with his Concept.
- Ocean Manipulation: Poseidon could conceptually interact with oceans rather than merely controlling a limited quantity of ordinary water.
- Divine Marine Authority: His Concept granted supernatural authority over the maritime domain represented by the Seas.
- Conceptual Sea Authority: Poseidon's control was based upon Divinity rather than conventional elemental [[Magic]], allowing the Seas to function as part of his Divine identity.
- Transferable Divinity: After Poseidon's complete death, Victor could appropriate the Concept of the Seas and make use of its authority himself.[1]
- Creation Resource: The stolen Concept became useful to Victor when developing the World of Water and the foundation for new marine life.[1][2]
The exact maximum range of Poseidon's Sea Manipulation has never been directly demonstrated in an extended on-page battle. Claims that he could automatically control every ocean on a planetary or universal scale should therefore not be assumed without a specific feat.

#### Divine Authority
- Divinity Manipulation: Poseidon could wield the Divine Concept integrated into his Soul and Divine existence.
- Divine Aura: His existence naturally carried the metaphysical pressure expected from a major ancient God.
- Blessing Bestowal: As an established God, Poseidon possessed the general capacity to grant Divine Blessings within the limits of his authority, although no major Poseidon-specific Blessing has been demonstrated directly.
- Curse Bestowal: Poseidon possessed the general supernatural capacity associated with Divine curses, although no named curse belonging specifically to him has been shown.

#### Political and Non-Combat Skills
- Rulership: Poseidon ruled [[Atlantis]] for an immense period and possessed extensive experience managing a Divine territory.
- Divine Politics: As one of the [[Big Three]], he operated near the highest political level of the [[Greek Pantheon]].
- Ancient Knowledge: Poseidon accumulated enormous amounts of supernatural and historical knowledge during his eons of life.
- High Potential: [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] specifically criticized Poseidon because someone born with his enormous advantages should have been capable of becoming far more than what he ultimately became.[5][1]

### Power and Scaling
Poseidon was one of the [[Big Three]] alongside [[Zeus]] and [[Hades]], making him one of the strongest members of the principal Olympian generation. His classification as a God-King placed him above ordinary Low-, Mid- and High-Level Gods in the Divine hierarchy.
This status should not be confused with evidence that Poseidon was equal to Zeus in every combat category. Zeus possessed his own specialized Divinities, God-King authority over [[Olympus]] and powerful Divine Artifacts, while Hades possessed his authority over the [[Greek Underworld]]. The title Big Three identifies all three brothers as extraordinarily important and powerful Gods without making their abilities identical.
Poseidon's direct combat feats are unusually sparse relative to his rank. His most prominent scenes involve strategic discussion, captivity and his later execution rather than a prolonged battle demonstrating the upper limits of his Seas Divinity.
By [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s mature era, the difference between them had become completely insurmountable. Poseidon was no longer treated as a meaningful combat opponent; Victor could kill him, absorb his Divinity and reduce his remaining existence to Energy without Poseidon possessing any realistic method of resistance.[1][2]

### Weaknesses and Limitations
- Arrogance: Poseidon's confidence in Olympian power caused him to underestimate situations that had fundamentally changed from the victories of the ancient era. His insistence that [[Olympus]] could fight an adult [[Typhon]] despite radically inferior circumstances is the clearest example.[6]
- Poor Judgment: Poseidon's enormous lifespan and power did not necessarily translate into good decisions. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] considered his life an example of extraordinary potential wasted through entitlement and lack of meaningful self-improvement.[5][1]
- Divinity Suppression: The shackles used after his defeat could suppress Poseidon's Divine power enough that the injured God-King could not meaningfully resist his captors.[3]
- Soul Vulnerability: Poseidon's Divine Soul was powerful but not immune to beings capable of directly manipulating or consuming Divine spiritual structures.
- Concept Theft: Victor ultimately killed Poseidon and removed the Concept of the Seas from his existence.[1]
- Psychological Breakdown: Years of torture under [[Medusa]] eventually destroyed most of Poseidon's former pride and desire to resist.[5][2]
- Inferior to Higher Existences: By the mature Imperial era, Poseidon's former God-King status was irrelevant against beings on Victor's level.

### Relationships

#### Medusa Elderblood
[[Medusa]] Elderblood was one of the people most profoundly harmed by Poseidon and ultimately became the central reason [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] refused to simply kill him during the collapse of [[Olympus]].
Long before Poseidon physically appeared, Victor promised Medusa that the people responsible for her suffering would eventually belong completely to her. Victor treated the promise seriously enough that, once Olympus finally fell, Poseidon was deliberately kept alive despite Victor exterminating numerous other Greek Gods.[4]
After the war Poseidon was chained, weakened and delivered to Medusa together with [[Athena]]. His former Divine rank was considered irrelevant; Victor explicitly framed his continued life as existing because Medusa still had a use for him.
Medusa spent years exacting revenge until eventually becoming satisfied and bored with continuing the punishment.[2]
Only after Medusa no longer cared what happened to him did Victor finally kill Poseidon.

#### Athena
[[Athena]] was connected to the same traumatic history involving [[Medusa]] and was consequently punished alongside Poseidon.
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] explicitly reminded Poseidon that both he and Athena had hurt Medusa and that their treatment under his faction existed because of those actions.[5]
Following [[Olympus]]' fall, Poseidon and Athena were both placed beneath Medusa's authority and taken away for her revenge.[3][8]

#### Zeus
[[Zeus]] was Poseidon's brother and one of the other members of the [[Big Three]]. Both remained important parts of the Olympian order, although Zeus held the superior political position as King of [[Olympus]].
Poseidon remained with Zeus' faction throughout the decline of the Pantheon rather than joining the Goddesses who defected. Their political order ultimately collapsed when [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] permanently killed Zeus during the [[Greek Underworld]] conflict.
Zeus' death marked the definitive end of the hierarchy in which Poseidon had spent most of his Divine existence.

#### Hades
[[Hades]] was Poseidon's brother and the third member of the [[Big Three]].
While [[Zeus]] ruled [[Olympus]] and Poseidon was associated with [[Atlantis]] and the Seas, Hades governed the [[Greek Underworld]]. Hades' increasing political isolation from Olympus eventually contributed to the fragmentation that allowed the old Greek order to collapse.
The novel has not extensively explored Poseidon's personal feelings toward Hades.

#### Hestia Elderblood
[[Hestia]] Elderblood was Poseidon's sister and represented a worldview almost opposite to the one Poseidon had embraced.
During Poseidon's final conversation with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]], the values Victor displayed—family, responsibility and attachment despite overwhelming power—reminded Poseidon of ideas associated with Hestia. Poseidon found such principles difficult to understand precisely because his own conception of godhood treated extreme power as freedom from ordinary restraint.[5]
Victor's continued adherence to those values became part of what forced Poseidon to reconsider the life he had lived.

#### Rhea
[[Rhea]] was Poseidon's mother and one of the ancient members of the Greek family who eventually abandoned the old Olympian order.
While Poseidon remained aligned with [[Zeus]], Rhea joined the Goddesses who left [[Olympus]] and ultimately entered [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s growing faction. Their paths therefore separated during the final collapse of the Pantheon.

#### Kronus
Kronus was Poseidon's father and former ruler of [[Olympus]]. Poseidon belonged to the generation that succeeded the Titan order and later became one of the [[Big Three]] beneath [[Zeus]]' Olympian regime.
Kronus eventually returned as leader of the Titan faction and later formed a temporary alliance with Zeus during the final Greek war. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] ultimately killed Kronus during the same broader conflict that resulted in Poseidon's capture.

#### Victor Elderblood
[[Victor Elderblood]] became Poseidon's conqueror, captor and eventual executioner.
Victor's hostility toward Poseidon was personal long before the two directly interacted because Poseidon had hurt [[Medusa]]. Victor promised Medusa that she would eventually be allowed to determine Poseidon's fate and later used the collapse of [[Olympus]] to fulfil that promise.[4]
After capturing Poseidon, Victor stripped his former titles of practical meaning and left him beneath Medusa's control rather than granting him a quick death.
Years later, after Medusa had finished with him, Victor summoned Poseidon for one final conversation. Their discussion exposed the philosophical divide between them. Poseidon could not understand why someone with Victor's power remained so attached to family and responsibility, while Victor regarded Poseidon's failure to use his own extraordinary potential responsibly as evidence that Poseidon had wasted nearly everything he had been given.[5][1]
Victor then completed his final purpose for preserving Poseidon: he killed him, absorbed his Seas Divinity and redirected the acquired Concept toward the creation of something new.[1][2]

### Death and Legacy
Poseidon's death occurred only after every other reason for keeping him alive had disappeared.
His first purpose as a prisoner was [[Medusa]]'s revenge. Once she became satisfied after years of torture, [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] no longer needed Poseidon alive for personal reasons.[2]
The remaining reason was his Divinity.
Victor had begun developing a new marine project and recognized that Poseidon's Concept of the Seas could be repurposed. The same Divine authority that had formed the foundation of Poseidon's identity for eons was useful as raw Conceptual material for Victor's creation.[1]
Victor completely killed Poseidon and took the Seas Concept for himself. By the beginning of Chapter 1073, [[Gaia]], Nyx and [[Amaterasu]] could observe Poseidon beside Victor and immediately understood that the God was finally being finished because Medusa had no further interest in him. Poseidon's remaining form ultimately disappeared into pure Energy in Victor's hand.[2]
Victor did not resurrect Poseidon as an Immortal Soldier, reincarnate him or preserve his independent identity. The individual Poseidon ceased to exist.
His Concept survived through Victor.
Immediately afterward, Victor continued work involving the World of Water and the creation of new marine beings. Poseidon's final contribution to the future was therefore involuntary but significant: a Concept previously monopolized by an ancient Sea God became part of the foundation Victor used while creating new life.[1][2]

#### Skills and Abilities
- God Physiology: As one of the strongest Third Generation Gods and the King of [[Atlantis]], Poseidon possesses all of the gods basic abilities to a greater degree, like Superhuman Strength, Superhuman Speed and Reflex, Enhanced Durability, Enhanced Agility, Enhanced Sense, Regeneration, [[Immortality]], Divine Power, Divinity, Blessing and Curse Bestowal,....,etc.
- Divinities: Poseidon has major divinities of Seas and minor on Water, Wind, Earthquakes and Horses.

- Poseidon can create storms, hurricanes, tempests, floods, rain and hale.
- Hydrokinesis: Poseidon can manipulate and control water as he wishes.
- Anemokinesis: Poseidon can manipulate air to create currents of wind.
- Seas and Oceans Lordship: Poseidon can control seas and oceans and all things in them including living creatures.
- Creation: Poseidon is the god who created horses.
