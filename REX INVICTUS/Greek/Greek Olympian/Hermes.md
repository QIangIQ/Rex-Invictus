**[[Hermes]]** is the [[Olympians|Olympian]] messenger of the gods and god of travelers, roads, thieves, merchants, trade, language and boundaries. He guides souls to the [[Greek Underworld]] as psychopomp. He is the son of [[Zeus]] and the nymph Maia. His Roman counterpart is [[Mercury]]. In the Empire he is [[Divine Being 'The One Upon Swift Wings'|The One Upon Swift Wings]] and, in the old timeline, "Hermes Clepsiphron".

## Greek History (IRL)

**Birth.** Born at dawn in a cave on Mount Cyllene, by noon he had invented the lyre from a tortoise shell, and by evening he had stolen fifty cattle from [[Apollo]], walking them backward to hide their tracks. He traded the lyre to [[Apollo]] for the cattle and the caduceus.

**Roles.** Herald of [[Zeus]], guide of the dead, god of luck, trade, cunning and roads. He killed the hundred-eyed Argus to free Io. He led Persephone back from the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]]. He gave Perseus the winged sandals and Odysseus the herb moly against [[Circe]]. His symbols are the caduceus, the winged sandals (talaria) and the traveler's hat.

**Primary sources:** *Homeric Hymn to Hermes*, Homer's *Odyssey*, Ovid.

## PJO Canon

Hermes is the father of [[Luke Castellan]], whose bitterness at his father's absence drives him to [[Kronos]]. He takes in unclaimed demigods in [[Hermes' Cabin]] (Cabin 11). In [[The Sea of Monsters]] he gives [[Percy Jackson|Percy]] a thermos of winds and vitamins. In [[The Last Olympian]] he reveals that he knew [[Luke Castellan|Luke]]'s fate from the Oracle and blames himself. His snakes George and Martha live on his caduceus. He also fathered the Stoll brothers and [[Chris Rodriguez]].

He has salt-and-pepper hair and an easy smile, and he is ticking off pre-flight checks on a caduceus that doubles as a clipboard, beside the city-sized *Spear of Zeus*. He is friendly, folksy and paternal, calling people "hero" and "my boy", until someone accuses the gods of betrayal. Then his eyes harden and he delivers a cold lecture: the gods made demigods, fought for them and gave them power, and owe them nothing. The gods are tired of walking the same roads and fear losing control of mortals, so they leave for "new frontiers, new lands, and new people". He isn't privy to Zeus's plans. In a moment of pity, unable to deny a broken hero completely, he swears "upon my name, Hermes Clepsiphron" that the gods will return, then vanishes in a scatter of feathers. The oath goes unkept for millennia.

Hermes is a Third Generation Olympian God, the son of [[Zeus]] and Maia, and a former member of the Twelve Olympians of the [[Greek Pantheon]]. He served [[Olympus]] as a Messenger God and is directly identified as the God of Thieves, a Speed-Aspected God, and, much later in the story, a God of Language.[3][2][5][4] His relationship with Speed made him one of the principal benchmarks for extreme movement among Earth's Gods; Hermes personally considered himself the fastest God, while other characters independently placed Speed-Aspected Gods such as him among the very small number of beings capable of contending with specialists such as Natashia.[5][1]
Hermes' Divine authority over Language also gives him a previously unexplored connection to Runes. In Chapter 1185, Victor Elderblood explains that Gods of Language are Gods of Runes because Runes are fundamentally languages of power used to issue instructions to Reality. Victor specifically names Hermes as an ancient example and states that Hermes possessed the potential to become a Rune Master, although the story has never demonstrated Hermes actually reaching that level of Runic mastery.[4]
Hermes has one of the stranger status histories among the surviving Olympians. Nyx explicitly remembers Hermes having died during the collapse surrounding the Greek Pantheon, yet he later appears alive in Chapter 795 and attempts to assist [[Erebus]] against Victor.[6][1] No chapter explains who originally killed Hermes, how he died, or how he returned. His reappearance should therefore be recorded as an unexplained return rather than evidence for a Hermes-specific Resurrection ability.
The destruction of Zeus' Olympian order ended Hermes' former political position. Although surviving supernatural factions on Earth were eventually absorbed beneath the wider order created by Victor, the reviewed material does not directly show Hermes personally joining Dragons Nest or receiving a specific office within the [[Elderblood Empire]]. His last direct physical appearance remains his retreat from Victor in Chapter 795, while Chapter 1185 refers to him retrospectively during Victor's explanation of Runes.[1][4]

### Physical Appearance
The novel has never provided a detailed permanent physical description for Hermes. His hair color, eye color, exact height and normal clothing have not been established sufficiently in the text for them to be treated as canonical characteristics. The current portrait is therefore an artistic representation rather than a definitive depiction.
Hermes possesses the humanoid appearance expected of an Olympian God. During his encounter with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] in the [[Greek Underworld]], Victor first perceived him simply as a man rushing toward the confrontation at extraordinary speed. The scene focuses almost entirely upon Hermes' movement rather than his appearance.[1]

### Personality
Hermes is pragmatic, expressive and considerably more willing than many Olympians to acknowledge when a battle is not worth fighting. When the adult [[Typhon]] returned and [[Olympus]]' Gods discussed how to respond, Hermes immediately recommended fleeing. His response was initially treated as cowardly by more prideful Gods, but subsequent discussion supported the underlying logic: Olympus' ancient victory had occurred against a younger Typhon under much more favorable conditions and had still been catastrophically difficult.[3]
This survival instinct does not make Hermes completely unwilling to take risks. During the [[Greek Underworld]] conflict he deliberately attempted to interfere with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] while the latter was confronting [[Erebus]], using his greatest advantage—Speed—to create an opportunity for the Primordial God. He had no personal need to approach Victor at that moment, suggesting at least situational loyalty toward the Greek side and Erebus.[1]
Hermes is also extremely confident in his Speed. Victor's ability to perceive and react to him genuinely shocked him, prompting Hermes to openly question how Victor could respond when Hermes was supposed to be the fastest God. His surprise implies that having opponents fail to properly perceive his movements was normally expected rather than exceptional.[1]
At the same time, Hermes is not so prideful that he will continue an obviously failing engagement merely to protect his reputation. After Victor matched his movement and Erebus bluntly told him that his intervention was not working, Hermes called Erebus ungrateful and immediately escaped instead of attempting another suicidal attack.[1] This combination of confidence and self-preservation distinguishes him from Olympians whose pride repeatedly pushed them into battles they could no longer win.

### Synopsis

#### Demonic War
Hermes made his first direct appearance during the crisis created by the return of [[Typhon]]. The Beast's emergence temporarily overshadowed the internal conflict consuming the [[Greek Pantheon]], forcing [[Zeus]] and the remaining Olympians to consider whether they could survive a second war against the creature that had once nearly destroyed [[Olympus]].[3]
The meeting quickly revealed that the current situation was substantially worse than the ancient battle remembered by the Gods. Typhon had matured, Olympus was internally divided, many valuable allies were gone, and several Goddesses had already concluded that the Pantheon was approaching disaster. Hermes immediately proposed the simplest solution: flee.[3]
[[Poseidon]] objected to abandoning Olympus, but [[Apollo]] supported the central logic behind Hermes' warning. The first victory against Typhon had occurred while the Beast was comparatively young, the Pantheon was in a better position, and Fate itself had favored Olympus. Even then the conflict had resulted in enormous losses. Attempting to face the fully mature Beast under the Pantheon's current conditions bordered on suicide.[3]
The situation deteriorated further when [[Athena]] and [[Ares]] reported that [[Aphrodite]] Elderblood, [[Hestia]] Elderblood, [[Demeter]], [[Rhea]], Nike and other Goddesses had removed enormous quantities of Olympus' wealth before leaving. Zeus responded by sealing Olympus and forbidding the remaining Gods from escaping, directly preventing Hermes from following the strategy he had advocated.[3]
Hermes did not directly participate in [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s campaign through Hell, but his reputation was used to establish the scale of Victor's rapidly increasing Speed. In Chapter 654, Victor slaughtered more than forty thousand Demons and a Demonic Duke within approximately three seconds. The resulting speed was compared with the kind of extreme movement normally associated with Messenger Gods such as Hermes.[2]
Hermes was again invoked as a benchmark during the confrontation between the Olympian Goddesses and Victor's Countesses. Agnes explained the danger of Natashia's speed and identified only Victor, specialized Speed-Aspected Gods such as Hermes, and [[Scathach]] using her strongest Technique as beings who possessed a realistic chance of directly contending with her movement.[5] The comparison demonstrates that Hermes' reputation was not based solely upon his own boast; other powerful and experienced supernatural beings independently recognized him as one of Earth's premier speed specialists.

#### King Among Kings
Hermes' situation became considerably more confusing after the Demonic War. During Chapter 706, Nyx recalled observing the chaos within the [[Greek Pantheon]] after Hermes died, describing him as one of the Gods associated with athletics.[6] No battle involving his death is shown, and the story does not identify his killer, cause of death or the condition of his Soul afterward.
The report becomes especially important because Hermes later returns without explanation. By Chapter 795, he is physically active in the [[Greek Underworld]] during [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s intervention in the conflict between the remaining Olympian and Titan factions.[1]
Victor had confronted the ancient [[Primordial Gods]] [[Erebus]] and [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]]. While Victor was dealing with Erebus, the surrounding world appeared to slow from his perspective and he detected another man moving toward them at extraordinary speed. The newcomer was Hermes.[1]
Hermes evidently expected his speed to allow him to interfere before Victor could properly react. Instead, Victor's enhanced Draconic perception followed his movement. Red Lightning covered Victor's body and he accelerated until he was capable of responding at approximately the same speed.[1]
Victor attacked. Hermes successfully reacted and dodged the strike, confirming that his reflexes were not merely sufficient for running at extreme speed but also for reacting to another combatant who had accelerated into his movement. The evasion was uncontrolled enough that Hermes immediately lost his footing and crashed face-first into the ground.[1]
Hermes was stunned less by the fall than by Victor's reaction itself. He openly questioned how Victor had perceived him before declaring that he was the fastest God. Erebus then asked what Hermes was doing, to which Hermes answered that he was trying to help. Erebus dryly remarked that it did not appear to be working. Hermes called him ungrateful and immediately fled the area.[1]
The encounter was brief, but it provides nearly all of Hermes' direct combat feats. He could move quickly enough to force the already exceptionally fast Victor into an immediate Lightning-assisted acceleration, could react after Victor matched his movement, and could evade Victor's counterattack. Conversely, the encounter also established that Hermes' speed was not an absolute advantage: Victor could perceive, match and threaten him once sufficiently focused.[1]
Hermes escaped the immediate confrontation and is not shown among the Gods Victor permanently killed during the subsequent destruction of the old Greek order. [[Zeus]] and Kronus eventually died, [[Olympus]] ceased functioning under its former political structure, and the surviving populations and Divine factions were reorganized beneath the new supernatural order. Hermes' exact personal activities after his retreat are not directly depicted.

#### Those Who Rule Reality
Hermes does not physically appear during the Imperial era, but Chapter 1185 reveals a previously unknown aspect of his Divinity while [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] teaches his daughters about the true nature of Runes.[4]
Victor explains that Runes are fundamentally a language of power. A Rune communicates a structured command to Reality, with effectiveness depending upon factors such as the user's understanding of Creation, racial compatibility, power and the significance of the supernatural language being used. This leads one of his daughters to ask what would happen if a God possessed authority over Runes themselves.[4]
Victor answers that such Gods already existed. He explains that Gods of Language are Gods of Runes and specifically names Hermes as an example from an ancient Pantheon.[4] This establishes that Hermes' Divine portfolio was broader than his previously demonstrated Thievery and Speed associations: he also possessed a Divine Concept related to Language.
Victor further indicates that Hermes possessed the potential to become a Rune Master. This should not be confused with confirmed mastery. Victor's explanation immediately emphasizes that Runecraft depends much more strongly upon knowledge and understanding of Reality than simply owning an appropriate Divine Concept. A God of Language does not automatically become an exceptional Runemaster merely because Language and Runes are conceptually related.[4]
Hermes therefore represents an important example in Victor's later explanation of Divine specialization. His Language Divinity granted extremely favorable conceptual compatibility with Runes, but developing that compatibility into true high-level Runecraft would still have required the study, understanding and mastery necessary to translate a Divine relationship into practical control.[4]

### Skills & Abilities
Hermes is a Third Generation Olympian God whose most prominent combat characteristic is his extraordinary Speed. He also possesses confirmed Divine relationships with Thievery and Language, with the latter producing natural conceptual compatibility with Runecraft.[3][5][4]

#### God Physiology
- God Physiology: Hermes is an ancient Olympian Divine Being and possesses the enhanced supernatural body, Soul, senses and Energy associated with the Gods of the [[Greek Pantheon]].
- Agelessness: Hermes has existed for an enormous period without suffering conventional mortal aging.
- Superhuman Physical Characteristics: His Divine body possesses physical strength, durability, agility, reflexes and sensory abilities vastly beyond ordinary Humans.
- [[Divine Energy]] Manipulation: Hermes can utilize Divine Energy to sustain his supernatural existence and express his Divinities.
- Divine Soul: As a God, Hermes possesses a supernatural Soul capable of supporting Divine Concepts and Divine Energy. His unexplained appearance after being reported dead should not, however, be used as proof of a unique personal Resurrection power.[6][1]

#### Divinities
- Divinity Manipulation: Hermes possesses multiple Divine Concepts corresponding to his traditional functions within [[Olympus]].
- Speed: Hermes is directly classified alongside Speed-Aspected Gods and personally claims to be the fastest God. Speed is the overwhelmingly dominant characteristic demonstrated in his combat scenes.[5][1]
- Thievery: Chapter 623 directly identifies Hermes as the God of Thieves. The novel has not yet demonstrated a specific supernatural theft technique derived from this Divinity, so mythological abilities should not be added without further evidence.[3]
- Language: Chapter 1185 identifies Hermes as a God of Language. Because Runes are themselves supernatural languages used to issue commands to Reality, [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] explains that Gods of Language possess a direct conceptual relationship with Runes.[4]

#### Speed Manipulation
- Supernatural Speed: Hermes is one of the greatest dedicated speed specialists explicitly identified among Earth's Gods. Both his reputation and his own statement place Speed at the center of his Divine combat style.[5][1]
- Extreme Movement Speed: Hermes can move so rapidly that his motion becomes extremely difficult for even powerful supernatural observers to process normally.[1]
- Extreme Acceleration: He can rapidly transition into his high-speed state and enter an ongoing confrontation before conventional combatants would be able to react.[1]
- Accelerated Perception: Hermes can perceive and respond to events while moving at his extreme speeds; otherwise his own movement would render controlled combat impossible.[1]
- Enhanced Reaction Speed: After [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] detected Hermes and accelerated with Red Lightning, Hermes still recognized the counterattack and changed his movement quickly enough to avoid the strike.[1]
- High-Speed Evasion: Hermes successfully dodged Victor after the latter accelerated to approximately his movement speed.[1]
- Rapid Battlefield Intervention: Hermes attempted to enter Victor and [[Erebus]]' confrontation almost instantaneously, demonstrating the tactical use of his Speed for rescue and interference.[1]
- Rapid Long-Distance Travel: His function as a Messenger God and the repeated use of Messenger Gods as a benchmark for extraordinary Speed indicate that extreme traversal is one of the natural applications of his Divine specialization.[2][1]
- Speed-Based Combat: Hermes can use his movement to approach, evade and reposition around opponents before ordinary fighters can respond.
- Messenger-Class Movement: Victor's Chapter 654 feat of massacring more than forty thousand Demons and a Demonic Duke in approximately three seconds was described as entering the kind of speed normally associated with Messenger Gods such as Hermes.[2]
- Elite Speed Scaling: Agnes identified Speed-Aspected Gods such as Hermes among the limited beings who could potentially contend with Natashia's extreme movement, alongside Victor and [[Scathach]] using her strongest Technique.[5]
Clarification: The scene in Chapter 795 describing the surrounding world as effectively slowing should not be treated as Hermes possessing Time Manipulation. The effect occurs in the context of extreme movement and perception; no chapter states that Hermes literally stopped or slowed Time.[1]

#### Language Divinity
- Language Manipulation: Hermes possesses Divine Authority associated with Language. This power is directly confirmed by [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] in Chapter 1185 rather than inferred solely from real-world mythology.[4]
- Divine Language Affinity: Hermes possesses an inherent conceptual relationship with languages and their supernatural expression through his Divinity.[4]
- Rune Affinity: Victor explicitly explains that Gods of Language are Gods of Runes because Runes function as languages capable of issuing commands to Reality.[4]
- Rune-Master Potential: Hermes possessed the natural conceptual foundation necessary to potentially develop into a genuine Rune Master.[4]
- Reality-Command Compatibility: Since Runic language translates meaning into supernatural instructions imposed upon Reality, Hermes' Language Divinity would provide unusually favorable compatibility with the system if combined with sufficient knowledge and training.[4]
Limitation: Hermes is not confirmed to have actually become a Rune Master. Victor specifically explains that Rune effectiveness depends far more upon the user's understanding of Creation, compatibility and practical knowledge than simply possessing an appropriate Divinity. Language Authority therefore provides potential and conceptual affinity, not automatic mastery.[4]

#### Thievery Divinity
- Thievery Manipulation: Hermes is directly called the God of Thieves during the Olympians' discussion of [[Typhon]].[3]
- Divine Authority over Thievery: His Divine portfolio encompasses the Concept associated with thieves and thievery.
The novel has not yet shown Hermes using this Divinity to steal powers, Concepts, Souls, possessions or abstract properties. Such applications should not be added from mythology or external power systems without a direct novel statement.

#### Messenger God
- Messenger God: Hermes served as one of the messenger-type Gods of [[Olympus]] and is directly called a Messenger God during the Greek conflict.[2][1]
- Rapid Information Delivery: His extraordinary Speed makes him naturally suited to delivering information across large distances.
- Rapid Deployment: Hermes can arrive at active conflicts extremely quickly, as demonstrated by his sudden intervention while [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] was dealing with [[Erebus]].[1]
- Mobility Specialization: The story treats Messenger Gods as an archetype associated with exceptional Speed rather than merely ceremonial messengers.[2]

#### Athletic Ability
- Enhanced Athleticism: Nyx refers to Hermes as one of the athletes of the Gods when recalling his reported death.[6] This is consistent with his exceptional bodily coordination, reflexes and movement specialization, although the statement does not by itself establish a separate canonical Divinity of Athletes.

#### Combat Skill
- Speed-Oriented Combat: Hermes' fighting style centers upon exploiting superior movement and reaction speed rather than overwhelming opponents through the raw destructive power associated with Gods such as [[Zeus]].
- Evasion: His clearest combat feat is avoiding [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s strike after Victor accelerated with Red Lightning to intercept him.[1]
- Surprise Intervention: Hermes attempted to exploit an ongoing confrontation by approaching Victor at a speed he expected would exceed Victor's perception.[1]
- Combat Awareness: Once Victor demonstrated the ability to perceive him, Hermes reacted instead of continuing blindly into the attack.[1]
- Tactical Retreat: Hermes does not hesitate to withdraw once a confrontation becomes unfavorable. He escaped immediately after his attempt to aid [[Erebus]] failed.[1]

### Limitations
- Speed Is Not Absolute: Hermes' greatest advantage can be overcome by opponents possessing sufficiently advanced perception and comparable acceleration. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s Draconic perception successfully identified his movement and Red Lightning allowed Victor to respond.[1]
- Not Unreactable: Hermes was surprised precisely because Victor managed to react to him, establishing that his status as the "fastest God" should not be interpreted as a metaphysical law preventing every other being from ever matching or exceeding his speed.[1]
- High-Speed Control Can Fail: Hermes avoided Victor's attack but immediately crashed face-first afterward, showing that successfully evading at extreme speed did not mean every movement remained perfectly controlled.[1]
- Rune Affinity Is Not Rune Mastery: Language Divinity provides conceptual compatibility with Runes, but Victor explicitly states that true Runecraft depends primarily upon understanding, knowledge and compatibility. Hermes is therefore only confirmed to possess Rune-Master potential.[4]
- Thievery Techniques Unknown: Although Hermes is the God of Thieves, no specific abstract theft, power theft or Concept-stealing ability has been shown.[3]
- Can Die: Hermes was explicitly reported dead by Chapter 706, proving that his Divine existence was not immune to lethal defeat.[6]
- Return Mechanism Unknown: His reappearance in Chapter 795 is unexplained. It should not be listed as automatic Resurrection, reincarnation, regeneration or Pantheon revival without a chapter establishing the mechanism.[6][1]

### Relationships

#### Zeus
[[Zeus]] was Hermes' father, God-King and political superior within the old Olympian hierarchy. Hermes served Zeus' [[Olympus]] as a Messenger God and one of the Twelve Olympians, but he did not share Zeus' insistence that every threat had to be confronted through pride or force.
Their difference is clearest during [[Typhon]]'s return. Hermes immediately believed survival required abandoning Olympus, while Zeus eventually sealed the Divine Realm and prohibited anyone from leaving after discovering that several Goddesses had already fled with substantial portions of its treasury.[3]
Zeus' permanent death during [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s conquest ended the political structure under which Hermes had served for much of his existence.

#### Maia
Maia is Hermes' mother. The novel follows the traditional Olympian genealogy in identifying Hermes as one of [[Zeus]]' children, but no substantial interaction between Hermes and Maia has been shown.

#### Erebus
[[Erebus]] was one of the ancient [[Primordial Gods]] connected to the [[Greek Pantheon]]. Hermes attempted to help him when [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] physically confronted him in the [[Greek Underworld]].[1]
Hermes' assistance was voluntary and placed him directly in Victor's path. After Victor reacted to his speed, Erebus bluntly stated that Hermes' attempt did not seem to be helping. Hermes responded by calling him ungrateful and escaping.[1]
The scene suggests situational loyalty toward Erebus or the Greek faction, although the novel does not establish an especially close personal relationship between them.

#### Victor Elderblood
[[Victor Elderblood]] is the only character shown directly surprising Hermes by reacting to his supposedly overwhelming speed. Before the two ever fought, Victor's own movement had already reached a level comparable to Messenger Gods such as Hermes.[2]
Their direct encounter occurs in Chapter 795. Hermes approached while Victor was dealing with [[Erebus]], expecting his speed to provide the advantage. Victor's Draconic perception tracked him and Red Lightning increased Victor's movement enough to launch a counterattack at comparable speed. Hermes avoided the strike but was stunned that Victor had responded at all.[1]
The confrontation did not become an extended battle. Hermes recognized that the intervention had failed and immediately escaped. Victor subsequently destroyed the political system Hermes had served by permanently killing [[Zeus]] and overwhelming the remaining hostile Greek factions.
Hermes also later became Victor's example of the relationship between Divine Language and Runecraft. During his Chapter 1185 lesson, Victor specifically remembered Hermes when explaining that Gods of Language possess a conceptual relationship with Runes and that Hermes could have developed into a Rune Master under the appropriate conditions.[4]
Following Victor's marriage to [[Persephone Elderblood]], Hermes is also one of Victor's brothers-in-law through Zeus' bloodline.

#### Annasthashia Elderblood Fulger
Hermes and Natashia have never been shown fighting directly, but Agnes used Hermes' Divine specialization as a benchmark when explaining Natashia's speed.[5]
According to Agnes, only [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]], Speed-Aspected Gods such as Hermes, or [[Scathach]] using her strongest Technique possessed a realistic chance of contending with Natashia in a confrontation centered upon extreme Speed.[5] The statement is one of the clearest independent confirmations that Hermes' reputation extends beyond his own claim of being the fastest God.

#### Persephone Elderblood
[[Persephone Elderblood]] is Hermes' paternal half-sister through [[Zeus]]. Their personal relationship has received little direct attention, but [[Persephone]]'s later marriage to [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] places Hermes within Victor's extended Greek family.

### Clarifications
- Hermes is directly confirmed as the God of Thieves in Chapter 623.[3]
- Hermes is directly treated as a Messenger God in the novel.[2][1]
- Hermes is directly grouped with Speed-Aspected Gods in Chapter 661.[5]
- Hermes calls himself the fastest God in Chapter 795. This is a canonical statement by Hermes, but it should be distinguished from an omniscient declaration that no later or higher-sector God can ever surpass him.[1]
- Chapter 1185 reveals that Hermes is also a God of Language. This is a major part of his Divine portfolio missing from older versions of the article.[4]
- [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] explains that Gods of Language are conceptually Gods of Runes because Runes function as languages of power.[4]
- Hermes possessed Rune-Master potential, but he is not confirmed to have actually become a Rune Master.[4]
- The old classifications God of Traveling and God of Athletes should not be presented as confirmed novel Divinities without a direct statement. Chapter 706 calls Hermes one of the "athletes of the Gods", but that description does not by itself establish an Athletes Divinity.[6]
- Hermes' extreme-speed scene does not establish Time Manipulation. The apparent slowing of the surrounding world is presented through accelerated movement and perception.[1]
- Hermes should not automatically possess every ability associated with the mythological Hermes or [[Mercury]]. Only powers established by [[MTWABV Series|My Three Wives Are Beautiful Vampires]] should be indexed on this page.
- Hermes should not be given automatic Resurrection as a personal ability solely because he was reported dead in Chapter 706 and later appeared alive. The mechanism of his return is not shown.[6][1]
- Hermes' current membership in Dragons Nest is not directly demonstrated in the reviewed chapter material. The old [[Greek Pantheon]] collapsed and Earth's surviving supernatural powers later came beneath Victor's wider order, but political annexation should not be confused with confirmed direct Pantheon membership for every surviving God.
- The name Mercury is the Roman equivalent traditionally associated with Hermes; the novel itself primarily refers to the character as Hermes.

### Trivia
- Hermes is one of [[Zeus]]' many Divine children and was part of the Third Generation of the Greek Gods.
- He is one of the former Twelve Olympians.
- His first direct appearance occurs during the emergency meeting concerning [[Typhon]]'s return.[3]
- His first proposed solution to the adult Typhon crisis was simply to flee.[3]
- Hermes' Speed was already sufficiently famous that characters used his Divine archetype as a benchmark without Hermes being physically present.[2][5]
- Chapter 654, rather than Chapter 651, contains the comparison between [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s three-second massacre and the speed of Messenger Gods such as Hermes.[2]
- Nyx explicitly remembers Hermes as having died before Chapter 706.[6]
- The novel never reveals who killed Hermes or how that death occurred.
- Hermes was alive again by Chapter 795 without an explanation for his return.[1]
- Hermes managed to dodge Victor's Red Lightning-assisted counterattack despite being shocked that Victor could perceive him.[1]
- He immediately fell face-first after performing the dodge.[1]
- His attempt to aid [[Erebus]] ends with Erebus telling him that the attempt is not working, Hermes calling him ungrateful and fleeing.[1]
- Chapter 1185 significantly expands Hermes' lore by identifying him as a God of Language and connecting his Divinity with the fundamental theory behind Runes.[4]
- Hermes is one of the clearest examples demonstrating Victor's later explanation that possessing an appropriate Divinity does not automatically grant perfect mastery of every discipline related to that Concept. Despite Language being intimately related to Runes, Hermes would still have required sufficient understanding of Creation to become a true Rune Master.[4]
