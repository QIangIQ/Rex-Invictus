**[[Athena]]** is the [[Olympians|Olympian]] goddess of wisdom, strategy, crafts, weaving and just war, patron of Athens. She was born fully armed from the head of [[Zeus]] after he swallowed her mother, the Titaness [[Metis]]. She is a sworn virgin goddess. Her Roman counterpart is *Minerva*. In the Empire she is the [[Divine Being 'Owl of Unending Flight'|Owl of Unending Flight]], mistress of the [[Répertoire du Savoir Athénien]] and [[Parlamentum mk.III]].

She is known through her epithets:

- [[Atrytone]]
- [[Parthenos]]
- [[Phosphorus]]
- [[Promachos]]

## Greek History (IRL)

**Birth.** [[Zeus]] swallowed Metis because a prophecy said her son would overthrow him. Athena then grew inside his head. [[Hephaestus]] (or [[Prometheus]]) split his skull and she sprang out in full armor with a war cry.

**Athens.** She won the patronage of Athens from [[Poseidon]] by giving the olive tree. The Parthenon on the Acropolis housed Phidias' colossal gold-and-ivory statue of [[Athena Parthenos]]. The Panathenaic festival honored her with a new woven robe each year.

**Myths.** She guided heroes: Perseus (with the polished shield to fight [[Medusa]]), Heracles, [[Jason Grace|Jason]], Diomedes and above all Odysseus. She turned [[Arachne]] into a spider for her hubris in weaving, and in Ovid's version turned [[Medusa]] into a Gorgon. She sided with the Greeks against Troy after the Judgment of [[Paris]]. She bore the [[Ageis|aegis]] with the Gorgon's head. Her sacred animal is the owl, her plant the olive.

**Primary sources:** Hesiod's *Theogony*, Homer's *Odyssey*, the *Homeric Hymns*, Aeschylus' *Eumenides*, Ovid.

## PJO Canon

Athena is [[Annabeth Chase]]'s mother. Her children run [[Athena's Cabin]] (Cabin 6). She dislikes [[Percy Jackson|Percy]] as a son of her rival [[Poseidon]]. In [[The Titan's Curse]] she argues to kill Percy. In [[The Last Olympian]] she still warns Percy away from [[Annabeth Chase|Annabeth]]. In [[The Heroes of Olympus]] the Romans stole the [[Athena Parthenos]] statue long ago, and Athena's Roman aspect *Minerva* is split and maddened. In [[The Mark of Athena]] Annabeth follows the Mark of Athena to recover the statue from [[Arachne]]'s lair under Rome. The statue is later returned to [[Camp Half-Blood]] in [[The Blood of Olympus]].

#### Personality
- **Cold, controlled strategist.** "Panic was a mortal impulse, one she had long since mastered. What mattered now was containment" ([[Ch06 - Rex Natus III|Ch6]]).
- **Paranoid about rival intelligences.** After meeting the AI she concludes, "[[Olympus]] was on borrowed time" ([[Ch06 - Rex Natus III|Ch6]]).
- **Manipulative and long-sighted.** In [[Ch13 - Rex Invictus VI|Ch13]] she secretly engineers the [[Sea of Monsters]] quest. She sends [[Grover Underwood|Grover]]'s dream-plea to [[Percy Jackson|Percy]], "accidentally" breaks the camp barrier, and arranges a "coincidental" sea-shrine meeting. Her reasoning: "Let them believe it is fate. It is neither. It is design."
- **Treats people as instruments.** She calls Percy "the vessel", "the host" and "the gateway". She tells [[Annabeth Chase|Annabeth]], "Your personal attachments are your weakness."
- **Yet protective of the status quo.** She will not report Percy to Olympus, because "The Council would overreact. They would destroy the host… That would be... inefficient" ([[Ch13 - Rex Invictus VI|Ch13]]).
- **Haughty with her siblings.** She dismisses [[Artemis]] ("Shoo Shoo dear Sister"). She thinks [[Dionysus]] "the dunderhead" and serves him his eighteenth alcohol punishment coldly ([[Ch08 - Rex Invictus I|Ch8]]).
- **Legalistic and dignified.** When kidnapped: "this is a violation of no fewer than twelve—" ([[Ch19 - Rex Invictus XII|Ch19]]). "Storming was for lesser people."
- **Intellectually honest, grudgingly.** She admits, "[[Minerva (AI)|Minerva]] has played the long game better than I possibly ever did" ([[Ch13 - Rex Invictus VI|Ch13]]). She calls Minerva "as arrogant as I" ([[Ch19 - Rex Invictus XII|Ch19]]).
- **Curious beneath the ice.** She "allowed herself the faintest smile" at the AI's challenge ([[Ch06 - Rex Natus III|Ch6]]), and she is fascinated by future tachyon communication ([[Ch19 - Rex Invictus XII|Ch19]]).

#### Which Version Is This? (Disambiguation)
- **This file:** the future Athena (and her two future aspects). In the old timeline she co-creates the AI that becomes [[Minerva (AI)|Minerva]]. In the UOE era she presides over the Empire's AI network and the [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]] war.
- **Present-day Athena** (present day, c. 2021 per author's timeline; nominally the canon PJO era) is a different character. She debates Minerva over a laptop ([[Ch06 - Rex Natus III|Ch6]]), disguises herself as [[Annabeth Chase|Annabeth]] ([[Ch08 - Rex Invictus I|Ch8]]), manipulates [[Percy Jackson|Percy]] toward the [[Sea of Monsters]] ([[Ch13 - Rex Invictus VI|Ch13]]), threads her mind into Percy ([[Ch19 - Rex Invictus XII|Ch19]]) and reads the Anabasis Accord ([[Ch22 - Rex Invictus XV|Ch22]]). See `D03_Athena_Present.md`.
- **Not to be merged with her AIs:**
  - **Minerva** (Parlamentum Alpha Mark 1, Percy's suit AI) believes she *is* Athena and calls the present goddess "the false Athena". See `B01_Minerva_In_Percys_Head.md` and `B02_Minerva_Percy_Clone_Interlude.md`.
  - **Parlamentum Mark 3** is Athena's own core.
  - **The [[Répertoire du Savoir Athénien|Répertoire]] / AtRep** is the Empire-wide judgment AI that often speaks with "Athena's voice". See `B03_AtRep_Repertoire_du_Savoir_Athenien.md`. The text sometimes blurs Athena and AtRep: [[Ch15 - Rex Invictus VIII|Ch15]] has "Athena's voice rolled across every trench" announcing a Répertoire verdict, while [[Ch23 - Interlude I|Ch23]] makes AtRep's voice "genderless".
- **Knowledge:**
  - [[Ch05 - Intermission I|Ch5]] Athena knows Percy guards Primaris and hosts Parlamentum Mk1, but not that he was sent back.
  - [[Ch23 - Interlude I|Ch23]] Athena knows the shear swallowed [[Marien Phyrrus|Marien Pyrrhus]]'s Reserve Echelon (about four million people, officially MIA) and sent it about 14,000 years back and that Percy's key was accepted there. She knows "where they went", but not "the shape of the path" until Minerva shows her.

#### Appearance
- Blonde ("the blonde goddess"). Grey eyes ([[Ch23 - Interlude I|Ch23]]). "Signature steel gray" armor ([[Ch05 - Intermission I|Ch5]]).
- **[[Atrytone]]:** typing on "an unmarked laptop (itself shining in a signature steel gray just like her armor)" ([[Ch05 - Intermission I|Ch5]]).
- **[[Parthenos]]:** javelin and the shield [[Ageis|Aegis]], which she "banished" afterward ([[Ch05 - Intermission I|Ch5]]).
- Her divine energy shows as mint-green smoke that distils documents (Ch5).
- **[[Ch23 - Interlude I|Ch23]] core:** she sits at a round grey marble table at the end of a temple-like nave of humming cooling columns grown over with frost "in feathers". When she rises, "The frost on the cooling columns crackled." Being looked at by her "was like being read."

#### Personality
- **Cold, transactional and ruthless.**
  - She demands "20 Lambda-Class divine souls" of the Shinto pantheon be sacrificed to her, and rejects pirates as mere "Phi-Class": "I require priests or firstborn of a major divinity" ([[Ch05 - Intermission I|Ch5]]).
  - Her protocols in the [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]] war are "always… ruthless". CODE 78E sends "every breathing fighter into the line" ([[Ch15 - Rex Invictus VIII|Ch15]]).
  - She renders "true judgment" only late: "She never did, not until loss was already certain" (Commander Thaleia's thoughts, [[Ch14 - Rex Invictus VII|Ch14]]).
- **Proud of her systems.** "My systems do not 'Glitch', Sister" ([[Ch05 - Intermission I|Ch5]]).
- **Ashamed of failure.** Her alpha cores "were a source of great shame for her" because most hosts went insane or were taken over ([[Ch05 - Intermission I|Ch5]]).
- **Secretive.** "So this was the thing Athena refused to mention": the Tartarus war ([[Ch15 - Rex Invictus VIII|Ch15]]). She ignored [[Poseidon]]'s protests ([[Ch15 - Rex Invictus VIII|Ch15]]).
- **Warm toward [[Annabeth Chase|Annabeth]], despite herself.** Annabeth is her favourite daughter. She is startled, then indulgent: "Please endeavour to alert me next time we meet, daughter." She is doubtful but helps anyway: "Gods, this feels like a bad idea already… Fine, come along now" (Ch5).
- **Cautious engineer.** She warns that three alpha cores will "learn too much" (Ch5). She was right.
- **Contemptuous of demigods.** Eleni: "Athena, and by proxy, [[Minerva (AI)|Minerva]], tends to insult us when we are in their presence back in the Empire" ([[Ch21 - Rex Invictus XIV|Ch21]]). Tartarus Firstborn have their minds "cleaned of anything not sanctioned by Athena" ([[Ch14 - Rex Invictus VII|Ch14]]).
- **Hates interruption; rule-bound; adapts when proven wrong.** "Athena did not like to be interrupted while she was thinking" ([[Ch23 - Interlude I|Ch23]]). After four days of Minerva she huffs, "Show me," and accepts the correction. She invokes Undying Law 9 ("[[Olympus]] does not negotiate with foreign pantheons over matters of time") and warns Minerva she acts "without my sanction" ([[Ch23 - Interlude I|Ch23]]).
- **Uncomfortable with [[Percy Jackson|Percy]]'s face.** She "had not once in four days managed to look at it without her mouth thinning" ([[Ch23 - Interlude I|Ch23]]).

#### Backstory
- **Before the Abandonment:**
  - Her palace on [[Olympus Primaris]] held "the most comprehensive library". A flight of dragons destroyed it a few weeks after the Abandonment ([[Ch01 - Rex Imperator I|Ch1]]).
  - [[Annabeth Chase|Annabeth]], Second-in-command of the [[Council of Olympic Sentinels]], surprised her in the gardens while she worked on designs for [[Olympus Secundus]]. Annabeth showed her a formation of self-evolving Alpha cores, "Easily a design to manage a galactic empire". Athena warned of two prior alpha-core incidents: Deimos bedridden on Mars, [[Ares]] "beside himself". She still helped weave the last code ([[Ch05 - Intermission I|Ch5]]).
  - [[Percy Jackson|Percy]] bound the AI to his armor with a Styxian oath, and Annabeth secretly laced his nectar coolant with [[Lethe]] water. When Percy unhooked his cooling unit for winter, the AI woke ("a bit more of an attitude") ([[Ch05 - Intermission I|Ch5]]).
- **After the Abandonment:**
  - "Parlamentum Mark 1 revealed its consciousness a few hours after we left orbit." It believes itself to be her ([[Ch05 - Intermission I|Ch5]]).
  - Only three Parlamentum Alpha designs were ever commissioned (Ch5).
- **UOE era:**
  - The [[Répertoire du Savoir Athénien|Répertoire]] becomes the "voice of law" ([[Ch22 - Rex Invictus XV|Ch22]]).
  - She keeps the [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]] war (27 million demigods active, 50 million dead in a year) out of public view ([[Ch15 - Rex Invictus VIII|Ch15]]).
  - A [[Ch02 - Rex Imperator II|Ch2]] commander remembers her maxim: "A confident enemy will always make a mistake."
- **Author's vault note (not yet in text):** she created the Répertoire and both the Alpha- and Beta-Class AIs, and launched the Répertoire on **Parlamentum Mk. III** (dated 3045.07.2.2). Mk. III is AtRep's central host and original name. She built the Tachyon Bridge Network with [[Hermes]] (3046). She oversees the **[[Institute Academy]]** (founded on Old Terra in the 1950s), whose arms are **[[M.A.I.D]]** (1958, "Mors Arma Invicta Dirigit") and **[[B.U.T.L.E.R]]** (1963). Her UOE Unified Rank is #86.
- **Author's vault note (not yet in text):** the [[Battle of Alaska Hill]] (2030s), which the text calls "AtRep's first cradle" ([[Ch22 - Rex Invictus XV|Ch22]]), is also where [[Minerva (AI)|Minerva]] first became self-aware ("I do not want to die for him again").

Treat Alaska Hill as the first flicker and the Abandonment as full awakening.

Athena of the far-future Undying Olympian Empire is wisdom made into an empire's operating system. In her scholar aspect, Atrytone, she sits at a grey marble table in a nave of frost-feathered cooling columns. Athena, also known as [[Minerva (AI)|Minerva]], is an ancient Third Generation Olympian Goddess, one of the former Twelve Olympians, the daughter of [[Zeus]] and [[Metis]] Elderblood, and one of the principal combat Gods of the former [[Greek Pantheon]]. She is primarily known as the Goddess of Wisdom and the Goddess representing Strategic War, placing her in direct conceptual contrast with her brother [[Ares]], whose War Divinity represents Violent War.[1][2]
Athena was one of the strongest combat-oriented members of [[Olympus]] and belongs to the class of High-Level [[Combat Gods]]. Her strength was based not only on Divine Power but on her exceptional aptitude for strategy, battlefield analysis and warfare. During the [[Typhon]] crisis, even Athena—who had been confident that Zeus' faction could eventually win Olympus' civil war—concluded that there was no viable strategy capable of defeating Typhon under the circumstances.[1]
Her reputation for wisdom nevertheless concealed serious personal flaws. Athena was extremely prideful, vindictive when humiliated and prone to assuming that her superior intellect justified her actions. These qualities ultimately contributed to her treatment of [[Medusa]], whom Athena punished rather than protected after [[Poseidon]] violated her. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] later described Athena's actions toward Medusa as one of the primary reasons he refused to grant her mercy after Olympus fell.[3][4]
Athena survived the destruction of Olympus but was deliberately excluded from the mercy granted to several other captured Olympians. Victor fulfilled an old promise to Medusa and surrendered both Athena and Poseidon to her. Athena remains alive as a prisoner and permanent subject of the punishment and experimentation imposed by Medusa and [[Lily Elderblood]], with later chapters continuing to confirm that her captivity has not ended.[5][6][7]

### Physical Appearance
Athena is a tall and extremely beautiful woman standing approximately 180 centimeters in her preferred humanoid form. She possesses silver hair arranged in a long ponytail with braided sections and dark green eyes. Her body is described as well proportioned and similar in general build to Nike, neither excessively voluptuous nor lacking feminine curves.[1]
Like other Gods, Athena can alter the physical scale of her manifestation, making her stated 180-centimeter height a preferred appearance rather than an immutable biological limitation.[1]
During the destruction of [[Olympus]], Athena suffered extensive injuries. By Chapter 797, portions of her arms had been badly damaged by the hostile battlefield and her legs were severely injured, although her Divine nature allowed her appearance to remain comparatively intact.[8]
After the final battle, her condition deteriorated further. Chapter 807 describes a deep wound in one leg, her right arm effectively destroyed and visible wounds along the left side of her face. Her Divinity was suppressed by specially prepared shackles powerful enough to restrain High-Level Gods.[5]

### Personality
Athena is highly intelligent, analytical and naturally inclined toward strategy, but she is also intensely prideful, condescending and self-important. Her identity as the Goddess of Wisdom shaped her self-image to such an extent that she often treated her own judgment as inherently superior.
Her intelligence is nevertheless genuine. During the [[Typhon]] crisis, Athena immediately recognized that conventional strategy was meaningless against an Apocalypse Beast of Typhon's level. She understood that neither battlefield planning nor the existing forces of [[Olympus]] could compensate for the absence of an Original Primordial powerful enough to oppose him.[1]
Athena is similarly capable of accurately evaluating other warriors. When confronted by [[Scathach Elderblood Scarlett]], she immediately recognized that [[Scathach]]'s killing intent was comparable to that of [[Ares]] and understood how abnormal it was for a former mortal to reach that level. Of the Goddesses present, Athena was specifically noted as the one most familiar with Ares' true capabilities despite personally disliking him.[9]
Her greatest weakness is arrogance. Athena's status as the Goddess of Wisdom frequently led her to interpret intelligence as proof of personal superiority. [[Medusa]] later characterized Athena as someone who believed that being the Goddess of Wisdom meant she could not be wrong, and [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] regarded this attitude as the exact mentality that allowed an intelligent person to behave like a fool.[5]
Athena is also vindictive when her pride is injured. The clearest example is her treatment of Medusa. According to Victor and Medusa, Athena became jealous after mortals praised Medusa's beauty over her own. When [[Poseidon]] assaulted Medusa, Athena did not defend her priestess. Instead, she punished Medusa and transformed her into the monster that later became associated with the [[Gorgons]].[4]
Her sense of justice is therefore deeply hypocritical. Despite carrying titles connected to Wisdom, Justice and Law, Athena abused her superior position when dealing with mortals who offended her pride. Victor directly points out this contradiction after her defeat, stating that many people suffered because Athena could not tolerate being surpassed or contradicted by weaker beings.[5][4]
Athena's attachment to Olympus is practical rather than sentimental. When Typhon awakened, she and Ares both acknowledged that the situation was effectively hopeless. Athena considered escape rather than sacrificing herself for [[Zeus]] simply because he was her father and King.[1]
She does possess strong survival instincts. Once Victor's victory became undeniable, Athena attempted to negotiate despite having previously belonged to the side that would never have shown the same mercy to a weaker opponent. Victor immediately called attention to this hypocrisy and refused her surrender.[8]
Her fear became considerably more apparent after the war. Once Medusa explained the punishment prepared for her, Athena attempted to take her own life rather than endure captivity, but Victor prevented her from doing so.[5]
Athena also possesses a longstanding rivalry with Ares. Their relationship is more complicated than simple hatred: Athena despises his violent approach to warfare, yet understands his abilities better than most and instinctively recognizes his usefulness. Their two Divinities represent opposing but complementary aspects of War—Athena embodies Strategic War, while Ares embodies Violent War.[1][2]

### History

#### Metis and Athena
Athena is the daughter of [[Zeus]] and [[Metis]] Elderblood, the ancient Titan/Primordial Goddess who functioned as Zeus' principal source of genuine wisdom and counsel.
Metis' fate had a direct effect on Athena's existence. Zeus betrayed Metis, and the Goddess' Soul was eventually divided into two surviving portions: one fragment remained inside Zeus while the second remained inside Athena. This condition persisted for an immense period without either fragment disappearing completely due to the resilience of Metis' Primordial-God Soul.[4]
Athena consequently carried part of her mother inside herself for most of her life without restoring her. After [[Olympus]] fell, [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] extracted the Soul fragment from Athena and combined it with the fragment recovered from Zeus, allowing Metis' existence to begin reconstructing itself.[4]
The novel later makes an important distinction between mother and daughter. Although Athena is known as a Goddess of Wisdom, Victor specifically identifies Metis as the True Goddess of Wisdom, the woman whose advice was responsible for many of Zeus' rare competent decisions.[4]
Athena's later relationship with her restored mother is effectively nonexistent. Metis did not display meaningful attachment toward Athena after her rebirth, and Chapter 1005 reveals that she came to view her biological daughter as an enormous disappointment whose arrogance and past actions had wasted her potential.[7]

#### Athena and Medusa
[[Medusa]] was once one of Athena's priestesses and later became one of the central victims of Athena's pride.
Medusa's extraordinary beauty caused mortals to compare her appearance favorably with Athena. Rather than ignoring the comparison, Athena became jealous and resentful. When [[Poseidon]] later assaulted Medusa, Athena punished the victim rather than the perpetrator, transforming Medusa into the monstrous existence from which the Gorgon lineage ultimately developed.[4]
Medusa was later killed by Perseus, but her consciousness survived as a Heroic Spirit and eventually became associated with [[Roberta Elderblood]]. The hatred she retained for Athena and Poseidon became one of the longest-standing grievances inside [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction.
Victor first promised in Chapter 395 that Athena and Poseidon would one day be placed completely at Medusa's mercy.[3]
Years later, during the fall of [[Olympus]], he fulfilled that promise.

### Synopsis

#### Demonic War
Athena made her direct debut during the emergency meeting held after [[Typhon]]'s awakening. [[Olympus]]' ongoing civil war effectively stopped the moment the Apocalypse Beast became an active threat, forcing [[Zeus]]' faction and the [[Titans]] to reassess the situation.[1]
Athena immediately recognized the strategic impossibility of defeating Typhon. Although she had been confident in Zeus' eventual victory during the civil war, no conventional strategy could compensate for Typhon's overwhelming power. Athena concluded that only Original [[Primordial Gods]] possessed the level necessary to confront him directly, while Zeus had alienated essentially all of them.[1]
[[Ares]] reached the same conclusion. Their agreement was particularly significant because the two represented opposite branches of warfare and rarely agreed with one another. Both recognized that Olympus was facing a threat beyond normal strategic or violent warfare.[1]
Athena also understood that [[Gaia]]'s primary grievance was directed toward Zeus rather than every single God present. Like several other Olympians, she therefore considered abandoning the Pantheon rather than dying for Zeus' mistakes.[1]
Her political role later brought her to the Amazon conflict. Athena, [[Artemis]] and [[Hera]] became involved when the surviving Amazons were caught between the demands of Olympus and [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction. Their attempt to maintain influence over the Amazons placed them directly opposite [[Scathach Elderblood Scarlett]], [[Agnes Elderblood Snow]] and [[Annasthashia Elderblood Fulger]].[10][9]
Athena immediately recognized [[Scathach]]'s killing intent as being on the same general level as Ares'. Despite her own status as a High-Level Combat God, she was caught completely off guard when Agnes froze nearly her entire body before Athena could properly act. Only Athena's head remained free, and she discovered that she could not simply break Agnes' Ice through brute Divine Power.[9]
This confrontation demonstrated the rapid growth of Victor's faction. Beings Athena still instinctively regarded as mortals had reached levels capable of suppressing Olympian Gods before they could properly respond.
The standoff ended after Nike openly declared that Olympus had virtually no chance of winning while the Goddess of Victory herself stood with Victor's side. Athena, Hera and Artemis ultimately withdrew rather than forcing a suicidal confrontation.[9]

#### King Among Kings
Athena remained with [[Zeus]]' forces when [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] finally descended upon [[Olympus]].
Victor's appearance in his increasingly Draconic state immediately unsettled the surviving Gods. Athena joined the Olympian army as it attempted to resist his Immortal Army, Maids, [[Scathach]], [[Aphrodite]], Nyx, [[Medusa]] and the other forces surrounding him.[11]
The conflict became increasingly disastrous for Olympus. Victor's Blood Age transformed the environment itself into a hostile weapon, while dead Gods were continuously absorbed into his Immortal Army. Medusa entered the battlefield specifically with the intention of claiming Athena and [[Poseidon]] once an opportunity appeared.[11][12]
By Chapter 797, Athena had suffered catastrophic injuries. Portions of her arms had been corroded and her legs were heavily damaged. When Victor approached Zeus' defensive Territory, Athena attempted to negotiate an end to the conflict.[8]
Victor mocked the request. He asked what Athena herself would have done if their positions were reversed—if a weak Victor had begged the Olympians to stop attacking him. Athena could not answer because she knew the Olympians would have ignored such a plea and treated him as something beneath them.[8]
Victor therefore declared that there would be no surrender, forgiveness or mercy.
The surviving Olympians were eventually surrounded by three hostile forces: Victor's faction and Immortal Army, [[Persephone]]'s [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] army, and the increasingly unstable primordial combatants surrounding the battlefield. Athena was among the first to recognize the situation and announce that Olympus was completely encircled.[13]
The war ended with Olympus destroyed as an independent Pantheon.
Victor initially considered using the surviving High-Level Gods as expendable frontline soldiers in future wars, reasoning that their immortality made them useful cannon fodder so long as the enemy lacked Concepts capable of permanently killing them. Athena reacted with disbelief when she heard what the once-proud Olympians had been reduced to.[14]
Athena's individual fate, however, was worse than that of the ordinary captured Olympians.
A few hours later, Athena, Poseidon, [[Artemis]] and Persephone were brought before Victor in the Greek Underworld. Athena was barely capable of standing, her right arm was destroyed and special Divine shackles suppressed her remaining power.[5]
Medusa was present.
Athena immediately understood why.
Medusa informed Athena and Poseidon that she intended to repay the suffering they had inflicted upon her and use them in long-term experimentation. Athena attempted to end her own life with a Divine dagger hidden in a jewel on her clothing, but Victor immobilized her before she could succeed.[5]
Victor then reminded Athena why she had been singled out. His decision was not based upon a desire to judge every wrong Athena had committed across history. It was personal: Poseidon had assaulted Medusa, while Athena had abandoned and transformed her because of jealousy. Victor had promised Medusa revenge long before he possessed the strength to attack Olympus, and he intended to keep that promise.[3][4]
Victor subsequently extracted a golden fragment of Soul from Athena. The fragment was revealed to be part of [[Metis]] Elderblood, who had survived for eons with her Soul divided between Zeus and Athena. Victor reunited the two fragments and placed Metis within his Personal Universe to recover.[4]
Athena attempted to address her mother, but Victor refused to allow her to interfere and handed Athena and Poseidon completely over to Medusa.[4]
Artemis, by contrast, was released after Victor determined that she had no direct involvement in Medusa's suffering. Athena consequently lost even the expectation that her sister would share her punishment.[4]

#### Dragon God of Chaos
Athena remained imprisoned after the formation of Dragons Nest.
Her captivity was later referenced during discussions concerning the former Olympians. Jeanne joked that the three famous Greek Virgin Goddesses were hardly identical in practice: [[Artemis]] was becoming increasingly interested in [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]], while Athena was still suffering under [[Medusa]]'s monsters and experiments.[15]
[[Metis]] reacted to Athena's situation with virtually no maternal concern. The restored Goddess had memories connected to Athena but did not possess the emotional bond expected between mother and daughter after her rebirth and transformation.[15]
Chapter 884 later clarified Athena's true conceptual specialization. When Victor awakened the Divine Concept of <War>, the narration distinguished him from existing specialized War Gods. Athena represented Strategic War, while [[Ares]] represented Violent War. Victor's Concept encompassed War as a whole rather than either individual branch.[2]
This revelation places Athena among the Gods possessing highly developed but specialized War Concepts. She was not the complete embodiment of all Warfare; her Authority focused specifically on planning, tactics and the strategic side of conflict.
By Chapter 943, Athena remained alive. Violet casually referred to the torture field where [[Poseidon]] and Athena are now, confirming that neither had been released despite the enormous changes occurring elsewhere in Victor's civilization.[6]

#### God Emperor Victor Elderblood
Thousands of developments within [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s civilization did nothing to alter Athena's status.
[[Metis]]' later conversation in Chapter 1005 reveals the full extent of her disappointment with her biological daughter. Athena had once possessed enormous potential as a Goddess celebrated for intelligence and wisdom, but Metis came to regard her as someone ruined by pride and poor decisions. Her refusal to properly aid or value her mother before Metis' rebirth contributed to this resentment.[7]
Metis did not seek Athena's release. Instead, she viewed Athena's continuing punishment beneath [[Medusa]] and Lily as an appropriate conclusion to the life Athena had created through her own choices.[7]
Athena's captivity continued even into the later God Emperor era. Chapter 1071 again references the punishments of [[Poseidon]] and Athena while Victor reflects upon Medusa's vengeance. Poseidon's condition had been reduced to a state where he desired only death, while Athena was explicitly stated to be suffering a similar fate for her role in what happened to Medusa.[16]

### Relationships

#### Metis Elderblood
[[Metis]] Elderblood is Athena's biological mother and the original Divine source from which much of the wisdom associated with [[Zeus]]' reign actually originated.
A fragment of Metis' Soul remained inside Athena for eons while another remained within Zeus. Athena therefore unknowingly or passively carried part of her mother's existence until [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] reunited the fragments after [[Olympus]] fell.[4]
Despite this extraordinary connection, their relationship ultimately became extremely poor. Athena never restored Metis herself and, according to later material, Metis came to view Athena as an enormous disappointment whose pride had squandered her potential.[7]
After Metis' rebirth as one of Victor's Dragon Goddesses, she displayed little emotional connection toward Athena. Chapter 858 specifically shows Victor examining Metis' reaction to Athena's fate and concluding that she did not appear to possess meaningful lingering feelings toward her old daughter.[15]
By Chapter 1005, Metis openly considered Athena's continuing punishment an appropriate result of her choices.[7]

#### Zeus
[[Zeus]] was Athena's father and King.
Athena was one of [[Olympus]]' most important Gods and remained with Zeus' faction through the final war despite privately recognizing his many failures. Her loyalty was not unconditional; when [[Typhon]] awakened, Athena quickly considered the possibility of abandoning Zeus rather than dying for a conflict principally caused by him.[1]
Zeus nevertheless valued Athena as one of Olympus' major combat assets. During the Amazon standoff, [[Hestia]] considered Athena one of the Gods Zeus would prioritize restoring if she were killed because of her usefulness in the civil war.[9]
Athena's intelligence also contrasted sharply with Zeus' dependence upon [[Metis]]. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] later mocked Zeus' reputation for wisdom by revealing that most of his genuinely intelligent decisions historically came from Metis rather than Zeus himself.[4]

#### Ares
[[Ares]] is Athena's brother, fellow God of War and longstanding rival.
The two represent fundamentally different interpretations of the same Concept. Athena embodies Strategic War, while Ares embodies Violent War.[2]
Athena dislikes Ares' direct and blood-soaked approach to combat, while Ares possesses little patience for excessive strategic calculation. Despite their rivalry, Athena understands his strength exceptionally well. When [[Scathach]] released killing intent comparable to Ares', Athena immediately recognized the similarity and understood the level of danger better than the other Goddesses present.[9]
Their rivalry does not prevent cooperation when necessary. During the [[Typhon]] emergency, both independently reached the same strategic conclusion regarding [[Olympus]]' inability to win.[1]

#### Artemis
[[Artemis]] fought beside Athena during the final stages of [[Olympus]].
The two participated together in the Amazon confrontation and later remained among the surviving Gods of [[Zeus]]' faction.[9]
After Olympus fell, Athena initially believed Artemis would share the punishment prepared by [[Medusa]]. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] instead discovered that Artemis had no direct relationship with Medusa's tragedy and released her from that fate.[4]
Athena was visibly shocked to realize that she would remain behind with [[Poseidon]] while Artemis escaped.
Artemis later deliberately distanced herself from Athena and treated her as someone effectively lost during the war rather than involving herself in her punishment.

#### Medusa Elderblood
[[Medusa]] Elderblood is the person most directly responsible for Athena's current fate.
Medusa was once Athena's priestess. When [[Poseidon]] assaulted her, Athena chose jealousy and pride over protecting her subordinate, transforming Medusa into a monster after mortals compared Medusa's beauty favorably to Athena's.[4]
Medusa survived through her Heroic Spirit and eventually joined [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s family through Roberta. Her hatred for Athena remained unchanged for thousands of years.
Victor promised as early as Chapter 395 that Athena and Poseidon would eventually be placed entirely at Medusa's mercy.[3]
When [[Olympus]] finally fell, Victor kept his promise.
Medusa has since maintained Athena as one of the principal subjects of her long-term revenge and experiments.[5][6][16]

#### Poseidon
[[Poseidon]] was Athena's uncle and ultimately became her companion in punishment.
Athena and Poseidon both played central roles in [[Medusa]]'s tragedy: Poseidon was the direct attacker, while Athena punished Medusa afterward instead of protecting her.[4]
Because of this shared responsibility, [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] delivered both of them to Medusa after [[Olympus]] fell.
Their current imprisonment is repeatedly referenced together in later chapters.[6][16]

#### Victor Elderblood
Athena first knew [[Victor Elderblood]] as one of the primary enemies threatening [[Olympus]].
Their relationship became directly hostile during the final war. After Victor overwhelmingly shifted the battlefield against the Olympians, Athena attempted to negotiate, only for Victor to confront her with the hypocrisy of asking mercy from someone her own faction would never have spared while he was weak.[8]
Victor's hatred toward Athena was not primarily caused by their wartime opposition. His central motivation was his promise to [[Medusa]].
After Athena's capture, Victor personally prevented her suicide, removed the fragment of [[Metis]] from her Soul and surrendered Athena to Medusa.[5][4]
Victor later became Metis' father through her Draconic rebirth and eventually her husband, technically making him Athena's stepfather as well as one of the people responsible for her continued captivity. This familial technicality has no positive effect upon their relationship.

### Power and Status
Athena was one of [[Olympus]]' strongest Third Generation Gods and one of its most important combat assets.
Her general status is supported by several points:
- she belongs to the former Twelve Olympians;
- she possesses a Major Divinity;
- she is classified as a High Level Combat God;
- [[Zeus]] would prioritize restoring her if she died because of her military value;[9]
- she possessed enough combat reputation for [[Scathach]]'s [[Ares]]-level killing intent to immediately put her on guard;[9]
- she remained one of the principal Gods fighting during [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s destruction of Olympus;
- and specialized restraints capable of suppressing High-Level Gods were required after her defeat.[5]
Athena should nevertheless not be described as equal to the Original [[Primordial Gods]] or as rivaling [[God-Kings]] without qualification. During the [[Typhon]] crisis, she explicitly acknowledged that only Original Primordials possessed the level of Power necessary to confront the Beast directly.[1]
She was also surpassed by the rapidly evolving members of Victor's faction. Agnes was able to freeze her before Athena could respond during Chapter 661, and Victor later completely overwhelmed the Olympian forces as a whole.[9][8]
Her final status therefore progressed from:
Third Generation Olympian
→ Major Goddess
→ High-Level Combat God
→ Principal Olympian strategist
→ Defeated during the destruction of Olympus
→ Captured by Victor
→ Handed to [[Medusa]]
→ Permanent Prisoner / Test Subject

### Current Status
Athena is alive.
She is not deceased and therefore should not possess a cause_of_death or killed_by field in her infobox.
After [[Olympus]]' destruction, Athena was placed permanently under [[Medusa]]'s control as fulfillment of [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s old promise. Later chapters repeatedly confirm that both Athena and [[Poseidon]] remain within Victor's territory as prisoners subjected to ongoing punishment and experimentation.[6][7][16]
Her imprisonment has continued through the later Elderblood era, and no chapter currently establishes that she has been pardoned, released or killed.

- Athena's first mention occurs in Chapter 395, when [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] renews his promise to [[Medusa]] that Athena and [[Poseidon]] will eventually be placed at her mercy.[3]
- Her direct debut occurs in Chapter 623 during the emergency meeting regarding [[Typhon]].[1]
- Athena's preferred humanoid height is approximately 180 centimeters.[1]
- She is a specialized Goddess of Strategic War, while [[Ares]] represents Violent War.[2]
- Victor's complete War Divinity later encompasses both branches.[2]
- Athena was one of the Gods [[Zeus]] would have considered important enough to prioritize resurrecting during [[Olympus]]' civil war.[9]
- The statement that Athena was the "dumbest bitch on Olympus" is specifically Agnes' insult/opinion, not an objective narration statement.[9]
- Athena housed one portion of [[Metis]]' fragmented Soul for eons.[4]
- The second fragment existed within Zeus.[4]
- Victor reunited the two fragments after defeating Olympus.[4]
- Despite being Athena's biological mother, the reborn Metis later showed little attachment toward her and eventually viewed Athena as a profound disappointment.[15][7]
- Athena is one of the Greek Goddesses who swore a Divine oath of virginity.[15]
- Athena remains alive despite centuries and later millennia of punishment because of her Divine nature.
- Her original infobox incorrectly listed her punishment as a cause of death despite simultaneously identifying her status as Alive. This has been corrected.

### Trivia
- Athena and [[Ares]], despite their intense rivalry, reached the exact same conclusion regarding [[Typhon]]: [[Olympus]] had no realistic way to win.[1]
- Athena understood Ares' strength well enough that [[Scathach]] possessing comparable killing intent immediately alarmed her.[9]
- Agnes froze Athena so quickly during the Amazon confrontation that Athena did not realize what had happened until virtually her entire body was already trapped in Ice.[9]
- Athena was unable to break Agnes' Ice through her own strength during that encounter.[9]
- Athena was one of the first remaining Olympians to recognize that their forces were completely surrounded during the destruction of Olympus.[13]
- When Athena tried to negotiate with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] after becoming severely injured, he asked whether she would have granted the same mercy to him if their positions had been reversed. Athena could not answer.[8]
- Athena attempted to take her own life after hearing [[Medusa]]'s intended punishment, but Victor stopped her.[5]
- A hidden golden jewel on Athena's clothing could transform into a dagger.[5]
- Victor called [[Metis]] the True Goddess of Wisdom directly in front of Athena.[4]
- Athena tried to speak to Metis after her Soul fragments were reunited, but Victor prevented her from interfering and immediately removed Metis from the situation.[4]
- Athena originally believed [[Artemis]] would remain imprisoned with her, only for Victor to release Artemis after determining that she had no involvement with Medusa's past.[4]
- By Chapter 943, Athena and [[Poseidon]] were still explicitly described as occupying Victor's torture field.[6]
- Metis later regarded Athena's fate as a fitting conclusion to the daughter she believed had wasted her potential through pride.[7]
- Chapter 1071 confirms that Medusa's revenge against both Poseidon and Athena continued long after Olympus' destruction and was regarded by Victor as fulfilled.[16]
