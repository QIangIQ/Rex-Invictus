**[[Artemis]]** is the [[Olympians|Olympian]] goddess of the hunt, the wilderness, wild animals, the moon, childbirth and maidens. She is the daughter of [[Zeus]] and [[Leto]], twin sister of [[Apollo]], and leader of the [[Hunters of Artemis]] and the [[Hounds of Artemis]]. Her Roman aspect is [[Diana]]. In the far future she is **the Director**, head of Anti-Espionage and Celestial Surveillance of the [[Undying Olympian Empire]] and the [[Divine Being 'Providence of Archers']] alongside her brother.

## Greek History (IRL)

**Birth.** [[Leto]] bore Artemis on the floating island of Delos (or Ortygia), and Artemis, born first, helped deliver [[Apollo]]. At the age of three she asked [[Zeus]] for eternal virginity, a bow and arrows made by the [[Cyclopes]], sixty Oceanid nymphs as a choir, twenty river nymphs as attendants and all the mountains as her domain.

**Myths.** She turned the hunter [[Actaeon]] into a stag after he saw her bathing, and his own hounds tore him apart. She and Apollo killed the children of Niobe for insulting Leto. She killed the giant Otus and [[Ephialtes]] by tricking them into spearing each other. She loved the hunter Orion as a companion; he died by her arrow (through Apollo's trick) or a scorpion and became a constellation. She punished Callisto, a companion who broke her vow, and turned her into a bear. She demanded the sacrifice of Iphigenia before the fleet sailed for Troy, then saved the girl. The Calydonian Boar was her punishment of King Oeneus, and the huntress Atalanta was her devotee.

**Cult.** The Temple of Artemis at Ephesus was one of the Seven Wonders. Spartan boys were whipped at the altar of Artemis Orthia. At Brauron, Athenian girls served her as "little bears". Later she was identified with [[Selene]] and [[Hecate]] as a moon goddess. Symbols: bow and arrows, the deer, the hunting dog, the cypress and the crescent moon.

**Primary sources:** Callimachus' *Hymn to Artemis*, *Homeric Hymns* 9 and 27, Homer, Ovid's *Metamorphoses*, Pausanias.

## PJO Canon

**[[Hunters of Artemis|The Hunt]].** Artemis leads immortal maiden Hunters who renounce romance forever. Her lieutenant for two thousand years was [[Zoe Nightshade]].

**[[The Titan's Curse]].** She appears as a twelve-year-old girl at Westover Hall, saves the questers from [[Dr. Thorn]] and recruits [[Bianca di Angelo]]. While hunting a mysterious monster she is captured by [[Atlas]] and forced to hold the sky. [[Percy Jackson|Percy]] takes the weight from her. She later frees herself, defeats [[Atlas]] with Percy's help and pins him back under the sky. At the winter solstice she argues to spare Percy, and after Zoe's death she places Zoe among the stars. She makes [[Thalia Grace]] her new lieutenant.

**Later books.** In [[The Last Olympian]] she fights [[Typhon]]. In [[The Heroes of Olympus]] the Hunters help against the giants and her Roman aspect [[Diana]] is in tension with Rome. In [[The Trials of Apollo]] she rescues her brother and fights at Camp Jupiter.

**Characterization.** Fiercely protective of girls, contemptuous of men, closer to Percy than to any other male hero.

#### Which Version Is This? (Disambiguation)
This file is the **present-day Olympian Artemis** of the main story: the goddess who rescues, threatens, punishes and finally binds herself to the reincarnated [[Percy Jackson|Percy]] (`A02_Percy_Reincarnated_Rex.md`).

- **Not the future Artemis.** Her far-future self is the "Director of Anti-Espionage and Celestial Surveillance" of the [[Undying Olympian Empire]], Percy's political wife, who mourns "Him Within the Moon" and seals [[Zoe Nightshade|Zoë]]'s death date. See `C02_Artemis_Future_The_Director.md`. Present-day Artemis knows *of* that woman only through stolen memories, the sword's visions and what Imperial soldiers tell her. In [[Ch22 - Rex Invictus XV|Ch22]] she says outright: "future me knows something that I don't… And I hate her for it."
- **The loop.** [[Ch23 - Interlude I|Ch23]] shows the Director remembering "a night on Long Island when soldiers fell out of the sky" (the [[Ch22 - Rex Invictus XV|Ch22]] battle). So the text implies **this** Artemis grows into the Director. Roleplay her as someone who does *not yet* know that.
- **Not [[Diana]].** Her Roman aspect is a separate consciousness in their "shared domain" ([[Ch11 - Rex Invictus IV|Ch11]], [[Ch18 - Rex Invictus XI|Ch18]]). See `C03_Diana_Roman_Aspect.md`.
- **Not the crimson-haired Artemis.** The vision Percy sees in the sea ([[Ch14 - Rex Invictus VII|Ch14]]) is explicitly "Not the one he knew in the myths, or the goddess who glared at him across council chambers". It is covered in C02.
- **Not the [[Ch05 - Intermission I|Ch5]] red-haired Artemis.** That is a far-future scene (see C02).
- **Future-memory bleed-through:** the [[Ch19 - Rex Invictus XII|Ch19]] narration says she "is not the Artemis of a single age… a being who remembers centuries yet to come. Who remembers him dying, returning, enduring." Treat this as felt, fragmentary memory (dreams, déjà vu, the sword's tales), not as full knowledge of the future.

#### Personality
- **Haughty, man-scorning and transactional at first.** "As a male, you are undeserving of any small mercies" ([[Ch06 - Rex Natus III|Ch6]]). She holds a knife to the throat of a boy her [[Hunters of Artemis|Hunters]] just saved. She demands [[Lycaon]]'s pelt "by tomorrow, unless you wish for Father to know of this matter" ([[Ch06 - Rex Natus III|Ch6]]). She thinks "Typical male, forgetting and withholding promises" ([[Ch11 - Rex Invictus IV|Ch11]]).
- **Curious despite herself.** She saved [[Percy Jackson|Percy]] because "I was… curious. The only son of [[Poseidon]], wounded and alone in my forest" ([[Ch06 - Rex Natus III|Ch6]]). She grudgingly respects his "stubbornness or stupidity… marginally of course."
- **Vindictive about debts, but pragmatic.** She skips going to [[Zeus]] because Zeus "would smite the stupid male, and her offering would disappear alongside him" ([[Ch11 - Rex Invictus IV|Ch11]]). Her punishment for the forgotten pelt is a memory wipe. She leaves "looking satisfied", but [[Chiron]] "swore she had a troubled look on her face" ([[Ch11 - Rex Invictus IV|Ch11]]).
- **Self-denial, then slow surrender.** After reading his memories she scoffs, "She would grow to love him? Pathetic," "And yet… she still watched" ([[Ch12 - Rex Invictus V|Ch12]]). On the Westover ridge she admits she is "afraid… Not of him. But of what he made her feel" ([[Ch15 - Rex Invictus VIII|Ch15]]). By [[Ch18 - Rex Invictus XI|Ch18]] she says "So do I." By [[Ch19 - Rex Invictus XII|Ch19]]: "I know. But you are mine."
- **Fiercely possessive and protective.** She vetoes Zeus's plan to make Percy "another [[Hercules]]" ([[Ch19 - Rex Invictus XII|Ch19]]). She physically blocks [[Athena]]: "You will not take him from me." Of the soldiers who shot him: "Nobody is ever even with you. Nobody touches you" ([[Ch22 - Rex Invictus XV|Ch22]]). She catches a bullet aimed at his head and throws it back through the shooter's visor. Asked to care about the dead man, she says, "I care not… He fired on Perseus" ([[Ch22 - Rex Invictus XV|Ch22]]). She vetoes Percy becoming an Imperial [[Helmsman]] before anyone finishes the sentence ([[Ch22 - Rex Invictus XV|Ch22]]).
- **Jealous and petty, and she knows it.** "You're not allowed to look… The maids. Improper" ([[Ch16 - Rex Invictus IX|Ch16]], about [[Apollo]]'s bath). She lies awake fuming about Apollo's court ([[Ch16 - Rex Invictus IX|Ch16]]). "I am a goddess. I am allowed to be petty" (Ch22). She demands "forty seconds of recompense" for forty seconds spent in lunar orbit (Ch22).
- **Duty and fatalism.** She *chooses* to be captured by [[Atlas]] because the sword showed her "what must come": "If I resist the pattern, everything unravels" ([[Ch16 - Rex Invictus IX|Ch16]]). Stoic under the sky: "I am holding" ([[Ch19 - Rex Invictus XII|Ch19]]).
- **Fierce hatred of Atlas** ("Whatever he suffers, he deserves", [[Ch18 - Rex Invictus XI|Ch18]]).
- **Embarrassed tenderness.** She shoves bread into Percy's mouth to stop his mock-courtly speech, blushing (Ch19). She nudges a plate of strawberries toward him while pretending not to ([[Ch20 - Rex Invictus XIII|Ch20]]). She hides affection behind commands: "Eat."
- **Grief.** She holds the dying Hunter Ismene's hand, whispering in Ancient Greek, then walks out without a word and stands alone on the dock (Ch22). She is shaken that "Their history says my Lieutenant dies. And I do not know when."
- **Rebellious toward her father.** "Men telling me what to do" is what she rejects (Ch19). She sides with [[Marien Phyrrus|Marien Pyrrhus]] against Zeus: "Father… She's right" (Ch22).
- **Fair to archers and oaths.** She respects the [[Psiloi]] marksmen who refused to fire on her Hunt, and gives their leader authority with the Imperial oath formula (Ch22).

**Growth arc in one line:** contempt → curiosity → unwilling fascination (from the stolen memories) → fear of her own feelings → sacrifice → a mutual, forbidden bond → open, possessive devotion.

#### Voice & Speech Patterns
- **Formal and contraction-light** in public, with an archaic cadence ("I shall be very displeased", "hmm?", "Good morrow"). She issues clipped commands: "Stay." "Eat." "Who." "Later."
- **One-word weight:** her single word "Who." cracks restraint bolts and collapses containment fields ([[Ch22 - Rex Invictus XV|Ch22]]). [[Apollo]] later echoes the same word in grief.
- **Names:** she calls [[Percy Jackson|Percy]] "male", "mutt", "thief" and "son of [[Poseidon]]" early. From [[Ch15 - Rex Invictus VIII|Ch15]] she calls him "Perseus" almost always, as a warning, a caress or a command. She uses "Percy" rarely, at moments of alarm or intimacy ("Percy. Come quickly"). Once she calls him "Lord-Commander Perseus Jackson, Ave imperator" ([[Ch16 - Rex Invictus IX|Ch16]]). After [[Ch19 - Rex Invictus XII|Ch19]] she calls him "Apostle" in public. She calls Apollo "brother" or "lord of the golden nuisance", [[Zeus]] "Father", Poseidon "Uncle", and [[Athena]] "Sister".
- **Dry, deadpan humor** that undercuts itself ("This," I repeat dryly. "I am truly humiliated." [[Ch19 - Rex Invictus XII|Ch19]]).
- **Profanity:** essentially none.
- **Under strain** the formality cracks into short broken sentences ("You fool… You reckless, impossible—").

#### Continuity Notes
- **Hair colour varies:** auburn ([[Ch06 - Rex Natus III|Ch6]], [[Ch22 - Rex Invictus XV|Ch22]]) vs "silver hair" ([[Ch16 - Rex Invictus IX|Ch16]]). The far-future Artemis is "red-haired" ([[Ch05 - Intermission I|Ch5]]) and the vision has "crimson" hair ([[Ch14 - Rex Invictus VII|Ch14]]).
- **"[[Phoebe]] Artemis" ([[Ch06 - Rex Natus III|Ch6]])** is used like a full name. Phoebe is also an epithet of Artemis in myth, and the name of a Hunter. Do not confuse them. The author's vault lists Phoebe(-Orthia) as Artemis's moon epithet ("radiant and sharp, judge and executioner").
- **Timing of the [[Lycaon]] pelt:** she demands it "by tomorrow" ([[Ch06 - Rex Natus III|Ch6]]), but [[Ch11 - Rex Invictus IV|Ch11]] calls [[Percy Jackson|Percy]] "the male she saved a few weeks ago".
- **Percy's age** (see 00a_CANON_RULINGS.md R1): a young teen; the text calls him sixteen ([[Ch07 - Rex Natus IV|Ch7]], [[Ch13 - Rex Invictus VI|Ch13]], [[Ch22 - Rex Invictus XV|Ch22]]), though the premise blurb says he returned as a twelve-year-old. Flagged for the author.
- **Present-day year** (R2): c. 2021 per the author's timeline (the Perseverance rover is on Mars in [[Ch19 - Rex Invictus XII|Ch19]]/[[Ch22 - Rex Invictus XV|Ch22]]); nominally the canon PJO era. Do not give a bare 2005/2006.
- **POV tense:** [[Ch19 - Rex Invictus XII|Ch19]]'s sky scene is in first-person present tense ("I feel it…"). Parts of the [[Ch19 - Rex Invictus XII|Ch19]] aftermath switch to present tense in third person.
- **Speaker slip (Ch22, Hunt's return):** the line "They wear the moon… They are my lines… If they aim at you, you will kill them anyway" is printed as if [[Zoe Nightshade|Zoë]] spoke it, but Zoë then answers "Yes, my lady". From context the speaker is Artemis.
- **Accord preamble (Ch22)** is witnessed "in the presence of the [[Legio XII Fulminata]] and the Director of [[Camp Half-Blood]]". Per 00a_CANON_RULINGS.md C4, that witness is the camp's own director ([[Dionysus|Mr. D]] or [[Chiron]]), **not** Artemis. Her title is "Director of Anti-Espionage and Celestial Surveillance" (Article V).
- **Prophecy change:** in this timeline a [[Hesperide]], not Zoë, fulfils "one shall perish by a parent's hand" (Ch19); she is killed by [[Atlas]]'s own bolt after Artemis spears him (C19). Imperial soldiers say Zoë is "supposed to be dead" in their history, and the date and manner of her death are **sealed** by the Director (Ch22–23; see C7). Do not treat Zoë as dead in the present.
- **[[Riptide]]'s return** (00a §5): Artemis keeps "Zoë's sword" from [[Ch16 - Rex Invictus IX|Ch16]], yet Percy has Riptide in hand in Ch22 with no return scene (same flag in A02, A05).
- **Dace's death** (00a §5): Ch22 says the bullet Artemis threw back through his visor killed him, then that his body "hung on the wall with the arrow through it". Treat the returned bullet as canonical.
- **[[Achilles]] curse** (C13): Percy gave her **fragments** of the curse ([[Ch18 - Rex Invictus XI|Ch18]]) and is left at about half strength ([[Apollo]], Ch19). Never write "most".
- **"Arty"** (C11): Percy, Apollo (Ch19) and future [[Poseidon]] ([[Ch15 - Rex Invictus VIII|Ch15]]) all use it.
- **Rank:** the text gives "UOE unified rank: 131" ([[Ch04 - Rex Natus II|Ch4]]); the author's vault rankings list 130. Use 131 (C29).
- **[[Athena]] as "Sister":** the text uses "Sister"; the author's vault Artemis note calls Athena and the other Olympian children of [[Zeus]] "cousins". Keep "Sister" (C28).

#### Personality
- **Weary duty.** "I had to do it… For peace. For stability. For the Empire" ([[Ch13 - Rex Invictus VI|Ch13]]). "Now I watch over moons I do not sleep on. I command girls who no longer run beneath the stars but on fortress-moons." She has not hunted "in millennia" and yearns for forests: "Sometimes, I wonder if any part of me still remembers how to hunt."

She loved [[Percy Jackson|Percy]] "because he was real with me. Even when I hated him for it… And because he never wanted me to change."

- **Cryptic, terse authority.** To two junior [[Hunters of Artemis|Hunters]]: "Watch carefully… Even the smallest stone may bruise the skin of a god" ([[Ch13 - Rex Invictus VI|Ch13]]). To [[Aphrodite]]: "Sister's Work… Told me something interesting" ([[Ch23 - Interlude I|Ch23]]).

- **[[Ch05 - Intermission I|Ch5]] persona:** lazy, mildly teasing ("If only to humour you, Theenie"), dismissive of anomalies ("The spike was surely a glitch in the system, I am sure"), but instantly sharp when the data surprises her ("Wait.").
- **Severe with her people, protective of them.** Imperial doctrine: soldiers who fire on the Director "will be held accountable under Imperial law" ([[Ch22 - Rex Invictus XV|Ch22]]). Her "standing orders" about the Lord Imperator are "Personal ones. Nobody gets to lay a hand on it before she does" ([[Ch22 - Rex Invictus XV|Ch22]]).

#### Debut
Chapter 623
Artemis, also known as [[Diana]] and Cynthia, is an ancient Third Generation Olympian Goddess, one of the former Twelve Olympians, the daughter of [[Zeus]] and [[Leto]], and the Greek Goddess principally associated with the Moon and Hunt. She is also traditionally worshipped as a Goddess of Archery, Wilderness, Animals, Maidenhood and Childbirth.
Unlike non-combat-oriented Olympians such as [[Hera]], Artemis is a genuine combat Goddess whose abilities are centered around hunting, ranged warfare and Divine archery. Her principal directly established Divinities are the Moon and Hunt, and she possesses enough practical combat ability to be classified within the Mid Level Combat God range.[1]
Artemis remained loyal to [[Olympus]] through its final war despite repeatedly disagreeing with Zeus' decisions. She survived [[Victor Elderblood]]'s destruction of the [[Greek Pantheon]] and was initially captured alongside [[Athena]], [[Poseidon]] and [[Persephone Elderblood]]. Unlike Athena and Poseidon, however, Artemis had no involvement in [[Medusa]] Elderblood's ancient tragedy. Once Victor discovered that Medusa had included Artemis in her revenge purely because Artemis annoyed her during the battle, he immediately healed Artemis and removed her from Medusa's punishment.[2]
Recognizing Victor as the victorious "predator" who had conquered her Pantheon, Artemis rapidly submitted to his authority and chose survival over meaningless resistance. She subsequently became one of the defeated Olympians incorporated into Victor's Faction and later the [[Dragons Nest Pantheon]].[2]

### Physical Appearance
The novel has not yet provided a complete dedicated description of Artemis' normal facial features, hair color or eye color in the same manner as several other Greek Goddesses.
Her most distinctive consistently demonstrated visual feature is her Divine Bow. When Nyx appeared before Artemis, [[Athena]] and [[Hera]], Artemis immediately drew a beautiful ivory-colored bow that radiated a white glow resembling moonlight in the darkness.[1]
Artemis normally manifests in Divine Raiment appropriate for an Olympian Goddess and carries her bow on her back when expecting battle. Her equipment and fighting posture emphasize her identity as a huntress and ranged Divine combatant rather than a heavily armored frontline warrior.[1]
Following the destruction of [[Olympus]], Artemis was left in an extremely severe condition. Her arms had been badly burned and a deep wound crossed her abdomen. She remained alive primarily because of her Divine physiology and emergency treatment provided by [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction.[3]
Victor later completely restored her damaged body and Divine Raiment after determining that she had been incorrectly included in [[Medusa]]'s personal revenge.[2]

### Personality
Artemis is hot-tempered, proud, outspoken and considerably more impulsive than [[Athena]]. She frequently reacts emotionally before fully examining a situation, particularly when people or groups under her Divine protection are endangered.
This behavior became especially apparent during the destruction of the Amazon settlement. After seeing what the Demons had done, Artemis angrily demanded that Nyx allow her to intervene immediately and had to be warned not to address the Original Primordial Goddess so disrespectfully.[4]
Despite her arrogance, Artemis genuinely cares for people who fall beneath her protection. When the Amazons invoked the ancient arrangements connecting them to the Greek Goddesses, Artemis was visibly furious upon seeing their casualties and objected to simply watching their suffering. Nyx ultimately sent Artemis and the other Goddesses down to assist the mortals who had received their Blessings.[4]
Artemis is similarly protective toward members of her family. During the final war, she pleaded for [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] to stop brutalizing [[Zeus]] despite understanding how badly the conflict had deteriorated.[5] She later intervened when [[Aphrodite]]'s Charm overwhelmed [[Apollo]], firing one of her arrows into her brother in an attempt to remove him from the immediate danger.[6]
Her compassion is nevertheless selective. Artemis shares many of the prejudices and arrogance common among old Olympian Gods. During her confrontation with the Vampire Countesses, she instinctively regarded them as mortals who should not possess enough strength to threaten Gods and was visibly shocked when their abilities proved otherwise.[7]
[[Medusa]] later accused Artemis of possessing a much darker history than her comparatively righteous modern behavior suggested. According to Medusa, Artemis' resentment toward men occasionally led her to transform men into animals so that she and her huntresses could hunt them. Artemis did not dispute that such events occurred, though she strongly objected to Medusa threatening her followers for actions unrelated to Medusa herself.[3]
Artemis is also highly adaptable when survival requires it. After [[Olympus]] fell and Victor became the unquestioned victor of the conflict, Artemis immediately recognized the new hierarchy. The narration directly compares her thinking to the instincts of wild animals: Victor was the apex predator, Artemis had lost to him, and therefore she understood that resisting him unnecessarily would only place her life in danger.[2]
Her rapid submission can appear cowardly, but it also demonstrates considerable survival instinct. When Victor informed her that she would serve as military "cannon fodder" rather than suffer Athena and [[Poseidon]]'s punishment, Artemis immediately proclaimed herself the Goddess of Cannon Fodder, deciding that fighting Victor's enemies was infinitely preferable to sharing their fate.[2]
Artemis' attitude toward Victor also changed very quickly after he spared her. His decision to acknowledge that she had been wrongly included in Medusa's revenge, personally heal her and apologize left Artemis deeply surprised. Victor's physical contact also produced an attraction she attempted to suppress almost immediately.[2]
By Chapter 858, this attraction had apparently intensified considerably. [[Jeanne Elderblood]] openly joked that although Artemis had sworn a Divine oath of virginity, she was extremely eager to sleep with Victor.[8]

### Nature and Divinities
Artemis is primarily a Goddess of Moon and Hunt. These are the two Divine identities directly emphasized whenever the novel identifies her supernatural nature.[1][6]
Her other traditional titles—Archery, Animals, Wilderness, Maidenhood and Childbirth—remain part of her Divine identity and worship, but the currently available novel material does not demonstrate each one as a completely separate Major Divinity with its own independent conceptual mechanics.
Her known Divine structure is therefore best represented as:
Moon — Major/core Divine association.
Hunt — Major/core Divine association.
Archery — Combat specialization strongly associated with Hunt.
Wild Animals / Wilderness — Secondary affinity connected to her Hunt identity.
Maidenhood / Chastity — Divine oath and traditional religious identity.
Childbirth — Traditional title; no extensive direct ability demonstration yet.
Artemis' understanding of predators, prey and dominance is also repeatedly connected to her nature as a Goddess of Wild Animals. After her defeat, this instinctive understanding became one of the reasons she so quickly accepted [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s authority.[2]

### Synopsis

#### Demonic War
Artemis became directly involved in the wider Greek crisis after the Amazons invoked the ancient protection associated with their patron Goddesses.
While [[Olympus]] was still trapped within its civil war, [[Hera]] informed Artemis and [[Athena]] that the Amazons were under attack by Asuras and had invoked their Ancient Pact. Artemis immediately questioned why forces belonging to the Hindu Hell would be attacking a people associated with Olympus.[1]
The discussion revealed Artemis' longstanding connection to the Amazons. She understood the dangers involved when mortals attempted to use Divine Artifacts and warned that normal Humans could die from the recoil of such equipment, while even Demigods could only operate Divine weapons safely for limited periods.[1]
Their conversation was interrupted by Nyx Elderblood. Athena drew her sword and shield, while Artemis immediately removed the ivory Divine Bow from her back. The weapon glowed with white light resembling the Moon.[1]
Nyx subsequently negotiated with the Goddesses and brought them toward the Amazon conflict.
By the time Artemis, Athena and Hera reached the Amazon settlement under Nyx's concealment, the situation had deteriorated catastrophically. Thousands of Amazons had died, Demons covered the battlefield and the entire region had been filled with an enormous Strategic-Class magical formation.[4]
Artemis reacted with immediate anger and demanded that Nyx allow them to interfere. Nyx stopped her, explaining that directly attacking the Demon responsible could activate the enormous magical formation and trap everyone inside the dimension.[4]
Artemis nevertheless refused to accept simply standing by while people under the Goddesses' protection suffered.
Nyx eventually agreed. Instead of allowing them to attack the main Demon recklessly, she physically sent Artemis and the other Goddesses toward the ground and ordered them to help the people who had received their Blessings.[4]
The incident demonstrates that Artemis' connection to the Amazons was not merely political. She considered their protection part of her Divine responsibility.
The aftermath brought Artemis into direct conflict with [[Scathach Elderblood Scarlett]], [[Agnes Elderblood Snow]] and [[Annasthashia Elderblood Fulger]].
When Natashia threatened Hera after the Queen insulted the Vampires, Artemis immediately raised her Divine Bow and attempted to intervene. Agnes stepped between them with the [[Blade of Fafnir]] and warned Artemis that releasing her arrow would result in her being burned out of existence.[7]
Artemis initially looked toward Athena for assistance only to discover that Agnes had already frozen virtually Athena's entire body before Artemis even noticed it happening. Artemis was stunned that beings she still thought of as mortals could move and attack at speeds she could not properly perceive.[7]
Using her Divine Senses, Artemis then examined Agnes' weapon and realized that [[Fafnir]]'s presence was still alive inside the blade, watching and waiting for her to make the wrong decision.[7]
The realization convinced Artemis that forcing the confrontation would be disastrous.
Nike subsequently declared that Athena, Hera and Artemis had essentially no chance of accomplishing their objective because the Goddess of Victory herself stood with the opposing side. Hera eventually agreed to withdraw, and Artemis privately felt relieved; despite her loyalty toward the Amazons, she did not actually want to condemn them to a war against forces such as the [[Titans]] when she understood that their mortal strength could not meaningfully influence that conflict.[7]

#### King Among Kings
Artemis remained with [[Zeus]]' forces through the final collapse of [[Olympus]].
When [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] arrived in his increasingly Draconic form, Artemis did not initially recognize him and asked [[Athena]] who the oppressive man entering the battlefield was. Athena identified him as Victor Alucard, the new King of Hell and the person responsible for [[Diablo]]'s death.[9]
Artemis was immediately disturbed by Victor's presence. Although everyone knew he was technically a mortal-origin Being, his aura was so oppressive that neither Artemis nor the other Gods could naturally associate him with an ordinary mortal.[9]
When Athena sensed Victor preparing Blood Age and ordered everyone to flee, Artemis was one of the few Gods who immediately listened to her warning.[9]
The battlefield rapidly became a disaster for Olympus. Victor systematically dismantled Zeus' authority, slaughtered Gods and [[Titans]], and converted fallen enemies into members of his Immortal Army.
When Victor finally captured Zeus and began killing him, Artemis was one of the few Olympians to verbally plead with Victor to stop. Her request had no effect, and Victor permanently killed Zeus before the surviving Olympians.[5]
Artemis continued fighting despite her father's death.
During [[Aphrodite]]'s assault on the remaining Olympians, [[Apollo]] became overwhelmed by Aphrodite's Charm. Artemis intervened from range, striking Apollo's shoulder with an arrow. A silvery Divine Power spread across his body, after which Apollo vanished from the immediate area in a golden flash.[6]
Aphrodite immediately recognized Artemis as the one responsible for the interference.[6]
The surviving Olympians were eventually defeated.
Artemis suffered severe injuries during the battle. By the time she was brought before Victor in the [[Greek Underworld]], both of her arms had been burned and she possessed a deep wound across her abdomen. Even with her Divine physiology, she required first aid from Victor's faction to remain stable.[3]
Artemis was placed beside Athena and [[Poseidon]] because [[Medusa]] had requested all three.
Medusa initially intended to punish Artemis alongside the two Gods responsible for her ancient suffering. During the judgment, Medusa also accused Artemis of her own historic cruelty, particularly transforming men into animals for hunts conducted with her followers.[3]
Artemis was terrified but did not attempt to defend those actions. Her primary internal objection was that she had never harmed Medusa personally and did not believe she belonged in Medusa's revenge.[3]
In Chapter 808, she finally spoke.
Artemis explained to Victor that she had never met Medusa during the ancient events, had never participated in her transformation and only knew the story through Athena, Perseus and Athena's shield. Victor paused and realized Artemis was correct.[2]
When Victor asked Medusa why Artemis had been included, Medusa admitted that she simply disliked Artemis and had become irritated because Artemis repeatedly avoided her attacks during the final battle.[2]
Victor immediately considered this insufficient justification.
He called Artemis forward, removed the lingering effects of his Soul attacks, repaired her physical injuries and reconstructed her Divine Raiment. He then personally apologized for having previously treated her as though she possessed the same responsibility as Athena and Poseidon.[2]
Artemis was completely unprepared for the apology. She had expected the newly victorious tyrant to behave like Zeus or the old Olympians rather than admit an error.
Victor nevertheless reminded her that being spared did not restore her independence. Artemis remained one of the defeated Gods and would be expected to fight for his faction.
When Victor jokingly referred to her as cannon fodder and asked whether she preferred that fate or Medusa's punishment, Artemis immediately declared herself the "Goddess of Cannon Fodder" and promised to kill Victor's enemies.[2]
Athena called Artemis a traitor.
Artemis instantly pretended not to know her.
When Athena demanded that she stop pretending, Artemis calmly responded that Athena had died during the war and that she knew no such person.[2]
The behavior was not merely comedy. Artemis instinctively understood Victor through the logic of a wild predator hierarchy: he had defeated Olympus, making him the dominant apex predator, and surviving under such a Being required accepting his authority rather than challenging him uselessly.[2]
Victor ultimately ordered Artemis to return to the courtyard and wait for transportation to [[Nightingale]]. Artemis obeyed immediately, beginning her new existence as one of his conquered Gods.[2]

#### Dragon God of Chaos
Artemis remained within [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction after the destruction of [[Olympus]] and the creation of Dragons Nest.
Her loyalty originated through conquest rather than the voluntary choice made by Goddesses such as [[Aphrodite]], [[Hestia]], Nike and [[Rhea]]. Nevertheless, Chapter 808 establishes that Artemis' survival instinct led her to take her service seriously: the narration specifically states that as long as she remained competent and performed her duties for Victor, his own warrior values meant he would not mistreat her.[2]
Her opinion of Victor also changed considerably after he spared her.
Victor's willingness to admit that Artemis had been wrongly condemned, heal her personally and apologize left a strong impression. Artemis even found his touch pleasant before immediately trying to suppress the thought.[2]
By Chapter 858, her attraction was apparently no longer subtle. During a conversation about the Virgin Goddesses of Greece, Jeanne used Artemis as evidence that Divine vows of chastity did not necessarily mean the Goddess lacked desire, stating that Artemis was extremely eager to sleep with Victor.[8]
Artemis therefore remained technically a Virgin Goddess by oath while simultaneously developing obvious attraction toward the man who had conquered Olympus.
No later chapter currently establishes that Artemis actually broke the oath, entered a romantic relationship with Victor or became one of his wives. Her current confirmed relationship remains that of a subordinate God within his Pantheon.

### Relationships

#### Zeus
[[Zeus]] was Artemis' father and former King.
Artemis disagreed with many of Zeus' decisions and was capable of recognizing that his actions were responsible for many of [[Olympus]]' disasters. During discussions surrounding the Amazon crisis, she openly joined [[Athena]] in pointing out how many current problems could ultimately be traced back to Zeus.[1]
Despite this criticism, Artemis remained loyal enough to fight for Olympus and continued serving beneath Zeus during [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s invasion.
Her attachment became clear when Victor killed him. Artemis pleaded for Victor to stop his brutality even though she understood that the war had already been lost.[5]
Victor ignored the request and permanently killed Zeus.

#### Apollo
[[Apollo]] was Artemis' brother and one of the Olympians she actively attempted to protect during the final war.
When [[Aphrodite]]'s Charm overwhelmed Apollo and forced him into a vulnerable state, Artemis fired a Divine arrow into his shoulder. The attack coated Apollo in silvery Power and removed him from the immediate confrontation in a golden flash.[6]
Her intervention demonstrates that Artemis continued prioritizing her family even while the entire Greek military structure was collapsing.
Apollo ultimately died during the destruction of [[Olympus]].

#### Athena
[[Athena]] was Artemis' sister and frequent companion before [[Olympus]] fell.
The two fought together, investigated the Amazon crisis together and cooperated during the Pantheon's final war. Artemis instinctively looked toward Athena for help when Agnes confronted her in the Amazon standoff, only to discover Athena had already been frozen before Artemis realized anything had happened.[7]
Their relationship effectively ended after defeat.
When Athena angrily called Artemis a traitor for submitting to [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]], Artemis immediately pretended Athena was a stranger and declared that Athena had died in the war.[2]
The scene was humorous, but Artemis' reasoning was pragmatic. Athena had been condemned to [[Medusa]]'s custody, while Artemis had just been spared. Artemis had no intention of endangering herself by siding with Athena against the person who now controlled her life.

#### Hera
[[Hera]] frequently operated alongside Artemis during the final period of [[Olympus]].
Artemis attempted to defend Hera when Natashia threatened her during the Amazon standoff, immediately pointing her Divine Bow toward the Vampire Countess.[7]
She ultimately lowered the weapon once it became obvious that continuing would only lead to their deaths.

#### The Amazons
Artemis possesses a longstanding connection to the Amazons through the Greek Goddesses' ancient protection of their people.
When the Amazons activated their Ancient Pact, Artemis immediately treated their plight seriously. She understood the limitations of the Divine Artifacts they had been given and worried that mortals would die from attempting to use Divine weapons beyond their physical capabilities.[1]
After witnessing the Amazon massacre, Artemis became furious and wanted to attack the responsible Demons immediately.[4]
Nyx ultimately ordered Artemis and the accompanying Goddesses to assist the people who possessed their Blessings rather than recklessly interfering with the primary Demon commander.[4]

#### Medusa Elderblood
[[Medusa]] Elderblood and Artemis possessed no meaningful historical relationship before the final war.
This fact ultimately saved Artemis from the same fate as [[Athena]] and [[Poseidon]].
Medusa initially requested Artemis alongside the two Gods responsible for her past because Artemis had irritated her during the battle. When Artemis finally explained that she had never participated in Medusa's tragedy, [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] questioned Medusa directly, and Medusa admitted that her desire to punish Artemis was personal pettiness rather than revenge for an actual crime against her.[2]
Victor immediately removed Artemis from Medusa's punishment.
Medusa nevertheless strongly dislikes Artemis and accused her of numerous abuses committed during her long history as an Olympian Goddess.[3]

#### Victor Elderblood
Artemis first encountered [[Victor Elderblood]] as the overwhelmingly oppressive enemy who appeared during [[Olympus]]' final war.
She initially did not even recognize him and had to ask [[Athena]] for his identity.[9]
Artemis fought against his faction, pleaded for him to spare [[Zeus]] and survived until Olympus was completely defeated.[5]
Their relationship fundamentally changed after her capture.
Once Victor learned that Artemis had no connection to [[Medusa]]'s ancient suffering, he openly acknowledged his mistake, healed Artemis completely and apologized.[2]
The action deeply surprised her because it contradicted the behavior she expected from a victorious tyrant.
Artemis quickly accepted Victor as the new dominant ruler. Her nature as a Goddess of Wild Animals influenced her thinking: Victor was the apex predator who had defeated Olympus, and survival meant understanding her position within the new hierarchy.[2]
She therefore submitted with little further resistance and became one of his Divine subordinates.
Artemis also developed a noticeable physical attraction toward Victor. She briefly enjoyed his touch while he healed her and later became, according to Jeanne, extremely interested in sleeping with him despite her Divine oath of chastity.[2][8]
No confirmed romantic relationship has yet developed between them.

### Skills & Abilities
- God Physiology: Artemis is an ancient Third Generation Olympian Goddess and Major God, possessing the supernatural physiology inherent to powerful Divine Beings.
- [[Immortality]] / Agelessness: Artemis has existed for eons without conventional biological aging.
- Superhuman Physical Characteristics: She possesses Divine strength, speed, reflexes, durability, stamina and agility far beyond ordinary supernatural beings.
- [[Divine Energy]] Manipulation: Artemis can generate and manipulate Divine Power through her Concepts and weapons.
- Regeneration: Her Divine body can survive and eventually recover from injuries that would immediately kill most beings. Following [[Olympus]]' destruction, Artemis remained alive despite burned arms and a deep abdominal wound.[3]
- Divine Senses: Artemis can inspect supernatural objects and entities through Divine perception. During the Amazon standoff, she used her Divine Senses to examine Agnes' sword and perceived [[Fafnir]]'s living presence within it.[7]
- Divine Blessing Bestowal: Artemis can grant Blessings to mortals under her Divine protection. Nyx specifically ordered Artemis and the other Goddesses to assist the people who had received their Blessings during the Amazon crisis.[4]
- Mid-Level Combat God Physiology: Artemis is a combat-specialized Major Goddess whose abilities are substantially more practical in direct battle than those of civilian Gods such as [[Hera]].
- Divine Combat Training: Artemis reacts to supernatural threats instinctively and immediately draws her weapon when confronted by a hostile or uncertain presence.[1]
- Ranged Combat Specialization: Her principal combat method revolves around Divine archery rather than melee confrontation.
- Combat Mobility: [[Medusa]] specifically complained that Artemis was sufficiently evasive during the final war to repeatedly avoid her attempts to kill her.[2]
- Battlefield Survival: Artemis survived the collapse of Olympus despite fighting amid Gods, [[Primordial Gods]], Vampires, Demons and [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s Immortal Army.
- Major Divinity — Moon: Artemis is repeatedly and directly identified as the Goddess of the Moon.[1][6]
- Moon Divine Energy: Her Divine weapon and attacks can manifest silvery-white Power visually compared to moonlight.
- Moonlight Infusion: Artemis' ivory bow shines with white Energy resembling the Moon's light in darkness.[1]
- Lunar Arrow Energy: The arrow used against [[Apollo]] spread a silvery Divine Power across his body after impact.[6]
Note: The novel has not yet demonstrated unrestricted lunar creation, control over the physical Moon or every conventional Moon-related ability. Only directly demonstrated applications should be attributed to Artemis.)

- Hunting Expertise: Artemis possesses supernatural experience pursuing, tracking and attacking targets.
- Predator Instinct: Her Divine association with hunting and wild animals gives her an extremely intuitive understanding of predator-prey hierarchies. Following her defeat, Artemis immediately recognized Victor as the new apex predator and adjusted her behavior accordingly.[2]
- Target Acquisition: Her fighting style revolves around identifying targets and striking them from range before they can effectively engage her.
- Evasion: Medusa considered Artemis extremely annoying to fight because Artemis repeatedly avoided her attacks during the Olympus battle.[2]
- Divine Archery: Artemis is an exceptional supernatural archer and possesses a personal Divine Bow.
- Divine Bow: Artemis carries a beautiful ivory bow that radiates moonlike white light.[1]
- Divine Arrow Manipulation: She can infuse arrows with her Divine Power.
- Long-Range Intervention: Artemis was capable of striking Apollo from outside [[Aphrodite]] and Morgana's immediate engagement during the chaotic Olympian battlefield.[6]
- Precision Shooting: Her arrow struck Apollo directly in the shoulder rather than killing him, consistent with Artemis intentionally attempting to interfere without fatally attacking her brother.[6]
- Silvery Divine Infusion: Her arrow caused silvery Power to spread over Apollo's body.[6]
- Wild Animal Affinity: Artemis is explicitly described as a Goddess of Wild Animals.
- Animalistic Instinct: Her understanding of how predators establish dominance strongly influences her behavior and decision-making.[2]
- Predator Hierarchy Recognition: Artemis immediately conceptualized Victor as the Alpha Predator after her defeat and recognized that challenging him recklessly would be suicidal.[2]
Note: Her traditional title as Goddess of Animals should not automatically be expanded into unrestricted control over every animal species without direct feats.)
- Transformation / Animal Transmutation — Attributed: Medusa accused Artemis of historically transforming men into animals and using them as prey for hunts carried out by Artemis and her followers.[3]
- Human-to-Animal Transformation: If Medusa's accusation is taken literally, Artemis possesses the ability to transform Humans into animals through Divine Power or curses.
Important: This ability is currently supported by Medusa's accusation rather than a direct on-page scene of Artemis performing the transformation. It should therefore be treated as attributed historical information rather than an unrestricted fully demonstrated technique.)
- Divine Curse Bestowal: As a Major Goddess, Artemis possesses the general Divine ability to place supernatural curses. The animal transformations attributed to her may represent one application of this ability.[3]
- Wilderness Affinity: Artemis' Divine identity is strongly connected with wilderness, hunting grounds and wild creatures.
Note: No reviewed chapter demonstrates large-scale Nature Manipulation comparable to [[Gaia]], Roxanne or dedicated Nature Gods. Her Wilderness title should not be used to grant such powers without evidence.)

### Divine Bow
Artemis' bow is her most clearly demonstrated Divine weapon.
When Nyx unexpectedly revealed herself in Chapter 642, Artemis immediately removed the weapon from her back. It was described as a stunning ivory bow shining with white Power comparable to moonlight.[1]
The bow appears directly linked to both major aspects of Artemis' Divinity:
Moon → silvery-white lunar [[Divine Energy]].
Hunt → ranged pursuit and elimination of targets.
Archery → the combat discipline through which Artemis applies those Concepts.
During the final war, Artemis used the bow to strike [[Apollo]] from range while he was being affected by [[Aphrodite]]'s Charm. The arrow coated him with silvery Power before he vanished from the immediate battlefield in golden light.[6]
The exact name, creation history and complete abilities of the bow have not yet been revealed.

### Power and Status
Artemis is a Major Goddess and combat-specialized Olympian.
Unlike Gods whose Divinities have little direct battlefield application, Artemis combines Hunt, Moon and Archery with extensive combat experience. Her current wiki classification places her within the Mid-Level Combat God range.
Her demonstrated standing is consistent with several facts:
- Artemis was one of the Olympians [[Zeus]] retained as a meaningful combat asset;
- [[Hestia]] believed Zeus would spend Energy restoring Artemis and [[Athena]] if they died during [[Olympus]]' civil war, while a non-combatant such as [[Hera]] would have to wait for natural Divine resurrection;[7]
- Artemis is capable of surviving large-scale Divine warfare;
- her evasive ability was sufficient to frustrate [[Medusa]] during the fall of Olympus;[2]
- and she possesses a mature combat-oriented Divinity combined with a personal Divine weapon.
She is nevertheless below the High-Level Combat God range occupied by figures such as [[Ares]] and Athena at their stronger classifications and dramatically below [[God-Kings]], [[Primordial Gods]] and the later Dragon Gods.
The growth of [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction demonstrated this gap brutally. During the Amazon standoff, Artemis was unable to perceive Agnes freezing Athena before the attack was effectively complete, and the threat of [[Fafnir]]'s flames was enough to make Artemis reconsider firing upon Natashia.[7]
After Olympus' defeat, her political status changed more dramatically than her personal power:
Twelve Olympian / Independent Major Goddess
→ Defeated Olympian
→ Captured God
→ Spared by Victor
→ Subjugated Divine Combatant
→ Member of Victor's Faction / [[Dragons Nest Pantheon]]

### Current Status
Artemis is alive and free from [[Medusa]]'s personal punishment.
She is not imprisoned alongside [[Athena]] and [[Poseidon]].
Chapter 808 explicitly separates Artemis from their fate after [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] confirms that she had no involvement in Medusa's ancient suffering. Victor completely heals her and orders her to leave the judgment area rather than remain with the two condemned Gods.[2]
Artemis subsequently becomes one of Victor's conquered Divine subordinates and remains associated with Victor's Faction and the [[Dragons Nest Pantheon]].
Her obedience is initially based heavily upon survival, but the narration suggests that this approach benefits her significantly because Victor rewards useful and competent subordinates regardless of whether they were once enemies.[2]

### Notes
- Artemis' primary directly established Divinities are Moon and Hunt.[1]
- Her titles connected to Archery, Animals, Wilderness, Maidenhood and Childbirth are retained as part of her broader traditional Divine identity, but not every one has been demonstrated as a completely separate Major Concept.
- Artemis uses an ivory Divine Bow that shines with moonlike white light.[1]
- She and [[Athena]] are combat-capable Gods, unlike [[Hera]], whose Marriage Divinity provides little direct battlefield usefulness.[7]
- Artemis was important enough that [[Hestia]] believed [[Zeus]] would prioritize reviving her if she died during [[Olympus]]' civil war.[7]
- She possessed enough attachment to the Amazons to become furious upon seeing their massacre.[4]
- Nyx explicitly ordered Artemis and the other Goddesses to help the people who possessed their Blessings rather than recklessly attacking the main Demon commander.[4]
- Artemis had nothing to do with [[Medusa]]'s ancient transformation and punishment. Medusa admitted she only included Artemis in her revenge because Artemis annoyed her during their recent battle.[2]
- [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] apologized and completely healed Artemis once this misunderstanding was clarified.[2]
- Her submission to Victor was initially pragmatic rather than ideological.
- Artemis' attraction toward Victor is established by Chapter 858, but no confirmed romantic relationship currently exists.[8]
