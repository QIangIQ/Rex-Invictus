**[[Hades]]** is the god of the dead and king of the [[Greek Underworld]], lord of the riches beneath the earth. He is the eldest son of [[Kronos]] and [[Rhea]], one of the [[Big Three]], husband of [[Persephone]] and father of [[Bianca di Angelo]], [[Nico di Angelo]] and, through his Roman aspect [[Pluto]], [[Hazel Levesque]]. In the Empire the [[Hades UOE]] arm serves him, and he owns the [[Helm of Darkness]].

## Greek History (IRL)

**Origins.** Firstborn son of [[Kronos]] and [[Rhea]], swallowed and freed by [[Zeus]]. In the [[Titanomachy]] the Cyclopes gave him a cap of invisibility. In the drawing of lots he received the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]].

**Domain.** He rules the dead, assisted by [[Charon]] the ferryman, the three-headed [[Cerberus]] and the judges Minos, Rhadamanthus and Aeacus. He is stern and just rather than evil. Because the earth's wealth lies beneath it he was also called Plouton, "the wealthy one". Mortals avoided naming him and rarely built him temples.

**Persephone.** He abducted [[Persephone]], daughter of [[Demeter]], and she ate pomegranate seeds that bound her to spend part of the year with him.

**Other myths.** He let Orpheus lead Eurydice out on condition he not look back. Heracles borrowed [[Cerberus]] as his twelfth labor. [[Asclepius]]' resurrections angered him enough to complain to Zeus. Symbols: the bident, the cap of invisibility, Cerberus, the cypress and narcissus.

**Primary sources:** Hesiod's *Theogony*, *Homeric Hymn to Demeter*, Homer's *Odyssey* Book XI, Ovid.

## PJO Canon

In [[The Lightning Thief]] [[Percy Jackson|Percy]] travels to Hades' palace to retrieve his mother. Hades accuses Percy of stealing his [[Helm of Darkness]] and takes [[Sally Jackson]] hostage. In [[The Titan's Curse]] it is revealed that Hades is the father of [[Bianca di Angelo|Bianca]] and [[Nico di Angelo|Nico]], born before the Pact of the Big Three. In [[The Battle of the Labyrinth]] Nico seeks his aid. In [[The Last Olympian]] he finally joins the Olympians against [[Kronos]] after Nico persuades him, and earns a cabin at camp (Cabin 13).

In [[The Heroes of Olympus]] his Roman aspect [[Pluto]] is father of Hazel. In [[The House of Hades]] his realm is threatened by the Doors of Death.

### Appearances

#### First Mention
Chapter 298
Hades is an ancient Olympian God, one of the three principal sons of Kronus and [[Rhea]], the elder brother of [[Zeus]] and [[Poseidon]], and the former legitimate Ruler of the [[Greek Underworld]]. Although Zeus governed [[Olympus|Mount Olympus]] as its God-King, Hades possessed an independent realm and Authority beneath the Earth, where he acted as the recognized King of Greek Hell and maintained responsibility over the systems connected to its dead, its gates and [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]].[1][2]
Hades was significantly more dangerous than his political isolation from Olympus initially suggested. Nyx later stated that the reason many Greek Gods feared him was straightforward: Hades was stronger than Zeus. His position also gave him access to the immense wealth and Divine treasures accumulated within the Underworld, with Nyx considering the treasures of Mount Olympus incomparable to those under Hades' control.[3] Much of this strength was connected to his status as a legitimate Ruler. A Ruler operating within their own Hell receives support from the realm itself comparable in principle to the territorial Authority a God-King possesses over their Divine plane.[3]
Despite this power, Hades was deeply isolated from the rest of the [[Greek Pantheon]]. Zeus feared both Hades himself and the strategic importance of his position as guardian of Tartarus, reasoning that Hades could release imprisoned entities and potentially initiate another war capable of overthrowing Olympus. Zeus consequently used his political influence to keep his elder brother separated from the Olympian establishment, creating a long-standing divide between Mount Olympus and the Greek Underworld.[1]
The relationship eventually collapsed completely during the Demonic War. Hades secretly joined the wider infernal coalition associated with [[Diablo]] and the leaders of the Seven Hells, abandoned his position in the Greek Underworld, took [[Cerberus]] with him and moved against Olympus while Zeus was attending the [[Supernatural Gathering]].[4][5] His departure devastated the functioning of Greek Hell and ultimately resulted in his former wife [[Persephone]] undergoing the trial of [[The Judges of The Abyss]] and replacing him as the realm's legitimate Ruler.[2][3]
Hades has not been confirmed dead. After abandoning the Greek Pantheon and joining Diablo's wider alliance, he disappeared from the central storyline and did not participate in the later destruction of Olympus. His exact current location and allegiance therefore remain unknown.

### Physical Appearance
Hades has never received a complete direct physical appearance in the novel. Although other characters repeatedly discuss him, his power, his artifacts and his actions, the story has not provided a canonical description of his height, build, hair, eyes, clothing or Divine manifestation.

### Personality and Traits
Hades is primarily characterized through the memories and testimony of people who lived under his authority rather than through direct dialogue. He was powerful, stern, possessive and highly controlling, particularly toward [[Persephone]]. After taking Persephone into the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] and marrying her, Hades strongly restricted her ability to leave because he disliked the idea of his wife being outside his territory. Persephone became increasingly bored and emotionally isolated by this treatment, eventually leaving the Underworld whenever circumstances allowed her to escape his attention.[1]
Persephone later reevaluated the relationship far more critically. Although she once believed that she genuinely loved Hades, she eventually recognized that the affection had developed after being abducted while young, confined inside his realm and forced into a marriage in which Hades possessed overwhelming authority over her environment. By Chapter 810, she described her former attachment to him as essentially the result of Stockholm Syndrome.[6]
Hades also appears to have possessed a deep resentment toward the Olympian political order. [[Zeus]] distrusted him, excluded him from ordinary Olympian affairs and deliberately encouraged prejudice against both Hades and the Gods associated with his realm because he feared Hades' control over [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]] and the possibility that his elder brother could overthrow him.[1] Hades' eventual alliance with [[Diablo]] cannot be reduced solely to this resentment, but the hostility between him and [[Olympus]] created an obvious political foundation upon which Diablo could build.
His betrayal during the Demonic War demonstrates that Hades was capable of placing his own objectives above his responsibilities as Ruler. Whatever agreement he reached with the infernal coalition was important enough that he abandoned the Greek Underworld while its Soul system was still dependent upon a legitimate Ruler, removed [[Cerberus]] from the gates and left Tartarus vulnerable. The resulting chaos forced Persephone, [[Thanatos]], [[Hypnos]] and the remaining Underworld Gods to deal with a crisis Hades had effectively created through his absence.[7]
Hades should not, however, be reduced to an irrational or incompetent ruler. He successfully became a legitimate Ruler of Hell, meaning he had previously passed the supernatural qualification imposed upon candidates by the system governing the afterlife. Nyx explicitly compared Persephone's later trial-induced transformation with Hades' own experience, confirming that Hades had undergone the Ruler selection process rather than merely claiming the throne through inheritance or military strength.[3]

### History
Hades was born to the Titan King Kronus and [[Rhea]] as one of the Divine siblings who would later form the central family of the [[Greek Pantheon]]. Alongside [[Zeus]] and [[Poseidon]], he belonged to the group commonly treated as the [[Big Three]], although their later political positions were radically different. Zeus became the God-King of [[Olympus|Mount Olympus]], Poseidon maintained his authority over the seas, and Hades became associated with the [[Greek Underworld]].
At some point Hades obtained the Helm of Hades, a Divine Artifact directly linked to the position of legitimate Ruler of Greek Hell. [[The Helm]] was not merely a symbol of office. It functioned as a connection point to the greater system governing Souls and could place a candidate before [[The Judges of The Abyss]] for the trial required to become a recognized Ruler.[2] Chapter 641 confirms that Hades himself had previously experienced the Ruler trial, establishing that his authority over the Underworld was genuine rather than ceremonial.[3]
As Ruler, Hades held the real power of the Underworld. His wife [[Persephone]] possessed the title of Queen but, before replacing him, was comparatively weak and had little genuine administrative control over the dimension. Nyx later described pre-awakening Persephone as little more than a decorative Queen because Hades was the person who actually possessed the realm's power.[3]
Hades was additionally responsible for guarding [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]], placing him directly above one of the most dangerous prisons in the Greek cosmology. Zeus' fear of this responsibility shaped their relationship for eons. The God-King understood that if Hades deliberately released the [[Titans]] and other dangerous prisoners contained within Tartarus, the resulting conflict could threaten Zeus' throne. Zeus therefore isolated Hades politically and encouraged the wider Pantheon to regard the Underworld with suspicion.[1]
Hades eventually abducted the young Persephone and made her his wife. Their marriage produced multiple children and lasted for an enormous period, but Hades' controlling treatment restricted Persephone's freedom so heavily that she repeatedly sought opportunities to leave the Underworld. Their marriage was effectively over by the time Hades betrayed the Greek Pantheon during the Demonic War, and Persephone later treated him unequivocally as her former husband.[1][6]

### Synopsis

#### Discoveries, Changes, And War
Hades is first substantially described through [[Persephone]] while [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] interacts with the supernatural connection inherited from [[Adonis Snow]]. Persephone explains that Hades, her husband and uncle, was intensely possessive and rarely allowed her to leave the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]]. She also explains the political reason Hades had become so isolated: [[Zeus]] feared him because Hades guarded [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]] and possessed the means to potentially cause another Divine war by releasing what had been imprisoned there. Zeus consequently spent considerable effort isolating his elder brother and encouraging hostility toward the Greek Underworld.[1]
These details establish that Hades' separation from [[Olympus]] was not simply a traditional division of Divine responsibilities. His realm, strength and strategic access to Tartarus made him a potential rival to Zeus, and the God-King treated him accordingly.

#### Demonic War
Hades' resentment toward the existing Greek order eventually became open betrayal during the Demonic War. While [[Zeus]] and the other supernatural leaders were occupied at the [[Supernatural Gathering]], [[Diablo]] revealed that the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] factions of several Pantheons had already begun moving. Zeus examined [[Olympus]] and discovered hostile shadows spreading across his territory, immediately recognizing Hades' involvement and realizing that his brother had returned against him.[4][8]
The consequences inside the Greek Underworld were catastrophic. Hades disappeared from the realm, [[Cerberus]] vanished from the gates, [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]] was opened and the old [[Titans]] imprisoned after the ancient Greek war began emerging again. The normal flow of death and Souls deteriorated, the gates of the Underworld remained inadequately protected and escaping Souls threatened the stability of the entire afterlife system. [[Persephone]], [[Thanatos]], [[Hypnos]], the Furies and the remaining Underworld forces were forced to contain the escaped beings without the Ruler who had previously governed the dimension.[7]
Hades' actions were eventually explained more directly. Nyx revealed to Persephone that Hades had betrayed Mount Olympus and joined a coalition involving the leaders of the Seven Hells. According to Nyx, Hades intended eventually to return with Demons from another Hell and claim the Greek Underworld as part of whatever larger plan had been developed by the infernal alliance.[5]
By this point Hades had also abandoned the supernatural office of Ruler. Nyx offered Persephone the original Helm of Hades, explaining that she had managed to obtain the Divine Artifact before it completely left the [[Greek Pantheon]]. She proposed that Persephone inherit the empty position, usurp Hades' former dominion and become the second legitimate Ruler of Greek Hell.[5]
Persephone initially refused because she correctly suspected Nyx was attempting to manipulate her, but the situation continued deteriorating until preserving the Underworld required an actual Ruler. She eventually accepted the Helm and entered the trial administered by [[The Judges of The Abyss]]. The trial was not a contest of raw strength; it tested whether the candidate could judge Souls impartially and place the functioning of the afterlife above personal affection, prejudice or Divine arrogance.[2]
Persephone ultimately succeeded. The Underworld recognized her as its new Ruler, restored the missing central authority of Greek Hell and elevated her into the God-King realm while she operated with the realm's support. Nyx was stunned by the extent of the transformation because she had expected Persephone to become merely a High-Level Goddess. Instead, Persephone's Divine Power exceeded even Hades'.[3]
Nyx's internal assessment simultaneously revealed the true scale of Hades' former strength. Hades had been feared because he was stronger than Zeus, and the wealth and Divine treasures accumulated within his Underworld exceeded those of Mount Olympus. Persephone becoming even more powerful therefore meant she had not merely inherited an empty office—she had surpassed one of the strongest Gods of the old Olympian order.[3]
Hades did not return to reclaim the Underworld during the remainder of the Demonic War. Persephone closed the realm under her own Authority, and the infernal administration Hades had once controlled became hers.

#### King Among Kings
Hades remained absent when the Greek conflict eventually entered its final phase. By this time [[Persephone]] had completely consolidated her position as the Ruler of the [[Greek Underworld]], while Hades' former territories and the independent Domains belonging to Gods such as [[Hecate]], [[Hypnos]], Nyx and [[Erebus]] continued existing within the larger dimension.[9]
When [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction eventually moved against [[Zeus]] and the surviving Olympian order, Hades did not appear to defend either [[Olympus]] or his former realm. Zeus and Kronus were killed, the old Olympian government collapsed and Persephone purged Zeus' remaining influence from her Underworld without Hades making a confirmed appearance.
Persephone later confronted the emotional consequences of her past relationships. After the Greek war she admitted that the love she once believed she felt for Hades had been created under profoundly unhealthy circumstances. Hades had abducted her while she was young, confined her within his realm and controlled much of her life; Persephone ultimately identified her attachment as Stockholm Syndrome rather than a healthy marriage.[6]
She also regarded Hades' later actions as a personal betrayal. Whatever feelings remained from their long marriage were destroyed by his decision to abandon the Underworld, join the infernal coalition and leave Persephone and their children to deal with the consequences. Hades' disappearance therefore marked not only the end of his political rule but the effective destruction of his family relationship with the former Queen.
No later chapter has confirmed Hades' death. His whereabouts following the Demonic War remain unknown.

### Power and Authority
Hades was one of the strongest Gods of the old [[Greek Pantheon]]. His danger came from the combination of his natural Divine strength, his long experience and his position as a legitimate Ruler rather than from a single demonstrated combat technique.
- God Physiology: Hades is an ancient Third Generation Olympian God with the supernatural physical, spiritual and energetic characteristics inherent to major Divine beings.
- God-King-class Power: Although [[Zeus]] occupied the formal God-King throne of [[Olympus]], Nyx explicitly considered Hades physically and Divinely stronger than Zeus.[3]
- Ruler Authority (Formerly): Hades successfully became the legitimate Ruler of the [[Greek Underworld]] and possessed the Authority granted by the supernatural system administering the realm.[2][3]
- Underworld Empowerment (Formerly): A legitimate Ruler receives the support of their Hell while operating within it. Nyx compared this territorial Authority to the advantage a God-King possesses inside their own Divine dimension.[3]
- Soul-System Connection (Formerly): As Ruler, Hades was connected to the system responsible for processing, judging and routing Souls belonging to the Greek Underworld. This authority was local and subordinate to the greater mechanisms maintained by [[The Judges of The Abyss]].
- Soul Judgment (Formerly): The trial required to become a Ruler specifically exists to determine whether the candidate can judge Souls impartially. Hades' previous success therefore confirms that he possessed the knowledge and spiritual perception required to fulfill that function.[2][3]
- Territorial Authority (Formerly): While recognized as Ruler, Hades possessed supernatural authority throughout the Greek Underworld rather than merely political control over its inhabitants.
- [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]] Authority / Guardianship (Formerly): Hades was responsible for Tartarus and its prisoners, a position important enough that Zeus feared he could trigger another enormous Divine war by using that access against Olympus.[1]
- Immense Divine Wealth: Hades controlled an extraordinary accumulation of supernatural treasure inside the Underworld. Nyx considered its wealth superior to that stored on Mount Olympus.[3]
- Divine Artifact Mastery: Hades formerly possessed the Helm of Hades, an artifact directly connected to the Greek Ruler system.[5][2]
The novel has not directly shown Hades fighting on-page, so individual techniques such as unrestricted [[Necromancy]], Dream Manipulation, Mining Manipulation or similar powers traditionally associated with mythological Hades should not be treated as confirmed personal abilities unless they are separately demonstrated in the story.

### The Helm of Hades
[[The Helm]] of Hades is the most important artifact associated with Hades and should not be treated merely as a conventional helmet granting invisibility. Within MTW, its primary significance is its relationship with the legitimate rulership of Greek Hell.[5][2]
The Helm acts as a connection point between its bearer and the greater supernatural system governing the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]]. A potential successor who accepts the Artifact can be taken before [[The Judges of The Abyss]] and subjected to the Ruler trial. Passing that trial establishes the candidate as the legitimate Ruler and provides access to the memories, knowledge and administrative Authority required to govern the afterlife.[2]
Hades successfully used the position before the current story, while Chapter 641 strongly implies that he underwent the same general type of trial later experienced by [[Persephone]]. Nyx specifically compared Persephone's psychological transformation after the test with Hades' own and noted that Hades had not changed nearly as drastically.[3]
Before Hades completely separated from the [[Greek Pantheon]], Nyx obtained the original Helm. After Hades abandoned his responsibilities, Nyx offered the Artifact to Persephone, who eventually became its recognized successor. Persephone later discovered that the Helm could be transformed into a dark Divine Scythe and integrated with her new Ruler regalia, but this particular manifestation belongs to Persephone and should not automatically be attributed to Hades without direct evidence.[5][3]

### Relationships

#### Persephone Elderblood
[[Persephone]] was Hades' niece, wife and Queen of the [[Greek Underworld]]. Hades abducted her while she was comparatively young and brought her into his realm, where his possessiveness caused him to heavily restrict her freedom and discourage her from leaving the Underworld.[1]
Although Persephone eventually developed genuine emotional attachment toward him, she later recognized that those feelings had formed after prolonged captivity and dependence upon the person controlling nearly every aspect of her environment. By Chapter 810, she described the relationship as love produced through Stockholm Syndrome.[6]
Whatever remained of their marriage ended completely when Hades abandoned Greek Hell and allied with the forces surrounding [[Diablo]]. Persephone considered his actions a betrayal both of the Underworld and of herself. She eventually inherited Hades' former Ruler position, surpassed his Divine Power and became the permanent sovereign of the realm he had abandoned.[5][3]

#### Zeus
[[Zeus]] was Hades' younger brother and principal political rival within the old Greek Divine order. Zeus' fear of Hades was one of the main causes of the division between [[Olympus]] and the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]]. Because Hades guarded [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]], Zeus believed his brother possessed the means to release ancient enemies and potentially initiate a war capable of dethroning him.[1]
The fear was not baseless in terms of Hades' personal strength. Nyx later confirmed that Hades was stronger than Zeus despite Zeus possessing the formal position of God-King.[3]
Their hostility eventually became open warfare when Hades moved against Olympus during the Demonic War while Zeus was absent at the [[Supernatural Gathering]].[4]

#### Diablo
[[Diablo]] became Hades' most important known external ally during the Demonic War. Diablo spent years gathering Dark-aligned and [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] factions from multiple Pantheons and successfully drew Hades into the wider coalition involving the Seven Hells.[4][5]
The exact agreement between Hades and Diablo has never been revealed. Nyx nevertheless stated that Hades intended to return with an entourage of foreign Demons and reclaim Greek Hell for their larger plan, establishing that his cooperation went substantially beyond a temporary attack on [[Zeus]].[5]

#### Thanatos and Hypnos
[[Thanatos]] and [[Hypnos]] were two of the major Gods living beneath Hades' old administration. After Hades disappeared, both remained with [[Persephone]] and attempted to stabilize the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] instead of following their former Ruler into [[Diablo]]'s coalition.[7]
Their decision demonstrates that Hades' betrayal was not representative of every Chthonic God. Many of the Underworld's inhabitants remained loyal to the realm itself even after its King abandoned it.

#### Cerberus
[[Cerberus]] guarded the gates of the [[Greek Underworld]] during Hades' reign and was closely associated with him. When Hades disappeared, Cerberus vanished as well, leaving the entrance to the Underworld without its traditional guardian and contributing to the collapse of its normal Soul-control mechanisms.[7]
The timing strongly connects Cerberus' disappearance to Hades' departure, although the novel has not shown exactly where Hades took the creature afterward.

#### Nyx Elderblood
Nyx understood Hades' strength, political position and Ruler system better than most of [[Olympus]]. After Hades betrayed the [[Greek Pantheon]], she deliberately moved to replace him before foreign infernal forces could permanently seize the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]].[5]
Nyx obtained Hades' original Helm, selected [[Persephone]] as the most suitable successor and manipulated circumstances until Greek Hell possessed a functioning Ruler again. Her plan succeeded far beyond expectations: Persephone not only replaced Hades but acquired greater Divine Power than he had possessed.[3]

### Former Rulership of the Greek Underworld
Hades' most important role in the cosmology was his position as one of the recognized Rulers responsible for an established Hell. A Ruler is distinct from a God-King. A God-King governs the primary Divine society of a Pantheon, while a Ruler administers its afterlife and maintains its connection with the wider Soul-management system.
During Hades' reign, the hierarchy broadly functioned as:

This position explains why Hades abandoning the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] produced consequences far beyond an ordinary king leaving his country. Hell was a metaphysical system responsible for the dead. Removing its legitimate Ruler while simultaneously leaving [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]] and the gates unstable disrupted processes directly connected to the meaning of death for the Greek supernatural population.[7][2]
[[Persephone]] permanently succeeded Hades after completing the Judges' trial. From that point onward, Hades no longer possessed recognized rulership over Greek Hell.

### Current Status
Hades' current whereabouts are unknown. The last major information concerning him places him outside the [[Greek Underworld]] after betraying [[Olympus]] and joining the coalition of Underworld powers associated with [[Diablo]].[5]
He did not return during [[Persephone]]'s ascension, did not contest her recognition as the new Ruler, and did not appear during [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s later invasion and destruction of the old Olympian political order. No chapter has shown Hades being killed, consumed or otherwise permanently destroyed.
Until the novel explicitly reveals his fate, he should therefore remain indexed as Alive — Whereabouts Unknown rather than Deceased.

### Trivia
- Hades is the former legitimate Ruler of the [[Greek Underworld]], not merely its former political King.[2][3]
- Hades successfully underwent a Ruler trial before the present storyline. Nyx later compared [[Persephone]]'s psychological transformation after her trial with Hades' own reaction to the process.[3]
- Nyx explicitly states that Hades was stronger than [[Zeus]].[3]
- Hades' wealth was similarly extraordinary; Nyx considered the treasures of [[Olympus|Mount Olympus]] inferior to the treasures accumulated within the Underworld.[3]
- His strength should not automatically be interpreted as proof that he possessed Zeus' formal God-King office. Hades' principal institutional power came from being the Ruler of Greek Hell.
- The Ruler possesses God-King-like territorial Authority within their Hell because the dimension itself supports the recognized sovereign.[3]
- Hades' former position as guardian of [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]] was one of the principal reasons Zeus feared him.[1]
- Zeus deliberately isolated Hades because he believed Hades could potentially release the prisoners of Tartarus and initiate another war capable of dethroning him.[1]
- Hades' treatment of Persephone was considerably darker than the old version of this article suggested. Persephone was abducted while young, heavily restricted within the Underworld and later described her love for him as Stockholm Syndrome.[1][6]
- Hades abandoned the Greek Underworld during the Demonic War and allied with the coalition associated with the leaders of the Seven Hells.[5]
- [[Cerberus]] disappeared at approximately the same time as Hades, leaving the gates of the Underworld without their traditional guardian.[7]
- Hades' disappearance coincided with the opening of Tartarus and the collapse of much of the normal Greek afterlife administration.[7]
- Nyx obtained the original Helm of Hades before it completely left the [[Greek Pantheon]] and later gave it to Persephone.[5]
- Persephone became the second Ruler of the Greek Underworld after Hades.[5][3]
- Persephone's Divine Power after passing the Ruler trial surpassed Hades'.[3]
- Hades never appeared to contest Persephone's succession.
- Hades did not participate in the later final war that destroyed Zeus' Olympian government.
- No canonical chapter presently confirms Hades' death.
- Hades has not received a complete direct physical description in the novel.
- Traditional mythological stories concerning Hades helping Heracles, lending his Helm to Perseus, resurrecting Orpheus' wife or possessing numerous additional mythological Divinities should not automatically be treated as events demonstrated within MTW unless the novel specifically confirms them.
- The story distinguishes the Ruler of the Underworld from the actual Greek personification/Divine authority of Death represented by beings such as [[Thanatos]]. Hades' primary confirmed position is therefore Ruler and God of the Underworld rather than the singular embodiment of Death.
- [[Prometheus]] should not currently be marked deceased in Hades' family information; later material identifies Prometheus as missing rather than confirmed dead.
- Despite being historically grouped with Zeus and [[Poseidon]] as one of the [[Big Three]], Hades' political base was the Underworld rather than Mount Olympus.

#### Skills and Abilities
As one of the [[Big Three]] and the eldest son of Kronus and [[Rhea]], Hades is considered to be insanely powerful. He's powerful enough to rival [[Zeus]] and even defeat him. Hades is powerful enough to have two Primordials as subordinates.
- God Physiology: As one of the strongest Third Generation Gods and the Ruler of The [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]], Hades possess all of the gods basic abilities to a greater degree, like Superhuman Strength, Superhuman Speed and Reflex, Enhanced Durability, Enhanced Agility, Enhanced Sense, Regeneration, [[Immortality]], Divine Power, Divinity, Blessing and Curse Bestowal,....,etc.
- Divinities: Hades has major divinities of Mining, Wealth, Treasures, Funerals and Riches. He also has lesser divinities of Dreams and Fertility.
- Soul Storage: According to [[Persephone]], Hades can store souls inside his body just like [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] but he has a limit, which if he goes past that, he has the risk of going mad with so many souls.
- Miasma Manipulation: As the God of the Underworld, Hades can manipulate miasma.

#### Former Powers
[[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] Manipulation: Hades, as a Ruler, can control the Underworld and everything within, including any creatures that may reside in it and even the souls within.
- Underworld Lordship: Hades holds dominion in the Underworld and has absolute power in the Underworld. He is able to control, manipulate, lock, open and shape the Underworld as he desires. He's also able to command any creature of the Underworld and he's able to banish anyone from it as well.
