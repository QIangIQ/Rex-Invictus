**[[Hera]]** is the Queen of [[Olympus]] and the [[Olympians|Olympian]] goddess of marriage, women, family and the sky's majesty. She is a daughter of [[Kronos]] and [[Rhea]], wife and sister of [[Zeus]], and mother of [[Ares]], [[Hephaestus]], [[Hebe]] and [[Eileithyia]]. Her Roman counterpart is [[Juno]]. Her Divine Being title in the Empire is [[Divine Being 'Matrimony'|Matrimony]].

## Greek History (IRL)

**Origins.** Hera was swallowed by [[Kronos]] and freed by [[Zeus]]. She was raised by [[Oceanus]] and [[Tethys]] during the [[Titanomachy]]. Zeus courted her disguised as a cuckoo, and their wedding took place in the [[Garden of the Hesperides]], where [[Gaia]] gave her the tree of [[Golden Apples]].

**Domain and cult.** Goddess of marriage, childbirth and the household. Her great sanctuaries were the Heraion of Argos, the Heraion of Samos and the Temple of Hera at Olympia, which is older than the temple of Zeus there. Her symbols are the peacock, the cuckoo, the pomegranate, the diadem and the lotus-tipped scepter.

**Myths.** Most of her stories involve jealous punishment of Zeus's lovers and children: [[Leto]] (barred from giving birth on land), Io (turned into a cow and tormented by a gadfly), Semele (tricked into seeing Zeus's true form) and above all [[Hercules|Heracles]], whose name means "glory of Hera" and whom she harried all his life. She threw the infant [[Hephaestus]] from [[Olympus]] for being lame. She sided with the Greeks in the [[Trojan War]] after the Judgment of [[Paris]] and led a failed revolt against Zeus, who hung her from the sky in golden chains.

**Primary sources:** Homer's *Iliad*, Hesiod's *Theogony*, the *Homeric Hymns*, Pausanias.

## PJO Canon

In [[Percy Jackson and the Olympians]] Hera is cold toward demigods and especially dislikes [[Percy Jackson|Percy]]. In [[The Battle of the Labyrinth]] she appears to Percy and [[Annabeth Chase|Annabeth]] on the road, offers help and reveals herself as the goddess of family. Annabeth refuses her help, earning her lasting dislike.

In [[The Heroes of Olympus]] she is the driving force of the series. She swaps [[Percy Jackson]] and [[Jason Grace]] between the two camps to unite Greeks and Romans against [[Gaia]]. In [[The Lost Hero]] she is imprisoned at the [[Wolf House]] by [[Gaia]]'s forces and freed by [[Jason Grace|Jason]], Piper and Leo. Her Roman aspect [[Juno]] is more martial. She blasts Percy's memory, guides the Argo II and is finally reconciled with [[Zeus]].

In [[The Trials of Apollo]] she is part of the council that sees [[Apollo]]'s punishment through.

Hera, also known as [[Juno]], is a Third Generation Olympian Goddess, daughter of Kronus and [[Rhea]], sister and former wife of [[Zeus]], and the former Queen of the [[Greek Pantheon]]. Her principal Divine domains are Marriage, Women, Childbirth, Fidelity and Faithfulness. Despite her position as an ancient Major Goddess, Hera's Concepts are predominantly social and familial rather than combat-oriented, leaving her considerably less effective in direct warfare than Gods whose Divinities were designed around battle or destruction.[2]
For much of her existence, Hera's identity was inseparable from her position beside Zeus. His repeated infidelity created ages of resentment, but Hera remained Queen partly because her Divinity as a Goddess of Marriage made simply severing her own marriage far more complicated than an ordinary separation and partly because she openly enjoyed the tremendous authority granted by her station.[3][4] When Zeus' increasingly irrational behavior eventually made remaining beside him dangerous even to her, Hera abandoned [[Olympus]], committed an irreversible act of treason against Zeus and entered the protection of [[Victor Elderblood]]'s faction.
Unlike several other surviving Greek Goddesses, Hera has not been established as one of Victor's wives, a member of Clan Elderblood or a True Dragon. Her position within Victor's sphere began as that of a politically valuable refugee and former Queen whose knowledge of Zeus and Olympus made her useful to the emerging Dragons Nest faction.[1]

### Physical Appearance
The novel has not provided a sufficiently detailed and consistently repeated description of Hera's hair, eyes or other identifying physical characteristics to establish a definitive canonical appearance. Her current portrait should therefore be treated as an artistic representation rather than evidence for canonical coloration or clothing.
As an Olympian Goddess, Hera possesses the supernatural physiology of the Divine races. Her blood is golden, a physical characteristic displayed by Gods and directly shown when Hera retrieved the Divine weapon she later used against [[Zeus]].[4]
Hera's manner and bearing reflect the immense period she spent as Queen of Olympus. She carries herself with aristocratic pride and was accustomed to receiving deference from other Gods and supernatural beings. Even after losing the institutional protection of [[Olympus]], much of that royal manner remained deeply embedded in her behavior.

### Personality
Hera is exceptionally proud, vain, aristocratic and status-conscious. Being Queen of Olympus was not simply an occupation but one of the central pillars of her identity. When discussing why she had remained with [[Zeus]] despite their catastrophic marriage, Hera admitted that her Divinity was not the only reason: she had also been unwilling to surrender the tremendous authority, privileges and social position attached to being Queen.[4] This attachment to status explains much of the arrogance she displayed toward individuals whom she considered socially beneath an Olympian Goddess.
Her arrogance frequently caused her to misjudge people existing outside the traditional Divine hierarchy. During the confrontation between the Olympian Goddesses and [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s Countesses, Hera attempted to speak condescendingly toward [[Scathach Elderblood Scarlett]], [[Agnes Elderblood Snow]] and Natashia. The disparity between ceremonial rank and actual combat ability became immediately apparent when Natashia moved behind Hera and placed a blade at her neck before Hera could meaningfully respond. Agnes likewise regarded Hera as effectively a civilian when compared with women whose lives and powers were centered around combat.[2]
Hera is also vindictive, jealous and impulsive. Zeus' affairs had accumulated an enormous amount of resentment inside her, and people familiar with Hera were capable of recognizing the expression she displayed when contemplating revenge. [[Hestia]] understood that, if Hera ever truly decided to betray Zeus, she would not choose a subtle or easily reversible method. Hera ultimately validated that assessment by committing an act deliberately extreme enough that Zeus could never plausibly accept her as his Queen again.[4]
At the same time, Hera is not recklessly brave in ordinary circumstances. She is highly self-preserving and politically opportunistic, recognizing when she lacks the combat power to force an outcome herself. She attempted to exploit Hestia's compassion when she first wanted an escape from [[Olympus]], later tolerated hostile negotiations with Victor's wives when she needed their protection and accepted being placed under supervision once it became obvious that she possessed no safer alternative.[2][1] Her willingness to avoid disadvantageous confrontations should therefore be distinguished from simple cowardice.
Her association with Nyx substantially darkened her temperament. For several months, Nyx made Hera perform increasingly unpleasant assignments as payment for her assistance, including killing, torture, lying and other actions that the former Queen would ordinarily have preferred to delegate to subordinates. Nyx eventually wondered whether she had pushed Hera too far and altered her personality more than originally intended.[4] Hera consequently became more personally ruthless and considerably less removed from the brutality occurring around her.
Hera also retains a cruel and judgmental side toward her own family. Although she acknowledged the extraordinary craftsmanship of the golden scythe created by [[Hephaestus]], she could praise her son's skill while simultaneously insulting his appearance.[4] Her affection for relatives therefore does not necessarily prevent her vanity, pride or harsh judgments from surfacing.
The central contradiction of Hera's character remains her status as the Goddess of Marriage and Faithfulness while possessing one of the most dysfunctional marriages in the [[Greek Pantheon]]. She valued the concept, institution and authority surrounding marriage while simultaneously despising the repeated betrayals of the husband beside whom she remained for ages. Her eventual defection represented not only treason against Zeus, but the destruction of a royal identity she had protected for an enormous portion of her existence.

### Synopsis

#### Discoveries, Changes, And War
Hera is first mentioned when [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] learns more about the nature of Gods and Divinities. During a discussion concerning Divine Concepts, Hera and the Norse marriage Goddess [[Frigg]] are used as examples while Victor questions whether a God is behaviorally imprisoned by the Concept they represent. It is explained that a Divinity is closer to an intrinsic belief, doctrine or part of the God's identity than literal mind control. Hera therefore remained capable of betraying [[Zeus]] despite being the Goddess of Marriage; her Concept did not erase her free will or mechanically compel absolute obedience to her husband.[5]
This distinction later became important to understanding Hera's marriage. Her Divine nature made voluntary separation from the person she had married extremely difficult and contrary to the identity she embodied, but it did not prevent her from hating Zeus, opposing him or eventually committing treason against him.

#### Demonic War
Hera made her first direct appearance after the Amazons invoked their ancient relationship with [[Olympus]] during the conflict involving the Asuras. As Queen of Olympus, Hera became aware of the developing crisis and brought [[Artemis]] and [[Athena]] into the matter, while Nyx independently became involved with the situation.[6]
Hera, Athena, Artemis and Nyx subsequently traveled to the Amazon territory. Rather than allowing the younger Olympian Goddesses to immediately interfere, Nyx forced them to observe what was occurring. She temporarily extended part of her perception to the others, allowing Hera and her companions to perceive supernatural phenomena surrounding Souls, death and the battlefield that their ordinary senses could not normally observe in the same manner.[7]
The crisis brought Hera into direct contact with several of [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s most powerful women. Hera, Athena and Artemis initially carried themselves with the pride expected from Olympian Goddesses, but Hera's condescension rapidly created hostility with [[Scathach]], Agnes and Natashia. The confrontation exposed the enormous difference between Divine rank and combat specialization. Hera was an ancient Major Goddess, but Marriage was a poor Concept for direct warfare. Natashia was capable of reaching Hera and placing a weapon against her neck before the Goddess could properly respond, while Agnes treated Hera as someone possessing political importance rather than the capabilities of a genuine frontline combatant.[2]
[[Hestia]] intervened before the confrontation escalated unnecessarily. Hera then attempted to persuade her sister to take her away from Olympus, already showing that her desire to escape [[Zeus]] existed well before her eventual formal defection. The women surrounding Hestia immediately recognized that Hera was attempting to use her sister's compassion to solve her own dangerous political situation. Hestia nevertheless remained unwilling to simply abandon Hera to a potentially fatal outcome.[2]

#### King Among Kings
By the time [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] traveled through Samar, Hera's relationship with [[Zeus]] had deteriorated beyond the dysfunctional equilibrium that had existed for ages. Following the attack involving the [[Titans]] and the intervention of an unidentified ally who had helped [[Olympus]] survive, Zeus' behavior became increasingly irrational and unstable. His treatment of Lesser Gods worsened, and his aggression eventually reached the point where Hera herself believed remaining inside Olympus could lead to her death.[8][3]
Hera approached Victor's faction with Nyx and sought asylum. Her request immediately created a political problem. Hera was not an obscure refugee but the current Queen of Olympus, wife of Zeus and one of the most recognizable representatives of the Pantheon. Sheltering her without convincing proof of defection could place an apparent Olympian agent directly inside Victor's growing faction. Victor's wives therefore refused to accept her solely because Zeus had begun treating her badly.[3]
Nyx attempted to turn the matter into a broader political transaction and proposed payment through part of Olympus' accumulated treasure. The negotiation escalated until a nominal agreement involving half of Olympus' treasures was discussed, but [[Scathach]] immediately identified the fundamental flaw: Nyx did not actually possess unrestricted authority to surrender the entire Pantheon's treasury. Hera's situation therefore could not be solved merely by offering resources belonging to Olympus.[4]
The discussion forced Hera to confront the deeper problem created by her own identity. She was still Zeus' Queen and still associated with Olympus. Hera explained that simply separating herself from the person she had married was exceptionally difficult because of the nature of her Divinity, but she also admitted that Divine principle had never been her only reason for staying. She enjoyed being Queen. The political authority and enormous privilege attached to Zeus had given Hera a powerful personal incentive to tolerate a marriage she otherwise despised.[4]
Rather than continue arguing, Hera decided to create evidence of treason so overwhelming that nobody could reasonably believe she intended to return to Zeus. Nyx transported her back to Olympus, where Hera retrieved a golden Divine scythe created by her son [[Hephaestus]]. The weapon could be compressed into a tiny tattoo upon her hand, allowing Hera to conceal it without visibly carrying an artifact.[4]
Hera then approached Zeus while he had no reason to consider his longtime wife an immediate enemy. After spending ages beside Hera, Zeus did not maintain the defensive caution around her that he would have used against an assassin or battlefield opponent. Hera deliberately exploited that familiarity. She manifested the scythe and castrated Zeus before he could properly defend himself, took what she had severed as physical proof of her betrayal, declared that he would need to find another Queen and escaped Olympus through Nyx.[4]
The feat was not evidence that Hera possessed combat power superior to Zeus. Hera herself acknowledged how easy the attack had been specifically because Zeus did not keep his guard raised around his wife. Its significance was political and personal rather than a straightforward power-scaling accomplishment. She had assaulted and humiliated the King of Olympus in a manner designed to eliminate any realistic possibility of returning to her former position.[4]
The incident also demonstrated how much Hera had changed during the preceding months. Nyx reflected upon the killing, torture, deception and other morally reprehensible tasks she had forced Hera to perform as payment for her help and wondered whether she had hardened the former Queen more than intended. [[Hestia]], however, recognized another part of the behavior as fundamentally Hera: her sister had always been arrogant, vain and impulsive, and once she genuinely decided to betray Zeus, an outrageously decisive act was entirely in character.[4]
Hera returned to Victor's faction covered in Divine blood and presented the result of her treason. Her action transformed the political calculation surrounding her asylum. She had now made herself an obvious enemy of Zeus and could no longer plausibly claim that the entire defection was an elaborate deception.[4]
[[Ruby Elderblood Scarlett]] and the other women involved in the discussion then examined the strategic value of accepting Hera. As former Queen, Hera possessed information regarding Zeus, Olympus and the internal affairs of the Greek Gods that conventional intelligence networks could not easily obtain. Her defection also possessed immense symbolic value. If even Zeus' own Queen could abandon him and receive sanctuary beneath Victor, other abused, endangered or politically dissatisfied Goddesses could begin viewing Victor's faction as a legitimate alternative to remaining under the old Pantheons.[1]
Hera was ultimately accepted as an Ancient Goddess Queen under strict conditions. Refugees protected by Victor's faction would be expected to remain useful rather than simply consume its protection indefinitely. Hera would remain near Hestia and [[Rhea]], who could supervise her integration, while Victor's wives imposed an especially direct rule: Hera was to remain far away from Victor. They had no intention of allowing the former Queen to transform political asylum into another attempt at acquiring status through their husband.[1]
The negotiations surrounding Hera also helped establish a broader strategy toward the collapsing [[Greek Pantheon]]. Victor's faction began considering which Gods and Goddesses were worth saving before Olympus destroyed itself, prioritizing useful individuals who could coexist with the emerging faction while avoiding those whose arrogance, hostility or political baggage outweighed their potential value.[1]
Hera's defection remained one of the personal humiliations attached to Zeus as the Greek conflict approached its final stage. During Victor's eventual confrontation with Zeus, Victor deliberately invoked the fact that he had taken away Zeus' wife alongside the treasures and other things the Olympian King had lost, using Hera's abandonment of him as another symbol of the complete collapse of Zeus' authority.[9] Hera's importance to the fall of Olympus was therefore not that she suddenly became one of its greatest frontline warriors, but that the Queen herself had rejected Zeus and joined the political order replacing him.

#### God Emperor Victor Elderblood
Long after [[Olympus]] ceased functioning as the independent power it had once been, Hera continued to be recognized through her fundamental identity as the Goddess of Marriage. During discussions surrounding the enormous population requirements of [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s expanding civilization, Violet proposed a Family Formation Policy that would provide incentives for exceptional individuals to establish larger families and would accommodate plural marriages where those involved possessed the ability and resources to support them. [[Aphrodite]] immediately predicted that [[Hestia]] and [[Rhea]] would enjoy aspects of the program while Hera would deeply hate it precisely because she was the Goddess of Marriage.[10]
The remark demonstrates that Hera's old Divine identity remained relevant even after the political system in which she had once been Queen disappeared. However, no confirmed material establishes that Hera followed the path taken by several other Greek women who entered Victor's immediate family. She has not been confirmed as Victor's wife, a member of Clan Elderblood or a True Dragon. Her established relationship with Victor's civilization remains that of a surviving ancient Olympian who defected from [[Zeus]] and was accepted beneath the much larger faction that eventually replaced the political order of Olympus.

### Powers and Abilities
Hera is an ancient Third Generation Olympian Goddess and a Major God whose existence predates modern supernatural civilization. Her [[Divine Energy]], physiology and age place her enormously above ordinary mortals; however, the nature of her personal Divinities is primarily social, reproductive and relational. The novel explicitly distinguishes her Divine rank from practical combat ability. Being an ancient Goddess does not automatically make Hera comparable to [[Combat Gods]], specialized warriors or martial monsters such as the women surrounding [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]].[2]

#### Divine Physiology
- God Physiology: Hera possesses the supernatural body and existence of an Olympian Goddess.
- [[Immortality]]/Longevity: Hera has existed for eons and does not naturally age according to mortal biological limits.
- [[Divine Energy]] Manipulation: As a Goddess, Hera possesses and controls Divine Energy.
- Enhanced Physical Capabilities: Her Divine body naturally possesses strength, speed, durability and senses enormously superior to ordinary mortals, although her demonstrated combat performance remains below that of specialized Divine and supernatural warriors.[2]
- Supernatural Regeneration: Hera possesses the regenerative properties associated with Divine beings, allowing her to survive and recover from injuries far beyond mortal limits. This does not make her immune to sufficient Divine, Soul or Conceptual damage.
- Golden Divine Blood: Hera possesses the golden blood characteristic of Gods, directly displayed during the events surrounding her betrayal of [[Zeus]].[4]
- [[Divine Presence]]: As an ancient Major Goddess and former Queen, Hera possesses the supernatural presence expected of a high-ranking Divine Being.

#### Divinity
- Divinity Manipulation: Hera possesses and embodies several related Divine Concepts associated with marriage, women and family.
- Marriage: Hera's central and most consistently emphasized Divinity. Her existence is deeply connected to the institution and Concept of Marriage.[5][4]
- Women: Hera possesses a Divine domain associated with women.
- Childbirth: Hera possesses Divine authority related to childbirth and the reproductive aspects traditionally connected to her worship.
- Fidelity: Hera possesses a domain associated with marital fidelity.
- Faithfulness: Her Divine identity also encompasses faithfulness and loyalty within marriage.

#### Marriage Divinity
Hera's relationship with her Marriage Divinity requires an important distinction. The Concept does not function as absolute mind control. The nature of Divinity was explained early in the story using Hera herself as an example: being the Goddess of Marriage did not make her physically incapable of betraying [[Zeus]] or remove her ability to choose actions contradicting him.[5]
What the Concept did affect was Hera's own relationship with the institution she embodied. During her attempt to escape [[Olympus]], Hera indicated that simply separating herself from the person she had married was not an ordinary option for her because of her Divine nature.[3][4] Marriage was part of Hera's identity rather than an external ability she could casually deactivate.
This should not be interpreted as supernatural obedience to Zeus. Hera remained capable of hating him, plotting against him, attacking him, humiliating him and fleeing his territory. Her eventual betrayal conclusively demonstrates that the Marriage Divinity did not erase her agency.[4]

#### Weapon Usage
- Scythe Proficiency: Hera is capable of wielding the golden Divine scythe forged by [[Hephaestus]]. She successfully used it to grievously mutilate [[Zeus]] through a surprise attack.[4]
- This feat should not be used to scale Hera above Zeus. Her attack succeeded specifically because Zeus trusted his longtime wife enough to remain unguarded around her.[4]
- Divine Artifact Usage: Hera can wield Divine equipment appropriate to her Divine physiology.
- Weapon Concealment: Hera's scythe can compress itself into a small tattoo on her hand, allowing the weapon to remain completely concealed until summoned.[4]
- Surprise Attacks: Hera demonstrated that she is willing to exploit personal familiarity, lowered defenses and an opponent's assumptions rather than engaging a vastly stronger combatant through conventional battle.[4]

#### Political and Non-Combat Skills
- Political Experience: Hera ruled beside [[Zeus]] for an enormous period as Queen of Olympus and possesses extensive experience with Divine courts, Pantheon politics, status, hierarchy and internal disputes.
- Intelligence Gathering and Institutional Knowledge: Hera's position beside Zeus gave her access to information concerning him and [[Olympus]] that outsiders could not easily obtain. This became one of the principal reasons [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s wives considered protecting her strategically worthwhile.[1]
- Political Symbolism: Hera possesses exceptional symbolic value because of her former status. Her public defection from Zeus demonstrated to other Greek Goddesses that even one of the central figures of Olympus could abandon the old order and survive under Victor's protection.[1]
- Negotiation: Hera is capable of participating in high-level political bargaining and presenting the value she can offer to another faction, although the asylum negotiations also demonstrated the limits of her position once she no longer possessed Olympus' institutional authority.[3][1]
- Deception: Her work under Nyx forced Hera to participate in lies, covert behavior and morally compromising assignments beyond the ceremonial responsibilities of a Queen.[4]
- Psychological Exploitation: Hera understands personal relationships well enough to exploit familiarity and emotional assumptions, most dramatically demonstrated when she weaponized Zeus' trust in her to bypass the defenses he would normally maintain against an enemy.[4]
- Adaptability: Hera successfully transitioned from Queen of one of Earth's dominant Pantheons to a politically vulnerable refugee and adapted her behavior sufficiently to secure a place under a new and increasingly powerful faction.

#### Combat Limitations
Hera is an ancient Major Goddess, but she is not a dedicated Combat God. Her principal Divinity is Marriage, and the confrontation with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s Countesses explicitly demonstrated how poorly her specialization translated into direct wartime performance. Her Divine age, political rank and accumulated Energy should therefore not be used to automatically scale her to Gods whose Concepts, bodies or lives are centered upon battle.[2]
Against Natashia, Hera was unable to prevent the Vampire Countess from reaching her neck with a weapon before she could meaningfully react. Her successful attack against [[Zeus]] occurred under completely different circumstances and depended upon centuries of marital familiarity causing Zeus to lower his defenses around her.[2][4]
No confirmed scene establishes Hera possessing unrestricted control over every marriage, woman or childbirth in existence simply because those subjects fall beneath her domains. Likewise, no destructive property such as Death, Soul Severing or Harvest Manipulation should be attributed to her scythe solely because of the weapon's shape.

### Relationships

#### Zeus
[[Zeus]] was Hera's brother, husband and the King beside whom she ruled [[Olympus]] for ages. Their marriage became one of the central contradictions of Hera's existence. Hera represented Marriage, Fidelity and Faithfulness, while Zeus repeatedly betrayed the relationship through countless affairs.
Hera accumulated immense resentment over those betrayals and became infamous for her jealousy and desire for retaliation. Nevertheless, she remained with Zeus for an enormous period. Her Marriage Divinity made ordinary separation fundamentally difficult for someone whose identity embodied the institution itself, but Hera later admitted that this was not the entire explanation. She also valued being Queen and did not want to surrender the extraordinary political authority and privilege accompanying the position.[5][4]
Their already toxic relationship finally became untenable when Zeus' mental and emotional instability escalated after the conflict involving the [[Titans]]. His behavior became sufficiently dangerous that Hera no longer believed her position as Queen protected her from him.[8][3]
When Hera sought sanctuary from [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction, her continued position as Zeus' wife became one of the principal obstacles preventing the others from trusting her. Hera consequently chose to make reconciliation impossible. She approached Zeus while his guard was lowered, manifested [[Hephaestus]]' scythe, castrated him, took physical evidence of the attack and told him to find another Queen before escaping Olympus.[4]
The humiliation remained politically significant during Zeus' final conflict with Victor. Victor later invoked Hera's defection while taunting Zeus over everything that had been taken away from him, demonstrating how thoroughly the loss of his own Queen had become a symbol of his collapsing rule.[9]
Their final relationship was therefore that of former spouses and enemies. Hera's betrayal ended her political identification with Zeus long before his eventual death destroyed the old Olympian order completely.

#### Hestia Elderblood
[[Hestia]] Elderblood is Hera's sister and one of the relatives who continued caring about her despite Hera's difficult personality. During the confrontation between the Olympian Goddesses and [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s Countesses, Hestia intervened before Hera's arrogance provoked a battle she was poorly equipped to survive.[2]
Hera attempted to convince Hestia to take her away from [[Olympus]], relying upon her sister's compassionate disposition. The women surrounding Hestia recognized the self-serving nature of the request, but Hestia nevertheless remained concerned about Hera's future.[2]
Hestia also understood Hera well enough to predict the character of her eventual betrayal. When Hera disappeared to prove her treason against [[Zeus]], Hestia expected her impulsive, arrogant sister to do something sufficiently extreme that Zeus would never want to see her again. Hera's attack validated that assessment.[4]
After Hera was granted asylum, Hestia and [[Rhea]] were assigned responsibility for keeping the former Queen close and helping supervise her place within Victor's faction.[1]

#### Rhea
[[Rhea]] is Hera's mother and one of the surviving senior members of the Greek Divine family. Once Hera's defection was accepted, [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s wives placed her under the care and observation of Rhea and [[Hestia]] rather than allowing a politically ambitious former Queen unrestricted access to Victor's inner circle.[1]
Rhea's presence provided Hera with a familial connection inside her new environment while simultaneously giving Victor's faction a trusted ancient Goddess capable of helping control any political problems Hera might create.

#### Nyx Elderblood
Nyx was Hera's benefactor, manipulator, employer and temporary political partner during Hera's escape from [[Zeus]]. Hera required someone powerful enough to move freely around [[Olympus]] and communicate with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction without immediately falling under Zeus' control, while Nyx recognized that the Queen could become an extremely valuable piece in the approaching collapse of the [[Greek Pantheon]].
Nyx did not provide that assistance freely. Across several months she assigned Hera increasingly dark tasks involving killing, torture, lying and other acts that pushed the former Queen far outside the comparatively insulated role she had occupied in Olympus. By the time Hera finally sought asylum, Nyx herself wondered whether those experiences had changed Hera too severely.[4]
Nyx subsequently acted as the principal intermediary during Hera's negotiations with Victor's faction and attempted to package Hera's asylum inside a much larger bargain involving the treasures of Olympus. When Hera decided that only an unmistakable act of treason could resolve the problem, Nyx transported her back to Olympus and later extracted her after the attack against Zeus.[4]
Their relationship should therefore not be mistaken for simple friendship. Nyx helped Hera because Hera was useful to her plans, while Hera tolerated Nyx's manipulation because Nyx provided something she desperately needed: a viable path out of Zeus' control.

#### Hephaestus
[[Hephaestus]] is one of Hera's sons and the creator of her golden Divine scythe. Hera considered his craftsmanship exceptional and trusted one of his weapons during the most consequential act of her defection from [[Olympus]].[4]
Her respect for his abilities does not translate into consistently kind treatment. Even while admiring the perfection of his work, Hera was capable of insulting his appearance, demonstrating the vanity and cruelty that frequently complicate her relationships with members of her own family.[4]

#### Victor Elderblood
[[Victor Elderblood]] is Hera's current superior and the ruler whose faction granted her sanctuary after she betrayed [[Zeus]]. Their relationship should not be categorized as romantic.
Hera approached Victor's organization because very few supernatural powers possessed enough strength and political independence to protect Zeus' fugitive Queen. Victor himself did not need to personally manage the entire negotiation. By this period, his wives increasingly functioned as a ruling council capable of evaluating potential allies, refugees and political assets on behalf of their family.[1]
Ruby and the others ultimately concluded that Hera's knowledge of [[Olympus]], familiarity with Zeus and symbolic importance outweighed the risks involved in sheltering her. Nevertheless, their acceptance came with strict limitations, including an explicit instruction that Hera remain far away from Victor. His wives had no interest in allowing the former Queen to use proximity to their husband as a route toward recovering the prestige she had lost by abandoning Olympus.[1]
Victor later described Hera's defection as having effectively taken Zeus' wife away from him while taunting the Olympian King.[9] This was a political humiliation directed toward Zeus, not evidence that Hera had become Victor's spouse.
No confirmed material establishes Hera as one of Victor's wives, lovers, [[True Dragons]] or members of Clan Elderblood.

#### Aphrodite Elderblood
[[Aphrodite]] Elderblood was among the women involved in evaluating Hera's attempt to enter [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s sphere. Hera's former title did not automatically command the deference it once had inside [[Olympus]]; she was instead required to demonstrate why protecting her would benefit the faction.[3][1]
Much later, Aphrodite still associated Hera strongly with the institution of marriage. When Victor's Empire discussed population policies that could encourage large and plural families, Aphrodite immediately predicted that Hera would deeply dislike the arrangement because of her identity as the Goddess of Marriage.[10]

#### Athena and Artemis
[[Athena]] and [[Artemis]] accompanied Hera during the crisis involving the Amazons. The three initially arrived as Olympian Goddesses responding to the Amazons' ancient relationship with [[Olympus]] before Nyx prevented them from interfering prematurely.[6][7]
Their association during this period reflected the old structure of Olympus, where Hera still acted as Queen and could involve other prominent Goddesses in matters affecting the Pantheon. That structure eventually disintegrated as [[Zeus]]' rule collapsed and the individual Greek Goddesses chose different futures beneath the emerging order surrounding [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]].
