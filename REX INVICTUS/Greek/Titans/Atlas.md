**[[Atlas]]** is the [[Titan]] general, son of [[Iapetus]] and [[Clymene]], condemned to hold up the sky. He is the father of the [[Hesperide|Hesperides]], including [[Zoe Nightshade]], and leads [[Kronos]]' forces from [[Mt. Othrys]].

## Greek History (IRL)

Atlas led the Titans in the war against [[Zeus]] and was punished by having to hold the heavens on his shoulders at the western edge of the world. Heracles took the weight while Atlas fetched the golden apples, then tricked him into taking it back. Perseus turned him to stone with [[Medusa]]'s head, forming the Atlas Mountains. Sources: Hesiod, Pseudo-Apollodorus, Ovid.

## PJO Canon

In [[The Titan's Curse]] Atlas tricks [[Artemis]] into holding the sky, wounds [[Zoe Nightshade|Zoë]] fatally, and is forced back under the sky by [[Percy Jackson|Percy]] and Artemis. In later books he remains trapped.

Chapter 623
Atlas is an ancient God, a Titan of the [[Greek Pantheon]] and one of the surviving Second-Generation [[Primordial Gods]] belonging to Kronus' faction. Unlike the ordinary Olympian generation, Atlas belongs to the older Titan lineage born from the original Greek Primordial Gods [[Gaia]] and [[Ouranos]]. The same generation includes powerful Divine beings such as Kronus and [[Helios]], placing Atlas far above ordinary Gods by natural Divine classification.[1][2]
Atlas was one of Kronus' most important supporters during the renewed Greek civil war and possessed enough authority among the [[Titans]] to function as one of their principal commanders. He displayed an extreme hatred toward [[Zeus]] and the Olympian order, openly celebrating the possibility of Zeus' reign collapsing and expressing a desire not merely to kill the Olympians but to personally torture and humiliate them in retaliation for the Titans' past suffering.[1]
During the final war in the [[Greek Underworld]], Atlas survived [[Victor Elderblood]]'s initial massacre despite losing an arm and suffering severe injuries. He personally delivered the report that more than seventy percent of Kronus' forces had already been killed and that even the surviving elites were injured.[3] After Victor killed both Zeus and Kronus, Atlas remained active and, together with [[Ares]], was immediately recognized as one of the natural leaders capable of taking command of the surviving Greek forces.[2]
The novel explicitly classifies Atlas as a Second-Generation Primordial God, placing him within the same broad Divine generation as Kronus, Helios, [[Thanatos]] and [[Hypnos]].[2][4] His exact Divinities, however, have not yet been directly identified in the story. Traditional associations with Strength, Endurance, the Heavens, Astronomy or supporting the world should therefore not automatically be treated as confirmed MTW powers.

### Physical Appearance
Atlas has no complete canonical physical description presently available. His hair color, eye color, height, clothing and normal Divine appearance have not been described in sufficient detail to establish a definitive design.
His clearest physical condition is shown during the final Greek conflict. After [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s opening assault devastated the combined armies of Kronus and [[Zeus]], Atlas had lost one of his arms and his remaining body was described as severely injured.[3]
Despite those injuries, Atlas remained conscious, capable of communicating with Kronus and sufficiently functional to continue serving as a senior commander. By Chapter 799, he remained present among the surviving Titan and Olympian forces and was still considered capable of taking command.[2]
The image used on this page is therefore an artistic interpretation rather than a confirmed canonical depiction.

### Personality
Atlas is one of the more openly vindictive [[Titans]] shown in the series. His first substantial scene establishes that he harbored enormous resentment toward [[Zeus]] and the Olympian Gods for what had happened to the Titans.
When [[Typhon]]'s emergence threatened Zeus' reign, Atlas responded with celebration rather than fear. He openly called Zeus his foolish nephew and expressed delight at the possibility of watching the Olympian King's rule collapse.[1]
His hatred went considerably further than wanting Zeus dead. Atlas specifically did not want Zeus to receive a quick death. He wanted the opportunity to torture him through every method available and reduce Zeus and the other Olympians until their former status as Gods became meaningless. Even the possibility of inflicting lesser humiliation upon figures connected to Zeus, such as [[Poseidon]], provided Atlas with some satisfaction.[1]
This hatred did not make Atlas completely irrational. When circumstances threatened to deny him the personal revenge he wanted, he acknowledged that throwing a tantrum would accomplish nothing and redirected his hatred toward other members of Zeus' faction. He could therefore restrain immediate emotional impulses when the strategic situation demanded it.[1]
Atlas is also a competent military leader. Following the deaths of Zeus and Kronus, the remaining Greek army had abruptly lost both of its principal commanders. Nevertheless, Atlas and [[Ares]] were described as natural leaders, allowing the surviving forces to reorganize without immediately collapsing into disorder.[2]
His actions during the battlefield disaster also show discipline beneath his brutality. Despite losing an arm and sustaining severe injuries, Atlas remained focused enough to accurately evaluate Kronus' army, report its casualties and continue functioning within the command structure.[3]

### Background

#### Titan Lineage
Atlas belongs to the ancient Titan generation of the [[Greek Pantheon]]. The story identifies the [[Titans]] confronting [[Persephone]]'s forces as children born from [[Gaia]] and [[Ouranos]], placing them within the Second Generation of Greek [[Primordial Gods]].[1]
This differs from some traditional mythological genealogies associated with Atlas. Within the novel's own Divine-generation framework, Titans such as Atlas are directly grouped among the descendants of the original Greek Primordials rather than requiring the complete external mythological genealogy to be imported into the setting.
Atlas' own dialogue supports this relationship. He refers to [[Zeus]] as his nephew, placing himself within the Titan generation immediately preceding the Olympian royal family.[1]
The hierarchy can therefore be summarized as:
GAIA + OURANOS
Original Greek Primordial Gods
↓
TITAN GENERATION
Kronus • [[Rhea]] • Atlas • [[Helios]] • others
↓
OLYMPIAN GENERATION
Zeus • [[Hades]] • [[Poseidon]] • [[Hera]] • [[Hestia]] • [[Demeter]]
The complete list of Atlas' siblings has not been individually established through direct story material and should not be reconstructed solely from external mythology.

#### The Titanomachy and Imprisonment
Atlas' extreme hatred toward [[Zeus]] and the later Olympians is connected to the ancient conflict in which the Titan order was defeated and Kronus lost control of the [[Greek Pantheon]].
The story makes clear that the returning [[Titans]] carried ancient grudges against Zeus and his Divine order. By the modern era, Kronus and his followers had re-emerged as one of the primary factions participating in the Greek civil war.[1]
The old version of this article stated that Zeus personally imprisoned Atlas within a "Pillar of the World" following the [[Titanomachy]]. That specific punishment has not been sufficiently established in the currently indexed novel material and is therefore not treated as confirmed canon here.
Likewise, the traditional mythological image of Atlas physically carrying the heavens or the world should not be converted into an MTW feat unless the novel explicitly confirms it.

### Synopsis

#### Demonic War
Atlas is first directly relevant during the instability consuming the [[Greek Pantheon]]. [[Zeus]]' authority had deteriorated into an escalating civil war, [[Persephone]] controlled the [[Greek Underworld]], Kronus and the [[Titans]] had re-emerged, and [[Gaia]] and [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]] had unleashed [[Typhon]] against [[Olympus]].[1]
When Typhon's roar reached Mount Olympus, the conflict temporarily changed character. Gods who had been fighting one another were suddenly forced to consider the possibility that the Beast of the Apocalypse could destroy the entire existing political order.
Zeus gathered his Gods for an emergency response while the Titans under Kronus reacted very differently. They celebrated, believing that Zeus' reign might finally collapse.[1]
Atlas was the most openly enthusiastic among them. With a savage expression, he celebrated the idea of watching his nephew's rule fall and revealed the depth of his hatred toward Zeus. Atlas did not merely want Zeus dead; he wanted an opportunity to torture him personally. His resentment extended to Zeus' allies and relatives, whom Atlas intended to break physically and psychologically in retaliation for what the Titans had endured.[1]
Despite the celebration, Kronus' faction remained aware that Typhon was dangerous to everyone. Gaia's hatred was not limited exclusively to Zeus, and the Titans understood that she could eventually turn against Kronus as well. Their preparations therefore included contingencies for what would happen after Zeus' possible death, including attempting to contain Typhon or abandoning Olympus if control of the Beast proved impossible.[1]
Atlas consequently belonged not merely to a group seeking revenge but to a Titan faction actively considering how to survive and reclaim a position after the collapse of Zeus' rule.
The Titans also represented a serious enough military threat that even powerful Gods treated them with caution. Persephone's side specifically viewed Kronus, Atlas and the unknown forces supporting them as threats that could overwhelm even Primordial-class defenders when acting together.[1]

#### King Among Kings
The Greek conflict eventually escalated into a final confrontation in the [[Greek Underworld]]. Kronus and [[Zeus]], despite their ancient hatred for one another, temporarily cooperated because they considered [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]], [[Persephone]] and the changing balance of the Pantheon an even greater threat.
Atlas entered this confrontation as part of Kronus' Titan forces.
Victor's intervention immediately destroyed the strategic assumptions upon which the combined Greek armies depended. Instead of simply defeating individual Gods, Victor used his authority over Souls and his rapidly developing powers to permanently kill Divine opponents and incorporate the dead into his own Immortal Army. Numerical superiority therefore became increasingly disastrous: every God or Titan who died simultaneously weakened the Greek coalition and strengthened Victor's side.
The combined armies suffered catastrophic casualties.
By the time Kronus requested a report, Atlas himself had already lost one arm and sustained severe injuries throughout his body. Despite his condition, he remained with the command group and delivered the battlefield assessment:
More than 70% of Kronus' forces had been killed, and nearly everyone remaining belonged to the elite forces while also being injured.[3]
Zeus' army was in even worse condition, having lost more than ninety percent of its personnel. Figures such as [[Helios]] and [[Ares]] had likewise been weakened, while the defection of the Greek healing and medical Goddesses left the surviving army without the support normally required to rapidly treat Divine injuries.[3]
Atlas' survival in this state demonstrates the resilience expected from a Titan of Primordial classification. Losing an entire arm and sustaining extensive bodily trauma did not remove him from battlefield command.
Victor eventually killed both Zeus and Kronus. Kronus' heart was removed and devoured while his body was consumed by Victor's Sea of Blood, after which the dead Titan King was brought back as one of Victor's Immortal Soldiers.[5]
The loss of Kronus left Atlas' faction without its original sovereign.
When the surviving armies regrouped, Atlas was still alive. [[Olympus]]' remaining side consisted of wounded [[Titans]]—including Atlas and Helios—alongside Olympians such as [[Apollo]], [[Athena]], [[Artemis]], Ares, [[Dionysus]] and [[Poseidon]]. The narration explicitly classified Atlas and Helios as Second-Generation [[Primordial Gods]].[2]
Although both Zeus and Kronus were dead, the remaining forces did not immediately collapse because Atlas and Ares were natural leaders. The two assumed battlefield command and maintained enough organization for the remaining Greek forces to continue confronting Victor, Persephone and the other Primordial Gods gathering around the battlefield.[2]
Atlas' actions after this point are not individually detailed in the currently available material. His last clearly confirmed status is alive and commanding surviving forces during Chapter 799.

### Skills & Abilities
Atlas' exact Divinities have not been revealed directly in the novel. His confirmed abilities should therefore be based upon demonstrated feats and his explicit classification rather than traditional mythology.

#### Second-Generation Primordial God Physiology
- God Physiology: Atlas is a genuine Divine Being belonging to the [[Greek Pantheon]].
- Titan Physiology: Atlas belongs to the ancient Titan lineage descending from the original Greek Primordial generation.[1]
- Second-Generation Primordial God Physiology: Atlas is directly classified as a Second-Generation Primordial God alongside [[Titans]] such as [[Helios]].[2]
- [[Divine Energy]]: As a Primordial-class God, Atlas possesses Divine Energy vastly exceeding that of ordinary Mortal races. The exact quantity of his Energy has never been measured.
- Divine Soul: Atlas possesses the powerful Soul structure expected of an ancient Primordial God. The story has not demonstrated any unique Soul technique belonging to him.
- Agelessness: Atlas has existed since the ancient era of the Greek Gods and is unaffected by ordinary Mortal aging.
- Supernatural Physical Characteristics: As a Titan and Primordial God, Atlas possesses superhuman strength, speed, stamina and durability vastly beyond ordinary supernatural beings.
- Enhanced Durability: Atlas survived [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s devastating opening assault despite losing an arm and sustaining severe injuries across his body, remaining conscious and operational enough to continue serving in the command structure.[3]
- Enhanced Endurance: Even while heavily wounded and missing an arm, Atlas remained mentally composed enough to monitor military casualties, report directly to Kronus and later remain present among the surviving Greek leadership.[3][2]
- Divine Regeneration (General God trait; extent unknown): Gods possess supernatural recovery, but Atlas' injuries during the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] battle remained serious enough to affect him because the Greek forces had lost access to their healing specialists.[3]

#### Combat and Military Skills
- Military Leadership: Atlas is an experienced battlefield commander within Kronus' faction and was responsible for monitoring the condition of the Titan forces during the war.[3]
- Command Presence: Following the simultaneous loss of [[Zeus]] and Kronus, Atlas and [[Ares]] were immediately capable of taking command of the surviving coalition without major organizational collapse.[2]
- Strategic Awareness: Atlas remained capable of evaluating the state of an army even while personally injured, accurately identifying that more than seventy percent of Kronus' forces had been killed and that the surviving personnel consisted primarily of wounded elites.[3]
- Pain Tolerance: Losing an arm and suffering extensive injuries did not incapacitate Atlas or prevent him from performing command duties.[3]
- Ancient Combat Experience: Atlas belongs to the Titan generation and had participated in the ancient conflicts that produced his hatred of the Olympians. His precise personal combat history has not been described in detail.

#### Unknown Divinities
The older version of this page attributed several specific Divine domains to Atlas:
- Strength;
- Force;
- Endurance;
- the Cosmos;
- the Heavens;
- Astronomy.
These titles correspond heavily with external mythological traditions surrounding Atlas, but the currently indexed MTW chapters do not directly identify his principal Divinity.
Atlas should therefore not currently receive powers such as absolute Strength Manipulation, Force Manipulation, Cosmic Manipulation, Astronomy Manipulation or Heaven Manipulation without a future chapter establishing those Concepts.
His extraordinary physical resilience is a demonstrated feat, but that alone does not prove that Endurance is one of his formal Divine Concepts.

### Power
Atlas belongs to the Second-Generation Primordial God class.[2]
The author's Divine ranking places the Titan descendants of the original Primordials within the broad Primordial God range while emphasizing that this classification does not make every Primordial equally powerful. Original Gods such as [[Gaia]], Nyx, [[Erebus]] and [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]] possess much greater natural foundations than ordinary descendants, while individual Second-Generation Gods can still vary dramatically depending upon their Divinities and personal development.[4]
Atlas is nevertheless treated as a major combatant even among the [[Titans]]. [[Persephone]]'s perspective places Kronus, Atlas and their accompanying Primordial forces beyond what [[Thanatos]] could reasonably confront alone, despite Thanatos himself being an extremely powerful Second-Generation Primordial God associated with Death.[1]
During the final conflict, Atlas survived an assault that annihilated the majority of both Kronus' and [[Zeus]]' armies. More than seventy percent of the Titan forces died, yet Atlas remained alive despite losing an arm and sustaining heavy damage.[3]
His survival should not be interpreted as proof that he was unharmed by [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]. Atlas was explicitly badly injured. The feat instead demonstrates that his Primordial Titan physiology was sufficient to prevent immediately fatal collapse under conditions that killed most of the surrounding Divine army.

### Relationships

#### Kronus
Kronus was Atlas' fellow Titan, brother within the Titan generation and sovereign during the renewed Greek civil war.
Atlas remained loyal to Kronus throughout the Titan faction's campaign against [[Zeus]]. He operated high enough within the army to report casualty figures directly to Kronus and continued fighting even after the majority of their forces had been destroyed.[3]
The death of Kronus removed Atlas' principal leader. Contrary to the old stub, the story does not present Atlas simply standing aside afterward. He immediately became one of the figures responsible for continuing to lead the surviving Greek forces.[2]

#### Zeus
[[Zeus]] was Atlas' nephew and the individual toward whom Atlas displayed his strongest known hatred.
Atlas openly celebrated the prospect of Zeus losing [[Olympus]] and wanted to personally torture him rather than allow him a quick death. The Titan's resentment was sufficiently extreme that merely killing Zeus would not have satisfied him.[1]
His hatred also extended toward the Gods surrounding Zeus. Atlas imagined breaking the Olympians physically and mentally and was willing to redirect his revenge toward Zeus' associates if [[Typhon]] or another force denied him the opportunity to torture Zeus personally.[1]
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] ultimately killed Zeus before Atlas could achieve the revenge he wanted.

#### Ares
[[Ares]] fought on the Olympian side of the final Greek conflict, making him politically separate from Atlas during much of the war.
After the deaths of [[Zeus]] and Kronus, however, survival became more important than the previous factional division. Atlas and Ares were both described as natural leaders and jointly became the principal figures capable of maintaining command among the surviving wounded Greek forces.[2]
Their cooperation demonstrates how thoroughly [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s intervention had changed the war. Titan and Olympian leadership that had previously been attempting to defeat one another now had to coordinate simply to prevent the complete destruction of what remained.

#### Helios
[[Helios]] was another Titan fighting among the surviving Greek forces and is directly classified alongside Atlas as a Second-Generation Primordial God.[2]
Both survived [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s early attack but were injured during the wider battle. The exact personal relationship between Atlas and Helios has not been explored beyond their shared Titan lineage and allegiance within the Greek conflict.

#### Gaia Elderblood
[[Gaia]] belongs to the original Greek Primordial generation and is identified as the mother of the Titan generation involved in the conflict.[1]
Atlas' personal relationship with Gaia has not been directly explored. [[Titans|The Titans]] were aware that Gaia's vengeance was dangerous even to Kronus' faction, and they understood that her hostility toward [[Zeus]] did not guarantee permanent loyalty to them.[1]

#### Ouranos
[[Ouranos]] belongs to the original Greek Primordial generation and is identified alongside [[Gaia]] as the progenitor of the Titan generation.[1]
No direct interaction between Atlas and Ouranos has been shown.

#### Victor Elderblood
[[Victor Elderblood]] became the greatest immediate threat Atlas faced during the final Greek war.
Victor's attack destroyed more than seventy percent of Kronus' army, severed one of Atlas' arms and left the Titan severely wounded.[3] Victor then killed both [[Zeus]] and Kronus, leaving Atlas and [[Ares]] to reorganize the survivors.[5][2]
Atlas has not received an individual duel against Victor. His injuries were suffered as part of Victor's larger assault upon the combined Greek forces rather than through a separately narrated one-on-one battle.

### Current Status
Atlas' last clearly confirmed appearance occurs during Chapter 799.
At that point:
- he is alive;
- he has lost an arm;
- he is heavily injured;
- Kronus and [[Zeus]] are both dead;
- most of the Greek armies have been destroyed;
- Atlas remains among the surviving Second-Generation Primordial [[Titans]];
- and he is functioning as one of the principal commanders alongside [[Ares]].[2]
No later chapter currently indexed in the project explicitly confirms Atlas' death. His status should therefore remain Alive unless later material establishes otherwise.
