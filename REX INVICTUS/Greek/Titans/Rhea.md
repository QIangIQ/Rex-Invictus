Rhea, also known as Ops, is an ancient Titaness, a Second Generation Primordial Goddess, the daughter of [[Gaia]] and [[Ouranos]], and the former Queen of [[Olympus|Mount Olympus]]. She was the wife of Kronus and is the mother of [[Hestia]], [[Hades]], [[Hera]], [[Poseidon]], [[Demeter]] and [[Zeus]], making her the matriarch of the principal generation of Olympian Gods.
Known throughout the Greek Divine hierarchy as the Mother Goddess, Rhea is one of the ancient Titan Gods possessing a fully developed Major Divinity in a Concept. When [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] first examined the large group of Goddesses evacuated from Olympus, Rhea was counted among the comparatively small number possessing a true Major Concept rather than an incomplete or Minor Divinity.[1]
Despite her enormous age and former status as Queen of Olympus, Rhea is not portrayed primarily as a warrior. Her most important strengths are her ancient Divine nature, experience, knowledge of Pantheon affairs, maternal authority and exceptional political experience. These qualities eventually led to her becoming the formal representative of the Goddess Faction within Victor's Faction, while [[Aphrodite]] Elderblood, Hestia Elderblood and later [[Metis]] Elderblood remained its principal internal leaders.[2][3]
Following the collapse of Olympus, Rhea abandoned the old Greek order entirely and became part of Victor's growing faction and later the [[Dragons Nest Pantheon]].

### Physical Appearance
Rhea is a mature and extraordinarily beautiful woman with long golden hair and violet eyes. Her appearance carries the mature dignity expected of an ancient Mother Goddess and former [[Hera|Queen of Olympus]]. When she comforted Nike shortly after joining [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction, she was specifically described as a mature woman with long golden hair and violet eyes.[4]
Her general demeanor further reinforces this image. Rhea normally carries a gentle expression and maternal atmosphere, speaking to younger Goddesses with a calm and comforting tone rather than displaying the overwhelming arrogance common among many ancient Gods.[4]
Victor mentally classified her as the "strongest Mommy" when the Olympian Goddesses were first introduced to him, reflecting both her mature appearance and her status as the Mother Goddess among the group.[1]

### Personality
Rhea is a gentle, maternal, experienced and comparatively level-headed Goddess. Unlike many members of the old [[Greek Pantheon]], she possesses enough self-awareness to acknowledge her own failures and does not attempt to excuse the damage caused by her family's long history of internal conflict.
Her maternal nature is genuine rather than merely ceremonial. She naturally comforts the people around her, speaks in a soft and reassuring manner and frequently takes on the role of mediator among the former Greek Goddesses. When Nike became apprehensive about [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] forcing her to train, Rhea calmly placed a hand on her shoulder and advised her to simply accept the inevitable, speaking with what the narration explicitly described as a gentle, motherly tone.[4]
At the same time, Rhea is not naïve. Thousands upon thousands of years surrounded by the Greek Gods left her with a highly realistic understanding of their flaws. After abandoning [[Olympus]], she agreed that bringing large numbers of male Greek Gods into Victor's faction would likely have created unnecessary internal conflicts, noting that the Goddesses were easier for herself, [[Hestia]] and [[Aphrodite]] to manage.[5]
Her greatest source of regret is her family. Rhea openly admitted to Hestia that she had not been a good mother and disliked being addressed distantly as "Lady Rhea" by her own daughter. Instead of defending herself, she asked Hestia to call her Mother again and embraced her when she finally did.[6]
Her guilt concerning [[Demeter]] is even deeper. Rhea apologized directly for failing to protect Demeter from the abuse inflicted upon her by members of their family. Demeter did not pretend that an apology could erase the past, but nevertheless accepted Rhea's concern and acknowledged that Rhea herself had suffered enormously during the repeated Greek civil wars. The resulting reconciliation ended with Rhea, Hestia and Demeter silently embracing one another.[6]
Rhea also possesses a surprisingly sentimental outlook concerning love. After witnessing the effects produced by Victor and Aphrodite's relationship, she recalled an ancient couple from the [[Egyptian Pantheon]] whose unconditional love endured until their eventual destruction. Rhea openly contrasted Victor with Kronus, stating that Victor was more genuine and that his ability to continue loving others unconditionally was something extraordinarily rare even among immortal Gods.[6]
This caused Rhea to openly admit that she envied Aphrodite for finding someone like Victor.[6] Her admiration should not automatically be interpreted as a confirmed romantic relationship; however, Rhea clearly considers Victor profoundly different from the men who dominated her former Pantheon.
Rhea is also highly pragmatic and politically experienced. Her former position as the First [[Hera|Queen of Olympus]] made her one of the faction's most useful sources of information concerning Divine societies, religious development, Pantheon politics and the behavior of ancient Gods. [[Jeanne Elderblood]] specifically recommended summoning Rhea alongside Aphrodite when Victor encountered an unusual religious situation because Rhea was both a Mother Goddess and a former Queen with substantial experience dealing with those matters.[7]

### Synopsis

#### Demonic War
Rhea became relevant during the chaos surrounding the release of [[Typhon]]. As [[Olympus|Mount Olympus]] descended into panic, [[Aphrodite]] attempted to convince several Goddesses to abandon the increasingly unstable [[Greek Pantheon]] rather than remain trapped between the civil war and an Apocalypse Beast.
[[Hestia]] initially refused to abandon Olympus because she still considered its inhabitants her family. Aphrodite harshly challenged this attachment, pointing out that most of Hestia's siblings had neglected her for thousands of years and explicitly stating that Rhea was one of the only members of the family who still regularly visited Hestia. Aphrodite ultimately admitted that Hestia, Rhea and Nike were the three Goddesses she personally cared enough about to rescue regardless of their strategic usefulness.[8]
When Typhon's roar finally spread across Olympus and made the danger impossible to ignore, the evacuation accelerated. Aphrodite, Hestia, [[Demeter]], Nike and Rhea gathered dozens of Lesser and Major Goddesses, emptied the important Divine resources and treasures they could access from Olympus' vaults and fled the Pantheon before the coming disaster. Their disappearance became such a severe political blow that [[Zeus]] and the remaining Gods realized not only that dozens of Goddesses had abandoned them, but that important Concepts such as Victory, [[Rex Invictus|Home]], Agriculture, Healing and other essential Divine functions were no longer supporting Olympus.[9]
Rhea formally appeared in [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s territory shortly afterward among the approximately fifty Greek Goddesses brought by Aphrodite. Victor identified her as the Mother Goddess, an ancient Titan and one of the comparatively few refugees possessing a genuine Major Divinity in a Concept.[1]
Rhea quickly became one of the senior members of the refugee group. Although Aphrodite represented the Goddesses politically and Hestia acted as their primary leader, the acceptance of this arrangement by Rhea substantially influenced the remaining Goddesses. As the ancient Mother Goddess and First [[Hera|Queen of Olympus]], her approval gave the developing leadership structure legitimacy among women who had spent millennia beneath the Greek hierarchy.[10]
Rhea also displayed immediate interest in Victor himself. Upon observing him closely, she noticed the overwhelming vitality of Nature surrounding his body and correctly realized that Victor possessed a connection with a World Tree. This discovery caused her curiosity toward the man who had somehow become Aphrodite's husband to increase considerably.[10]
Unlike Hestia, Nike, Aphrodite and [[Thetis]], Rhea was not identified as one of the group's principal military assets. Her value lay elsewhere: she was the Mother Goddess, an ancient Titan, the former Queen of Olympus and a source of political and Divine experience that the faction previously lacked.[10]
Ruby additionally mentioned ancient rumors claiming that sufficiently powerful Mother Goddesses could potentially create Life in a manner comparable to Gods of Creation. She specifically raised Rhea as the relevant example, although she openly admitted that she did not know whether the rumor was true and stated that Rhea herself would need to confirm it. Victor similarly treated the possibility cautiously because true Life remained connected to the universal Primordials.[10]
As Rhea settled into her new environment, she became increasingly involved with Victor's household. During the enormous supernatural phenomenon produced by the deepening bond between Victor and Aphrodite, Rhea and Hestia observed how Aphrodite's Love Divinity affected the surrounding territory. Rhea explained that she had witnessed something similarly powerful only once before, involving a couple from the [[Egyptian Pantheon]] whose unconditional love lasted until their destruction.[6]
The event became personal when Rhea compared Victor with Kronus and openly stated that Victor was considerably more genuine. She envied Aphrodite for obtaining someone capable of maintaining unconditional love despite immortality, something Rhea considered extraordinarily rare even among Gods.[6]
The conversation also led to one of Rhea's most important family reconciliations. Hestia finally called her "Mother" again, and Rhea admitted that she had not been a good parent. Demeter subsequently joined them, prompting Rhea to apologize for failing to protect her daughter from the abuse within their family. Demeter acknowledged the apology while also recognizing that Rhea herself had been one of the greatest victims of Olympus' repeated civil wars. The three eventually remained together in a silent family embrace.[6]

#### King Among Kings
Rhea's experience as an ancient Queen became increasingly valuable as [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction evolved from a collection of allies into a genuine supernatural political power.
When Victor encountered an isolated population whose desperation and accumulated Faith appeared capable of generating new Gods, Jeanne immediately recommended bringing both [[Aphrodite]] and Rhea into the investigation. Jeanne specifically identified Rhea's qualifications: she was a Mother Goddess and former Queen, meaning she possessed extensive experience with the development of Gods, Pantheons and the political consequences surrounding Divine populations.[7]
Rhea arrived through Alexios' portal alongside Aphrodite and participated in examining the abnormal religious situation. While Aphrodite analyzed the identities and characteristics of the foreign Gods involved, Rhea joined Jeanne and Aphrodite in investigating the Divine phenomena affecting the population.[7]
As Victor's territory became increasingly organized, the various species and factions beneath him required representatives. To the surprise of many, Rhea was selected as the official representative of the Goddesses. Aphrodite and [[Hestia]] were the more obvious candidates, but both already possessed extensive responsibilities and declined the administrative position. Rhea's composure, age and former royal experience made her a natural alternative.[2]
Her position did not make her the absolute ruler of every Goddess beneath Victor. Rhea served as their formal representative within the faction's wider administrative system, while the internal leadership of the Goddesses remained centered upon Aphrodite and Hestia and later expanded to include [[Metis]].[2][3]
Rhea's role as senior maternal authority also resurfaced after [[Hera]] betrayed [[Zeus]] and sought refuge within Victor's faction. Once Hera was accepted, she was placed under the care of Hestia and Rhea. This created the unusual situation in which Victor's faction housed both the First and the later former Queens of [[Olympus]] while the original Pantheon continued collapsing.[11]
During the increasing integration of Greek Goddesses into Victor's territory, Rhea remained one of the principal women capable of controlling their behavior. When Victor's wives questioned why Nyx had continued transporting almost exclusively female Gods into the faction, Rhea pointed out that the male Gods of Olympus would have been considerably more troublesome. She believed that the Goddesses could at least be controlled through the authority and influence of herself and Aphrodite without creating the same level of violent internal conflict.[5]
Rhea also observed the increasingly exclusive relationship between Victor and Aphrodite with surprise. Having known Aphrodite's previous behavior for ages, Rhea considered it remarkable that someone had finally caused the Goddess of Love to lose interest in pursuing anyone else. She similarly recognized why Victor's wives considered themselves fortunate, noting that Victor's personality caused them to feel genuinely loved rather than treated merely as objects of pleasure.[5]
The eventual destruction of Olympus permanently ended Rhea's connection to the old political order. When Victor returned after subduing the [[Greek Pantheon]] and evolving into a Blood Dragon, Rhea immediately sensed familiar Divine traces within him and questioned whether what she was feeling belonged to Zeus and Kronus. Victor calmly confirmed that both had died and that he had absorbed their Powers.[12]
Rhea later questioned Victor when he declared that he intended to create an entirely new Pantheon. Drawing upon her experience with the original Greek Divine structure, she explained that creating a true Pantheon was historically an extraordinarily difficult feat: the Greek Pantheon itself had required the cooperation of the ancient Original [[Primordial Gods]], and a genuine Pantheon required its own Dimension rather than merely territory or worshippers.[12]
Victor responded that he now possessed precisely the people necessary to accomplish it, including [[Gaia]], Nyx and the other powerful beings gathered beneath him. Rhea's disbelief gradually gave way to acceptance as Victor proceeded with the project that would become Dragons Nest.[12]
By the later stages of Victor's factional development, Rhea remained the Goddess Faction's representative while Aphrodite, Hestia and Metis acted as its true principal leaders.[3]
She also continued serving as a source of ancient Divine knowledge. When Violet's developing powers allowed her to interact dangerously with the future, Rhea immediately supported the warning that manipulating Time was something even Gods avoided, describing temporal matters as belonging to the territory of primordial existences rather than something Gods should interfere with carelessly.[13]
Rhea additionally retained contacts and knowledge extending beyond Greece. During the later [[Supernatural Gathering]] she recognized [[Amaterasu]] personally and explained that the two had met in the past and were approximately the same age, further demonstrating the immense span of Rhea's existence and her familiarity with ancient Divine rulers outside Olympus.[14]
With Olympus destroyed and the Goddess refugees integrated into Victor's civilization, Rhea's identity changed from former Queen of Olympus to one of the senior Divine administrators and Mother Goddesses serving beneath the new order established by Victor Elderblood.

#### God Emperor Victor Elderblood
Long after the destruction of [[Olympus]] and Rhea's integration into [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction, she continued living among the Elderblood family and remained recognized through her ancient position as the Mother Goddess.
By Chapter 1006, Rhea had become sufficiently integrated into the Imperial Family's daily life that [[Jeanne Elderblood]] selected her as an assistant while educating Victor's daughters. Rhea sat beside Jeanne during the lesson and was introduced to the children specifically as the Mother Goddess, Rhea. She greeted the girls warmly before helping with a lesson concerning Divine reproduction and the differences between Gods and mortals.[15]
Her involvement is significant because it demonstrates that Rhea's position within Victor's civilization extended beyond serving as a political representative of the former Greek Goddesses. Her immense age, experience as a mother of an entire Divine generation and knowledge of Divine biology made her useful in educating the next generation of the Elderblood family.[15]
Rhea remained associated with motherhood and fertility during the later development of the [[Elderblood Empire]]. When the Imperial leadership discussed large-scale population growth, restoration of extinct races and policies intended to encourage larger families, [[Aphrodite]] Elderblood remarked that [[Hestia]] and Rhea would love the resulting family-oriented system. This demonstrates that even in the later Imperial era Rhea remained strongly identified with motherhood, fertility and the growth of families rather than becoming an inactive relic of the old [[Greek Pantheon]].

### Relationships

#### Kronus
Kronus was Rhea's brother and former husband. Together they became the parents of the generation that would eventually dominate [[Olympus]], including [[Hestia]], [[Hades]], [[Hera]], [[Poseidon]], [[Demeter]] and [[Zeus]].
By the time Rhea entered [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction, her relationship with Kronus had effectively collapsed. Her later comments concerning Victor strongly imply that her experience with Kronus had left her deeply cynical about eternal love. When discussing Victor's relationship with [[Aphrodite]], Rhea specifically contrasted Victor with Kronus and stated that Victor was more genuine.[6]
Following Victor's conquest of the [[Greek Pantheon]], Rhea sensed traces of Kronus' Power inside Victor. Victor informed her that Kronus had been killed and that his Power had been absorbed.[12]
Rhea displayed shock at the revelation but no attempt to turn against Victor afterward, remaining within his faction and continuing her administrative role.

#### Hestia Elderblood
[[Hestia]] is Rhea's eldest daughter and the child who remained closest to her following the collapse of their family.
[[Aphrodite]] explicitly stated that Rhea was one of the few family members who continued visiting Hestia even when the rest of Hestia's siblings had effectively abandoned regular contact with her.[8]
Despite this, their relationship was initially emotionally distant enough that Hestia addressed her as Lady Rhea rather than Mother. Rhea admitted that she had not been a good mother and openly asked Hestia to stop treating her so formally. When Hestia eventually called her Mother again, Rhea immediately embraced her.[6]
Their reconciliation continued after both joined [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction, where Rhea and Hestia became senior figures among the rescued Greek Goddesses.

#### Demeter
[[Demeter]] is another of Rhea's daughters. Their relationship was severely damaged by the corruption and abuse that dominated the old Olympian family.
Rhea eventually apologized directly for failing to protect Demeter from her brothers. Demeter responded that an apology could not change what had happened, but nevertheless thanked Rhea for finally acknowledging it. Demeter also apologized for failing to recognize how badly the repeated Greek civil wars had affected Rhea herself.[6]
The reconciliation ended with Demeter, [[Hestia]] and Rhea embracing one another, representing one of the few examples of the Olympian family genuinely attempting to heal old wounds rather than perpetuating them.[6]

#### Hera
[[Hera]] succeeded Rhea as Queen of Olympus in the later Olympian order.
Following Hera's betrayal of [[Zeus]] and flight from [[Olympus]], [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction debated whether she should be allowed to remain. Once Hera was accepted, she was specifically placed under the supervision and care of [[Hestia]] and Rhea.[11]
This left Rhea in the unusual position of helping manage the daughter who had succeeded her as Queen after both women ultimately abandoned the system they once ruled.

#### Aphrodite Elderblood
[[Aphrodite]] is one of the few Greek Goddesses Rhea maintained a genuinely close relationship with.
Aphrodite openly stated that Rhea, [[Hestia]] and Nike were among the very few Goddesses she personally wanted to rescue from [[Olympus]] even if they possessed no strategic usefulness.[8]
Rhea later supported the leadership arrangement established by Aphrodite and Hestia among the refugee Goddesses, and her approval helped convince the other women to accept it.[10]
Rhea also deeply admires Aphrodite's relationship with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]. After witnessing the unusual phenomenon produced by their love, Rhea openly stated that Aphrodite was fortunate and admitted that she envied her for finding a partner capable of genuine, unconditional devotion.[6]

#### Victor Elderblood
Rhea originally encountered [[Victor Elderblood]] as the strange Vampire Progenitor who had somehow become [[Aphrodite]]'s husband and given dozens of fleeing Greek Goddesses somewhere safe to live.
Her interest in him increased when she sensed the vitality of a World Tree surrounding him and realized that Victor's existence was already considerably more complicated than his species suggested.[10]
Over time, Rhea developed substantial respect for Victor. She considered him radically different from Kronus and other men she had known, particularly because of the sincerity with which he loved his family. Rhea even admitted that she envied Aphrodite for obtaining such a partner.[6]
Politically, Rhea eventually became one of Victor's senior subordinates. She represented the Goddess Faction within his growing organization and remained beneath his authority after [[Olympus]] was destroyed.[2]
Victor later killed Kronus and [[Zeus]] and absorbed their Powers. Although Rhea was shocked after sensing their traces within Victor, she did not rebel against him and instead remained part of the new Pantheon he created.[12]
Victor later married both Rhea's daughter [[Hestia]] Elderblood and her granddaughter [[Persephone Elderblood]], making him simultaneously Rhea's son-in-law and grandson-in-law.
Rhea has shown admiration for Victor and is categorized among his potential romantic interests, but no confirmed marriage, [[Soul Marriage]] or equivalent relationship between the two has presently occurred.

### Power and Status
Rhea's ancient lineage makes her one of the oldest surviving Gods associated with the former [[Greek Pantheon]]. As the daughter of [[Gaia]] and [[Ouranos]], she belongs to the generation immediately beneath the Original [[Primordial Gods]].
However, her classification as a Second Generation Primordial Goddess should not be interpreted as proof that she is automatically stronger than every younger God. The novel repeatedly establishes that Divine generation determines one's initial foundation and potential rather than functioning as an absolute final power ranking.
Rhea's most firmly established status is:
- direct daughter of Gaia Elderblood and Ouranos;
- ancient Titaness;
- Second Generation Primordial Goddess;
- former First [[Hera|Queen of Olympus]];
- Mother Goddess;
- holder of a fully developed Major Divinity;[1]
- senior member of the approximately fifty Greek Goddesses that joined [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]];[1]
- formal representative of the Goddess Faction;[2]
- member of Victor's Faction and later [[Dragons Nest Pantheon]].
Despite these credentials, Rhea is not portrayed as one of the principal frontline warriors among Victor's Greek Goddesses. Victor's discussion in Chapter 630 specifically identified [[Hestia]], Nike, [[Aphrodite]] and [[Thetis]] as the major military assets of that group.[10]
Rhea's influence is consequently much greater in leadership, Divine administration, ancient knowledge and maternal authority than in demonstrated direct combat.
