Chronos (not to be confused with the Greek titan [[Kronos]]/Cronus) is an Elder who is notable for his mastery over time magic. He is instrumental in the downfall of the [[Disir]], exchanging [[Zephaniah]]'s eyes for an ability to gaze through the threads of time. Zephaniah uses this ability to select and assemble a team of warriors that together brought down their empire in an event known as Ragnarok. Chronos' magic is also key to [[Marethyu]]'s ability to travel through time and in organising the fate of [[Danu Talis]].
Though Chronos does not personally appear in the series, he is discussed several times. He is noted by [[Prometheus]] as "puny," and physically repulsive ([[Tsagaglalal]] explaining that his flesh had been warped by the process of time-travelling; it may be partially caused by the Change). He was widely despised, though Marethyu and Abraham both counted him as a great friend and sought his assistance on numerous occasions. The network of 13 leygates Marethyu used to travel through time were created by Chronos. In [[The Enchantress]], Chronos appears in a flashback in which he shows an assortment of [[Elders]] a series of timelines to demonstrate the consequences of their actions.

Kronus was the ancient Titan of Time, King of the [[Titans]], former God-King of the [[Greek Pantheon]] and ruler of [[Olympus|Mount Olympus]] before the reign of his son [[Zeus]]. Born from [[Gaia]] Elderblood and [[Ouranos]], Kronus belonged to the second generation of the Greek Divine lineage and reached the state of a Second Generation Primordial God, placing his existence fundamentally above ordinary Gods and conventional Major Gods.[1]
His defining Divine Concept was Time. Kronus was not the universal Concept of Time itself, but his Divinity represented and anchored Time within the Greek Pantheon's Divine system to an extraordinary degree. His importance was demonstrated at the moment of his permanent death: when Victor tore out his heart, the normal progression of Time in Mount Olympus stopped, leaving the realm unable to properly move from its present toward a future until its Divine structure could be reorganized.[1]
Kronus had once ruled Olympus before being overthrown by his children during the ancient [[Titanomachy]]. The resulting hatred between Kronus and Zeus persisted for eons, while Gaia herself eventually regarded Kronus as one of the children who had betrayed her. Nevertheless, when the final crisis of the old Greek order made cooperation strategically useful, Kronus and Zeus suppressed their ancient hatred and temporarily joined forces in an attempt to seize the [[Greek Underworld]].[2][3]
The alliance ended catastrophically once Victor Elderblood entered the conflict. Victor neutralized Kronus' greatest advantage by shielding himself with concentrated Negative Energy, physically overwhelmed the Titan and tore out his heart. Victor then consumed the heart and acquired Kronus' accumulated memories and understanding of Time. Because most of Kronus' Soul remained sufficiently intact, Victor also converted his corpse into one of his Immortal Soldiers, reducing the former King of the Titans to a subordinate within the army of the man who killed him.[1]

### Physical Appearance
Kronus manifested as a humanoid male Titan, but the novel does not provide a sufficiently detailed description of his normal appearance to establish a canonical hair color, eye color, exact height, clothing or facial features. His direct appearances in the final Greek conflict focus almost entirely upon his Divine presence, Time authority and position as Titan King rather than describing his physical design.[3][1]
As a Second Generation Primordial God, Kronus possessed a Divine body of extraordinary durability. During his final battle [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] repeatedly struck his head with enough force to catastrophically destroy his skull, yet Kronus' body continued breathing even after he was effectively incapable of fighting. His physical survival at that stage was attributed to the extraordinary resistance inherent to his Primordial-level Divine existence rather than ordinary mortal durability.[1]
His post-death form is likewise not described in detail. Victor placed Kronus' remains into the Sea of Blood and returned him as an Immortal Soldier, but the chapter does not establish whether the reanimated Kronus visually differed from his original Divine form.[1]

### Personality
Kronus was intensely proud, arrogant, authoritative and conscious of his ancient status. Even after [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] had displayed Energy reserves comparable to the greatest Divine beings present in the [[Greek Underworld]], Kronus continued dismissing him as a Mortal and relied heavily upon the assumption that age, Divine classification and overwhelming numerical superiority would decide the battle.[4]
His arrogance was nevertheless tempered by considerably greater caution than that displayed by many Olympians. When [[Typhon]] appeared during the Greek civil war, several [[Titans]] celebrated because the Beast represented an opportunity for [[Zeus]] and [[Olympus]] to be destroyed. Kronus immediately recognized the flaw in that thinking. [[Gaia]] hated Zeus, but she also regarded Kronus as a traitor, meaning the destruction of Zeus would not guarantee the Titans' safety. Kronus therefore prepared for the possibility that his forces would eventually need either to contain Typhon themselves or abandon Olympus entirely.[5]
This caution originated partly from his complicated relationship with Gaia. [[Hestia]] Elderblood explained that Gaia considered herself betrayed first by Kronus and later by Zeus, placing both generations of rulers within the same repeating cycle of Greek familial betrayal.[2] Kronus understood that Gaia temporarily directing her hatred toward Zeus did not mean that she had forgiven him.
Kronus was also highly pragmatic. Few relationships illustrate this better than his alliance with Zeus. Father and son had spent eons defined by hatred, rebellion and competition for authority, yet both were willing to suppress that hostility once cooperation offered them a realistic path toward seizing the Greek Underworld and rebuilding the Pantheon beneath a new political arrangement.[3]
He was an ambitious ruler rather than a simple destroyer. During the final Greek conspiracy, Kronus' objective was not merely to kill Zeus or satisfy a historical grudge. He intended to regain political influence, preserve the Titans and participate in determining who would control the surviving Greek Divine territories after the conflict.[5][6]
Kronus' confidence eventually deteriorated into desperation as Victor dismantled the combined Greek army. Kronus' side lost more than seventy percent of its forces, while Zeus' losses exceeded ninety percent. The two ancient rulers had entered the battle believing numbers and Divine status made defeat practically impossible; within a short period, they were forced to watch their supposedly superior armies become permanent additions to Victor's own Immortal forces.[7][6]
His final mistake was the same conceptual pride Victor criticized in other ancient Gods: Kronus treated his relationship with Time as if controlling a fundamental Divinity made him indistinguishable from the universal Concept itself. Victor explicitly shattered that assumption during their battle, explaining that Kronus remained a Divine user and representative of Time rather than a true Primordial Entity completely merged with the Aspect of Creation.[1]

### Synopsis

#### Demonic War
Kronus was first discussed while [[Hestia]] Elderblood explained the increasingly unstable history of the [[Greek Pantheon]]. [[Gaia]]'s hostility was not restricted to [[Zeus]]. Hestia revealed that Gaia considered herself betrayed by Kronus before later suffering another betrayal through Zeus, creating a cycle in which each generation of rulers turned against the older powers that had helped establish them.[2]
By this period Kronus had returned to prominence as leader of the Titan faction opposing [[Olympus]]. The Greek Pantheon had already fragmented into competing groups when the roar of [[Typhon]] abruptly changed the situation. Conflicts that had seemed politically important became secondary once the Gods realized that a fully developed Beast of the Apocalypse capable of devastating the entire Pantheon was active.[5]
[[Titans|The Titans]] initially reacted with celebration. [[Atlas]] and others regarded Typhon's appearance as the opportunity they had been waiting for: Zeus and the Olympians might finally be annihilated without the Titans needing to bear the entire cost themselves. Kronus was far less enthusiastic. He understood Gaia well enough to realize that her greater hatred toward Zeus did not erase her resentment toward him. If Typhon destroyed Zeus, nothing guaranteed that Gaia would allow Kronus and the Titans to reclaim Olympus afterward.[5]
Kronus therefore prepared contingencies rather than trusting Typhon to solve his problems. His forces considered both attempting to contain the Beast after Olympus had been weakened and abandoning the Divine territory if the situation became impossible to control. This demonstrated that Kronus' objective was survival and restoration of Titan power, not simply watching Zeus die.[5]
The Titan faction remained one of the principal powers within the developing Greek civil war. Kronus himself stood at its center as a Primordial-class God, while ancient Titans such as Atlas gave the faction enough strength that even the other major Greek forces could not dismiss them as remnants of a defeated age.

#### King Among Kings
The Greek conflict reached its decisive stage inside the [[Greek Underworld]]. Nyx Elderblood, [[Scathach Elderblood Scarlett]], [[Morgana Elderblood]] and [[Aphrodite]] Elderblood discovered that the Underworld's gate was being forced open, and the invading army revealed an almost unthinkable sight: [[Zeus]] and Kronus were cooperating.[3]
Their alliance did not represent reconciliation between father and son. The ancient hatred remained, but the wider conspiracy surrounding [[Erebus]], [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]] and control over the Underworld created enough common interest for both former [[God-Kings]] to cooperate. The coalition intended to remove [[Persephone Elderblood]] from control of the realm, establish a ruler useful to their interests and consolidate enough Greek territory that outside Pantheons would have difficulty interfering with the reconstruction of Greece.[3][6]
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s sudden arrival destroyed the assumptions underlying their plan. The combined Olympian and Titan forces initially possessed hundreds of Gods and Divine combatants, while Kronus and Zeus stood among the greatest individual powers present. Even after sensing that Victor possessed enormous reserves, Kronus argued that equal Energy meant little when Victor was surrounded by numerous Divine enemies.[4]
Victor immediately demonstrated that the confrontation could not be treated as a conventional contest of reserves. The supernatural conditions generated around him interfered with the Divine powers of the opposing Gods. Kronus attempted to invoke his Time authority with the others and openly realized that the ability was not responding normally.[4]
The battle then turned into a massacre. Victor's attacks damaged and severed Souls, preventing the defeated Gods from simply recovering through ordinary Divine immortality. Even worse for Kronus and Zeus, Gods killed by Victor could immediately reappear as members of the Demon King's Immortal Army. Every casualty therefore simultaneously weakened the Greek coalition and strengthened Victor's side.[7]
Kronus' Titan forces rapidly collapsed. By the time Victor confronted the surviving elites directly, more than seventy percent of Kronus' forces had already been destroyed, while Zeus' side had suffered even greater losses. The numerical superiority upon which both rulers had relied had effectively become meaningless.[6]
Victor then began deliberately provoking the two ancient rulers. He treated their age and reputations with contempt and made clear that he had no intention of accepting surrender from the hostile Greek regime. Kronus and Zeus had expected to restructure the Greek world; Victor instead intended to eliminate the order that had produced them.[6]
During the final confrontation, Kronus attempted to rely upon the authority that had defined his existence for eons. He targeted Victor with his Time Divinity and stopped the progression of Victor's personal Time, apparently freezing him completely.[1]
Kronus initially believed the technique had succeeded and approached Victor. Victor then simply turned toward him and attacked.
Victor explained why the Time Stop had failed. Kronus possessed a tremendously powerful relationship with Time, but he was not Time itself. Only a genuine Primordial Entity completely merged with a fundamental Concept could claim that level of existential unity. Kronus remained a Primordial God possessing and representing Time through Divinity, which meant his authority could still be resisted or interfered with.[1]
Victor demonstrated one such counter through Negative Energy. By covering his existence in sufficiently concentrated Negative Energy, he prevented Kronus' temporal authority from properly influencing him. Victor acknowledged that a true Primordial Entity of Time might have presented an entirely different problem, but Kronus had never reached that state.[1]
With his greatest conceptual advantage neutralized, Kronus was forced into a physical battle against an opponent who massively surpassed him in direct close combat. Victor seized the Titan and repeatedly struck his head with overwhelming force. The violence of the assault caused the surrounding Underworld to tremble, and Victor internally recognized that releasing the same level of force without restraint on ordinary Earth could endanger the planet.[1]
Kronus' skull was eventually reduced to a catastrophically damaged state. He was effectively incapable of fighting and would have been considered dead by ordinary standards, yet his Second Generation Primordial God physiology kept the body breathing. When Tartarus approached the crippled Titan, Victor immediately forced him away and made clear that Kronus belonged to him.[1]
Victor lifted Kronus before the remaining Greek forces, drove his hand into the Titan's chest and tore out his heart. That act permanently killed the original Kronus.[1]
The consequences extended far beyond the death of another warrior. The moment Kronus truly died, Time within [[Olympus|Mount Olympus]] stopped progressing. The Greek Divine territory ceased moving normally between past, present and future, leaving its temporal system stagnant in the present. The phenomenon proved how deeply Kronus' existence had become integrated into the Pantheon's internal structure even though he was not universal Time itself.[1]
Victor then ate Kronus' heart. Consuming it granted him access to Kronus' enormous accumulation of memories and temporal knowledge, immediately deepening Victor's understanding of Time and allowing him to perceive aspects of its structure that had previously been inaccessible to him.[1]
The inherited memories also revealed the limitations under which Kronus had always operated. Even a Primordial God of Time could not freely leave his own Pantheon and rewrite the temporal structure of Reality on an enormous scale. Fundamental Concepts such as Time, Life and Death were protected by higher-order systems and beings responsible for maintaining Balance, preventing Gods from casually destabilizing the wider Cosmos.[1]
Kronus' original existence ended, but Victor did not waste the remaining resources. Most of Kronus' Soul had survived his death in substantially better condition than Zeus'. Victor threw his remains into the Sea of Blood and resurrected the fallen Titan as an Immortal Soldier. The King who had once ruled Olympus thus ended the conflict as a subordinate within the army of his own killer.[1]

### Skills & Abilities
Kronus was a Second Generation Primordial God, placing his fundamental Divine existence far above ordinary Gods. His greatest ability was the Divinity of Time, which functioned as both a personal conceptual authority and an important component of the [[Greek Pantheon]]'s own metaphysical structure.[1]

#### Second Generation Primordial God Physiology
- Primordial God Physiology: Kronus possessed a Divine body and Soul belonging to a Second Generation Primordial God rather than an ordinary Olympian or Titan.[1]
- Immense [[Divine Energy]]: Kronus possessed enormous reserves of Divine Energy. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s own reserves during the [[Greek Underworld]] conflict were discussed within the same broad highest range as Kronus and [[Zeus]], although equivalent reserves did not imply equivalent overall combat ability.[4]
- Superhuman Physical Characteristics: His Divine physiology granted strength, speed and durability vastly exceeding ordinary supernatural beings.
- Primordial Resilience: Kronus remained physically alive even after Victor catastrophically destroyed his skull and reduced him to a condition that would already count as death for almost any ordinary being.[1]
- Divine [[Immortality]]: Kronus did not age naturally and had existed for eons. Permanently killing him required methods capable of overcoming his Divine body, Soul and Primordial resilience.
- Powerful Divine Soul: Most of Kronus' Soul remained intact even after Victor killed him, allowing Victor to repurpose the fallen Titan into an Immortal Soldier.[1]
- Extreme Longevity: Kronus had survived from the earliest generations of the Greek Divine order through the rise of the Olympians and into the modern supernatural era.

#### Time Divinity
- Divinity of Time: Kronus' defining Divine authority was Time. His relationship with the Concept was deep enough that he represented an important temporal foundation within the [[Greek Pantheon]].[1]
- Time Manipulation: Kronus could directly manipulate temporal progression through Divine authority rather than conventional [[Magic]].[1]
- Time Stop: Kronus could stop the personal Time of another target. He attempted to freeze [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] completely during their final battle.[1]
- Temporal Immobilization: A target affected by Kronus' Time Stop would be prevented from moving because their temporal progression itself had been halted rather than because their body was conventionally restrained.[1]
- Conceptual Time Manipulation: Kronus' ability operated through the Divine Concept of Time and therefore existed on a fundamentally higher level than ordinary time-based spells or techniques.[1]
- Temporal Authority: Within the Greek Divine framework, Kronus possessed extremely high authority over Time as one of the Pantheon's principal temporal Gods.
- Pantheon Temporal Anchoring: Kronus' existence functioned as an anchor for the progression of Time in [[Olympus|Mount Olympus]]. His permanent death immediately caused normal temporal progression within the realm to stop.[1]
- Temporal Knowledge: Kronus accumulated an extraordinary understanding of Time throughout his eons of existence. Victor inherited this knowledge after eating his heart.[1]
- Temporal Perception: His accumulated understanding allowed him to perceive Time as a fundamental phenomenon rather than merely as an Element or magical effect. Victor's own perception of Reality improved after absorbing those memories.[1]
- Transferable Temporal Understanding: Kronus' knowledge was sufficiently complete that consuming his memories allowed Victor to begin interacting with Time at a level he had not previously possessed.[1]

#### Conceptual Existence
- Divine Conceptual Embodiment: Kronus could be described as a Divine embodiment or representative of Time within the [[Greek Pantheon]] because his Divinity had become integrated deeply enough into the Pantheon's temporal system that his death directly disrupted it.[1]
- Conceptual Anchoring: His continued existence helped maintain a fundamental function within [[Olympus]] rather than merely providing Kronus with a personal combat ability.[1]
- Divine Authority: Kronus could impose the authority of his Divinity upon other beings, as demonstrated when he directly targeted [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s personal Time.[1]
Kronus' conceptual state must not be confused with a true Primordial Entity. Victor explicitly rejected the claim that Kronus was Time itself. Kronus possessed, represented and partially embodied Time through Divinity, but a deeper separation between Kronus the individual and Time the universal Concept still existed.[1]

#### God-King and Titan Authority
- God-King Authority: Kronus was the former ruler of [[Olympus|Mount Olympus]] and one of the ancient [[God-Kings]] of the Greek Divine order.
- Titan Kingship: Even after losing Olympus, Kronus retained authority over the surviving Titan faction and was recognized as their King.[5][3]
- Leadership: Kronus successfully reorganized the surviving [[Titans]] into a functional military and political faction during the Greek civil war.[5]
- Army Command: The Titan forces followed Kronus into the [[Greek Underworld]] during the coalition's final attempt to seize control of the Greek realms.[3]
- Political Strategy: Kronus was willing to form temporary alliances with ancient enemies when doing so improved his chances of survival or restored political influence.[3]
- Strategic Foresight: He correctly recognized that [[Gaia]]'s temporary focus upon [[Zeus]] did not guarantee long-term safety for the Titans and prepared contingencies surrounding [[Typhon]] accordingly.[5]
- Immense Combat Experience: Kronus had existed through the foundational wars of the [[Greek Pantheon]], including the era preceding the [[Titanomachy]] and his later conflicts with the Olympian order.

#### Combat Abilities
- Primordial-Level Combat: Kronus belonged to the small class of Greek beings whose raw existence and Divine reserves placed them beyond conventional Major Gods.
- Concept-Based Combat: His principal battlefield advantage came from manipulating Time directly rather than relying only upon physical attacks.
- Close-Quarters Combat: Although Time was his greatest specialization, Kronus possessed a Primordial Divine body capable of participating directly in high-level combat.
- Large Energy Reserves: Kronus could participate in a prolonged battle involving hundreds of Gods and [[Titans]] without being treated as a fragile support specialist.[4]
- Combat Adaptability: He was capable of adjusting strategy when events such as [[Typhon]]'s appearance rendered previous plans obsolete.[5]

#### Post-Death Immortal Soldier State
After his original death, [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] placed Kronus' remains into the Sea of Blood and transformed him into an Immortal Soldier.[1]
- Undead/Reanimated Existence: Kronus' body and surviving Soul were repurposed rather than allowed to pass normally into an afterlife.
- Immortal Army Membership: The former Titan King became subordinate to Victor as part of the same army produced from other enemies killed during the Greek conflict.[1]
- Loss of Sovereignty: Whatever independence or political authority Kronus possessed as Titan King ended with his death. His reanimated state existed beneath Victor's control.
- Preserved Spiritual Foundation: Enough of his Soul survived for Victor to reconstruct him as a soldier, unlike [[Zeus]], whose Soul had been damaged much more severely.[1]
The novel does not directly demonstrate the full combat capabilities of reanimated Kronus after Chapter 798. His original Primordial body and Soul supplied the material for the Immortal Soldier, but the exact degree to which every aspect of his former Time authority remained independently usable in this state has not been shown.

### Conceptual Nature and Limitations
Kronus' most important limitation was the difference between being a Primordial God associated with a Concept and being a Primordial Entity fundamentally merged with that Concept.
Kronus = Divine representative, anchor and user of Time within the Greek system
Primordial Entity of Time = an existence fundamentally merged with Time as an Aspect of Creation
Kronus' death therefore did not erase Time from the Universe. It damaged the local Divine framework in which Kronus served as Time's representative, causing temporal progression in [[Olympus|Mount Olympus]] to stop while the wider Cosmos continued functioning.[1]
His authority was also restricted by Balance. Kronus' own memories revealed that Gods carrying powers connected to fundamental Concepts such as Time, Life and Death could not freely leave their appropriate domains and rewrite enormous portions of Reality without provoking intervention from the higher systems and existences responsible for preserving cosmic stability.[1]

#### Weaknesses and Counters
- Negative Energy: Concentrated Negative Energy could interfere with Kronus' Time authority. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] protected his existence with Negative Energy and remained capable of moving after Kronus attempted to stop his personal Time.[1]
- Conceptual Interference: Sufficiently incompatible or superior supernatural forces could prevent Kronus from freely exercising his Divinity. Victor's presence during the Greek battle interfered with the activation of multiple Divine abilities before their direct confrontation.[4]
- Not Universal Time: Kronus could not claim the absolute authority that would belong to an entity completely merged with the universal Concept of Time.[1]
- Restricted Cosmic Scope: Large-scale temporal interference outside the [[Greek Pantheon]]'s legitimate sphere was constrained by the forces responsible for maintaining Balance.[1]
- Physical Vulnerability After Bypass: Kronus' body remained extraordinarily durable, but once his Time authority was neutralized, Victor could overwhelm him through direct physical force.[1]
- Soul Vulnerability: His Soul was exceptionally powerful but not untouchable. Victor could claim and repurpose it after his death.[1]
- Heart Destruction/Removal: Kronus' Primordial body continued functioning after catastrophic cranial damage, but Victor permanently killed the original God by tearing out his heart.[1]
- Inferiority to Higher Conceptual States: Victor explicitly stated that a Time manipulator who had actually reached the existential level of a genuine Primordial Entity would represent a fundamentally more difficult opponent than Kronus.[1]

### Power and Scaling
Kronus was a Second Generation Primordial God, a classification that should not be confused with his former political title of God-King. He possessed both: Primordial God described the quality of his Divine existence, while God-King described the authority he once held as ruler of [[Olympus]].[1]
His Energy reserves placed him among the strongest Greek combatants present during the final [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] battle. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s reserves were recognized as belonging to the same broad range as Kronus and [[Zeus]], although the confrontation simultaneously demonstrated why Energy quantity alone cannot determine victory. Victor possessed abilities, physical power and conceptual counters capable of rendering Kronus' greatest specialty ineffective.[4][1]
Kronus was existentially superior to ordinary Major Gods and possessed greater natural resilience than conventional [[God-Kings]] lacking Primordial physiology. Zeus could nevertheless remain relevant within the same war through his own enormous power, God-King authority and Divine Artifacts, demonstrating that the upper ranges of these classifications can overlap in practical combat.
Victor defeating Kronus is one of the clearest milestones of his pre-Godhood progression. Victor permanently killed an actual Second Generation Primordial God before his later evolution into the Blood Dragon Primogenitor and subsequent ascension as a God.[1]
This feat reflects Victor's combat capability at the time and does not mean killing Kronus automatically granted him Kronus' racial or Divine classification. What Victor directly obtained was Kronus' heart, memories and accumulated understanding of Time.[1]

### Relationships

#### Gaia Elderblood
[[Gaia]] Elderblood was Kronus' mother and one of the Original [[Primordial Gods]] from whom the later Greek Divine generations descended.
Their relationship eventually became defined by betrayal. [[Hestia]] explicitly described Gaia as having been betrayed by Kronus before later experiencing another betrayal through [[Zeus]], causing Gaia to hold resentment toward both generations of rulers.[2]
Kronus understood this resentment well enough not to mistake Gaia's hatred of Zeus for an alliance with the [[Titans]]. When [[Typhon]] emerged, Kronus correctly assumed that Gaia could eventually turn against him once Zeus had been dealt with.[5]

#### Ouranos
[[Ouranos]] was Kronus' father and one of the Primordial ancestors of the Greek Divine family. Kronus belonged to the generation that followed the Original [[Primordial Gods]] and eventually became a Primordial God in his own right.
The present novel has provided substantially less direct information concerning Kronus' personal relationship with Ouranos than it has regarding [[Gaia]], [[Zeus]] or the later Titan conflict.

#### Rhea
[[Rhea]] was Kronus' sister and former spouse, as well as the mother of the principal Olympian generation that eventually displaced him.
Their children included [[Hestia]] Elderblood, [[Demeter]], [[Hera]], [[Hades]], [[Poseidon]] and [[Zeus]]. The novel's modern story focuses far more heavily upon the consequences of the [[Titanomachy]] than upon the everyday relationship Kronus and Rhea possessed before the Olympian rebellion.

#### Zeus
[[Zeus]] was Kronus' son, successor and greatest political enemy. Zeus' generation overthrew Kronus during the [[Titanomachy]], ending Titan rule over [[Olympus]] and replacing the old God-King with the Olympian order.
The hatred resulting from that overthrow persisted for eons. Their eventual cooperation during the invasion of the [[Greek Underworld]] was therefore shocking even to ancient beings familiar with the history of the Pantheon.[3]
The alliance remained entirely pragmatic. Neither father nor son had forgiven the other; their interests merely aligned long enough for both to attempt to restructure the Greek territories. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] ultimately destroyed both rulers during the same conflict.[3][1]

#### Hestia Elderblood
[[Hestia]] Elderblood was one of Kronus' daughters and one of the surviving Olympians who understood the ancient family conflict particularly well.
It was Hestia who explained that [[Gaia]] regarded herself as having been betrayed by Kronus and later [[Zeus]], contextualizing the hatred behind the Greek civil conflict.[2]
Unlike Kronus and Zeus, Hestia eventually abandoned the old Olympian political cycle entirely and joined [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s expanding family.

#### Atlas
[[Atlas]] was one of the [[Titans]] serving within Kronus' faction. During the crisis surrounding [[Typhon]], Atlas initially celebrated the possibility that [[Zeus]] would finally be destroyed, while Kronus responded more cautiously because he recognized the danger [[Gaia]] posed to the Titans themselves.[5]
Atlas remained part of the Titan power bloc that made Kronus' faction dangerous even to the established Olympian order.

#### Erebus and Tartarus
[[Erebus]] and [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]] were older Primordial relatives involved in the wider conspiracy surrounding the [[Greek Underworld]]. Their objectives were not identical to Kronus', but their actions helped create the conditions in which the temporary [[Zeus]]-Kronus alliance could attempt to seize the realm.[3]
During Kronus' final defeat, Tartarus attempted to approach the critically injured Titan. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] immediately prevented him from interfering and personally claimed Kronus as his kill.[1]

#### Victor Elderblood
[[Victor Elderblood]] was Kronus' killer and, following Kronus' death, effectively the master of his reanimated remains.
Kronus initially dismissed Victor as a Mortal whose large Energy reserves could not overcome an army of Gods. Victor systematically disproved that assumption by annihilating the Greek forces, suppressing their Divine advantages and eventually countering Kronus' Time authority itself.[4][7]
Their final confrontation became a direct clash between Kronus' conceptual confidence and Victor's understanding of the distinction between Gods and true [[Primordial Entities]]. Victor protected himself through Negative Energy, ignored Kronus' Time Stop and physically destroyed him before removing and eating his heart.[1]
Victor inherited Kronus' memories and understanding of Time through consumption. He then used the surviving Soul and corpse to recreate Kronus as an Immortal Soldier, transforming a former King of [[Olympus]] into a subordinate within his own army.[1]

### Death and Legacy
Kronus' death carried consequences extending far beyond the loss of a powerful Titan. Because he served as the Divine representative and anchor of Time within the [[Greek Pantheon]], his removal caused [[Olympus|Mount Olympus]]' temporal framework to cease functioning normally. The realm remained trapped in its present rather than progressing naturally toward the future.[1]
This event provides one of the clearest demonstrations of how deeply a sufficiently important Divine being can become integrated into the metaphysical infrastructure of a Pantheon. Kronus was not universal Time, yet his absence was still capable of damaging Time within the Divine system that recognized his authority.
His accumulated knowledge likewise survived him. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] gained access to Kronus' memories after consuming his heart, granting him a substantially deeper understanding of temporal phenomena and helping him begin interacting with Time through knowledge rather than merely possessing a borrowed magical effect.[1]
The memories also taught Victor that even extremely powerful Gods of fundamental Concepts operated beneath larger cosmic restrictions. Kronus could not casually travel throughout Creation rewriting Time however he wished; Balance and the beings responsible for preserving it imposed limits upon interference capable of destabilizing Reality.[1]
Kronus' corpse and surviving Soul were ultimately converted into an Immortal Soldier. His original independent existence remained dead, but the power and spiritual material of the Titan King became another resource incorporated into Victor's growing forces.[1]
