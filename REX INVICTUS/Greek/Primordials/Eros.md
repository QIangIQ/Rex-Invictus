### Appearances

#### First Mention
Chapter 330

#### Debut
Chapter 1144
Eros was a Third Generation Greek God, a Major God of the former [[Greek Pantheon]] and the son of [[Aphrodite]] Elderblood and [[Ares]]. His principal Divine identity was associated with Love, with his established Divine domains also including Lust, Desire and Sex. Although he possessed considerable status within the old Olympian hierarchy, Eros became better known in the story for his uncontrolled sexual behavior and his willingness to exploit the influence of more powerful people around him.[1]
Eros was first mentioned centuries before his physical debut when it was revealed that he had an affair with [[Vlad Dracula Tepes]]' Third Wife, the mother of [[Saul Tepes]]. The affair became known during the collapse of Vlad's original household and was one of several betrayals that resulted from the Vampire King's centuries of neglect toward his spouses.[2]
After the destruction of the old Greek political order, Eros avoided many of the consequences suffered by other Greek Gods because of his relationship with Aphrodite. By then, his mother had joined [[Victor Elderblood]], become one of the central women of his family and risen into an immensely powerful Dragon Goddess. Her influence protected Eros and allowed him to maintain a privileged position within the developing [[Elderblood Empire]]. Rather than genuinely reforming, however, he increasingly exploited Aphrodite's name and social authority for personal gratification.[1]
His behavior eventually exhausted Aphrodite's remaining attachment to him. After discovering that Eros had begun planning to pursue one of her own daughters while also harboring inappropriate thoughts toward Aphrodite herself, she permanently disowned him. Eros' Soul was subsequently subjected to a complete reincarnation process: [[Violet Elderblood Snow]] repaired and reorganized its damaged structure with Order, while [[Persephone Elderblood]] was instructed to erase his personality, memories and identity before reincarnating the remaining spiritual foundation as an entirely new Lesser Goddess of Love. Eros therefore ceased to exist as an individual even though the metaphysical material composing his Soul was preserved and recycled.[1]

### Physical Appearance
The novel provides almost no detailed description of Eros' ordinary appearance. He is explicitly male and physically manifests as a Greek God, but Chapter 1144 does not establish a canonical hair color, eye color, height, clothing style or other identifying physical characteristics. The artwork currently associated with the article should therefore be treated as an artistic interpretation rather than a direct adaptation of a detailed novel description.
During his confrontation with [[Aphrodite]], Eros' physical condition deteriorated rapidly once his mother stopped restraining her Draconic Divine presence. The pressure emanating from her existence forced him to the ground and left his body bloodied despite Aphrodite not needing to engage him in a conventional battle.[1]
The scene primarily demonstrates the enormous disparity between the old Greek Divine hierarchy and the beings produced by [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s mature Empire. Eros remained a Major God by the standards of [[Olympus]], but Aphrodite had evolved into a Dragon Goddess whose simple words could impose conditions upon Reality. When she commanded Eros to remain silent, the order was enforced directly by Reality itself rather than functioning as an ordinary verbal command.[1]

### Personality
Eros was exceptionally lustful, hedonistic, self-indulgent and lacking in restraint. [[Aphrodite]] stated that she had repeatedly warned him to control his libido and had already punished him severely on several previous occasions, yet none of those interventions produced any lasting change. His mother eventually compared his inability to control himself with the behavior of [[Zeus]], one of the clearest indications of how negatively she regarded his conduct.[1]
His involvement with Vlad's Third Wife demonstrates that this behavior existed long before the [[Elderblood Empire]]. Eros knowingly entered an affair with the wife of one of the most dangerous rulers in the supernatural world despite the obvious political and personal consequences such a relationship could create. Vlad's own neglect of his household was a major factor in his wives' betrayals, but Eros displayed no apparent concern over becoming involved in the situation.[2]
Eros was also opportunistic and socially parasitic. According to Aphrodite, he had barely treated her like a mother while they lived under the old Greek order. He rarely visited her, did not maintain a meaningful familial relationship and showed little concern when Aphrodite distanced herself from [[Olympus]]. His attitude changed only after Aphrodite became one of the most powerful and politically important women within [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s civilization.[1]
Once Aphrodite gained enormous Imperial influence, Eros returned to openly calling her "Mother" and began benefiting from the prestige attached to her name. Aphrodite recognized the self-interest involved but tolerated it because she considered the failures of their old relationship partly her own responsibility and genuinely wanted to become a better parent. Eros instead treated the reconciliation as an opportunity to improve his own position.[1]
He subsequently began invoking Aphrodite's name and authority while pursuing his desires. Imperial surveillance accumulated several reports regarding his conduct, and Aphrodite admitted that she had previously suppressed at least one incident through her influence because Eros had fortunately not yet done anything sufficiently severe to make intervention impossible.[1]
Eros was additionally impertinent and poorly aware of power differences when emotionally provoked. When Aphrodite insulted him during their final confrontation, he immediately answered back by reminding her that she was also Greek despite knowing that she was already furious. Aphrodite responded by simply commanding him to stop speaking, and her Reality-level authority rendered him physically incapable of producing sound.[1]
His greatest flaw was the assumption that Aphrodite would continue protecting him indefinitely. Eros failed to understand how completely his mother had changed after joining Victor. Aphrodite's loyalty had shifted away from the selfish culture of the old [[Greek Pantheon]] and toward Victor and their new family, yet Eros continued acting as though his biological relationship with her placed him beyond meaningful consequences.[1]
That assumption ended when Eros developed an inappropriate interest in one of Aphrodite's daughters and began constructing a plan involving her. He had not yet carried out the plan when Aphrodite confronted him, something Eros himself immediately recognized. Aphrodite had nevertheless anticipated that his behavior would eventually escalate and had deliberately monitored his thoughts.[1]
Reading deeper into his mind revealed another problem: Eros also possessed sexual thoughts concerning Aphrodite herself. Combined with his neglect of her before she gained power, exploitation of her status afterward and interest in one of her daughters, this convinced Aphrodite that the mother-son relationship she had tried to rebuild had never genuinely existed from Eros' perspective.[1]
Despite his arrogance, Eros was capable of extreme fear when confronted by something genuinely beyond him. Aphrodite's released presence immediately terrified him, while the hooded entities summoned to take custody of him produced an even deeper existential reaction. His final conscious experience before being removed was looking beneath one of their hoods and witnessing innumerable violet eyes surrounding an enormous mouth.[1]

### Synopsis

#### Discoveries, Changes, And War
Eros entered the narrative indirectly during the collapse of [[Vlad Dracula Tepes]]' original family. Information concerning Vlad's wives and children had been deliberately spread through the Royal Capital, exposing several relationships that the Vampire King considered betrayals. [[Alexios Alioth]] reviewed the situation while observing the aftermath and identified a different scandal surrounding each of Vlad's wives.[2]
Vlad's [[Violet Elderblood Snow|First Wife]] had become involved with her own son, while the Second Wife—the mother of [[Lucas Tepes]]—had betrayed him with a Werewolf. The Third Wife, mother of [[Saul Tepes]], had an affair with Eros, whom Alexios identified as a God associated with [[Aphrodite]] and the concept of love.[2]
The affair provided the earliest canonical information about Eros and established a pattern that remained unchanged thousands of years later. His involvement with the wife of the Vampire Progenitor demonstrated an extreme willingness to pursue sexual relationships regardless of danger, existing commitments or political consequences. Vlad killed his Third Wife after the scandal was exposed, although no confirmed scene establishes that he ever personally retaliated against Eros.[2]
Eros remained outside the main narrative throughout the later wars involving [[Olympus]]. His survival was eventually explained by Aphrodite herself. As the [[Greek Pantheon]] collapsed and numerous Gods were killed, displaced or absorbed into new factions, Eros escaped many of those consequences specifically because he remained Aphrodite's biological son. Her increasing association with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] effectively placed a layer of protection around him even before Eros made any genuine effort to rebuild their relationship.[1]

#### Those Who Rule Reality
By the mature era of the [[Elderblood Empire]], tens of thousands of subjective years had passed under the accelerated development systems used by [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s civilization. [[Aphrodite]] had long since ceased resembling the woman who once lived according to the customs of [[Olympus]]. She was now one of Victor's wives, a Dragon Goddess of overwhelming power and one of the central Divine authorities of the Empire. Eros had consequently acquired status far beyond what his own achievements justified simply because he was recognized as her son.[1]
Aphrodite eventually confronted him personally after receiving repeated reports concerning his behavior. Her opening reaction made clear that the situation was not new: she had warned Eros multiple times to restrain himself and had already punished him severely before, yet he continued behaving according to the same impulses that characterized the worst excesses of the Greek Gods.[1]
When Eros answered one of Aphrodite's insults by pointing out that she herself was Greek, Aphrodite commanded him to remain silent. The difference in their current levels became immediately apparent. Her words were no longer merely words; as a Dragon Goddess, her declaration imposed itself directly upon Reality, and Eros became physically incapable of speaking despite attempting to do so.[1]
Aphrodite then addressed the deeper problem in their relationship. Eros had never behaved like a devoted son during their life in Olympus. He rarely visited her and did not concern himself with her wellbeing when she distanced herself from the Pantheon. Only after she became one of the highest-ranking women in Victor's Empire did Eros suddenly begin treating their maternal bond as important.[1]
She produced several Imperial reports documenting how he had exploited her name and status. Eros was surprised that the Empire had been monitoring him, but Aphrodite explained that such surveillance was not unique to him. Imperial authorities monitored the civilization broadly enough that repeated abuses committed by someone invoking an Empress' name could not remain invisible indefinitely.[1]
Aphrodite admitted that she had already protected Eros once by suppressing a previous case. He had fortunately stopped short of committing something sufficiently severe that even she could not interfere, so she used her position to prevent the incident from escalating further. This became another attempt to give him an opportunity to correct his behavior.[1]
The final incident crossed a completely different boundary. Eros had developed an interest in one of Aphrodite's daughters and had begun planning how to pursue her. He immediately realized that he had not yet actually done anything, but this did not help him: Aphrodite was reading his thoughts and had specifically begun monitoring him because she expected another escalation.[1]
The daughter involved is never identified by name. What mattered to Aphrodite was that Eros intended to direct the same uncontrolled behavior she had spent ages condemning toward one of the daughters belonging to her new family. The target was therefore not simply another Imperial citizen but one of Aphrodite's own children and a member of the [[Elderblood Imperial Family]].[1]
Aphrodite manifested her Draconic characteristics and allowed more of her presence to escape. The pressure alone forced Eros down and left him bleeding, making any conventional resistance meaningless. She also revealed that reading his mind had exposed sexual thoughts directed toward Aphrodite herself, destroying the final remnants of the maternal relationship she had attempted to restore.[1]
Aphrodite formally disowned him. She prohibited him from referring to her as his mother and made clear that whatever relationship once existed between them had ended. She also warned that he was fortunate she had chosen to handle the matter personally. Had Victor Elderblood become directly involved, she believed whatever punishment he devised would have been vastly worse than ordinary death.[1]
Two hooded entities arrived to take Eros away. He attempted to look beneath the hood of one and perceived thousands of violet eyes surrounding an enormous mouth, an image that overwhelmed him with existential terror immediately before he lost consciousness.[1]
After Eros was removed, [[Violet Elderblood Snow]] revealed that she had remained hidden nearby during the confrontation. Violet was furious because her possessiveness had long since expanded beyond Victor himself to encompass nearly everyone she considered part of the family. She had refrained from personally acting only because Aphrodite wanted to deal with her biological son herself.[1]
Aphrodite admitted that she had genuinely tried to repair their relationship. She had accepted Eros returning to her because she believed some of his behavior might have been the consequence of her own failures during the old Olympian era. After finally confronting what Eros actually thought of her, she realized that the relationship she had attempted to recover had largely existed only in her own expectations.[1]
Rather than ordering Eros' Soul completely annihilated, Aphrodite requested that whatever useful spiritual value remained be recycled. Eros had been born from Aphrodite before she became a Dragon, and his Soul still possessed a worthwhile Divine foundation. Aphrodite proposed using that foundation to create a new Goddess of Love who could serve as one of her attendants, similar in general role to the specialized attendants associated with other members of Victor's family.[1]
Violet summarized the intended result as a Lesser Goddess of Love, and Aphrodite agreed. This was the final mercy Aphrodite extended toward Eros: the resources contained within his Soul would be reused, but the individual responsible for abusing her protection would not continue existing.[1]
Eros' Soul was subsequently delivered to [[Persephone Elderblood]], whose responsibilities within the Imperial system included the handling of Souls and reincarnation. [[Persephone]] noted that the Soul had already suffered damage during the process through which Eros had been taken into custody.[1]
Violet ordered a complete reincarnation. Every component responsible for Eros' identity—his memories, personality, accumulated individuality and continuity as the person called Eros—was to be erased. The useful spiritual structure and Love-related Divinity would remain, but nothing capable of recreating the former individual was intended to survive.[1]
Because the Soul was damaged, Persephone warned that the process would require additional work. Violet therefore directly invoked the Concept of Order. The effect appeared almost insignificant externally, but Persephone could perceive the deeper structure of the Soul being reorganized and repaired until it became suitable for reincarnation.[1]
Violet then left the completed Soul in Persephone's custody. Its eventual destination was the birth of an entirely new female Lesser Goddess of Love who would serve Aphrodite. Although that Goddess would originate from the spiritual material and Divinity once belonging to Eros, she would not possess his memories, personality or identity.[1]
Eros therefore died in a manner more absolute than ordinary bodily death without being completely erased as metaphysical material. The Soul continued, but Eros did not.

### Skills & Abilities
Eros was a Third Generation Greek God and classified as a Mid Level God. Although the story provides almost no direct combat feats for him, his Divine existence and the treatment of his Soul in Chapter 1144 establish several supernatural characteristics. His abilities should not be inflated with generic powers from real-world mythology that the novel never demonstrates.

#### Divine Physiology
- God Physiology: As an ancient Greek God, Eros possessed the supernatural body, Soul and [[Divine Energy]] characteristic of Divine beings.
- Superhuman Physical Characteristics: His Divine body naturally possessed strength, speed, durability and physical performance beyond ordinary mortal species.
- Divine Energy Manipulation: Eros possessed Divine Energy associated with his existence and Divinity.
- Divine Soul: His Soul contained enough supernatural value that [[Aphrodite]] considered destroying it wasteful even after deciding that Eros himself had to disappear.[1]
- Godly Longevity: Eros had existed for eons without dying from natural aging.
- Regeneration: As a God, Eros possessed the supernatural recovery normally associated with Divine physiology, although his encounter with Aphrodite demonstrates that overwhelming higher-order power could bypass his ability to meaningfully resist or recover.
- Major God Status: Eros possessed enough accumulated Divine power and religious importance to be classified as a Major God within the [[Greek Pantheon]].

#### Divinity
- Love Divinity: Eros possessed Divine authority associated with Love. His Love-related spiritual foundation remained sufficiently valuable after his identity was erased that [[Aphrodite]] specifically ordered the surviving Divinity to be used in the creation of a new Lesser Goddess of Love.[1]
- Lust Divinity: Eros is indexed with Divine authority associated with Lust, corresponding to the more physical and compulsive expressions connected to his broader Love-related nature.
- Desire Divinity: Eros possesses authority associated with Desire.
- Sex Divinity: Eros possesses a Divine domain associated with sexual attraction and intercourse.
- Divinity Preservation: Divine authority can remain within the spiritual structure of a God even after the individual's personality and memories are removed. Eros provides a direct example: his identity was scheduled for deletion while his useful Love-related Divinity was preserved for reincarnation.[1]

#### Mental and Social Influence
- Divine Attraction: As a God associated with Love and related Concepts, Eros possessed Divine potential to influence attraction and desire.
- Seduction: Eros had extensive experience pursuing lovers and establishing sexual relationships, including successfully becoming involved with Vlad's Third Wife.[2]
- Social Manipulation: During the Imperial era, Eros repeatedly exploited his relationship with [[Aphrodite]] and invoked her reputation to pressure others or advance his own interests.[1]
- Political Exploitation: Eros understood that his mother's position as one of [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s most powerful wives gave him social protection and used that connection as informal political capital.[1]

#### Limitations
- Inferior Conceptual Authority: Eros was completely incapable of resisting [[Aphrodite]] once she used her evolved Dragon-Goddess authority. A single declaration prevented him from speaking, and her released presence alone forced him to the ground bleeding.[1]
- Mind Reading Vulnerability: Eros possessed no demonstrated defense capable of preventing Aphrodite from reading his intentions and private thoughts.[1]
- Soul Manipulation Vulnerability: His Soul could be seized, damaged, repaired, reorganized and ultimately prepared for complete reincarnation by beings with sufficiently advanced Soul authority.[1]
- Identity Erasure Vulnerability: The Imperial reincarnation process was capable of removing everything defining Eros as an individual while preserving selected useful spiritual components.[1]
- Poor Self-Control: Eros' inability to restrain his libido repeatedly overrode his judgment even after multiple punishments and warnings from Aphrodite.[1]
- Dependence on Aphrodite's Protection: Much of Eros' later political security came not from his own power but from Aphrodite's status. Once she disowned him, that protection disappeared immediately.[1]

### Relationships

#### Aphrodite Elderblood
[[Aphrodite]] Elderblood was Eros' biological mother and the most important relationship in his later life. Their relationship had already become dysfunctional long before the main story. Aphrodite later stated that Eros rarely visited her during their life in [[Olympus]] and displayed little interest in maintaining a meaningful bond with her.[1]
After Aphrodite joined [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] and transformed herself both personally and metaphysically, she attempted to repair the relationship. She allowed Eros to return, accepted him calling her Mother and repeatedly tried to restrain or correct his behavior rather than simply abandoning him. She partly considered his character a consequence of the environment in which she herself had raised him.[1]
Eros instead used her improved status as an opportunity. He gained protection during the destruction of Olympus, inherited prestige inside the [[Elderblood Empire]] and invoked Aphrodite's name when pursuing personal desires. Aphrodite tolerated this longer than she normally would have because she wanted their reconciliation to succeed.[1]
Her discovery that Eros intended to pursue one of her daughters, combined with his sexual thoughts about Aphrodite herself, finally destroyed that effort. Aphrodite disowned him and explicitly forbade him from referring to her as his mother again.[1]
Even then, she chose recycling rather than absolute Soul destruction. Aphrodite considered the continued usefulness of his Divine foundation a final reason not to waste everything he had once been, but she deliberately ordered that the person called Eros never return.[1]

#### Ares
[[Ares]] was Eros' biological father. The current novel material provides almost no detailed interaction between father and son, and no direct evidence establishes that Ares attempted to intervene in Eros' fate during the Imperial era.

#### Vlad Dracula Tepes' Third Wife
Vlad's unnamed Third Wife, mother of [[Saul Tepes]], was one of Eros' confirmed lovers. She betrayed Vlad through an affair with Eros, and the relationship became public during the upheaval surrounding Vlad's Royal Family.[2]
Vlad subsequently killed her together with other family members he considered traitors. There is no confirmed scene in which Vlad personally confronted or punished Eros for the affair.[2]

#### Psyche
Psyche is indexed as Eros' wife. Their marriage is part of the established Greek family tree, but the currently available novel chapters provide almost no direct development of their relationship. Claims derived solely from real-world Greek mythology should therefore not be added to their MTW history without explicit chapter support.

#### Hedone
Hedone is indexed as the daughter of Eros and Psyche. The novel has not substantially explored her relationship with Eros in the currently verified material.

#### Victor Elderblood
[[Victor Elderblood]] became Eros' stepfather through his marriage to [[Aphrodite]], but Eros never became a genuine member of Victor's immediate family simply because of that relationship. His primary connection to Victor was indirect: being Aphrodite's son protected Eros because Victor respected Aphrodite and generally allowed her to handle matters concerning her previous family.[1]
Aphrodite deliberately prevented Victor from personally handling Eros' final punishment. She believed that if Victor learned the full details and acted directly, whatever he did to Eros would make death appear merciful.[1]

#### Violet Elderblood Snow
[[Violet Elderblood Snow]] assisted [[Aphrodite]] in disposing of Eros and was considerably less merciful than Aphrodite herself. By this stage Violet's possessiveness extended across the Elderblood family, meaning Eros' interest in one of Aphrodite's daughters was treated as a direct threat against someone she considered family.[1]
Violet respected Aphrodite's wish to determine Eros' fate personally, but after his capture she took direct responsibility for preparing his Soul for reincarnation. When [[Persephone]] identified damage within it, Violet invoked Order and reorganized the Soul into a suitable condition for complete recycling.[1]

#### Persephone Elderblood
[[Persephone Elderblood]] was entrusted with the reincarnation of Eros' Soul. Her authority and experience allowed her to perceive the damage already present within his Soul and determine what was required before it could be processed.[1]
After Violet repaired the Soul, [[Persephone]] was instructed to erase Eros' memories, personality and identity while preserving the useful Love-related Divine foundation. The intended result was an entirely new Lesser Goddess of Love rather than Eros returning under another form.[1]

#### Irene Elderblood
[[Irene Elderblood]] is Eros' maternal half-sister through [[Aphrodite]] and [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]. Chapter 1144 never identifies which of Aphrodite's daughters Eros had begun targeting, and Irene should therefore not automatically be assumed to have been the daughter in question without explicit confirmation from another chapter.

### Fate
Eros' death differed fundamentally from ordinary physical death. His body was not the important target; the Imperial Family deliberately dismantled the continuity of the person occupying his Soul.[1]
The first stage was [[Aphrodite]] formally severing their familial relationship and surrendering him for processing. The hooded entities who took Eros away damaged his Soul enough that [[Persephone]] later considered repairs necessary before reincarnation could proceed.[1]
The second stage was Violet's intervention. Using Order, Violet reorganized and repaired the Soul at a structural level. Persephone could perceive the process clearly due to her own expertise with Souls even though the alteration appeared comparatively subtle from the outside.[1]
The third and final stage was complete reincarnation. Persephone was ordered to erase every memory, personality trait and element of individual identity belonging to Eros. His Love-related Divinity and useful spiritual foundation would remain, but none of the continuity defining the former God was intended to survive.[1]
The Soul would then be reincarnated as an entirely new female Lesser Goddess of Love who would serve Aphrodite as an attendant. This future Goddess is not Eros transformed into a woman, resurrected in another body or reincarnated with suppressed memories. She is explicitly intended to be a new individual created from recycled spiritual material after Eros' former identity has been completely erased.[1]
For this reason, Eros is canonically best treated as deceased. His Soul-material survived; Eros did not.
