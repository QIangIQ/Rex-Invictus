### Appearances

#### Debut
Chapter 778
MOTHER, YOU PIECE OF SHIT, I WILL KILL YOU!
—Nemesis, after Nyx casually expelled her from her presence, Chapter 779
Nemesis, also known as Rhamnousia, is a Goddess of the former [[Greek Pantheon]], a Second Generation Primordial Goddess, and the divine personification most closely associated with Vengeance and Revenge. She is one of the children of the Original [[Primordial Gods]] Nyx and [[Erebus]], making her part of one of the oldest Divine bloodlines produced by the Greek Pantheon.[1]
Despite descending directly from Original Primordial Gods, Nemesis should not be confused with the true [[Primordial Entities]] such as Infinity or [[True Death]]. Her designation as a Primordial God refers to her Divine generation and ancestry within the Greek Pantheon. Unlike the Regulators, who embody universal functions of Creation itself, Nemesis is a God who wields and develops a Divine Concept. Her principal Concept is Revenge/Vengeance, and by the time of [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s ascension she was explicitly identified as the Greek Goddess possessing the greatest influence over the Concept of Revenge.[2]
Originally serving within the [[Greek Underworld]] beneath its old hierarchy, Nemesis later became one of [[Persephone]]'s Divine combatants. During the destruction of [[Olympus]], she fought as part of Persephone's Underworld army and turned her power against the Olympian Gods themselves.[3][4] Following the collapse of the old Greek order and the rise of Victor Elderblood, Nemesis became a faithful follower of Victor and entered the wider Divine structure surrounding his faction.[2]

### Physical Appearance
Nemesis is a tall woman with long black hair, gray skin and eyes described as being as black as pure darkness. Her most visually distinctive characteristic is the collection of dark, tribal-like markings covering her arms. These markings are not completely static; they are described as moving across her skin, giving her appearance an overtly supernatural quality even while she remains in a humanoid form.[1]
Her appearance strongly reflects her descent from Nyx and [[Erebus]], whose Divine characteristics are associated with Night and Darkness. However, the novel has not established that the moving markings themselves are an independent ability or that they should automatically be treated as a manifestation of either parent's Divinity.

### Personality
Nemesis' personality is heavily influenced by the Concept she represents. She is extremely emotional, confrontational and volatile, particularly when dealing with someone she has personally identified as the target of her vengeance. [[Thanatos]] explicitly considers this instability characteristic of Nemesis as the Goddess of Vengeance rather than merely a temporary mood.[1]
Her greatest emotional weakness is her family. Nemesis' anger toward Nyx initially appears almost murderous, but Chapter 778 makes it clear that the resentment originates primarily from abandonment rather than genuine hatred. She had kept track of exactly how long Nyx had failed to visit her—approximately 365 million days—and resented the fact that her mother would not even occasionally speak with her. Beneath her threats and explosive temperament, Nemesis still remembered the period when Nyx, [[Erebus]] and their children had been a close family and wondered what had happened between her parents to destroy that relationship.[1]
Nemesis is consequently far more sentimental than her initial aggression suggests. She was confused by the deterioration of Nyx and Erebus' marriage and remained unaware of what had actually caused their separation because neither parent had explained the matter to their children. Her fixation upon punishing Nyx was therefore mixed with a daughter's desire for acknowledgment and an explanation for why their once-united family had fallen apart.[1]
Despite her volatility, Nemesis is not irrational. She respects Thanatos even though she does not always like him, specifically because he remained present throughout their immortal lives when much of their family fell apart. When Thanatos sternly ordered her to be patient rather than recklessly approach Nyx, Nemesis reluctantly obeyed him.[1] She is also capable of accurately recognizing overwhelming differences in strength. While observing [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s massacre of [[Olympus]], Nemesis openly criticized [[Persephone]] for suggesting that she could deal with Victor later and bluntly admitted that she herself could not fight him.[5]
Her obedience toward Persephone is much more straightforward. Once Persephone finally ordered her forces to kill the Greek Gods, Nemesis responded immediately and enthusiastically, covering herself in Divine Power and launching herself into the enemy without hesitation.[4] This combination of volatility, loyalty and eagerness for retaliation makes Nemesis highly dangerous once she is given a legitimate target.
Her later loyalty to Victor appears to be genuine rather than merely political. Chapter 889 explicitly identifies Nemesis as a faithful follower of Victor. Her reputation surrounding the Concept of Revenge had become strong enough that Gods understood anyone attempting to deliberately obstruct Victor's advancement in that Concept risked suffering Nemesis' retaliation.[2]

### Synopsis

#### King Among Kings
Nemesis first appeared within the [[Greek Underworld]] while [[Scathach Elderblood Scarlett]], [[Aphrodite]] Elderblood, Nyx Elderblood and [[Morgana Elderblood]] were traveling through [[Persephone]]'s territory. Persephone, suspicious of the unexpected visitors, ordered [[Thanatos]] and the other Underworld Gods to investigate them and determine whether they represented a threat.[1]
Thanatos observed the group from a distance alongside [[Hypnos]] and Nemesis. While Hypnos focused on the impossibility of directly confronting a group containing [[Scathach]], Nyx, Aphrodite and Morgana, Nemesis cared almost exclusively about Nyx. She immediately wanted to approach her mother after realizing Nyx had already detected them, forcing Thanatos to order her to remain patient.[1]
The incident revealed the deep fracture within Nyx and [[Erebus]]' family. Nemesis remembered when the household had once been unified and could not understand what had caused the relationship between her parents to deteriorate. Nyx had remained absent from Nemesis' life for approximately 365 million days, an absence Nemesis had personally counted and transformed into an object of vengeance. Despite the intensity of her anger, her thoughts revealed that what she truly wanted was attention from her mother and an explanation for why their family had collapsed.[1]
Nemesis' patience eventually ran out. Nyx effortlessly dismissed her from her presence, treating the furious Second Generation Primordial Goddess almost like an unruly child. The humiliation only intensified Nemesis' anger. She returned to Persephone's stronghold and began violently venting her frustration, screaming that she would kill Nyx and causing explosions powerful enough for Persephone to become concerned that Nemesis would damage her castle. Persephone consequently ordered Thanatos to deal with his sister before she destroyed anything important.[6]
The political situation changed radically once [[Victor Elderblood]] launched his assault against [[Olympus]]. Persephone and her forces observed Victor slaughtering the Greek Gods and permanently severing their Souls while simultaneously turning many of the dead into members of his Immortal Army.[5]
Persephone initially claimed that if Victor eventually turned against the Underworld, she would deal with him herself. Nemesis immediately rejected the idea. Rather than allowing pride to distort her judgment, she bluntly told Persephone that the idea was foolish and pointed toward Victor's blood-soaked battlefield as proof. Nemesis openly admitted that she could not defeat what she was witnessing, while Persephone and Thanatos ultimately reached essentially the same conclusion.[5]
The war eventually brought the entire force of the Greek Underworld onto the battlefield. Persephone's army was composed primarily of children and descendants of Nyx and Erebus, including Second Generation [[Primordial Gods]] associated with Death, Dreams, Revenge and other Underworld-related Concepts. Nemesis stood among these forces together with Thanatos, Hypnos and their relatives.[3]
The arrival of [[Nocturnus]] shocked Nemesis and her brothers. Nocturnus closely resembled Erebus and possessed the Divinity of Void, immediately revealing the existence of another child their father had produced without their knowledge. When Nemesis, Thanatos and Hypnos looked toward Nyx and saw her reaction, they understood that even their mother had not known about him. The revelation exposed the betrayal that had helped destroy Nyx and Erebus' marriage and finally provided the siblings with part of the explanation they had been missing for ages.[3]
When Persephone finally committed herself to the battle, she ordered her forces to kill the Greeks while explicitly forbidding them from attacking Victor's group. Nemesis did not hesitate. She immediately covered herself in Divine Power and charged directly into the Olympian Gods. Thanatos, knowing his sister's impulsive nature, ordered the Furies to accompany and support her while the remainder of Persephone's forces carried out their own assignments.[4]
Nemesis consequently fought on the side that helped bring down the old Olympian order. Her participation marked the effective end of her loyalty to the traditional [[Greek Pantheon]] and reinforced her position beneath Persephone rather than Olympus' former rulers.[4]

#### Dragon God of Chaos
After [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s ascension into a true God and the destruction of the former Olympian order, Nemesis had become integrated into his wider sphere.
While examining Victor's development as a God, the narration explained that sufficiently knowledgeable Gods could interfere with another God's advancement in a particular Concept. Several Gods who held exceptional influence over Concepts related to Victor deliberately avoided doing so because those Concepts were already dominated by individuals loyal to him.[2]
Nemesis was explicitly identified as the Greek Goddess of Revenge with the greatest influence over the Concept of Revenge. More importantly, she was described as a faithful follower of Victor. Gods considering interfering with Victor's progress in Revenge understood that doing so would provoke Nemesis and bring swift retribution upon them.[2]
This establishes Nemesis' later position far more clearly than the old Greek political structure. She was no longer simply an [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] Goddess serving a regional Pantheon; she had become one of the Conceptually important Divine figures supporting Victor's continued growth and the developing Dragons Nest order.[2]

### Relationships

#### Nyx Elderblood
Nyx is Nemesis' mother and the central source of Nemesis' greatest unresolved emotional grievance. Nemesis spent approximately one million years without receiving a visit from her mother and kept a literal count of the elapsed days. As the Goddess of Vengeance, she transformed this neglect into an obsessive desire for retaliation, openly threatening to kill Nyx when the two finally crossed paths again.[1][6]
Her thoughts nevertheless make clear that the relationship is more complicated than hatred. Nemesis remembered the period when Nyx, [[Erebus]] and their children were a close family and genuinely wanted to understand why her parents separated. Much of her rage came from Nyx refusing to communicate with her rather than from any desire to permanently sever their relationship.[1]
Chapter 799 finally revealed part of the answer. When [[Nocturnus]] appeared as another son of Erebus, Nemesis and her siblings realized from Nyx's reaction that their father had betrayed her and produced a child outside their family. This gave Nemesis direct evidence concerning the event that had destroyed the relationship between her parents.[3]

#### Erebus
[[Erebus]] was Nemesis' father and one of the Original [[Primordial Gods]] of Greece. Nemesis remembered a period in which Erebus and Nyx maintained a close family together before their relationship deteriorated.[1]
For most of Nemesis' life, the true reason for the separation remained hidden from the children. The revelation of [[Nocturnus]] during the fall of [[Olympus]] demonstrated that Erebus had fathered another child with a different Primordial Goddess, providing Nemesis and her siblings with the missing context behind Nyx's hatred and estrangement.[3]

#### Thanatos
Nemesis possesses a complicated but ultimately respectful relationship with [[Thanatos]]. She does not always enjoy his authority or personality, but she recognizes him as the sibling who consistently remained present throughout their immortal existence. That loyalty is enough for Nemesis to listen when Thanatos becomes serious, even when her own Vengeance-driven impulses tell her to act immediately.[1]
Thanatos also understands Nemesis' temperament better than most. After Nyx aggravated her in Chapter 779, [[Persephone]] specifically sent Thanatos to stop Nemesis from demolishing the castle. During the [[Olympus]] battle, he similarly ordered the Furies to support his "foolish sister" the moment Nemesis charged alone into the Olympian army.[6][4]

#### Persephone Elderblood
[[Persephone]] is Nemesis' Queen and direct military superior. Nemesis served among the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] forces maintained by Persephone alongside [[Thanatos]], [[Hypnos]] and the Furies.[1][3]
Although Nemesis is willing to openly criticize Persephone when she believes the Queen is being unrealistic—as demonstrated when Persephone claimed she could eventually deal with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]—this does not prevent her from obeying legitimate orders. Once Persephone commanded her army to slaughter the Olympians, Nemesis immediately entered battle without hesitation.[5][4]

#### Victor Elderblood
Nemesis' relationship with [[Victor Elderblood]] began indirectly. She observed Victor's assault against [[Olympus]] from [[Persephone]]'s side and quickly recognized that his strength had reached a level she could not contend with. When Persephone suggested confronting Victor in the future, Nemesis bluntly admitted that she could not defeat him and considered Persephone's confidence foolish.[5]
Following the destruction of Olympus and Victor's ascension, their relationship changed dramatically. Nemesis became a faithful follower of Victor and one of the major Gods whose Conceptual influence protected his Divine progression. Her dominance over Revenge was sufficient that other Gods understood interference with Victor's development in that Concept could provoke her direct retaliation.[2]
Nyx's later union with Victor additionally made him Nemesis' stepfather in a technical familial sense, although Nemesis' demonstrated loyalty to Victor predates that family connection.

### Skills & Abilities
- God Physiology: Nemesis is an ancient Greek Goddess born directly from the Original [[Primordial Gods]] Nyx and [[Erebus]]. As a Divine Being, she possesses the supernatural body, longevity, [[Divine Energy]] and Conceptual potential inherent to the Gods.
- [[Immortality]]: Nemesis has existed for at least approximately one million years and remains physically unchanged by ordinary aging.[1]
- Superhuman Physical Characteristics: As a Second Generation Primordial Goddess capable of directly joining a battlefield populated by Olympian Gods, [[Titans]] and other Divine combatants, Nemesis possesses physical abilities far beyond ordinary supernatural species.[3][4]
- Divine Energy Manipulation: Nemesis can generate and surround her body with her Divine Power. When [[Persephone]] ordered the destruction of the Olympians, Nemesis immediately covered herself in Power before entering combat.[4]
- [[Divine Presence]]: As one of the ancient descendants of Nyx and Erebus, Nemesis possesses the Divine presence and spiritual existence of a high-ranking God.
- Agelessness: Her physical existence has persisted for immense periods without conventional biological deterioration.[1]
- Second Generation Primordial God Physiology: Nemesis belongs to the generation of Gods directly descended from the Original Primordial Gods of the [[Greek Pantheon]]. Chapter 799 identifies Persephone's forces as including Second Generation Primordial Gods descended from Nyx and Erebus and connected to Concepts such as Death, Dreams and Revenge.[3]
- High Divine Potential: Her direct descent from two Original Primordial Gods grants Nemesis an exceptionally old and powerful Divine lineage.
- Ancient Divinity: Nemesis' Divinity has been developing across an enormous period of time, allowing her to become the dominant Greek authority associated with Revenge.[2]
- Divinity / Concept of Revenge and Vengeance: Nemesis is the Personification of Vengeance within the Greek Divine system and the Goddess who possesses the greatest known influence over the Concept of Revenge among the Greek Gods.[1][2]
- Revenge Manipulation: Nemesis can wield Divine Authority connected to Revenge, allowing her supernatural power to operate through the concept she represents.
- Vengeance Manipulation: Her identity and Divine nature are fundamentally tied to vengeance and retaliation against those she considers deserving of punishment.[1]
- Retribution Manipulation: Nemesis' Divine role includes the punishment and retribution imposed upon targets whose actions provoke her Concept.
- Conceptual Authority: By Chapter 889, Nemesis possessed the greatest influence over the Concept of Revenge among the relevant Gods, placing her at the highest demonstrated level of development for that Divine Concept within her pantheonic sphere.[2]
- Conceptual Influence: High-level Gods can interfere with the development of another God's progress in a Concept. Nemesis' dominance and loyalty to [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] were enough to discourage other Gods associated with Revenge from interfering with Victor's growth.[2]
- Divine Retaliation: The narration explicitly warns that anyone obstructing Victor's progress in Revenge risked having "swift retribution" brought upon them by Nemesis.[2]
Note: The previous article separated Vengeance, Revenge and Retribution into three independent Major Divinities. The reviewed chapters clearly establish Nemesis as the Personification/Goddess of Vengeance and the leading Divine influence over the Concept of Revenge, but do not clearly demonstrate that all three synonymous titles are three mechanically separate Concepts. They are therefore grouped here under her principal Revenge/Vengeance Divinity rather than artificially multiplying her Concepts.)
- Combat Ability: Nemesis is an active Divine combatant rather than a purely conceptual or administrative Goddess. Persephone deployed her as part of the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]]'s army during the battle for [[Olympus]].[3][4]
- Close-Quarters Combat: Nemesis willingly entered directly into the formation of the Olympian Gods once ordered to attack, indicating confidence in fighting other Divine Beings at close range.[4]
- Divine Warfare Experience: Having existed for immense periods as part of the Greek Underworld's Divine hierarchy, Nemesis possesses experience operating among other ancient Gods and supernatural combatants.
- Aggressive Combat Style: Her battle mentality mirrors her personality. Rather than waiting for elaborate preparation, Nemesis immediately launched herself into the Olympian forces the moment Persephone provided a legitimate target.[4]
- Group Combat: [[Thanatos]] assigned the Furies to accompany Nemesis during the attack, allowing her to function as an aggressive frontline combatant while receiving support from other Underworld entities.[4]
- Enhanced Perception / Divine Senses: As an ancient Goddess, Nemesis is capable of observing and evaluating Divine-scale conflicts. She accurately recognized the overwhelming threat Victor represented during his destruction of Olympus rather than allowing pride to cause her to underestimate him.[5]
- High-Level Threat Assessment: Nemesis possesses considerably better judgment than her volatility initially suggests. After witnessing Victor's battlefield, she immediately understood that neither she nor Persephone could safely confront him at that stage and openly said so despite Persephone being her Queen.[5]
- Divine Loyalty / Conceptual Protection: By Chapter 889, Nemesis' allegiance to Victor was strong enough that her reputation itself acted as a deterrent against Gods attempting to interfere with his progression in Revenge.[2]

### Power and Status
Nemesis' exact position within the complete hierarchy of Second Generation [[Primordial Gods]] has never been numerically established. The previous version described her as one of the strongest members of her generation and stated that only Nyx, [[Erebus]], [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]] and [[Gaia]] surpassed her, but the reviewed chapters do not provide a direct ranking sufficient to support that exact claim.
What the novel does establish is that:
- Nemesis is a direct child of Nyx and Erebus, two Original Primordial Gods.[1]
- She belongs to the class of Second Generation Primordial Gods associated with the [[Greek Underworld]].[3]
- She was capable of participating in the final Divine war involving Olympians, [[Titans]] and other Primordial Gods.[3][4]
- She considered [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s Chapter 796 state overwhelmingly beyond her own ability to confront directly.[5]
- By Chapter 889, she possessed the greatest influence over the Concept of Revenge among the Gods relevant to Victor's progression.[2]
Her greatest confirmed distinction is therefore not an unsupported overall combat ranking but her exceptional development and authority over Revenge/Vengeance.
