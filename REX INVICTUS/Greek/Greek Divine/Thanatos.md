### Appearances

#### Debut
Chapter 467
Thanatos is an ancient Greek God, a Second Generation Primordial God, the son of Nyx and [[Erebus]], and the principal Personification of Death within the former [[Greek Pantheon]]. He served the rulers of the [[Greek Underworld]], initially under [[Hades]] and later becoming [[Persephone]]'s most reliable Divine subordinate after she assumed control of the realm.[1]
Thanatos is an exceptionally unusual Death God. While he is not [[True Death]] and explicitly states that Pantheon Death Gods are merely users of the Concept of Death, his connection to Death is profound enough that Nyx refers to him as the Embodiment of Death within the Greek Divine structure. Conventional God-killing methods are unable to permanently destroy him; after [[Scathach Elderblood Scarlett]] completely erased his head with an Anti-God attack, Thanatos' existence reconstructed itself without retaining the injuries, curses or debilitating effects imposed upon him.[1]
Nyx explained that permanently killing Thanatos requires an ability capable of bypassing his conceptual embodiment, specifically citing <END>—which can erase his existence outright—or <SOUL>—which can destroy the Soul supporting his Divine existence.[1] Thanatos himself later clarifies this distinction even further, acknowledging that only the cosmic Regulator True Death deserves to be called The Death, while he and other Death Gods merely wield derivative Divine Concepts associated with Death.[2]
Following the destruction of [[Olympus]], Thanatos remained loyal to Persephone and became incorporated into [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s Faction and the developing Dragons Nest order. His expertise over Death later became useful to Victor directly, including helping create a Divine staff capable of controlling an Aspect of Death.[3]

### Physical Appearance
Thanatos normally appears as a tall humanoid man almost completely concealed beneath a black cloak. His features are usually hidden beneath the dark cloth, emphasizing the traditional Reaper-like imagery associated with his position as the Greek embodiment of Death.
When interacting directly with [[Persephone]], he is described as a cloaked man standing before the Queen of the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]]. The concealment of his physical appearance appears to be habitual rather than evidence that Thanatos lacks a physical form.
His most iconic weapon is a scythe associated directly with his Death Authority. A God killed by Thanatos' scythe can be sent into the greater Soul-judgment process governed by [[The Judges of The Abyss]].[4]

### Personality
Thanatos is normally calm, serious, dutiful and considerably more disciplined than many members of the [[Greek Pantheon]]. He rarely seeks attention and tends to concern himself primarily with his responsibilities rather than the constant political conflicts and personal scandals that characterized [[Olympus]].
His strongest personality trait is his loyalty toward the ruler he recognizes. Under [[Persephone]], Thanatos developed into her most dependable subordinate. By Chapter 778, the narration explicitly states that he had remained beside Persephone throughout her growth as Queen and had become the person she trusted most whenever serious problems arose. When Persephone encountered a threat, Thanatos was normally among the first people she sent to investigate or eliminate it.[1]
He is proactive rather than passive. Thanatos generally prefers eliminating threats before they become serious problems, although he is sufficiently intelligent to recognize when violence is irrational. When Persephone ordered him to deal with Nyx, [[Scathach]], [[Aphrodite]] and Morgana inside the [[Greek Underworld]], Thanatos correctly concluded that direct combat against such a group would be suicidal and prioritized discovering their objective instead.[1]
Thanatos also possesses natural authority among his siblings. [[Nemesis]] may frequently dislike his attitude, but she respects him because he remained present throughout their immortal existence even after Nyx and [[Erebus]]' family broke apart. A single stern command from Thanatos was enough to make the extremely volatile Goddess of Vengeance restrain herself and wait rather than immediately attack Nyx.[1]
His attitude toward danger is normally stoic to the point of arrogance. Shortly before Scathach attacked him, Thanatos declared that he was Death incarnate and therefore did not experience fear. [[Hypnos]] immediately sensed that he would regret saying it. Moments later, Scathach's spear reached Thanatos before he could react, and the only emotion he experienced before his head vanished was genuine fear—apparently the first time he had ever felt it.[1]
The experience did not turn him into a coward, but it made him substantially more cautious regarding Scathach. When he later reported the encounter to Persephone, Thanatos no longer treated the intruders as enemies that could be casually eliminated.[5]
Thanatos is also respectful of personal boundaries. When Persephone later moved away from [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s group to hold a private conversation, Thanatos deliberately chose not to eavesdrop because he considered doing so rude and had little interest in involving himself in matters that did not concern him.[6]
Despite his grim occupation and serious demeanor, Thanatos is capable of surprisingly casual behavior. After Victor temporarily manifested an echo of [[Adonis Snow]], Thanatos agreed to play Uno with Victor and confidently stated that he had never lost at the game.[7] He subsequently became involved in Victor's increasingly bizarre plan to construct a religious Death artifact, demonstrating that he can adapt remarkably well to Victor's unconventional personality.[2][3]

### Nature as a Death God
Thanatos' position requires an important distinction between a God who embodies Death within a Pantheon and the universal Primordial Entity known as [[True Death]].
Chapter 778 repeatedly calls Thanatos the Personification or Embodiment of Death for the Greek Gods. This grants him extraordinary conceptual properties, including the ability to recover from destruction that would permanently kill ordinary Gods.[1]
However, Thanatos later explicitly rejects the claim that he is Death itself:
He is not Death itself. Like the other Pantheon Gods, Thanatos is a user of the Concept of Death, while the Being who can truly be called Death is the primordial Regulator representing Death and END.[2]
This distinction is reinforced by the author's later cosmological explanation. Divine Concepts related to Death and END are linked to Primordial Death, from whose greater cosmic Aspect those Divine Concepts draw their Power.[8]
Thanatos is therefore best understood as:
True Death → Universal primordial source/Aspect of Death and END.
Thanatos → Greek Primordial God wielding an exceptionally developed Divine Concept of Death.
Ordinary Death Gods → Lesser Pantheon Gods whose authority may also correspond to aspects of Death.
This is why the former title "God of True Death" is misleading if interpreted literally. Thanatos can inflict what the narration calls True Death upon other Gods, but he is not the cosmic True Death himself.[9][2]

### Synopsis

#### Discoveries, Changes, And War
Thanatos first appeared while [[Persephone]] was dealing with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s intrusion into matters surrounding the [[Greek Underworld]]. After Persephone returned to her throne, Thanatos stood before her and questioned why she had allowed Victor to leave alive despite the danger he represented.[10]
Thanatos believed Victor should have been eliminated before he became a larger problem and offered his own Death Authority as the means to accomplish it. Persephone nevertheless refused to pursue the matter. Following the disappearance of [[Cerberus]] and the increasing instability surrounding the Underworld, Thanatos remained one of the Divine beings responsible for protecting its entrances and maintaining order beneath Persephone.[10]
The interaction established the fundamental dynamic that would persist throughout Thanatos' later appearances: he served the ruler of the Underworld rather than the politics of [[Olympus]] itself, and he regarded threats primarily according to whether they endangered Persephone's realm.

#### Demonic War
Thanatos became critically important during the crisis created by [[Typhon]] and the collapse of [[Olympus|Mount Olympus]].
With [[Hades]] absent and the [[Greek Underworld]] exposed to enemies, [[Persephone]] and her subordinates became the front line protecting the realm. Thanatos, [[Hypnos]], the Furies and their allies fought the invading forces but were eventually overwhelmed by the number of powerful [[Titans]] operating against them.[9]
The narration emphasized Thanatos' strength during this period. As a Second Generation Primordial God, he was described as extremely powerful and capable of granting [[True Death]] even to a God. A small number of comparable opponents could potentially be handled by him, but confronting an entire army containing ancient Titans descended directly from [[Gaia]] and [[Ouranos]] exceeded even Thanatos' capabilities.[9]
The defeat demonstrated both the scale of Thanatos' Death Authority and its limits. His Concept made him one of the most dangerous individual Gods in the Greek Underworld, but possessing Death did not automatically allow him to defeat numerous enemies operating at the same broad Primordial-God level simultaneously.[9]
Around this period, Persephone's connection to the greater afterlife system was also explained in greater detail. The Judges revealed that a sufficiently powerful Being killed by Thanatos' scythe would not simply disappear automatically. Instead, the deceased Soul could be presented for judgment, after which the Judges would determine whether the Soul ceased to exist or returned to the Cycle of Reincarnation.[4]
This established that Thanatos is a Death God, not the ultimate administrator of Souls. His role is capable of producing or enforcing death, while the final judgment and recycling of Souls belong to The Judges of The Abyss and the wider reincarnation system.[4]

#### King Among Kings
By the time [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction began interfering directly with the Greek crisis, Thanatos had become [[Persephone]]'s most reliable subordinate. Persephone dispatched Thanatos, [[Hypnos]] and [[Nemesis]] to investigate why Nyx, [[Scathach]], [[Aphrodite]] and [[Morgana Elderblood]] had entered the [[Greek Underworld]].[1]
Thanatos immediately recognized the impossibility of the assignment. The group contained his mother Nyx, a Primordial Goddess vastly beyond him; Aphrodite, whose Charm was dangerous even to Gods; Morgana, a former Demon General; and Scathach, a mortal Godslayer whose true capabilities he did not yet understand. Rather than obey Persephone's order recklessly, Thanatos concluded that the priority should be discovering the group's purpose and fighting only if absolutely necessary.[1]
Scathach discovered the hidden Gods before Aphrodite's Divine Senses could locate them. She instantly transformed into her full Vampire Count state and threw [[Gae Bolg]] directly at Thanatos. The attack occurred too quickly for him to react. Gae Bolg pierced his skull and caused his head to vanish from existence while the impact devastated the surrounding Underworld and created a mountain of Ice.[1]
Despite the apparent fatality, Thanatos did not die permanently. Nyx explained that Scathach had chosen the wrong target. Her Anti-God enchantments could permanently kill conventional Gods, but Thanatos was the Greek Embodiment of Death itself. A pillar of morbid Darkness emerged from his body and his existence restored itself completely.[1]
The restoration did more than regenerate his head. Scathach noticed that every secondary effect applied by her weapon—Poisoning, Bleeding, Organ Failure, Bone Destruction, Weakening Curses and Tracking—had also disappeared. Thanatos' existence appeared to have been reset when he returned.[1]
Nyx then revealed the conditions required to kill him permanently: Thanatos would need to have his existence erased through the Concept of END or have his Soul destroyed through the Concept of SOUL or a sufficiently similar ability.[1]
After Nyx casually transported Thanatos and his siblings back to Persephone's castle, Thanatos reported the incident. His experience with Scathach made him considerably more cautious and helped convince Persephone that direct confrontation with Victor's group would be unwise.[5]
During Victor's eventual assault against [[Olympus]], Thanatos and Persephone observed his rapidly escalating Power. The destruction Victor produced forced Thanatos to seriously evaluate whether he could kill him. Thanatos believed he might potentially be capable of dealing with Victor, but he lacked confidence because Victor was both a Vampire Progenitor and a Being capable of directly affecting Souls. Thanatos understood that if Victor managed to sever his Soul, even his special status as a Death God would not save him.[11]
Once [[Zeus]] expanded Olympus' authority into the Underworld, Persephone finally declared war and ordered Thanatos to prepare the entire Underworld army. Thanatos immediately obeyed.[11]
The resulting force included numerous children and descendants of Nyx and [[Erebus]] associated with Death, Dreams, Vengeance and other Underworld Concepts. Thanatos functioned as one of Persephone's principal commanders as the Underworld entered the war against the remaining Olympian forces.[12]
When Persephone gave the final order to kill the Greek Gods while avoiding Victor's forces, Thanatos immediately began distributing commands. He sent the Furies to support Nemesis and instructed Hypnos to perform his own assigned role. Persephone then ordered Thanatos specifically to protect her while she concentrated on destroying the false authority Zeus had imposed over the Underworld with [[Poseidon|the Master]] Bolt.[13]
Following the destruction of the old Greek order, Thanatos remained with Persephone rather than opposing Victor.
Victor later devised a method of temporarily manifesting an informational echo of [[Adonis Snow]] from the [[Akashic Records]]. The ritual required multiple elements: access to the Greek Underworld's Soul infrastructure, Victor's exceptional Soul precision, Persephone's Ruler Authority and Thanatos' connection to Death. Thanatos helped anchor the temporary manifestation while Victor reconstructed enough of Adonis' recorded existence to allow him to interact with his family for several hours.[7]
Once the ritual succeeded, Thanatos' normally grim life took an unexpectedly mundane turn. Victor casually challenged him to Uno. Thanatos accepted and claimed that he had never lost. Victor confidently promised to become his first defeat, only for Thanatos to ultimately preserve his record and defeat Victor.[7][2]
During their conversation, Victor questioned the nature of Thanatos' Death Authority. Thanatos explicitly corrected the misunderstanding that he was Death itself, explaining that he and other Pantheon Death Gods were merely users of the Concept. He identified [[True Death]] as the only existence entitled to be called Death itself and openly acknowledged that he was insignificant compared with the cosmic Primordial.[2]
Victor then proposed another project: an artifact using Death-related properties to allow living mortals to interact temporarily with echoes of deceased loved ones. Thanatos immediately recognized that the proposed artifact would not truly possess Death itself; instead, it would manipulate lesser aspects of Death and Souls without giving its owner unrestricted authority over the Concept. He agreed to participate, with [[Hephaestus]] later assisting in its construction.[2]
Thanatos remained near Persephone during the aftermath of the Greek war. When Persephone moved away to hold a private conversation, both Thanatos and Aphrodite noticed her departure. Thanatos nevertheless deliberately refrained from listening because he considered eavesdropping rude and was not interested enough to violate her privacy.[6]

#### Dragon God of Chaos
Thanatos' integration into [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction became clearer after the family completed its reorganization.
Victor eventually finished the Death-related artifact he had discussed with Thanatos. Working alongside Thanatos and [[Hephaestus]], Victor created a staff capable of controlling an Aspect of Death. Victor originally intended to place only a small quantity of Power within it but ultimately strengthened the artifact significantly and incorporated an Aspect of his own existence as well.[3]
Thanatos' participation was essential because the project required genuine Death-related Divine knowledge rather than Victor simply imitating the concept from observation. The resulting artifact could interact with Souls and the dead without equating its owner with an actual Death God.
By this period, Thanatos was no longer operating as an independent Greek deity defending an isolated [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]]. He had become part of the much larger supernatural structure surrounding Victor, [[Persephone]] and the newly established Dragons Nest.

#### God Emperor Victor Elderblood
Thanatos was later used as one of the principal benchmarks for Death affinity when [[Hela Elderblood]]'s unusual nature was explained.
Hela possessed the ability to sense when an individual was approaching death or would cease to exist. The narration explicitly compared this talent with both Thanatos and the [[Heavenly Father]]'s Angel of Death, establishing that Thanatos possesses a similar form of Death perception or precognition. Hela's version was described as even more abnormal because of the circumstances surrounding her birth.[14]
This comparison confirms that Thanatos' connection to Death extends beyond simply killing targets. He can perceive approaching death as a supernatural phenomenon, although Hela later surpassed him in this particular application.[14]

### Relationships

#### Nyx Elderblood
Nyx is Thanatos' mother and one of the Original [[Primordial Gods]] of Greece.
Despite Thanatos becoming an immensely powerful God in his own right, he remained completely incapable of deceiving Nyx's perception. During his attempt to observe her group covertly in the [[Greek Underworld]], Nyx casually noticed him almost immediately, much to his frustration.[1]
Thanatos respects the overwhelming difference between himself and his mother. When [[Hypnos]] admitted that he could not imagine fighting Nyx, Thanatos did not dispute the assessment. Nyx subsequently treated Thanatos, Hypnos and [[Nemesis]] like disobedient children and transported all three back to [[Persephone]]'s castle with virtually no effort.[1]
The author later confirmed that this enormous difference was intentional. Although Thanatos and Nyx both belong broadly within the Primordial-God level, the gap between an original Primordial Goddess such as Nyx and a descendant such as Thanatos was described as comparable to Heaven and Earth.[15]

#### Erebus
[[Erebus]] was Thanatos' father and the Original Primordial God associated with Darkness.
Thanatos and his siblings originally remembered a time when Nyx and Erebus functioned as a united family. Their eventual separation remained unexplained to the children for an extremely long time because neither parent discussed the circumstances publicly.[1]
The appearance of [[Nocturnus]] during the final conflict revealed Erebus' hidden relationship with another Primordial Goddess and exposed one of the central betrayals that had destroyed the old family.[12]

#### Hypnos
[[Hypnos]] is one of Thanatos' closest siblings.
The two frequently work together and possess enough familiarity to understand one another's habits instinctively. Thanatos trusts Hypnos' abnormal sixth sense, particularly after that instinct became stronger through Hypnos' connection with [[Morpheus]].[1]
Immediately before [[Scathach]] attacked Thanatos, Hypnos warned that he had an extremely bad feeling concerning Thanatos' declaration that Death could not feel fear. Thanatos took the warning seriously, although he was struck before he could determine what the premonition meant.[1]
During [[Persephone]]'s war against [[Olympus]], Thanatos issued battlefield instructions to Hypnos, demonstrating that he often acts as the operational commander among the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] siblings.[13]

#### Nemesis
[[Nemesis]] is Thanatos' sister.
Although Nemesis frequently finds Thanatos irritating, she genuinely respects him because he remained present throughout their immortal lives after their family fell apart. His authority is strong enough to make the otherwise impulsive Goddess of Vengeance restrain herself when he speaks seriously.[1]
Thanatos understands Nemesis' temperament equally well. When [[Persephone]] ordered the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] army into battle, Nemesis immediately charged the Olympians alone. Thanatos responded by calling her his "foolish sister" and immediately assigning the Furies to support her before she got herself surrounded.[13]

#### Persephone Elderblood
[[Persephone]] is Thanatos' Queen and the person toward whom he displays his strongest demonstrated loyalty.
Thanatos remained beside Persephone as she developed from the politically trapped Queen of the [[Greek Underworld]] into its legitimate independent Ruler. Chapter 778 explicitly identifies him as her most reliable subordinate.[1]
Persephone routinely entrusts him with investigations, battlefield commands and personal protection. Thanatos responds to her instructions without questioning her legitimacy and continued serving her even after the collapse of the old [[Greek Pantheon]].[1][13]
He also respects her privacy. When Persephone participated in private family discussions after the Greek conflict, Thanatos intentionally refrained from listening despite possessing the opportunity to do so.[6]

#### Hades
[[Hades]] was Thanatos' former ruler when Hades still governed the [[Greek Underworld]].
Thanatos' loyalty appears directed more strongly toward the legitimate ruler of the Underworld than toward [[Olympus]] itself. Once Hades abandoned his former position and [[Persephone]] became the true Ruler, Thanatos' allegiance transferred naturally to her rather than following Hades elsewhere.

#### Victor Elderblood
Thanatos initially regarded [[Victor Elderblood]] as a dangerous external threat.
During his debut, he believed [[Persephone]] should have killed Victor before his power grew further.[10] His assessment changed dramatically after witnessing Victor's development. By Chapter 796, Thanatos admitted that he could not confidently defeat Victor because Victor possessed exactly the type of Soul-affecting power capable of bypassing Thanatos' unusual immortality.[11]
After Victor conquered the [[Greek Pantheon]], their relationship became surprisingly cooperative. Thanatos helped Victor manifest Adonis' temporary Akashic echo, played Uno with him, discussed the nature of Death and agreed to help with Victor's Death-artifact project.[7][2]
Victor later worked directly with Thanatos and [[Hephaestus]] to complete a staff containing an Aspect of Death.[3]
Nyx's later relationship with Victor additionally made Victor Thanatos' stepfather in a technical familial sense, although their principal relationship remains that of liege and Divine subordinate.

### Power and Status
Thanatos is one of the stronger Second Generation [[Primordial Gods]] associated with the former [[Greek Pantheon]], but the previous version of the article overstated his position by claiming that he was stronger than virtually every God of his generation and surpassed only by Nyx, [[Erebus]], [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]] and [[Gaia]].
The source material establishes a more nuanced position.
The author's original classification explicitly places Thanatos and [[Hypnos]] among the Primordial-God class alongside the Titan descendants of Gaia. However, being within the same broad rank does not make all such Gods equal. The author specifically uses Thanatos as the example when explaining the enormous gap between an original Primordial Goddess and her descendant:
the difference between Nyx and Thanatos is like Heaven and Earth.[15]
Chapter 623 nevertheless portrays Thanatos as exceptionally dangerous even among Divine opponents. He could grant [[True Death]] to Gods and was considered capable of handling a limited number of other Primordial-class enemies. His defeat came only when [[Persephone]]'s faction faced an entire group of [[Titans]] and external enemies simultaneously.[9]
Thanatos should therefore be treated as a highly dangerous Second Generation Primordial God whose Concept grants him unusual lethality and survivability, rather than given an unsupported fixed rank above every peer.

### Notes
- Thanatos is a Second Generation Primordial God, not a Primordial Entity.
- Chapter 623 loosely calls him a "Second-Generation Primordial Entity", but the author's formal power explanation classifies Thanatos as a Primordial God, while true [[Primordial Entities]] are the seven universal Regulators.[9][15]
- Thanatos is the principal Personification of Death for the Greek Gods but openly acknowledges that his existence is insignificant compared with [[True Death]].[2]
- His Divine Concept of Death ultimately belongs to the larger primordial framework associated with Primordial Death.[8]
- Nyx explicitly states that ordinary Anti-God effects capable of permanently killing other Gods are insufficient to permanently eliminate Thanatos.[1]
- His regeneration after [[Scathach]]'s attack is more accurately described as an existential reset/restoration than simple healing because all injuries and supernatural debuffs disappeared simultaneously.[1]
- A Soul destroyed by SOUL-like Authority cannot be restored through Thanatos' ordinary Death embodiment.[1]
- Thanatos' scythe does not automatically erase every victim from existence; powerful dead can still be judged by the Abyssal Judges and potentially reincarnated.[4]
- By Chapter 778, Thanatos had become [[Persephone]]'s most reliable subordinate.[1]
- His willingness to avoid fighting Nyx's group demonstrates that, despite his lethal Concept, Thanatos is not recklessly arrogant when presented with an obvious disadvantage.[1]
- Later comparisons confirm that Thanatos possesses an ability to perceive approaching Death, although Hela's version is superior.[14]

### Trivia
- Thanatos once confidently declared that, as Death incarnate, he could not feel fear. Seconds later, [[Scathach]]'s spear reached his face before he could react, causing him to experience genuine fear for apparently the first time.[1]
- [[Hypnos]] sensed that Thanatos would immediately regret claiming he could not feel fear.[1]
- Scathach's attack completely erased Thanatos' head and would have permanently killed an ordinary God, but Thanatos returned without lasting damage.[1]
- Even the Poison, Bleeding, Organ Failure, Bone Destruction, Weakening and Tracking effects on [[Gae Bolg]] vanished when his existence restored itself.[1]
- Thanatos refers to [[Nemesis]] as his "foolish sister" while nevertheless immediately ensuring the Furies support her when she charges into battle.[13]
- Thanatos was one of the Beings responsible for helping [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] temporarily manifest an echo of Adonis from the [[Akashic Records]].[7]
- He claimed to have never lost at Uno and subsequently maintained that reputation by defeating Victor.[7][2]
- Thanatos reacted with disbelief when Victor proposed creating a "Death Note"-style artifact and immediately accused him of trying to get sued.[2]
- The conversation surrounding the artifact produced one of the clearest distinctions between Pantheon Death Gods and [[True Death]], with Thanatos himself explaining that he is merely a user of Death rather than Death itself.[2]
- Thanatos later worked with Victor and [[Hephaestus]] to turn the absurd idea into an actual Divine artifact capable of manipulating an Aspect of Death.[3]
- Despite his intimidating appearance and association with death, Thanatos considers secretly listening to another person's private conversation rude.[6]
- Hela's supernatural ability to perceive approaching death is explicitly described as more abnormal than the corresponding abilities possessed by Thanatos and the Biblical Angel of Death.[14]
