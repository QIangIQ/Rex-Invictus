The Greek Pantheon was one of the principal Divine Pantheons associated with Earth and one of the oldest and most politically significant supernatural civilizations shown in the story. Its Divine society was composed of several generations of Gods, including the original [[Primordial Gods]], [[Titans]], Olympians, Chthonic Gods and numerous lesser Divine beings. The Pantheon was divided primarily between the celestial territory centered on [[Olympus|Mount Olympus]] and the [[Greek Underworld]], with the two regions possessing separate rulers, political factions and Divine populations despite belonging to the same overall Pantheon.[1][2][3]
The Pantheon's history was characterized by repeated internal succession wars. [[Ouranos]] originally ruled the celestial side alongside [[Gaia]], but the mistreatment of their children led Gaia to assist Kronus in overthrowing him. Kronus subsequently betrayed Gaia and maintained the imprisonment of several of his siblings, causing her to assist his children in overthrowing him as well. [[Zeus]] then became King of Olympus but eventually repeated the same pattern by betraying Gaia and imprisoning the defeated Titans and several other ancient beings in [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]].[1][4]
By the modern era, Zeus remained the recognized God-King of Olympus while [[Hades]] ruled the Greek Underworld. Hades later abandoned the Pantheon during the Demonic War, allowing [[Persephone Elderblood]] to become the true Ruler of the Underworld.[2] The release of Kronus, the Titans and [[Typhon]], together with the political fragmentation of Olympus, eventually plunged the Pantheon into a massive civil conflict.[4]
The Greek Pantheon was ultimately destroyed during Victor's campaign against Olympus. After Greek Primordial Gods and Olympians targeted Nyx Elderblood, [[Scathach Elderblood Scarlett]], [[Aphrodite]] Elderblood and [[Morgana Elderblood]] inside the Underworld, Victor Elderblood declared the Pantheon's destruction and personally fought its combined Divine forces.[5][6] Between Chapters 795 and 804, Victor and his allies killed large numbers of Greek Gods, defeated Zeus and Kronus, destroyed or captured multiple Primordial Gods, subjugated the surviving Olympians and effectively ended Olympus as an independent Pantheon.[7][8][9][10][11][12][13][14][15]
Victor later used the damaged Greek Pantheon Dimension as the foundation for his own Divine civilization. In Chapter 821, the [[Akashic Records]] restored and expanded the damaged Dimension using Victor's Energy, formally erased the old Greek Pantheon identity and renamed the structure Pantheon 7475909777, which later became known as the [[Dragons Nest Pantheon]].[16]

### Overview
The Greek Pantheon was not governed as one completely unified Divine kingdom. Its structure developed from several extremely ancient Divine families and territories whose relationships changed repeatedly throughout history. The celestial side of the Pantheon was centered on [[Olympus|Mount Olympus]] and was traditionally controlled by the reigning God-King, while the [[Greek Underworld]] possessed its own ruling authority, native Gods and administrative systems governing Souls.[2][3]
This division became especially important during the Pantheon's final war. By that period, Olympus and the Underworld were no longer politically aligned. [[Persephone]]'s Underworld forces actively fought the Olympian army, while several former Olympian Goddesses had already abandoned [[Zeus]] and joined [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction.[10][11]
The Greek Pantheon also contained several generations of Gods with vastly different origins and levels of authority. The oldest Gods such as [[Gaia]], Nyx, [[Erebus]], [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]] and [[Ouranos]] belonged to the first generation of the Pantheon, while the [[Titans]]—including Kronus and [[Rhea]]—formed a later generation. The Olympian dynasty descended primarily from Kronus and Rhea and eventually replaced the Titans as the dominant ruling faction.[1][5][9]
Although Greek mythology often describes the oldest Gods as literal embodiments of their Concepts, the novel later clarifies that they are Divine users and representatives of those Concepts within their Pantheon, not universal conceptual embodiments comparable to the [[Primordial Entities]]. Victor explicitly corrects Kronus on this distinction during their battle.[9]

### Divine Generations
The Greek Gods are separated broadly into several generations according to their ancestry and historical appearance.

#### First Generation Gods
The First Generation Gods are the oldest Divine beings belonging to the Greek Pantheon and form part of the foundation upon which later Greek Divine society developed. They include beings such as:
- [[Gaia]] Elderblood — Primordial Goddess associated with Earth, Nature, Life, Fertility and Motherhood.
- [[Ouranos]] — Primordial God associated with the Sky, Heaven and Creation.
- Nyx Elderblood — Primordial Goddess of Night.
- [[Erebus]] — Primordial God associated with Darkness.
- [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]] — Primordial God associated with the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]]/Abyss.
Gaia is specifically identified as the first Goddess born within the Greek Pantheon.[13] These beings predate the [[Titans]] and Olympians and possess authority deeply connected to the fundamental structure of their Pantheon.
The designation "Primordial God" should not be confused with [[Primordial Entities]]. Greek [[Primordial Gods]] possess Divinity over extremely fundamental Concepts but are not literal universal incarnations of those Concepts.[9]

#### Second Generation Gods
The Second Generation consists primarily of Gods born from or descended directly from the first generation, most prominently the [[Titans]] and several children of Nyx and [[Erebus]].
Important examples include:
- Kronus — God of Time and King of the Titans.[9]
- [[Rhea]] — Goddess of Motherhood and Fertility.
- [[Atlas]]
- [[Helios]]
- [[Prometheus]]
- [[Aphrodite]] Elderblood
- [[Thanatos]]
- [[Hypnos]]
- [[Nemesis]]
- [[Nocturnus]]
Although these beings belong to the same broad Divine generation, their strength varies enormously. Descendants of First Generation Gods can still possess Pantheon-defining Concepts, as demonstrated when the death of Kronus caused the progression of Time inside the Greek Pantheon to cease functioning normally.[9]

#### Third Generation Gods
The Third Generation contains most of the classical Olympians and their contemporaries, including [[Zeus]], [[Hades]], [[Poseidon]], [[Hestia]], [[Hera]], [[Demeter]], [[Athena]], [[Ares]], [[Apollo]], [[Artemis]], [[Hermes]] and numerous other Gods.
Many of these Gods became the principal rulers and administrators of the Greek civilization after Zeus defeated Kronus and the [[Titans]].[17][4]
Despite belonging to a younger generation, several Third Generation Gods became extremely powerful through accumulated Faith, Divine Concepts, Divine Weapons and governmental authority. Zeus, Poseidon and Hades each reached God-King-level political or combat status despite not belonging to the original Primordial generation.

### Major Territories

#### Mount Olympus
[[Olympus|Mount Olympus]] was the principal celestial territory and political capital of the Greek Pantheon. It served as the seat of the reigning God-King and the primary residence of the Olympian Gods.
Olympus was successively ruled by:
1. [[Ouranos]]
2. Kronus
3. [[Zeus]]
The territory contained the principal institutions and Divine population associated with the Olympian faction. Zeus's authority over Olympus was reinforced by his status as God-King and by [[Poseidon|the Master]] Bolt, one of the most powerful Divine Weapons belonging to the Pantheon.[8][9]
During the Pantheon's final conflict, Zeus shattered the Master Bolt in an attempt to force the surrounding [[Greek Underworld]] into an extension of Olympus' Territory. The action temporarily gave his faction greater access to their Divine authority but dramatically damaged both his Symbol of Power and the Pantheon's stability.[8][11]

#### Greek Underworld
The [[Greek Underworld]] was the afterlife territory belonging to the Greek Pantheon and functioned as a separate political and metaphysical region beneath the celestial government. It contained numerous Chthonic Gods and Divine beings related to Death, Dreams, [[Ghosts]], Revenge and other Negative-associated Concepts.[3]
Known major inhabitants included:
- [[Hades]]
- [[Persephone Elderblood]]
- Nyx Elderblood
- [[Erebus]]
- [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]]
- [[Thanatos]]
- [[Hypnos]]
- [[Nemesis]]
- [[Hecate]]
- [[Melinoë]]
- [[Macaria]]
- The Furies
- Numerous descendants of Nyx and Erebus
Hades originally served as King and Ruler of the Underworld. After he abandoned the Greek faction and aligned himself with [[Diablo]]'s side, Nyx attempted to elevate [[Persephone]] into the true Ruler capable of maintaining the realm independently of Hades.[2] Persephone eventually underwent the test of the Judges of The Abyss, became the true Ruler of the Underworld and gained authority over its Soul system.[2]
By the final Greek war, the Underworld was effectively hostile to [[Zeus]]. When Zeus forcibly imposed [[Olympus]]' authority over the region using [[Poseidon|the Master]] Bolt, Persephone interpreted it as a direct violation of her sovereignty and ordered her entire Underworld army to attack the Olympians while avoiding [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction.[10][11]

#### Tartarus
[[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]] existed as one of the deepest and most dangerous regions connected to the [[Greek Underworld]]. It functioned historically as a prison for defeated Divine beings and Monsters and was also associated directly with the Primordial God Tartarus.
[[Gaia]], the defeated [[Titans]], Hecatonchires and numerous other beings were imprisoned there following [[Zeus]]'s consolidation of power.[1][4] During her imprisonment, Gaia continued producing children and Monsters together with Tartarus for the purpose of eventually destroying [[Olympus]].[10]

### History

#### Era of Ouranos
The earliest known ruling order of the Greek Pantheon centered on [[Gaia]] Elderblood and [[Ouranos]]. Gaia was the first Goddess born within the Pantheon, while Ouranos eventually became the ruler associated with Heaven and the Sky.[13]
Their children included the [[Titans]], [[Cyclopes]] and Hecatonchires. Ouranos came to regard the Hecatonchires as repulsive and imprisoned them in [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]]. Gaia was deeply angered by his treatment of their children and began conspiring against him.[1]
Gaia eventually supported Kronus, one of their Titan children, in overthrowing Ouranos. The rebellion succeeded and Kronus replaced his father as ruler.

#### Reign of Kronus
Kronus became King of the [[Titans]] and ruler of [[Olympus]]. However, despite [[Gaia]]'s support, he continued several of the policies that had caused her to oppose [[Ouranos]] and failed to keep the promises he had made to her.[1][4]
Kronus also feared that his own children would eventually overthrow him. His actions against [[Zeus]] and his siblings ultimately reproduced the same cycle of generational betrayal that had ended Ouranos's reign.
Gaia therefore supported another rebellion, this time assisting Zeus and the younger generation against Kronus and the Titans.[1]

#### Titanomachy and Rise of Zeus
[[Zeus]] and his siblings eventually fought Kronus and the [[Titans]] in the conflict that established the Olympian order. [[Hades]], [[Poseidon]], [[Hestia]], [[Demeter]], [[Hera]] and Zeus belonged to the generation that emerged from Kronus and [[Rhea]] and participated in the transition to Olympian rule.[17][4]
After the Titans were defeated, Zeus became God-King and ruler of [[Olympus]], Poseidon gained authority over the seas, and Hades ruled the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]].[17]
[[Gaia]] expected the younger Gods to treat her and her children differently after helping them defeat Kronus, but Zeus eventually betrayed her as well. Zeus launched a wider war against the Titans and imprisoned Gaia, the Titans who refused submission and several ancient beings in [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]].[1]
This second betrayal transformed Gaia into one of Olympus' most persistent enemies.

#### Gaia's Retaliation
While imprisoned, [[Gaia]] and [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]] created additional beings and Monsters intended to eventually destroy [[Olympus]].[10] The most important of these was [[Typhon]], an apocalyptic creature possessing a Concept connected to the END and considered one of the greatest threats the Greek Gods could face.
Gaia's hatred was not limited exclusively to [[Zeus]]. She also retained resentment toward Kronus because he had previously betrayed her after overthrowing [[Ouranos]].[1][4]
Consequently, when the later civil war placed Zeus and Kronus against one another, Gaia had little reason to genuinely support either faction.

#### Olympian Era
[[Zeus]]'s reign lasted for an enormous period and established the political order most commonly associated with the Greek Pantheon. The Twelve Olympians and other Gods governed the celestial side, while [[Hades]] controlled the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] and other major Gods held independent territories such as [[Poseidon]]'s [[Atlantis]].
The Greek Pantheon nevertheless suffered from severe internal dysfunction. Several Gods abused their authority, interfered continuously with Mortals and other supernatural populations, and treated weaker beings as objects of personal desire. This behavior produced extensive resentment among their own relatives and subjects.[2][1]
Not every Greek God behaved identically. Goddesses such as [[Hestia]] Elderblood, [[Rhea]], Nike, [[Demeter]] and several others were specifically viewed by [[Aphrodite]] as useful or comparatively decent members worth rescuing when [[Olympus]] began collapsing.[1]
The older page's description that the entire Pantheon was simply "known for degeneracy" is therefore too broad. A large portion of its ruling class was indeed notorious for arrogance and abuse, but the Pantheon contained thousands of Gods and several distinct political factions whose behavior differed considerably.

#### Division between Olympus and the Underworld
The relationship between [[Zeus]] and [[Hades]] remained politically tense. Hades was extremely powerful and controlled a realm containing resources and treasures that [[Olympus]] could not easily match, while Zeus's distrust contributed to the isolation of the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]].[2]
The situation worsened when Hades entered an alliance connected to [[Diablo]] during the Demonic War. His actions effectively abandoned his responsibilities to the Greek Pantheon and left the Underworld without a trustworthy Ruler.[2]
Nyx subsequently approached [[Persephone]] and pushed her toward becoming the true Ruler of the Greek Underworld. Persephone initially resisted but ultimately accepted the position and passed the Judges' trial, gaining genuine Ruler authority.[2]
This marked a major political break between the celestial and Underworld factions.

#### Release of the Titans
During the Demonic War, Kronus and several imprisoned [[Titans]], [[Cyclopes]] and Hecatonchires escaped [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]] and attacked [[Olympus]].[4]
The resulting conflict created multiple simultaneous factions:
- [[Zeus]] and the Olympian Gods.
- Kronus and the Titans.
- [[Gaia]] and her creations.
- [[Persephone]] and the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]].
- Various Gods attempting to escape or remain neutral.
The situation became even more unstable when [[Typhon]] appeared.[4]
Rather than continuing their civil war normally, both Olympians and Titans recognized that Typhon represented an existential threat capable of destroying indiscriminately.

#### Exodus of the Goddesses
During the instability surrounding [[Typhon]], [[Aphrodite]] Elderblood returned to [[Olympus]] specifically to remove Goddesses she believed were worth saving.[1]
Her targets included Gods and Goddesses whose Concepts were useful outside direct combat, including:
- [[Hestia]] Elderblood
- [[Rhea]]
- [[Demeter]]
- Nike
- Other non-hostile or useful Goddesses
Aphrodite openly stated that she had no intention of saving the majority of the male Olympian Gods and intended to recruit the Goddesses into [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction.[1]
This event weakened Olympus further and established a large population of former Greek Goddesses inside Victor's growing faction before the Pantheon's final destruction.

### Final War of the Greek Pantheon
The Pantheon's final collapse occurs primarily across Chapters 794–804.

#### Ambush in the Underworld
The decisive conflict began when Nyx, [[Scathach]], [[Aphrodite]] and Morgana entered the [[Greek Underworld]] and encountered a conspiracy involving [[Erebus]], [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]], [[Zeus]], Kronus and allied forces.[5]
Erebus appeared and attempted to suppress Nyx's powers using a fragment associated with the World Tree, drastically reducing her authority.[5] Tartarus likewise entered the confrontation, while Zeus, Kronus and other Gods were revealed as part of the forces gathered against them.
Scathach used an emergency method to call [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]].
Victor immediately entered the Underworld and attacked Erebus and Tartarus, transforming the incident from an ambush into a full-scale war.[5]

#### Victor declares Olympus' destruction
After understanding that the Greek faction had deliberately targeted members of his family, [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] declared that the entire Pantheon would be destroyed.[6]
He activated Blood Age, spreading Blood and Negative Energy throughout the battlefield while creating armies and constructs from the Souls accumulated inside himself. Victor's forces included summoned Dragons, deceased warriors and Gods converted into his Immortal Army.[6][7]
The Pantheon's Gods began dying permanently because Victor's attacks damaged or severed their Souls rather than merely destroying their Divine bodies.[7]
The dead Gods were then immediately added to Victor's Immortal Army, turning each Greek casualty into another soldier fighting against [[Olympus]].[7]

#### Destruction of the Greek army
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s allies simultaneously entered the battle. [[Scathach]] fought and defeated Greek Gods with her martial abilities, Morgana participated using her Demonic powers, [[Medusa]] summoned [[Gorgons]] seeking revenge, and Victor's Maids received weapons capable of killing Gods.[7]
The Greek forces rapidly collapsed.
[[Atlas]] reported that the Titan faction had already lost the overwhelming majority of its army during Victor's assault.[8]
[[Zeus]] became increasingly desperate and shattered [[Poseidon|the Master]] Bolt, sacrificing his own Symbol of Power to impose [[Olympus]]' Territory upon the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]].[8]
The act alarmed even [[Erebus]] and [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]] because destroying such an important Divine Weapon weakened the Pantheon and threatened its long-term survival.[8]

#### Death of Zeus and Kronus
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] personally confronted [[Zeus]] and Kronus in Chapter 798.[9]
Despite their status as former and current [[God-Kings]], neither was capable of defeating him. Victor rejected Kronus's claim to conceptual supremacy and explained that Greek [[Primordial Gods]] are not literal embodiments of their Concepts in the same manner as the true [[Primordial Entities]].[9]
Kronus was ultimately killed when Victor destroyed his body and removed his heart. His death had immediate metaphysical consequences because Kronus possessed the Pantheon's major Divine authority over Time; the continued progression of Time inside the Greek Pantheon effectively ceased, leaving the Dimension trapped around its present state.[9]
Zeus was likewise defeated and killed during the collapse of the Olympian forces.[9]
Their deaths eliminated both principal claimants to the throne of [[Olympus]].

#### Persephone enters the war
[[Zeus]]'s decision to overwrite part of the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] with [[Olympus]]' Territory enraged [[Persephone]], who possessed true Ruler authority over the region.[11]
She ordered her forces to kill the Greek Olympians while explicitly instructing them not to attack [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s group.[11]
Her army included numerous children and descendants of Nyx and [[Erebus]], particularly Gods associated with Death, Dreams, Revenge and related Concepts.[10]
The Olympian remnants therefore became trapped between Victor's Immortal Army and the Greek Underworld's own Divine army.[10]

#### Battle against the Primordial Gods
[[Erebus]], [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]] and [[Nocturnus]] continued fighting [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] after the Olympian armies had largely collapsed.[10][12]
The battle escalated far beyond an ordinary Divine conflict. Their combined attacks involved Darkness, Space, Void and other fundamental Divine powers, while [[Gaia]] reinforced the surrounding Earth to prevent the battle from destroying the entire environment.[11]
Victor nevertheless continued overpowering them.
Tartarus was eventually destroyed and consumed, while Erebus and Nocturnus were defeated and their Souls ultimately fell under Victor's control.[12][13][15]

#### Gaia and Typhon
[[Gaia]] personally entered the conflict when [[Scathach]] attempted to interfere with matters connected to her family.[13]
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] immediately recognized that Gaia had placed a mark on Scathach's Soul, provoking him further.[13]
After being openly insulted and challenged by Victor, Gaia summoned [[Typhon]].[13]
Typhon's arrival terrified the surviving Greek forces because the creature possessed an END-related nature and had historically represented one of [[Olympus]]' greatest apocalyptic threats.
Following Victor's transformation into the Primogenitor of the [[Blood Dragons]], however, Typhon was no longer capable of meaningfully threatening him. Victor removed the creature from the battlefield, while Gaia ultimately abandoned resistance and was taken into Victor's Personal Universe.[14]

#### Surrender of Olympus
By Chapter 804, the old Greek military structure had completely collapsed.
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] announced that [[Olympus]] would fall and sealed the Dimension, preventing the remaining Gods from escaping.[15]
When one God attempted to reject Victor's authority, Victor immediately killed him. The surviving Olympian Gods subsequently fell to their knees and surrendered collectively.[15]
Victor chose not to immediately annihilate every [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] God because [[Persephone]]'s faction had assisted his side during the war. Instead, he declared that the Greek Underworld would be conquered and integrated into his own Hell rather than simply destroyed.[15]
The surrender in Chapter 804 effectively marks the political death of the Greek Pantheon.

### Destruction and Replacement
After the war, [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] possessed complete control over the Pantheon's Dimension and surviving Divine population.
The Dimension itself had been heavily damaged by the deaths of several Gods whose Concepts supported its internal systems, the destruction of Divine territories and the catastrophic battles involving Victor, [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]], [[Erebus]], [[Gaia]] and the other [[Primordial Gods]].[9][11][13]
Victor subsequently decided to use the Dimension as the foundation for a completely new Pantheon.
In Chapters 820–821, Victor and several of his allies constructed an enormous artificial world intended to serve as the center of his new civilization. Gaia Elderblood, Nyx Elderblood, [[Roxanne Elderblood]], [[Jeanne Elderblood]], Natalia Elderblood Alioth and others contributed to the planet's environmental and dimensional construction.[18][16]
Victor then invoked his authority as the owner of the Greek Pantheon Dimension and declared that [[Olympus]] would cease to exist and that a new Pantheon would rise from its remains.[16]
The [[Akashic Records]] analyzed the damaged Dimension, consumed approximately 95% of Victor's available Energy to repair and expand it, and formally changed the Greek Pantheon's designation to:
Pantheon 7475909777[16]
This became the foundation of the later [[Dragons Nest Pantheon]].
The Greek Pantheon therefore did not merely change rulers. Its old political identity, ruling dynasty and Pantheon designation were deliberately erased.

### Related Species and Beings
The Greek Pantheon contained or interacted closely with several supernatural species and Divine races.

#### Titans
[[Titans|The Titans]] were Divine descendants of [[Gaia]] and [[Ouranos]] and formed one of the earliest ruling populations of the Pantheon.

#### Cyclopes
The [[Cyclopes]] were ancient children connected to [[Gaia]]'s lineage and became involved in several of the conflicts between the old generations of Gods.

#### Hecatonchires
The Hecatonchires were among [[Gaia]] and [[Ouranos]]'s children. Their imprisonment by Ouranos was one of the principal causes of Gaia's rebellion against him.[1]
They later participated in the cycles of conflict between the [[Titans]] and Olympians.

#### Gorgons
The [[Gorgons]] were a supernatural species connected historically to the Greek Gods, most importantly through [[Medusa]] Elderblood. Their relationship with [[Olympus]] became extremely hostile because of the abuse suffered by Medusa and the later persecution of her people.
Medusa eventually joined [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] and directly participated in the destruction of the Greek Pantheon, summoning Gorgons to take revenge against the Greek Gods.[7]

#### Amazons
The Amazons maintained relationships with several Greek Gods and Goddesses and operated within the broader political sphere influenced by the Pantheon.
Their later allegiance became more closely associated with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction through [[Meya Neyku]] and related developments.

### Divine Concepts and Pantheon Dependence
The Greek Pantheon demonstrates how Divine Concepts can become structurally important to a Pantheon without the Gods themselves being universal embodiments of those Concepts.
When Kronus died in Chapter 798, his Divine authority over Time had become sufficiently fundamental to the Greek Pantheon's structure that the normal progression of Time ceased functioning there.[9]
This does not mean Kronus was the literal universal Concept of Time. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] explicitly explains that only the true [[Primordial Entities]] can properly be treated as embodiments of fundamental universal Concepts; Greek [[Primordial Gods]] instead possess Divine authority over those Concepts within the framework of their Pantheon.[9]
This distinction is one of the most important examples for understanding the difference between Gods and Primordial Entities.

### Divine Weapons
The Greek Pantheon possessed numerous powerful Divine Artifacts, several of which played major roles in its political and military history.
Known examples include:
- [[Master Bolt]] — [[Zeus]]'s principal Divine Weapon and Symbol of Power.
- [[Poseidon]]'s Trident — Divine Weapon belonging to Poseidon.
- Helm of [[Hades]] — Divine Artifact associated with Hades.
- Various weapons created by [[Hephaestus]].
The Master Bolt was particularly important because it greatly increased Zeus's authority and combat capability. During the final war, Zeus deliberately shattered it to temporarily impose [[Olympus]]' Territory over part of the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]].[8]
Destroying such a central Symbol of Power was considered an act of desperation and contributed to the weakening of the Pantheon.[8]

### Relationship with Humanity
Like most major Earth Pantheons, the Greek Gods historically accumulated Faith from Human worship and were deeply involved with mortal civilization.
Many Gods interacted repeatedly with Humans, producing Demigods, granting blessings, interfering in mortal wars and manipulating Human societies. Their treatment of Mortals varied widely according to the individual God.
The Pantheon's later internal corruption and abuse contributed to [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] and several former Greek Goddesses viewing the old Olympian culture as fundamentally dysfunctional.[2][1]
However, this should not be generalized into the claim that every Greek God was inherently malicious. [[Hestia]], several healing Goddesses and other members were explicitly regarded as useful or benevolent enough that [[Aphrodite]] actively sought to rescue them from [[Olympus]].[1]

### Relationship with Victor Elderblood
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s relationship with the Greek Pantheon developed gradually from political hostility into outright annihilation.
Several Greek Goddesses—including [[Aphrodite]], [[Hestia]] and later others—became closely connected to Victor before the Pantheon's destruction. This created increasing tension between Victor's faction and [[Zeus]]'s government.
Victor also developed direct hostility toward individual Greek Gods because of their actions against his allies and family.
The final line was crossed when the Greek Primordial/Olympian faction targeted Nyx, [[Scathach]], Aphrodite and Morgana in the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]].[5]
Victor's response was not framed as a conventional territorial war. He explicitly declared that [[Olympus]] would be annihilated and continued the campaign until the Greek Pantheon's political system ceased existing.[8][15]

### Survivors and Aftermath
The destruction of the Pantheon did not mean that every Greek God was killed.
Several categories of survivors existed:
- Goddesses who had already abandoned [[Olympus]] and joined [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]].
- Gods belonging to [[Persephone]]'s [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] faction.
- Olympians who surrendered after [[Zeus]]'s defeat.
- Captured Gods kept as prisoners or resources.
- Individuals such as [[Hades]] who escaped the conflict.
The surviving Gods no longer possessed an independent Greek state after Chapter 804.[15]
Some later integrated into Dragons Nest, while hostile or captured Gods remained subordinate to Victor. [[Poseidon]], for example, survived the initial destruction as a prisoner and remained alive for thousands of years before Victor eventually killed him and absorbed his Sea Divinity in Chapter 1072.[19]
The old Greek territorial infrastructure was then repurposed for Victor's own civilization.

### Replacement by Dragons Nest
The ultimate successor to the Greek Pantheon is the [[Dragons Nest Pantheon]].
The transition occurred formally in Chapter 821 when [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] declared:
"As the King of the Dimension, I declare... [[Olympus]] shall cease to exist, and a new pantheon shall rise from the ashes, a pantheon led by me."[16]
The damaged Greek Dimension was restored and expanded through Victor's Energy and subsequently renamed Pantheon 7475909777 by the [[Akashic Records]].[16]
The newly reconstructed Pantheon inherited the physical dimensional foundation of Greece but not its political identity.
Dragons Nest was designed from the beginning as a multi-racial Pantheon containing:
- Dragons
- Vampires
- Demons
- Gods
- Humans
- [[Werewolves]]
- Youkai
- Fairies
- Other supernatural species
The destruction of Greece therefore represents more than the fall of one God-King. It marks the complete replacement of one of Earth's ancient racial-religious Pantheons by a fundamentally different civilization.

### Current Status
The Greek Pantheon is destroyed and no longer exists as an independent Pantheon.
Its:
- God-King system has ended.
- Olympian government has collapsed.
- Titan government no longer exists.
- [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] was conquered and incorporated into [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s wider Hell system.
- surviving Gods were killed, captured, subordinated or absorbed into Victor's civilization.
- Pantheon Dimension was repaired, expanded and renamed into the foundation of [[Dragons Nest Pantheon]].[15][16]
The term Greek Pantheon therefore refers primarily to the historical civilization and Divine faction that existed before Victor's conquest.

### Trivia
- The Greek Pantheon had at least three successive principal celestial rulers: [[Ouranos]], Kronus and [[Zeus]].
- [[Gaia]] assisted in overthrowing both Ouranos and Kronus after each betrayed or disappointed her.[1]
- Zeus later betrayed Gaia as well and imprisoned her in [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]], continuing the Pantheon's recurring cycle of generational rebellion.[1]
- [[Hades]] and Zeus controlled separate parts of the Greek Pantheon, and Hades' [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] was politically distinct enough that Zeus could not simply exercise unrestricted authority there.[2][11]
- [[Persephone]] became the true Ruler of the Greek Underworld after Hades abandoned his responsibilities.[2]
- When Zeus forcibly imposed [[Olympus]]' Territory over the Underworld, Persephone immediately sided militarily against the Olympians.[11]
- Kronus' death caused the normal progression of Time inside the Greek Pantheon to cease because his Divine Concept had become structurally integrated with the Pantheon.[9]
- This does not mean Kronus was the universal embodiment of Time; [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] explicitly distinguishes Greek [[Primordial Gods]] from the true [[Primordial Entities]].[9]
- Zeus broke his own [[Master Bolt]] during the final war, an act that shocked even [[Erebus]] and Tartarus because of the damage it caused to Olympus' long-term strength.[8]
- Victor's Immortal Army converted killed Greek Gods into soldiers immediately after their deaths.[7]
- The Olympian remnants collectively surrendered to Victor in Chapter 804.[15]
- Victor chose not to annihilate Persephone's Underworld faction because they had assisted him against Olympus.[15]
- Gaia summoned [[Typhon]] during the final war after Victor provoked her directly.[13]
- The birth of Victor's Blood Dragon Primogenitor form occurred during the final collapse of the Greek Pantheon.[13][14]
- Several Greek Goddesses had already abandoned Olympus and entered Victor's faction before the final war, particularly through [[Aphrodite]]'s efforts.[1]
- The Greek Pantheon's Dimension became the direct dimensional foundation of Pantheon 7475909777 and later Dragons Nest.[16]
- [[Poseidon]] survived the initial Greek destruction as Victor's prisoner but was eventually killed in Chapter 1072, when Victor absorbed his Sea Divinity.[19]
- Describing the entire Greek Pantheon simply as "degenerate" is too broad. Although many Olympians were infamous for abuse, exploitation and arrogance, the Pantheon also contained individuals such as [[Hestia]] and numerous non-combat Goddesses who were viewed very differently.[1]
