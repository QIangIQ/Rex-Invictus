The Hammer of Hephaestus is a Divine Artifact belonging to [[Hephaestus]], the Olympian God of the Forge. Although the abilities of the genuine hammer have never been extensively demonstrated, the artifact became the center of a major supernatural incident after an unidentified Witch successfully stole it and used it as the basis for creating functional copies.[1]
The replicas represented an extraordinary technological and magical achievement. Genuine Divine Artifacts normally require the wielder to possess [[Divine Energy]], yet copied versions of Hephaestus' hammer could be operated by supernatural beings without such qualifications. One replica was used by a Werewolf Elder against [[Victor Elderblood]], where it enlarged, became covered in Divine red flames, activated numerous Runes and manipulated the surrounding terrain by summoning trees to restrain its target.[2][1]
Victor later acquired a total of 69 copies of Hephaestus' Divine hammer and stored them inside a hidden chamber in his Royal Capital residence. Despite being weaker than the original, Victor considered each replica powerful enough to devastate a wide area when used as an improvised projectile or "missile". He subsequently offered the entire collection to [[Eleanor Elderblood Adrasteia]] as part of an agreement for her to accompany him on future hunts.[3][4]
The theft and successful replication of a Divine Artifact caused concern far beyond Victor's faction. During the first [[Supernatural Gathering]], the Greek Gods formally raised the incident before the representatives of the supernatural races and attempted to establish a rule requiring stolen Divine Artifacts to be returned to their original owners. Although the original Hammer of Hephaestus had already been recovered, its copies remained outside Olympian control and the identity of the person responsible had not been publicly established.[5]

### Description
The exact canonical appearance of the original Hammer of Hephaestus has not been described in sufficient detail.
The known replicas are depicted as massive hammers covered in numerous unusual Runes. The copy wielded by the Werewolf Elder could dramatically increase in size when activated and possessed an ornate golden design while surrounded by Divine red flames.[2]
Because the replicas were modeled upon [[Hephaestus]]' genuine Divine Artifact, they retained recognizable characteristics from the original weapon. However, the story does not establish that every property demonstrated by the copies exists in exactly the same form or strength within the genuine hammer.

### Original Divine Artifact
The genuine Hammer of Hephaestus belongs to [[Hephaestus]], one of the greatest Divine craftsmen of the [[Greek Pantheon]].
As a Divine Artifact, the hammer was considered fundamentally different from ordinary supernatural equipment. When [[Ruby Elderblood Scarlett]] and Esther examined a replica obtained by [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]], Esther explained that normal Divine Artifacts cannot ordinarily be used by just any supernatural Being:
Only beings possessing [[Divine Energy]] should be capable of properly operating a Divine Artifact.[1]
This immediately created a contradiction.
The Werewolf Elder who had previously used the replica did not possess the qualifications Esther expected from a legitimate Divine Artifact user, yet he had activated several supernatural functions of the hammer without difficulty.[2][1]
The replicas had therefore reproduced enough of the original hammer's supernatural structure to remain extremely powerful while apparently bypassing at least some of the normal restrictions associated with genuine Divine equipment.

### The Theft
At some point before [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s investigation of the incident, an unidentified Witch operating in Greece successfully stole [[Hephaestus]]' Divine hammer.[1]
Stealing a Divine Artifact was considered an extraordinarily reckless crime.
The Gods placed great importance upon their personal Artifacts, while successfully taking one from an Olympian risked attracting the hostility of the entire Pantheon.
[[Aphrodite]] Elderblood began searching for the Witch responsible, eventually approaching Esther for information.[1]
The investigation initially appeared to concern nothing more than recovering a stolen Greek Divine Artifact.
The situation became significantly more serious once Ruby and Esther realized that the hammer in Victor's possession was not the genuine weapon.
It was a copy.

#### Replication
The existence of a functioning replica shocked Esther.
Producing an effective imitation of a Divine Artifact was something she considered far beyond what an ordinary Witch should have been capable of accomplishing.[1]
The discovery raised several questions:
- How had the Witch analyzed the original Divine Artifact?
- How had she reproduced its Runes and supernatural properties?
- How could the copies be used without the [[Divine Energy]] normally required by Divine Artifacts?
- How many copies had been manufactured?
- And whether the same technique could be applied to other Divine Artifacts.
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] immediately recognized the larger implications.
If a Witch could mass-produce usable copies of Divine equipment, she could potentially undermine both the political prestige of the Gods and the supernatural weapon economy maintained by Witches and other craftsmen.[1]
The technology also created enormous strategic possibilities.
Esther suggested that, if the Witch's replication method could reproduce Divine transportation artifacts, the technique might allow Victor and his family to copy something capable of travelling between Worlds and thereby return to [[Nightingale]].[1]

### Malleus Hephaestus
The first directly demonstrated copy of the Hammer of Hephaestus was wielded by an unnamed Werewolf Elder during [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s attack upon a large Werewolf force.[2]
While fighting Victor, the Werewolf initially used the hammer without fully activating its supernatural functions.
The weapon already demonstrated one unusual ability by causing trees to emerge from the ground as it was swung.[2]
When Victor continued overwhelming him, the Elder decided to use the hammer's greater power.
He raised it toward the sky as its Runes began glowing and invoked:
"Malleus [[Hephaestus]]!"
[2]
Whether Malleus Hephaestus is the formal name of that individual replica, the name of the copied hammer model, or an activation phrase for one of its functions has not been definitively clarified.
For this reason, it is best treated as the designation associated with the demonstrated replica rather than automatically assuming all 69 copies possess that exact proper name.

#### Transformation
Upon activation, the hammer underwent a dramatic transformation.
- its Runes began glowing red;
- the weapon increased substantially in size;
- Divine red flames covered its surface;
- and its appearance became significantly more threatening.[2]
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] immediately recognized that the copy was inferior to the genuine Divine hammer but remained interested in the weapon because of the supernatural properties it retained.

#### Divine Red Flames
The activated copy became enveloped in unusual red flames.
These flames were explicitly distinguished from ordinary Fire and identified as:
The flames of a God.
[2]
Although [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] possessed resistance to ordinary flames through the [[Snow Clan]] Bloodline, the Werewolf believed this Divine Fire could bypass that advantage.
The exact nature of the flames and their relationship to [[Hephaestus]]' own Divine Fire have not been explained further.

#### Runic Empowerment
Numerous Runes cover the hammer.
When its stronger state was activated, those Runes began glowing intensely.[2]
The Runes appear to form a major component of the replica's supernatural functions.
Known effects associated with the weapon include:
- changing size;
- producing Divine Fire;
- interacting with the terrain;
- and generating plant-based restraints.
However, the story does not provide the exact meaning or individual function of each Rune.

#### Terrain Manipulation
The hammer can influence the surrounding terrain.
During the Werewolf Elder's confrontation with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]], trees emerged from the ground in response to the weapon.[2]
When the Elder later struck the ground after activating Malleus [[Hephaestus]], trees wrapped around Victor's feet and attempted to restrain him in place before the main attack landed.[2]
This makes the copy more than a simple blunt-force weapon.
The demonstrated version combines direct physical power with supernatural environmental manipulation.

### Known Abilities of the Copies
The copied hammers have demonstrated several supernatural properties.

#### Divine Energy
Despite being replicas, the hammers contain sufficiently powerful supernatural Energy that [[Eleanor Elderblood Adrasteia]] immediately identified them as Divine-Artifact-level objects when [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] showed them to her.[3]
Victor nevertheless explicitly confirmed that they were copies rather than the original.[3]

#### Size Manipulation
The Malleus [[Hephaestus]] copy can drastically increase its physical size after activation.[2]
The exact maximum size is unknown.

#### Divine Fire
The demonstrated replica can surround itself with red Divine Fire associated with a God.[2]

#### Runic Activation
The hammer contains numerous supernatural Runes that visibly activate when greater power is invoked.[2]

#### Plant and Terrain Manipulation
The copy can cause trees to emerge from the ground and manipulate them to restrain opponents.[2]

#### Large-Area Destructive Power
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] stated that even a copied hammer possessed enough power to cause significant destruction across a large area.[3]
This was one reason he considered the weapons useful to Eleanor.

#### Projectile Use
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] suggested that the copied hammers could be used like:
"missiles".
[3]
Rather than requiring the wielder to master the hammer as a melee weapon, its high intrinsic Energy and destructive power meant simply throwing or launching one at an enemy could function as a devastating area attack.
This was particularly useful to Clan Adrasteia, whose territory regularly faced large numbers of dangerous Monsters.

#### Reduced Divine Restrictions
The replicas appear capable of being used by supernatural Beings who lack the [[Divine Energy]] that Esther stated would ordinarily be necessary to operate a true Divine Artifact.[1]
This is one of the most important differences between the copies and normal Divine equipment.
The mechanism responsible remains unknown.
It may result from:
- imperfections in the replication;
- deliberate alterations by the Witch;
- changed Runic recognition requirements;
- or another unknown replication process.
The novel has not confirmed which explanation is correct.

### The 69 Copies
By Chapter 279, [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] possessed:
69 copies of the Hammer of Hephaestus
[3]
The exact circumstances through which Victor obtained the full collection have not been shown in detail.
Victor stored the hammers inside a secret underground chamber beneath his residence in the Royal Capital.
The chamber had been designed by [[Ruby Elderblood Scarlett]] and constructed by June, while the copies were concealed within supernatural statues that only revealed their contents when the proper Energy signature was detected.[3]
The concealment system belonged to the room and statues rather than being an inherent function of the hammers themselves.

#### Offer to Eleanor
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] revealed the collection to Eleanor and offered all 69 copies to her.[3]
His reasoning was practical.
Clan Adrasteia constantly fought dangerous Monsters throughout Warfall, and each copied hammer possessed enough destructive power to serve as a large-area weapon.
Victor believed Eleanor could:
- deploy them against major Monster concentrations;
- use them as explosive-style projectiles;
- and eliminate troublesome enemies throughout her territory.
In exchange, Victor asked Eleanor to accompany him during future hunts.[4]
After a misunderstanding caused by Victor deliberately ambiguous wording, Eleanor accepted the agreement.
Victor instructed her to leave the hammers inside the hidden room temporarily and stated that he would personally transport them to her territory because that was safer.[4]
The exact later distribution and current status of all 69 individual copies has not been described.

### Political Importance
The replication incident became important enough to reach the first [[Supernatural Gathering]].
During the Gathering, the representative of the Gods revealed that:
- a Greek Divine Artifact had been stolen;
- copies had successfully been produced;
- the original had been recovered;
- the replicas had not been recovered;
- and the publicly known culprit remained unidentified.[5]
The [[Greek Pantheon]] proposed a new international rule:
If a stolen Divine Artifact were found by another supernatural faction, it should be returned to its rightful owner.
[5]
The proposal failed.
Several representatives rejected it because any faction obtaining an enemy Divine Artifact would naturally prefer to keep such a valuable trump card rather than return it voluntarily.[5]
The incident therefore demonstrated the strategic value attached to Divine equipment throughout supernatural society.

### Economic Importance
The ability to reproduce Divine Artifacts threatened existing supernatural weapon markets.
During Ruby and Esther's discussion, the possibility of mass-produced Divine-Artifact replicas immediately raised concerns about the supernatural economy.[1]
Witches traditionally earned considerable influence through:
- magical items;
- weapons;
- Talismans;
- contracts;
- and specialized supernatural services.
A single Witch capable of cheaply reproducing artifacts comparable to those of the Gods could disrupt those markets and alter the military balance among supernatural factions.
The potential technology was therefore valuable enough that:
- [[Aphrodite]] sought the Witch;
- [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] became interested in locating her;
- Esther considered exploiting her replication technique;
- and the [[Greek Pantheon]] later attempted to create international regulations surrounding stolen Divine Artifacts.

### Relationship with Divine Artifacts
The copied Hammer of Hephaestus became one of the earliest demonstrations that the distinction between Divine Artifact and non-Divine supernatural equipment was not absolute.
Before examining the copy, Esther believed a Divine Artifact required [[Divine Energy]] to operate properly.[1]
The Werewolf Elder contradicted that expectation.
This implies that replication could separate at least some artifact functions from the normal metaphysical requirements of the original.
The event foreshadowed later developments in the series where:
- Runes;
- Divine craftsmanship;
- supernatural technology;
- and artificial artifact construction
increasingly allowed [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s civilization to reproduce functions once considered exclusive to Gods.

### History

#### The Original Hammer
The original Divine hammer belonged to [[Hephaestus]] and formed part of the [[Greek Pantheon]]'s collection of Divine Artifacts.
Its normal function before the theft has not yet been shown.

#### The Theft and Copying Incident
An unidentified Witch in Greece stole the Hammer and somehow created functional copies.[1]
The original was eventually recovered by the Greeks, while the replicas remained missing.[5]
[[Aphrodite]] independently investigated the culprit rather than immediately exposing the entire matter to [[Olympus]], likely because someone capable of reproducing Divine Artifacts represented an extremely valuable resource.[1]

#### Werewolf Elder
One copy entered the possession of an unnamed Werewolf Elder.
During his battle with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]], the Werewolf used the hammer to manipulate trees and later activated its stronger state through the phrase Malleus [[Hephaestus]].[2]
Victor defeated the Werewolf and took the hammer.

#### Ruby and Esther's Investigation
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] later showed the weapon to Ruby while she was speaking with Esther.
Esther immediately realized something was wrong: the artifact resembled [[Hephaestus]]' hammer, yet its former Werewolf wielder should not have possessed the [[Divine Energy]] required to operate a true Divine Artifact.[1]
Victor remembered the Werewolf explicitly stating that his weapon was weaker than the real thing.
The group correctly concluded that Victor possessed a copied Divine Artifact.[1]
The discovery led Victor to consider searching for the Witch responsible.

#### The 69 Copies
By Chapter 279, [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] had accumulated 69 copies.[3]
He concealed the collection beneath his Royal Capital residence and eventually showed them to Eleanor.
Eleanor immediately reacted to the [[Divine Energy]] present within them and initially believed Victor had casually thrown a genuine Divine Artifact at her before he clarified that the weapon was a copy.[3]

#### Deal with Eleanor
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] offered the 69 copies to Eleanor in exchange for joining him on future hunts.[3][4]
Eleanor accepted.
Victor retained the hammers temporarily so he could personally transport them to Clan Adrasteia's territory rather than risk moving so many powerful supernatural weapons insecurely.[4]

#### Supernatural Gathering
During the first [[Supernatural Gathering]], the Greek Divine-Artifact theft was publicly raised before the world's supernatural representatives.[5]
The Greeks confirmed that the genuine Divine Artifact had been recovered but the copies remained missing.
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]], despite knowing substantially more about the situation than most of those present, concealed his involvement and reacted like everyone else.[5]
The proposed international rule requiring factions to return stolen Divine Artifacts was rejected.

### Limitations

#### Copies Are Inferior to the Original
The Werewolf Elder explicitly acknowledged that his copied hammer was weaker than the genuine artifact.[2]
The exact difference in Power has never been quantified.

#### Replica Mechanics Are Not Necessarily Original Mechanics
The Malleus copy demonstrated Divine Fire, size alteration and terrain manipulation.
The novel has not directly shown the genuine Hammer of Hephaestus performing those same abilities.
These properties should therefore be attributed to the demonstrated copy unless later chapters confirm they originate unchanged from the original.

#### Unknown Replication Method
The technique used to copy the Divine Artifact remains unexplained.
It is unknown whether the Witch:
- recreated the hammer through Runes;
- copied its supernatural information;
- required physical access to the original throughout production;
- used a unique personal ability;
- or relied upon another artifact or ritual.

#### Unknown Current Distribution
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] offered the 69 copies to Eleanor and stated that he would personally transport them later.[4]
The exact later whereabouts of every copied hammer have not been individually documented.

#### Original Abilities Poorly Documented
Despite the artifact's importance to the replication incident, the actual powers of [[Hephaestus]]' genuine hammer have not been directly explored.
The article should therefore not import abilities from real-world Greek mythology or from unrelated fictional versions of Hephaestus' hammer.
