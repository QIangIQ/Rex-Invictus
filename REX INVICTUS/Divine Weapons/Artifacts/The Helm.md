The Helm of [[Hades]], also known as Hades' Helm or simply the Helm, is an ancient Divine Artifact originally belonging to Hades, the former King of the Greek Underworld. Unlike ordinary Divine weapons, the Helm serves as a supernatural vessel containing the Authority to Rule Greek Hell, establishing a connection between its qualified wielder, the Underworld and the universal system responsible for the judgment, administration and recycling of Souls. Its importance therefore extends beyond combat, as it is one of the principal mechanisms through which a God can become the legitimate Ruler of an afterlife and assume the responsibilities associated with maintaining the cosmic Cycle of Death and Reincarnation.[1]
The Helm does not grant its complete Authority merely because someone possesses or wears it. A prospective Ruler must undergo an examination administered by [[The Judges of The Abyss]], the [[Primordial Entities]] responsible for judging Souls and maintaining important aspects of the universal afterlife system. The trial determines whether the candidate possesses the impartiality, judgment and resolve necessary to fulfill the responsibilities of an Underworld Ruler. Only after satisfying the requirements can the candidate obtain the corresponding Authority and establish the connection required to govern the realm properly.[1]
Originally wielded by Hades, the Helm became available following his departure from the Greek Underworld during the Demonic War. The Primordial Goddess Nyx recovered the original artifact and offered it to [[Persephone]], who had previously possessed the title of Queen without the complete powers of the realm's actual Ruler. Persephone initially resisted Nyx's proposal because she recognized the Goddess's manipulative intentions, but the danger posed by foreign supernatural forces eventually compelled her to accept the opportunity to protect her kingdom.[2]
After surviving the Judges' trial, Persephone succeeded Hades as the true Ruler of the Greek Underworld and obtained power exceeding his former authority. The artifact subsequently manifested as a dark crown decorated with a red jewel, while Persephone demonstrated an additional transformation involving a concealed scythe powerful enough to sever the arm of Nyx. Her new position granted her direct authority over the Underworld, allowing her to expel the Primordial Goddess from the realm and prohibit her return without permission or an appropriate alternative means of entry.[3]
The Helm remains associated with Persephone's authority as the recognized Ruler of Greek Hell. Although she later became part of [[Victor Elderblood]]'s family and participated in the administration of the expanded infernal system, no confirmed event establishes that she permanently surrendered the artifact or that another individual replaced her through its original succession process.

### Description

#### Nature and Purpose
The Helm of [[Hades]] is a Divine Artifact whose defining property is its connection to the Authority of the [[Greek Underworld]]. Rather than functioning primarily as a conventional enchanted helmet, it acts as an intermediary between a prospective Ruler and the supernatural mechanisms responsible for governing the dead. This distinction is essential because the ability to command an Underworld involves more than possessing powerful Divinities associated with Death, Darkness or Souls. The Ruler must also be recognized by the system that maintains the broader processes of judgment and reincarnation.[1]
Nyx explained that the Helm contains the authority necessary to rule Greek Hell and that wearing it initiates an examination of the candidate's impartiality toward Souls. Successful completion grants access to the system managing Souls, a privilege reserved for those recognized as legitimate Rulers of Hell. The artifact consequently operates as both a means of acquiring rulership and a connection point through which the resulting authority can be exercised.
The Helm's existence also establishes an important distinction between political titles and supernatural rulership. Before the succession, [[Persephone]] was already known as the Queen of the Underworld, but the true power of the realm remained with Hades. Her position did not automatically grant her complete access to the Underworld's Authority, its governing system or the supernatural advantages possessed by its recognized Ruler. The Helm provided the means through which she could claim that position independently.[3]
The artifact's capabilities are therefore closely tied to the nature of the Greek Underworld. Its greatest significance is not the protection it provides as a piece of equipment, but the relationship it establishes between the wearer and an entire supernatural dimension whose existence is essential to the processing of the dead.

#### Appearance
The original Helm is identified as a Divine Artifact belonging to [[Hades]], although the novel does not provide a complete description of its original color, material, dimensions or decorative features. Its physical appearance is less extensively documented than its supernatural properties.
During [[Persephone]]'s succession, Nyx summoned the original Helm through a manifestation of Darkness resembling the starry night. Persephone immediately recognized the artifact and understood its association with Hades, although she initially questioned why Nyx possessed something that should have belonged to the absent King of the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]].[2]
After Persephone completed the Judges' trial, the artifact demonstrated a different appearance. A helmet resembling the Helm of Hades manifested upon her face before transforming into a dark crown with a red jewel at its center. The transformation accompanied the manifestation of Persephone's new authority, while her clothing changed into black armor resembling a combination of ceremonial armor and a dress.[3]
The resulting crown visually represented her position as the true Ruler of Greek Hell. However, the artifact's transformations did not end with this appearance. Persephone subsequently manifested a dark scythe whose supernatural characteristics led Nyx to realize that the Helm had also been employed as a weapon.
The exact relationship between the crown and the scythe is not fully explained through a technical description. The novel establishes that Persephone used the Helm's power in connection with the weapon, but does not specify whether every manifestation represents a separate simultaneous component or whether the artifact possesses unrestricted shapeshifting capabilities.

### History

#### Hades' Rulership
The Helm originally belonged to [[Hades]], the former King of the [[Greek Underworld]] and the Divine authority responsible for governing its system of the dead. As the recognized Ruler, Hades possessed supernatural privileges extending beyond his personal Divinities, including the ability to interact with the governing mechanisms of Greek Hell.
The importance of this position originated from the Underworld's function within the broader cosmic order. Although mortals commonly associate Hell with punishment, its actual role involves the processing and recycling of Souls before their eventual return to the Cycle of Reincarnation. Maintaining the realm consequently requires an authorized Ruler capable of supervising its administration and ensuring that the systems connected to the dead continue operating correctly.[1]
Hades had previously undergone the succession requirements associated with rulership. Nyx later recalled that his experience of the Judges' trial had been considerably less demanding than [[Persephone]]'s because he lacked the same number of deeply important personal attachments. Although he successfully obtained the position, the power he acquired was correspondingly less than that eventually granted to his successor.[4]
The Helm therefore remained associated with his legitimate rulership until the circumstances surrounding the Demonic War disrupted the established political structure of the [[Greek Pantheon]].

#### Hades' Departure
During the Demonic War, [[Hades]] became involved with forces opposing the established leadership of [[Olympus]]. His departure created a serious problem for the [[Greek Underworld]], since the realm no longer possessed its recognized Ruler actively maintaining its responsibilities.
The consequences extended beyond the loss of ordinary political authority. According to Nyx, the systems responsible for managing the dead were no longer functioning properly in Hades' absence. Certain processes operated automatically, but the entire structure still required a legitimate Ruler to supervise its continued operation.[1]
The crisis also exposed the vulnerability of the Greek Underworld to foreign supernatural forces. Nyx was willing to allow many of the Olympian Gods to suffer the consequences of their decisions, but she refused to permit outsiders to seize territory belonging to her Pantheon. She consequently sought a replacement capable of obtaining genuine authority over Greek Hell.

#### Nyx's Recovery of the Helm
Nyx approached [[Persephone]] during the growing crisis surrounding [[Olympus]] and the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]]. Although Persephone had suffered severe injuries and remained uncertain about the true intentions of the [[Primordial Gods]], she was determined to prevent foreign forces from claiming her kingdom.
Nyx revealed that she possessed the original Helm of [[Hades]], explaining that she had recovered it before it could leave the [[Greek Pantheon]]. She offered Persephone the opportunity to inherit the position abandoned by Hades, acquire his authority and become the second recognized Ruler of the realm.[2]
The proposal promised considerably more than a political promotion. Nyx explained that the artifact could grant Persephone the authority necessary to restructure the Greek Underworld according to her wishes, increase her supernatural power and establish control over the dimension itself.
Persephone recognized that the offer was suspicious. Nyx had previously withheld important information and manipulated the circumstances surrounding her vulnerability, making it difficult to believe that the Primordial Goddess was acting entirely out of concern for the Underworld.
Rather than accept immediately, Persephone initially rejected the proposal because she refused to become Nyx's pawn. She preferred death to surrendering her independence to another Goddess whose intentions she could not trust.[2]
The situation changed when Persephone recognized that refusing the Helm would leave the Underworld exposed to the same foreign forces she was determined to expel. Although she remained suspicious of Nyx, she eventually chose to claim the authority necessary to defend her kingdom.

#### The Trial of the Judges
Nyx subsequently presented the Helm to [[Persephone]] and explained the requirements associated with its use. The artifact contained the authority to rule Greek Hell, but that authority would not simply pass to its wearer without examination. Persephone needed to demonstrate her impartiality toward Souls before she could obtain the connection required to administer the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]].
Upon placing the Helm upon her head, Persephone was transported into an extraordinary realm belonging to [[The Judges of The Abyss]]. She appeared upon a stone platform surrounded by a landscape filled with countless galaxies, where three immense manifestations represented the beings responsible for administering the trial. These manifestations included an enormous Mouth containing a galaxy, giant golden Eyes and equally immense Hands.[1]
The Judges declared that a new Ruler would be chosen and began testing Persephone's understanding of the principles governing the dead. Their examination differed fundamentally from the ordinary moral judgments familiar to mortals. Rather than simply asking whether particular actions violated laws or religious teachings, they required her to examine circumstances, intentions, free will, the condition of the Soul and the consequences of a person's actions.
Persephone initially made mistakes because she attempted to judge situations according to conventional assumptions. The Judges corrected those errors, explaining that the same outward action could possess different significance depending upon the circumstances surrounding it. An individual defending themselves against abuse, for example, could not necessarily be judged in the same manner as someone deliberately murdering an innocent person through jealousy.[1]
The trial also demonstrated the importance of questioning incomplete information. Persephone was expected to recognize when the details provided were insufficient for a fair decision rather than automatically accepting every statement presented by the Judges.
The examination consequently tested more than simple obedience. A legitimate Ruler needed enough judgment to distinguish between genuinely different circumstances, even when their superficial appearance suggested the same conclusion.

#### The Trial of Impartiality
After completing the initial examination, [[Persephone]] learned that the first stage had not been the most difficult part of the succession process.
The second trial required her to confront decisions involving the individuals she loved most. The Judges explained that a Ruler could not reserve impartial judgment exclusively for strangers while exempting family, lovers or other important people from the same principles.
Persephone was first confronted with representations of Adonis and her mother, [[Demeter]], and forced to choose which would survive. She initially attempted to dismiss the situation because the individuals appeared to be recreations rather than their actual counterparts. The Judges then explained that they possessed the ability to create genuine Souls and that the Souls involved in the examination were real.[4]
Unable to sacrifice her mother, Persephone chose Adonis to die. The Judges immediately demonstrated the consequences of her decision through a horrifying scenario involving [[Zeus]].
The following examination was even more devastating. Persephone was confronted with younger versions of her daughters, [[Melinoë]] and [[Macaria]], and again ordered to choose between their lives. Unable to decide which daughter should die, she hesitated and protested against the cruelty of the situation. The Judges had already established that refusing to choose would result in both Souls being lost, and Persephone was forced to witness the consequences of her inability to make the required judgment.[4]
The examination continued through further scenarios involving people important to Persephone. Her suffering became so severe that her physical body began reacting violently even while her consciousness remained within the trial.
Nyx, observing the process from the [[Greek Underworld]], restrained Persephone's body with Darkness to prevent her from injuring herself or others. She recognized that the experience was exceptionally cruel, but had deliberately selected Persephone because of the number of people whose loss could affect her.
According to Nyx, the severity of the trial was related to the power obtainable through successful completion. [[Hades]] had possessed fewer important personal attachments and had therefore passed through a comparatively easier experience, but the corresponding increase in power had also been less substantial.
Persephone's ordeal was considerably more demanding, creating the conditions for an unprecedented increase in the authority she would eventually acquire.[4]

#### Persephone's Ascension
[[Persephone]] eventually emerged from the examination and awakened within the [[Greek Underworld]]. Although she had survived, the experience left her deeply traumatized and furious with Nyx, who had deliberately placed her in circumstances where the suffering of the trial could be exploited for political purposes.
Her supernatural existence had nevertheless changed fundamentally. She was no longer simply the Goddess who possessed the ceremonial title of Queen of the Underworld. She had become its legitimate Ruler, possessing the authority necessary to govern the realm independently.[3]
As her new power manifested, black armor appeared around her body and a helmet resembling [[Hades]]' Helm formed upon her face. The helmet subsequently transformed into a dark crown decorated with a red jewel, visually representing her new position.
The entire Greek Underworld trembled as her authority spread through the dimension. Beings throughout the realm sensed the emergence of a new Ruler, while Nyx recognized that the power Persephone had obtained exceeded what she had originally anticipated.
Nyx had expected Persephone to reach the strength of a High-Level God after the succession. Instead, her new authority elevated her to the level of a God-King, supported directly by the Greek Underworld.
The change was particularly significant because the realm itself now reinforced Persephone's existence. Like [[Zeus]] within [[Olympus]], she possessed the supernatural advantages associated with legitimate rulership over an entire Divine dimension. Nyx recognized that this made Persephone substantially more difficult to manipulate than before, since the new Ruler no longer depended upon the political protection or approval of the Primordial Goddess.[3]

#### Transformation into a Divine Weapon
[[Persephone]]'s confrontation with Nyx demonstrated another major function associated with the Helm.
After awakening, Persephone accused Nyx of deliberately exploiting her vulnerability. Her anger escalated into a direct demonstration of power, during which she manifested a dark scythe and used it against the Primordial Goddess.
The weapon severed one of Nyx's arms, surprising the ancient Goddess because Persephone's original strength had been far below the level ordinarily required to injure her. Nyx restored the limb afterward, demonstrating that the attack had caused physical injury rather than permanently overcoming her existence.[3]
During the encounter, the scythe became invisible through a manifestation of dark supernatural power. Nyx could no longer see it but understood that the weapon remained within Persephone's grasp.
Nyx subsequently recognized the connection between the scythe and the Helm of [[Hades]]. She had initially believed that the artifact had simply transformed into the crown resting upon Persephone's head, but realized that Persephone had employed its power in the weapon as well.
The incident established that the Helm was not limited to remaining a conventional piece of headwear after its wielder acquired legitimate rulership. Persephone could employ its supernatural properties through a weapon manifestation, allowing the artifact to participate directly in combat.
The scythe's ability to become invisible also demonstrates a concealment function associated with the resulting weapon. However, the novel does not establish that the original Helm grants unrestricted invisibility to every wearer under every possible circumstance.

#### Banishment of Nyx
[[Persephone]]'s confrontation with Nyx culminated in another demonstration of the authority obtained through the Helm's succession process.
After condemning the Primordial Goddess for manipulating her, Persephone declared that Nyx was banished from the [[Greek Underworld]]. The command was immediately enforced by the realm, expelling Nyx and leaving her outside its gates.[3]
Nyx attempted to evaluate the situation but recognized that the decision possessed genuine supernatural authority. The Underworld had accepted Persephone as its Ruler, and the command of that Ruler functioned as Law within the territory.
Without Persephone's permission or an alternative means of entry, Nyx could no longer simply return to the realm as she pleased. This was especially significant because Nyx was an original Primordial Goddess whose personal supernatural abilities greatly exceeded those of Persephone before the succession.
The banishment demonstrated that rulership of an Underworld grants authority distinct from ordinary combat strength. Persephone did not need to defeat Nyx permanently in order to remove her from the realm; she exercised a right that the Underworld itself recognized and enforced.
The incident also marked Persephone's independence from Nyx. Although the Primordial Goddess had provided the Helm and arranged the circumstances of the succession, she could no longer dictate how Persephone would govern the realm after its Authority had accepted her.

#### Later Use of Underworld Authority
[[Persephone]] continued exercising the responsibilities associated with her position after the succession, eventually becoming more closely connected with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] and the expanding supernatural civilization under his control.
An important later development occurred when Victor entered the heart of the [[Greek Underworld]] with Persephone and members of his family. The group encountered the Core of the Underworld, a massive sphere through which enormous numbers of Souls continuously flowed.
The Core represented part of the broader Soul-management system established by the [[Primordial Entities]]. Victor recognized that the mechanism resembled the corresponding structure operating within his own Hell, with the principal differences involving the individual characteristics of their respective realms.[5]
The encounter further demonstrated the significance of Persephone's recognized rulership. Her connection with the Greek Underworld was directly relevant to interacting with the Core, and Victor restored that connection during the events surrounding his attempt to address Adonis' remaining spiritual information.
The scene established that the Helm's original succession involved access to genuine cosmic infrastructure rather than simply providing a symbolic title or additional [[Divine Energy]]. However, the later interaction also involved Victor's own extraordinary authorities and the participation of [[Thanatos]], so every capability demonstrated through the Core cannot automatically be attributed to the Helm alone.
Persephone subsequently participated in the administration of the larger infernal system associated with Victor's expanding civilization. During a crisis involving an enormous influx of Souls from the former Norse territories, [[Lily Elderblood]] requested assistance from Persephone, [[Aline Elderblood Valefar]] and Lilith because the existing administrators could not process the unexpected workload efficiently.
The crisis demonstrated that even after the establishment of powerful rulers, the afterlife still required practical administration, classification and supervision. Possessing a connection to the Soul system did not eliminate the consequences of an overwhelming influx of dead individuals, particularly when Souls originating from different Pantheons began entering jurisdictions where they did not ordinarily belong.[6]
Although Persephone's later Divine development exceeded the circumstances of her original succession, no confirmed passage establishes that the Helm was destroyed or permanently transferred to another owner.

### Authority of the Greek Underworld

#### The Role of Hell
The Helm's importance cannot be understood without examining the function of Hell within the cosmology of [[MTWABV Series|My Three Wives Are Beautiful Vampires]].
According to Nyx, the fundamental purpose of the various Hells and Heavens is the processing and recycling of Souls. While mortal religions frequently emphasize punishment and reward as their principal functions, the actual supernatural structure is considerably more complicated.
Souls are fundamental components of existence, and their continued circulation is necessary for the Cycle of Life, Death and Reincarnation. The dead pass through systems associated with different afterlives, where they may receive punishment, experience fulfillment, undergo judgment or have aspects of their existence separated before returning to the broader cycle.[1]
Each Hell possesses distinctive characteristics determined by the supernatural system and Pantheon with which it is associated. The [[Greek Underworld]] is consequently not identical to [[Biblical Hell]], [[Nordic Hell]] or the other afterlives existing throughout the Cosmos, although their underlying responsibilities share important similarities.
In the Greek system, the ruler must maintain the mechanisms through which Souls are received, administered and eventually returned to the cycle. The Helm establishes the connection and authority necessary for a qualified individual to perform those responsibilities.

#### The Judges of The Abyss
[[The Judges of The Abyss]] possess the highest confirmed authority over the judgment of Souls within the system described by Nyx.
They are not merely servants of the [[Greek Underworld]] or extensions of the powers belonging to [[Hades]] and [[Persephone]]. They are [[Primordial Entities]] whose responsibilities concern Souls throughout the wider structure of existence.
Their functions include evaluating the deceased, determining appropriate consequences, separating spiritual characteristics and supporting the creation of Souls required for new Life.
During Persephone's trial, they explained that Life creates the physical or metaphysical form of a Being, while the entities responsible for Souls provide the spiritual component necessary for that existence to develop. The cooperation between these principles forms part of the foundation supporting the universal Cycle of Reincarnation.[4]
The Helm does not create the Judges or grant its wielder their full authority. It allows a qualified individual to establish the appropriate connection with the system that the Judges administer.

#### Ordinary Souls
Nyx explained that ordinary Souls may undergo a process in which their positive and negative aspects are separated and directed toward the appropriate destinations.
Those associated with punishment are sent to an appropriate Hell, while the portions associated with positive experiences may enter a Heaven or comparable supernatural realm. After their respective processes have been completed, the Souls are recycled and returned to the Cycle of Reincarnation.[1]
The exact method depends upon the Pantheon and its afterlife. For example, Nyx described the Norse system as operating differently from certain other Hells, with warriors who die honorably entering Valhalla before eventually returning to the cycle.
The existence of these different arrangements does not mean that the Helm of [[Hades]] directly governs every Hell or Heaven. Its specific authority concerns the [[Greek Underworld]], while the broader system extends beyond that jurisdiction.

#### Non-Sentient Souls
Nyx also revealed that Souls are not exclusive to intelligent Humans, Vampires, Gods or other self-aware beings.
Plants, mountains and other entities within the Universe possess Souls of their own, although the nature of those Souls differs from that of ordinary sentient individuals.
When non-sentient existences die, their Souls may pass through the afterlife systems before returning to the Cycle of Life and Reincarnation. Much of this process operates automatically, meaning that a Ruler does not need to personally evaluate every plant or mountain that ceases to exist.[1]
Nevertheless, the governing system must remain functional. The absence of an authorized Ruler can disrupt its proper administration, demonstrating why the Helm's succession process possesses importance extending beyond the political status of the [[Greek Pantheon]].

#### Powerful Supernatural Souls
Nyx distinguished ordinary Souls from those belonging to exceptionally powerful supernatural individuals.
Powerful Vampires, [[Werewolves]], Gods and other Higher Beings can possess Souls whose spiritual foundations are far more resistant to division. Their judgment may consequently differ from the ordinary process of separating positive and negative aspects.
Instead of automatically undergoing the same division applied to weaker Souls, certain powerful individuals may be judged more directly and assigned to an appropriate destination according to the circumstances of their existence.[1]
Gods are subject to particularly consequential judgments because their status as Higher Existences distinguishes them from ordinary mortals. Depending upon the circumstances, a sufficiently powerful Divine Soul may be permitted to return to the Cycle of Reincarnation or may cease to exist as an individual.
Nyx theorized that dividing powerful Souls could damage them so severely that they would become unsuitable for reincarnation. However, she explicitly presented this explanation as her own theory rather than as a confirmed universal law.
The Helm consequently grants its Ruler access to an existing system containing different procedures for different categories of Souls. It does not establish that every dead Being is judged identically or that the Ruler can ignore the fundamental distinctions maintained by the Judges.

### Powers and Abilities
The Helm's abilities originate primarily from its role as an artifact containing [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] Authority. Its demonstrated weapon transformations provide additional combat applications, but the supernatural powers granted through successful rulership must be distinguished from the personal Divinities already possessed by [[Persephone]].

#### Rulership and Authority
- Divine Artifact: The Helm is an original Divine Artifact associated with [[Hades]] and the legitimate rulership of the [[Greek Underworld]]. Its supernatural properties extend beyond those of conventional enchanted equipment.[2]
- Underworld Rulership: The artifact provides the means through which a qualified candidate can inherit the Authority to rule Greek Hell.[1]
- Divine Authority Bestowal: Successful completion of the Judges' examination allows the candidate to acquire the supernatural authority associated with a recognized Underworld Ruler.[3]
- Soul System Connection: The Helm establishes a connection between its recognized wielder and the system responsible for managing Souls.[1]
- Soul Judgment Authority: A recognized Ruler can participate in the judgment and administration of Souls to the extent permitted by the broader afterlife system.[1]
- Underworld Manipulation: [[Persephone]]'s legitimate rulership grants authority over the Greek Underworld, including the ability to influence the realm according to her position as its sovereign.[2][3]
- Dimensional Dominion: Within Greek Hell, the recognized Ruler possesses authority over the surrounding dimension comparable in function to the privileges of a God-King within their own Divine territory.[3]
- Territorial Authority: The Underworld recognizes and enforces the commands of its legitimate Ruler, as demonstrated when Persephone expelled Nyx from the realm.[3]
- Banishment: Persephone used her newly acquired authority to remove Nyx from the Greek Underworld without requiring her permanent physical defeat.[3]
- Access Restriction: The Ruler can prohibit unauthorized individuals from entering the realm through ordinary means, subject to exceptions such as permission or alternative methods of entry.[3]
- Underworld Empowerment: The realm itself supports its recognized Ruler, increasing their effective supernatural power within its territory.[3]
- Divine Empowerment: Persephone's successful succession elevated her from a comparatively low-ranking Goddess to an existence possessing God-King-level power. The extraordinary extent of the increase was connected to the severity of her trial.[3]
- Supernatural Rulership Recognition: The Underworld and its inhabitants recognized Persephone's ascension, with the realm trembling when her new authority manifested.[3]
- Afterlife System Access: Rulership provides access to restricted mechanisms connected with the administration of the dead, including the Core of the Underworld.[5]

#### Soul-Related Functions
- Soul Manipulation: The authority associated with legitimate [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] rulership concerns the administration and judgment of Souls, although the exact scope of the Ruler's personal manipulation remains subordinate to the wider cosmic system.[1]
- Soul Judgment: A recognized Ruler can participate in determining the treatment of dead Souls, with the Judges of the Abyss retaining superior authority.[1]
- Spiritual Evaluation: [[Persephone]]'s trial required examination of the circumstances surrounding a Soul, including its condition, choices and the consequences of actions committed during Life.[1]
- Soul System Administration: The Ruler is responsible for maintaining the mechanisms through which Souls are received and processed within the jurisdiction of the Greek Underworld.[1]
- [[Reincarnation System]] Connection: The Helm's authority is linked to the afterlife processes that return recycled Souls to the wider Cycle of Reincarnation.[1]
- Afterlife Regulation: The Ruler possesses responsibilities concerning the proper operation of Greek Hell and its participation in the broader system governing the dead.[1]
These functions do not mean that the Helm independently grants unrestricted authority over every Soul in the Cosmos. The Judges of the Abyss remain responsible for the highest level of Soul judgment, while the recognized rulers of individual Hells operate within their respective jurisdictions.

#### Weapon Abilities
- Weapon Transformation: After [[Persephone]]'s succession, the Helm's power was employed in the manifestation of a dark scythe rather than remaining restricted to its original helmet appearance.[3]
- Crown Transformation: The artifact manifested as a dark crown decorated with a red jewel upon Persephone's ascension.[3]
- Invisibility: Persephone concealed the dark scythe through a supernatural effect that rendered the weapon invisible while it remained in her possession.[3]
- Supernatural Concealment: The scythe's concealment surprised Nyx, who initially believed the Helm had transformed only into Persephone's crown.[3]
- Divine Weapon Empowerment: The Helm-associated weapon manifestation possessed enough power to injure Nyx despite the extraordinary durability of an original Primordial Goddess.[3]
- Primordial God Damage: Persephone severed one of Nyx's arms during their confrontation. The feat establishes the ability to cause physical injury but does not prove unrestricted destruction of a Primordial God's entire existence.[3]

#### Trial and Succession Functions
- Impartiality Examination: Wearing the Helm initiates a test intended to determine whether the candidate can judge Souls without inappropriate favoritism.[1]
- Connection to the Judges' Realm: [[Persephone]] was transported into the domain of the Judges of the Abyss when she put on the artifact.[1]
- Rulership Qualification: The artifact does not automatically grant complete authority to an unqualified wearer; recognition depends upon the candidate satisfying the Judges.[1]
- Supernatural Succession: The Helm allows the authority associated with Greek Hell to pass from a former Ruler to an accepted successor.[2][3]
- Trial-Dependent Empowerment: The degree of power obtained through succession can vary according to the circumstances and severity of the trial, as demonstrated by the comparison between [[Hades]] and Persephone.[4][3]

### Limitations
The Helm is an extraordinary Divine Artifact, but its capabilities operate through a system possessing distinct requirements and limitations.
- Qualification Required: Possession of the Helm does not automatically grant complete rulership. The prospective Ruler must pass the examination administered by the Judges of the Abyss.[1]
- Impartiality Required: Candidates must demonstrate the judgment necessary to evaluate Souls without allowing personal attachments to override their responsibilities.[4]
- Potentially Devastating Trial: [[Persephone]]'s examination involved severe psychological suffering, while failure could prevent the candidate from obtaining the position.[4]
- Trial Results Vary: [[Hades]] and Persephone obtained different levels of power following their respective examinations. The artifact does not guarantee every successful user the same supernatural strength.[4][3]
- Jurisdictional Limitation: The Helm's authority concerns the [[Greek Underworld]]. It does not automatically establish rulership over every Heaven, Hell or afterlife in existence.[1]
- Superior Cosmic Authorities: The Judges of the Abyss retain authority over Soul judgment beyond the powers granted to individual Underworld rulers.[1]
- Administrative Responsibility: A recognized Ruler must maintain the functioning of the afterlife system. The possession of authority does not remove the practical burden of administering enormous numbers of Souls.[6]
- No Confirmed Universal Soul Control: The connection with the Soul system does not establish unrestricted control over every Soul throughout the Cosmos.
- No Confirmed Unlimited Reincarnation: The Helm does not independently demonstrate the ability to reincarnate any individual regardless of the governing cosmic rules.
- No Confirmed Absolute Concealment: The scythe's demonstrated invisibility does not prove that the Helm can conceal its wielder from every form of perception.
- No Confirmed Permanent Primordial Erasure: Persephone injured Nyx with the weapon, but the Primordial Goddess restored her severed arm. The event does not establish that the Helm can permanently eliminate every Primordial Entity.[3]
- Unspecified Original Construction: The artifact's creator, materials, original manufacturing process and complete inventory of enchantments have not been revealed.
- Dependence on Recognized Rulership: The benefits associated with the Greek Underworld's support require a valid connection to its governing authority rather than mere possession of an unrelated Divine title.

### Related Artifacts and Systems

#### The Judges of The Abyss
[[The Judges of The Abyss]] are the [[Primordial Entities]] responsible for the examination through which the Helm's authority is granted. The artifact connects its wearer to their trial, but its existence should not be confused with the Judges themselves.
The Judges possess responsibilities extending beyond any individual Pantheon, including the administration and creation of Souls and the maintenance of the wider system supporting reincarnation.[4]

#### The Core of the Afterlife
[[The Core of the Afterlife]] is part of the infrastructure through which the Soul-management system operates.
The Core of the [[Greek Underworld]] was later revealed as an enormous sphere containing the continuous flow of Souls, while [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] identified a comparable mechanism within his own Hell.
[[Persephone]]'s legitimate rulership was directly relevant to access to this system, demonstrating the practical importance of the connection obtained through the Helm.[5]

#### The Universal Tree
[[The Universal Tree]] is associated with the fundamental cosmic principles governing Life and Reincarnation.
After passing through the appropriate stages of judgment, punishment, fulfillment or recycling, Souls return to the broader cycle through which new Life can emerge.
The Helm is not a replacement for the Universal Tree. Its function is connected with the administration of the dead within Greek Hell, while the Universal Tree participates in the larger process through which Souls return to new existences.[1]

#### Key to Hell
The Key to Hell serves a comparable purpose within [[Biblical Hell]], functioning as a connection point for the authority of that realm's legitimate Ruler.
During the conflict surrounding [[Diablo]]'s departure, the novel explains that possessing the Key did not automatically make Diablo the true Ruler of Hell. He and Lilith had been maintaining important aspects of its administration without completing the requirements associated with genuine rulership.[7]
The comparison reinforces the principle established through the Helm of [[Hades]]: possessing an artifact associated with an [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] is not necessarily equivalent to receiving its complete Authority. The connection, qualifications and recognition of the Ruler remain essential.

### Current Status
The Helm of [[Hades]] remains associated with [[Persephone Elderblood]], who successfully inherited the Authority of Greek Hell after Hades' departure.
Her succession established her as the legitimate second Ruler of the [[Greek Underworld]], while subsequent events confirmed that she continued exercising responsibilities connected with the administration of Souls and the operation of the broader infernal system.
The artifact's exact physical configuration during the latest Imperial Era has not been independently described, but there is no confirmed event establishing that [[Persephone]] surrendered it, that the original Helm was destroyed or that another candidate completed the succession process in her place.

### Trivia
- The Helm originally belonged to [[Hades]], the former King of the [[Greek Underworld]].
- Its creator has not been identified in the verified novel chapters.
- The artifact was explicitly identified as the original Helm of Hades when Nyx presented it to [[Persephone]].[2]
- Nyx recovered the artifact before it could leave the [[Greek Pantheon]] following Hades' departure.[2]
- The Helm contains the Authority necessary to rule Greek Hell but does not automatically grant that position to every wearer.[1]
- Persephone initially refused Nyx's offer because she suspected the Primordial Goddess intended to manipulate her.[2]
- Wearing the Helm transported Persephone into the realm of the Judges of the Abyss.[1]
- The Judges' domain appeared as a cosmic landscape containing countless galaxies.[1]
- Persephone's succession examination involved both the evaluation of ordinary Souls and decisions concerning people personally important to her.[4]
- The Judges explained that their responsibilities include creating Souls in cooperation with the cosmic principle of Life.[4]
- Nyx deliberately selected Persephone because the number of important people in her life made her examination particularly demanding.[4]
- Hades had previously undergone a comparatively easier trial, resulting in a smaller increase in power.[4]
- Persephone's successful succession elevated her beyond the level Nyx originally expected, granting her God-King-level authority supported by the Greek Underworld.[3]
- After the trial, the Helm manifested as a dark crown decorated with a red jewel.[3]
- Persephone also employed the artifact's power in a dark scythe capable of injuring Nyx.[3]
- The scythe became invisible during the confrontation, surprising the Primordial Goddess.[3]
- Nyx initially believed that the Helm had transformed exclusively into Persephone's crown.[3]
- Persephone severed one of Nyx's arms, although the Primordial Goddess subsequently restored the injury.[3]
- The newly recognized Ruler expelled Nyx from Greek Hell through her Underworld Authority rather than permanently defeating her in battle.[3]
- The Ruler's command functions as Law within the Greek Underworld, subject to the supernatural rules governing that realm.[3]
- The Helm's Soul-system connection is a privilege associated with legitimate Underworld rulership rather than ordinary possession of a Divine weapon.[1]
- The Greek Underworld participates in the universal recycling of Souls and the wider Cycle of Reincarnation.[1]
- Plants, mountains and other non-sentient existences also possess Souls, and their deaths contribute to the same broader cycle.[1]
- Different Hells and Heavens possess distinct methods of processing the dead, despite sharing important functions within the cosmological system.[1]
- The Souls of powerful supernatural beings may undergo different judgments from those applied to ordinary mortals.[1]
- The Judges of the Abyss possess superior authority over Soul judgment compared with individual Hell rulers.[1]
- The Helm of Hades is functionally comparable to the Key to Hell associated with [[Biblical Hell]], although each artifact concerns its respective realm.[7]
- Persephone later participated in events involving the Core of the Greek Underworld, confirming the continuing importance of her recognized authority over the realm.[5]
- The artifact has not been confirmed to grant unrestricted authority over every afterlife, nor has the novel demonstrated its ability to eliminate all [[Primordial Entities]] permanently.
- No confirmed event establishes that the Helm has been destroyed or permanently transferred away from Persephone.
