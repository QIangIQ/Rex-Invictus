# Rex Invictus X

← [[Ch16 - Rex Invictus IX|Rex Invictus IX]] · [[Manuscript Index|Index]] · [[Ch18 - Rex Invictus XI|Rex Invictus XI]] →

The land stretched in silence beneath their march, the long road westward running like a scar through frozen valleys. The two Hunters moved in step without speaking, Bianca's once innocent eyes adapting sharply to every movement along the ridges, while Grover muttered nervously at the undergrowth's refusal to yield proper roots. Eleni strode at the front, alongside a contemplative son of [[Poseidon]]. His eyes were on the horizon, constantly.

He had learned long ago that the horizon was a lie.

The first sign came with the roadside shrine. A crude stone arch, leaning half-collapsed, carved in relief with symbols he bet nobody else here could recognize. A triple coil, a burning disc, and a form that resembled both man and vulture. Grover stopped, ears twitching.

"That's… not Greek. Like, at all."

Percy stepped forward, ignoring the Hunter's hand that rose instinctively to bar him. His fingers brushed the cold rock, and he murmured low, more to himself than to the others.

"*Ammit-Sekhem*… still sealed, then. Minerva was right in teaching me some Egyptian Runes, at least."

The air thickened. A shape flickered within the shadows of the arch, vast and hungry, scaled hide coiling into the earth. Crocodile teeth like obsidian saws gleamed, yet its eyes softened when they landed on Percy.

"You are no priest," it hissed. "Yet you call my name with the voice of one who watched me die."

"I watched you fall," Percy corrected, calm as if speaking to an old neighbor. "Different thing."

The guardian demon hissed. "Arrogant lies. You have grit, *Iawanu*… Greek." She added, looking at the Satyr's confused expression.

Eleni tilted her head, suspicion flickering across her face. The Hunters shifted, fingers tightening around their bows, but the beast only stared, then receded into the dark behind the arch. The shrine grew still.

They walked on.

The second encounter was subtler. A farmhouse lay ruined in a hollow, blackened by fire long since quenched. Upon its roof perched a figure of pale light, six wings folded close, its head faceless save for a single line of flame.

Percy only inclined his head. "Sariel," he said evenly, "your vigil is wasted. This is not your battlefield. Come, I have one for thee, Holy Watcher."

Zoё looked scandalized. Percy was actively attempting to bulldoze through as many Ancient Laws as he could.

The being tilted, as if confused by being recognized. The line of flame flared, and its voice carried no air, only thought. Y*ou are allowed to witness our kind?*

"Yeah," Percy said, voice flat. "And if you want to be of help, trail us. You might encounter something interesting."

The angel did not reply. It folded into itself, light shrinking until nothing remained but a charred beam collapsing to the ground.

They pressed forward without a word, even Eleni watching him now with a trace of unease.

The third was the worst. Near dusk, they came to a crossroads where three black steles jutted from the frozen earth, carved with script that shifted when eyes lingered too long. A disheveled figure sat there waiting—hood drawn low, shirtless, and threadbare shorts. The air smelled of wet ash and brine. When it lifted its head, a face half-eroded by salt stared out.

"Traveler," it said, and its voice was all shorelines collapsing. "You know of me."

Percy sighed, weary, as though seeing an old creditor. "Manannán mac Lir. Father told me about you. Weren't you supposed to be in Ireland?."

Manannán's gaze drifted seaward, past the horizon of time and space that Percy seemed able to perceive. His voice was low, even by divine standards, carrying a weight of currents and distant tides. "Ireland… is not where I am needed now."

Percy's brow furrowed. "Not needed? That doesn't make sense. You always have a reason. What, your underworld got moved next to [[Hades]]?"

The sea god's silver eyes glimmered with a salt-light that seemed older than memory. "I have obligations… elsewhere. Plans are… unfolding."

"I cannot ignore the Morrígan."

"The Morrígan?" Percy echoed, suddenly tense. "Why? She is in the Americas?"

The god's lips twitched with something like a shadow of humor. "Because she is contracted elsewhere, bound to serve some man's schemes for now. If left unchecked, her influence could unravel much that they wish preserved. I maintain a watch, lest fate tip too far from their intended course."

"She knows not to defy Fate or order. You being here is not necessary. [[Ares]] covers her domain here."

"Necessary," Manannán corrected, voice like waves breaking over rocks. "Ireland waits, always. But some currents must flow differently, and some storms must be steered quietly, unseen. Morrígan is a force I cannot allow to roam unchecked, even for individuals she is contracted to. Not when the balance hangs as it does."

"So, you're here watching her. Making sure she doesn't wreck everything while someone else pulls the strings."

"Exactly," Manannán said simply. The mist thickened around him, his form flickering like a reflection on restless water. "I am both present and absent. Guardianship is rarely visible, Perseus. You will learn that soon enough. Fare thee well."

Night fell before anyone spoke again. The road carried them west, but the silence pressed heavier than the snow.

Eleni kicked a rock with her steel boot. The highway stretched ahead, gray and unbroken, lined with snow-dusted pines. Percy, Zoë, Bianca, and Grover walked along the shoulder, careful to keep to the shadows where passing vehicles' headlights would only briefly skim them. The road was quiet, empty enough to be almost unreal, and the occasional hum of distant engines felt intrusive in the otherwise still landscape.

The daughter of [[Zeus]] kicked a pebble at Percy.

He hissed in pain. "What was that for?"

"The 32nd's guards are watching us. Be wary" Eleni said, eyes scanning the treeline.

Percy led them down a narrow, moss-hidden path branching off the highway, the detour weaving between thick pines and frozen undergrowth. The forest swallowed their presence, and the distant hum of engines faded into a soft, watchful silence.

Percy's eyes narrowed. "Something's off."

From the mist, a ripple of black smoke coalesced into a familiar shape—*Condottiero*. He hovered just above the snow, arms crossed, an impish tilt to his horned head. "Well, well, if it isn't the Greek demigod of Poseidon. Still playing hero, I see."

Percy straightened. "You again… Thought I'd seen the last of you, clown-dude."

"Thoughts are like fish!" Condottiero replied, voice teasing, "they slip away if you blink!" Before anyone could respond, the demon darted forward, leaving a brief trail of black flame, disappearing behind a nearby tree and reappearing with a flourish atop a low branch. "Come along, small humani. Your gift awaits its master."

The group followed, navigating through underbrush and shadows as Condottiero led them on a winding path, slipping behind trees and between rocks with an ease that mocked their cautious steps. Every time they slowed, he would flicker ahead, reappearing with a grin and a playful comment.

Finally, the forest gave way to a secluded clearing. Condottiero gestured to the fortress barely visible through the curling mist—its black walls more shadow than stone, unassuming yet resonating with a quiet, formidable presence. "Here we are," he said, hovering before the gates. "Do behave; the 32nd is… in a good mood as of late, thanks to you. Do *enjoy* your stay."

With a subtle wave of his clawed hand, the gates slid open silently. Condottiero led the five demigods inside, past corridors that seemed impossibly long and twisting, until they entered the throne chamber. There, [[Asmodeus]] waited, the Eye of the Cyclops pulsing faintly in his left socket.

Percy stepped forward, voice even. "Lord Asmodeus."

The demon lord's crimson eyes gleamed brighter, a toothy grin spreading across his face. "Ah… excellent. You have done more than bring me a mere artifact. You have flushed out every traitor hiding in my camp. Truly, you have earned my favor."

Percy bowed slightly, glancing at Bianca, Zoë, Eleni, and Grover. They were staring at him strangely. "I aim to be profitable. While we're here, if you would… share news? Anything of importance?"

Asmodeus leaned forward, smoke curling from his form. "The bastards of Evergreen still crawl in shadows, though gaps widen daily. Your gift has revealed weaknesses and traitors alike. Thor's absence leaves Norse lines weak in Canada. The Shinto pantheon… their watch falters. The leeches are in a conflict with them as of now."

Percy nodded, committing the information to memory. "Any mortal factions we should anticipate?"

The demon lord chuckled, fangs glinting. "The Roman Triumvirate Holdings is hunting you five. A King's ransom. Each. One singular ten-tens of gold Talents, or their equivalent. Roughly four-hundred-thirty million dollars for the young ones."

Percy inclined his head. "Thank you, Lord Asmodeus."

A shadow of approval crossed the demon's expression. "Go now. The outskirts of San Francisco, by the Hellway. Travel the road carefully. Your gift has earned me a rare boon. It would be horrible if you perished midway in your quest."

The fortress walls faded behind the five demigods as Condottiero guided them into the mouth of the Hellway. Shadows thickened, twisting into impossible geometries. Light bent upon itself, corridors stretching into infinity and folding back like a labyrinth of mirrored nightmares. Percy squinted, gripping his pack as the path convulsed around them.

"This… this is nothing like walking a road," Bianca muttered, clutching her bow.

"Flashing, but… Hell's own flavor," Percy said. "Keep an eye on him if you want to travel safely, Condottiero. He will guide us. He will not break his master's word."

The air hummed with tension, reality quivering. Every heartbeat felt like a mile traveled; every step consumed years in unseen realms. Trees, mountains, and rivers flickered past like ghostly projections, then vanished. The five moved as one through the shuddering path, voices swallowed by the immensity, guided only by the impish movements of demon-jester.

Back in the throne room, silence had descended. Asmodeus's grin lingered long after the demigods vanished. The Eye of the Cyclops pulsed faintly in his socket, rippling with the knowledge of spies flushed from his fortress. He inhaled, exhaling smoke that curled like serpents in the dim light. Satisfaction bloomed within him—a rare warmth in a being of shadow.

Then it came. A flash of searing light, an intrusion of pure celestial will. Sariel manifested without warning, wings unfurling to span the chamber, six blades of radiant light slicing the gloom. The air ignited with divine heat; the ground beneath Asmodeus hissed and cracked as the angel's presence warped the shadows.

"You dared manipulate the Way of Fate," Sariel's voice boomed, reverberating across stone and steel. "You upset the balance!"

Asmodeus chuckled, the sound low and dangerous, crimson eyes narrowing. "Balance? Child, I merely gained a reward from a finished contract. And yet… it seems you enjoy theatrics."

The first strike came as a column of light, crashing toward Asmodeus. He twisted, letting shadows absorb the impact, reforming his shape mid-flight, claws rending stone where the attack grazed. Smoke and flame coiled around him, striking back in blackened tendrils.

Sariel's wings beat, each stroke carving arcs of radiance that cleaved at the obsidian pillars of the throne room. Asmodeus countered with a storm of shadow, the Eye of the Cyclops tucked away, magicked away in the chaos.

He moved faster than any mortal eye could track, weaving between strikes, laughter echoing as Sariel's brilliance met impossibly resilient darkness. Every blow from the angel sang of pure judgment; every counter from the demon lord resonated with ancient cunning.

"You believe mere light can end what is eternal?" Asmodeus hissed, shadows coiling around him like vipers. "I have endured eons. I have seen pantheons rise and crumble. You… are but a flare."

Sariel responded with a voice of iron and storm. "Eternal or not, no shadow may claim unchecked dominion while the cosmos watches upon thee."

The chamber convulsed with each exchange. Throne, walls, and floor were scorched and cracked, splintering under the sheer force of their confrontation. A marvelous scene far away from prying eyes.

The Hellway spat them out like a sluice of liquid shadow, depositing the five demigods onto a ridge overlooking the outskirts of daytime San Francisco. Reality snapped back with a jolt, the sun low on the horizon, smog curling around the city like muted smoke. Condottiero lingered nearby, perched atop a lamppost that hadn't existed moments ago, grinning as though the city itself were a toy.

"San Fran's a little… less chaotic than Hell," Percy muttered, brushing the snow from his shoulders. Bianca's eyes scanned the streets, arrows still nocked for any untoward surprises.

"Quiet for now," Percy added. "But that could change fast. God knows what happens down there"

Condottiero lingered just long enough to watch the demigods descend from the ridge above the city, his shadow weaving through the cracks in the pavement like smoke. Percy led the group along the quiet side streets, the distant hum of traffic below carrying faint echoes of the city's pulse. Condottiero tilted his head, eyes glinting with a familiar mischief.

"You've grown," he murmured, voice low, almost to himself, watching Percy step over a puddle. "Not much, but enough to remember."

Percy glanced up. "We shall meet again," he said, tone flat but carrying recognition. Condottiero gave a brief, impish smirk.

"I'll escort you to the outskirts. After that, you're on your own. The city is too active with... *others* that dislike my kind." Condottiero said, floating ahead a few paces, then looping back with a theatrical spin that scattered a flock of pigeons from a nearby rooftop. "And keep your wits about you. Hellway exit points are never entirely polite."

The demigods followed, their pace steady. The streets grew narrower. Condottiero led them along back alleys and service roads, keeping his distance but visible, a flicker in the corner of their eyes.

They came to a sudden drop-off, the city sprawling below, the faint outline of the Bay shimmering beyond. Percy signaled to the group. "Here's your exit. Safe enough for the moment."

Condottiero hovered, the shadow around him curling like smoke. "San Francisco outskirts. The Romanus are nearby. A friendly bit of advice. Find the Alchemist, before anything… unpleasant begins."

Eleni smiled in relief. "And you're leaving?"

Condottiero's grin widened, faintly mocking. "I have errands elsewhere. Consider it my parting gift. Remember, demigods: some shadows are friendly, some… merely polite."

With that, he vanished into a fold of darkness, leaving Percy, Bianca, Zoë, Eleni, and Grover standing on the ridge. The city below seemed ordinary enough.

Percy crouched, scanning the streets. "We move carefully. Shop first, gather intel, then continue toward [[New Rome]]. Eyes open. Stay quiet. Something has to be happening for him to warn us."

The five began their descent, the edge of the ridge giving way to winding streets and narrow alleys, the quiet of the city punctuated by distant honks and the occasional shuffle of a late pedestrian.

Eleni looked around. "I don't like this, Milord Perseus. Too many aggressive signatures. We need to move to safety."

He looked at her. It wasn't normal for her to look so wary of her surroundings, with her power and all. "Where to?"

"I detect 3 neutral auras in the city. One is a mere 5 minutes away."

The five demigods ducked into a small shopping avenue, a Small Book Shop under a striped awning drawing their attention, the bell above the door jingling faintly. Inside, the air was warm and faintly scented with old paper and polished wood. Shelves lined every wall, stacked with everything from dog-eared paperbacks to glossy art tomes. A teenager was busy at the counter, unaware of the figures that had passed just moments before.

Percy led the group silently through the aisles. Bianca's eyes scanned the shelves, Zoë floated quietly above the ground, and Eleni kept a careful watch near the door. Grover's ears twitched, picking up subtle vibrations in the floorboards, but none of them gave any outward sign of concern.

Percy approached the counter, speaking quietly. "We need supplies: anything that can help in emergencies—light, bindings, wards. Nothing flashy, just practical."

He nodded suspiciously, pulling down small jars of herbs, candles, and neatly wrapped scrolls. Josh added a few thick tomes from a nearby shelf. The group handled the items with efficiency, offering coins and bagging them in plain canvas sacks.

"And could you get your boss here? We have to talk to him please… Josh." Eyeing his ID on his chest.

The city-boy employee scoffed at him and the group behind the son of Poseidon. "You? And what army? Your cosplaying friends? I don–"

A firm hand pressed on Josh's shoulder. The gesture was both grounding and commanding. "Josh," he said softly, voice low enough that only the boy could hear, "you're done here for now. Go across the street to Pernelle's Coffee Shop. Tell my wife I sent you, with some Greek guests."

Josh blinked, confused. "Wait... what about-"

"Now," Nicholas interrupted, gently but insistently. Josh nodded, his curiosity suppressed under Nicholas's calm authority, and moved toward the door, glancing back only once before stepping out into the warm afternoon light.

Nicholas then slid behind the counter, assuming Josh's place with the ease of someone who knew both the store and the stakes far better. His eyes swept the aisles, noting the subtle energies that Percy's group radiated, sensing the implications of Greek questers near the Roman camp just beyond the city's hills. The hum of magical tension was faint but unmistakable, enough for Nicholas to recognize that the normal rhythm of the shop had just been infiltrated by forces far beyond ordinary.

He gave a slight nod toward Percy when the demigod reapproached with his request. "Supplies," Nicholas said firmly, voice measured. "I have nothing for your kind. The Romani have a chokehold on your pantheon's resources here." He took a breath. The Immortal Alchemist smelled many aromas. The minty smell of his own Magics, the smell of paper tomes of his wife Pernelle, and the group in front of him. The dark haired boy had an overwhelming smell of the sea, the blue-eyed girl in futuristic armor emanated petrichor, the goat-man and the persian princess lookalike both smelled like moss and dirt, while the timid girl at the back smelled of… he wrinkled his nose. Sulfur and Brimstone. *So much like Robespierre. Bah, bad memories of him.*

Percy's group handled the items efficiently, unaware of the silent vigilance now behind the counter.

Within minutes, the group was on their merry way. Unaware of the events unfolding behind them.

What was that disgusting smell?

Pernelle Flamel had barely sat down in her lovely little cafe shop opposite on the road to her husband when she saw five demigods as they moved on down the street, their bags of supplies swinging lightly against their sides. She paused, nostrils flaring, as the stench assaulted her again. Rotten eggs, acrid and heavy, crawling into her senses from somewhere beyond the city street. Perhaps a daughter of [[Pluto]] or another death god. Or… Robespierre.

Closing her book, she straightened, glancing out the window at the quiet street beyond. The Small Book Shop's striped awning flapped slightly in a warm breeze.

She'd been working in The Coffee Cup since her husband and her settled down in San Francisco a few years ago, and normally she'd have noticed any strange scents lingering outside the shop. But today, something different rippled through the air.

She whirled around to look across the street just as all the little square windows of the bookshop abruptly developed cracks and two simply exploded into dust. Wisps of green and yellow smoke curled out into the street and the air was filled with the stench of rotten eggs.

The smell of peppermint. The older woman's lips moved, but no words came out. She quickly crossed the street in confusion and ducked inside a side entrance.

The bookshop was in chaos. The shelves and tables were splintered and disintegrated about the room in heaps. Bookcases were strewn everywhere, shelves with holes in them, texts and maps lay crushed on the floor. The stench of rot and decay hung about the room.

A small gray man stood in the center of the floor. He was fastidiously brushing dust off the sleeve of his coat as he looked around before locking eyes with the older woman.

The gray man turned as Pernelle dashed into the bookshop. He gave a neat little bow. "Ah, Madame Perenelle. I was wondering where you were". "Where is Nicholas?" She demanded.

"Downstairs, I believe. I am searching for him. I must congratulate his guests, they led me straight to your door." The man looked around, before refocusing on her.

"And you. You are looking as lovely as ever", the gray man said, eyes fixed on Perry. "You haven't aged a day." He bowed again, an old-fashioned, courtly movement that he performed effortlessly. "It is always a joy to see you."

"I wish I could say the same for you, Maximilien Robespierre of Free France." She moved farther into the room, eyes darting from side to side. "I recognized your foul stench". Robespierre closed his eyes and breathed deeply. "It wasn't mine, funnily enough. I rather like the smell of brimstone. It is so" He paused. "So dramatic". Then his gray eyes snapped open and the smile faded. "I've come for the Philosopher's stone, Perenelle. And don't tell me you've destroyed it", he added. "Your continued remarkable good health is proof indeed of its existence. The British wand whippers would raise hell too. Especially the disciple of Nick's… Dumbledore, was it?"

"You will never get it", Perry snapped.

"My masters will.", Robespierre said. "The *Triumvir* accumulated many other treasures over the years. Only the Stone of Life seems to be perpetually out of reach. Now, make it easy on yourself and tell me where it is."

"Never happening!"

"I knew you would say that", Robespierre said, raising his hands, glowing with purple-violet energy, at her. "Humans are so predictable".

Percy led the group carefully down the narrow streets, their supplies secured and the bustle of the city fading behind them. The ridge above had given way to winding roads that curved upward, toward the lower slopes of Berkeley Hills. From here, the looming silhouette of New Rome could be seen, its stone walls rising high, patrolled by its usual sentinels.

As they reached the main gate, two familiar figures stepped forward from behind the reinforced barricades: the twin front guards of the city's entrance. One was tall, broad-shouldered, with a bronze-toned breastplate that caught the sunlight, the other shorter but wiry, carrying a long spear tipped with polished steel. Both bore the insignia of New Rome, their faces composed and alert, eyes scanning the newcomers with practiced caution.

The twin guards at the entrance of New Rome were Castor and Pollux, the sons of Mercury who had been stationed as permanent sentinels.

As they approached, Percy spoke first, raising his hand in a neutral gesture. "Pax. We're *Negotiatio Officialis*. We're here on official diplomatic business. No trouble."

Castor's bronze breastplate glinted in the sunlight, his spear held vertical. Pollux, shorter but wiry, mirrored his stance, both eyes sharp and assessing.

Percy continued, carefully adjusting the introductions for New Rome's hierarchy. "Salve, I am [[Percy Jackson]], son of [[Neptune]]. With me are [[Bianca di Angelo]], daughter of Pluto, Zoë Nightshade, agent of Diana, Eleni of Jupiter's, and [[Grover Underwood]], a companion familiar with the wilds. Ave."

Pollux's eyebrow rose. "Ave, but why should we trust you?"

Percy's tone sharpened, authority clear. "Because letting unknown forces operate unchecked threatens all of Rome. You want your walls secure, don't you? Then we speak plainly, or we waste time."

Castor exchanged a glance with Pollux, their silent communication precise. Finally, Castor nodded. "Bene, passage granted to the perimeter. Stay under watch. One false move, and you're stopped. You know the boundary rules?"

Percy inclined his head. "Understood. We will respect the boundaries."

Zoë walked lightly behind Percy, while Bianca, Eleni, and Grover trailed behind.

The group passed between Castor and Pollux, entering New Rome proper, their Roman titles acknowledged and tensions eased enough to proceed deeper into the city.

Percy exhaled slowly as he was greeted by the familiar hive-acropolis of New Rome, its homely roman cityscape stretched out to the edges of his peripherals. Along the paved path, small rocks led the path down to a familiar river, the Mini [[Tiber]]. The group stopped at the edge of the river, seeing the Son of Poseidon's hesitation.

"Zoe, Bianca, Grover, cross. Eleni and I will take the alternate path."

The group nodded, confused.

With a grunt, the demigod mustered his powers, and slowly, a section of the rushing river lifted, revealing a few struggling fishes in now-dry rocks and slit. With a small head shake, the Son of Poseidon and the Daughter of Zeus ducked under the stream, avoiding the truth-revealing waters. Meeting up with the confused trio at the other side of the bridge, the river behind them settled back down, and they continued on, while Percy craned his neck, looking for [[Terminus]], who usually popped up around here. Instead, he spied a cloud of dust rapidly approaching them

"Halt!" The command cracked like a whip. Two centurions barred their path, their crimson plumes stark against the marble. Percy recognized Marcus, scar bisecting his lip, and Decimus, whose gaze lingered too long on Zoë's hunter's bow. "State your business, *Barbari*." Marcus's hand rested on his gladius. Percy offered a curt nod, his voice slicing through the humidity. "*Negotiatio Officialis*. We seek Reyna Ramírez-Arellano. Or, [[Jason Grace]]." Decimus snorted. "Praetor Reyna doesn't parley with strays." Percy's smile didn't reach his eyes. "She will. I know She will. By right of *ius gentium* and *lex sacrata*. I carry the seal of *Neptune,* and by the *Concordia Compact*, which I remember was ratified after the Second Punic Accord, any envoy of an Olympian sovereign possesses standing to petition the praetorship directly, bypassing both tribunician veto and centurion authority. You'll register my claim with the *scribae* at the *curia praetoria*, mark it under category *diplomatica externa*, and enter the time of request for record. Then, by the twelfth decree of *mos maiorum*, refusal of reception constitutes breach of *fides publica*."

Percy reached into his jacket, producing a blob of water, in the shape of a tablet stamped with the trident and Latin script denoting "Sigillum Imperii Neptuni." Marcus's expression faltered, the seal was unmistakable, and even though Neptune was commonly underlooked among the Romans, forging a sigil like that would constitute a smiting from the gods.

Marcus squinted, lowering his pilum a fraction but not relaxing. "Negotiatio what now?"

Percy didn't blink. "Official business. I need to see your praetors. Reyna Ramírez-Arellano or Jason Grace."

Decimus laughed. "You need to see them? That's funny. Buddy here thinks they'll drop everything because some soaked stranger shows up waving Latin words around?"

Percy's expression didn't shift. "Othrys."

That got silence. The two centurions traded glances, uncertain. Marcus finally scoffed. "You mean the Titan place? Yeah, we've heard the stories. So what, you want us to send a polite letter to Cronus?"

"Not letters," Percy said flatly. "An assault. Before they regroup."

Decimus snorted. "You're serious? You waltz in from nowhere, talk about attacking a mountain full of monsters, and think the Senate will sign off? You *even Roman*?"

"Yes," Percy said evenly.

That drew some uncertain murmurs, especially from his now *highly* confused questmates, but before Marcus could answer, the air shimmered beside them. A low growl rumbled through the street as two metallic wolves padded forward, Aurum and Argentum, their gold and silver hides gleaming in the midday light.

Aurum's nostrils flared, and the golden wolf circled Percy once, hackles raised. Instead of aggression, there was recognition. The creature sat, head bowed. Argentum mirrored the motion, tail flicking once in deference.

Percy inclined his head. "I usually don't lie, you know. *[[Legio XXI Rapax]]*, sworn to the [[Octavian]] of old, formed in the thirty-first year before the current era. I commanded under its standard, albeit temporarily."

A hush followed. Decimus looked shaken. Marcus stood still.

Without further challenge, he motioned to Decimus, who struck his pilum against the paving stones thrice. Within moments, the members of (if he remembered correctly) Cohort II formed up around the group and began jogging toward the praetors' hall.

As they approached the marble steps of the curia praetoria, the rhythm of stomping boots quickly quieted down as Percy halted before the bronze doors, sigil still in hand. The guards peeled back to form a semicircle, halberds crossed. Very cinematic.

A guard, mid forties by the looks of it, stepped forward. "By decree of the Senate and People of Rome, the praetors convene in closed session. State your petition."

Percy lifted his voice so it carried through the open forum. "On the authority of Neptune Ennosigaios, I call the praetors of New Rome to arms. Mount Othrys stirs again."

A familiar whistle cut across the courtyard.

**"Aurum! Argentum!"**

Reyna Ramírez-Arellano strode into view, her armor gleaming, a short purple cape brushing against her greaves. Jason Grace followed a step behind, expression caught somewhere between curiosity and concern. Reyna's pace slowed as she spotted her wolves sitting obediently at Percy's feet.

Her eyes narrowed. "They don't leave my side unless I tell them. Explain."

Marcus straightened instantly. "Praetor, he came bearing a diplomatic sigil. Claimed right of entry under hocus-pocus. Then your wolves arrived and…" He trailed off, gesturing helplessly toward the two metallic beasts now lounging beside Percy like they'd found a new master.

Jason's eyebrows rose. "You've got to be kidding. The wolves *like* him? They keep trying to bite my fingers off every time I try to *pet* them!"

"That seal's genuine. No idiot dares to forge a sigil." Reyna declared, half in disbelief.

Percy inclined his head. "Then you know I'm not wasting your time. The Titans are regrouping at Othrys. If they consolidate, you won't have the luxury of strategy later."

Jason crossed his arms. "Even if that's true, why tell us? You could've gone to your Greek base of yours."

"Because they don't have legions," Percy replied simply. "And I don't have time for councils that argue while the enemy rebuilds."

Reyna studied him. "You expect New Rome to march on the word of a stranger?"

"Not a stranger," Percy said evenly. "Citizen. Legio XXI Rapax."

Jason frowned. "That legion is gone"

Reyna's fingers brushed Aurum's neck, feeling the hum of divine energy beneath the metal. The wolves made no protest. "Then prove you're worth listening to."

Percy's gaze flicked to her. "How?"

Jason's grin was sharp, almost mischievous. "By tradition. Trial by Steel!." He gestured toward the Colosseum rising beyond the Senate complex. "You win, we hear you out. Lose, and you walk out the gate like everyone else."

Percy's expression didn't change. "Conditions?"

"No divine interference," Reyna said.

"Easy enough," Percy said. "Who am I fighting?"

Jason looked to Marcus, then to Decimus, who both straightened like men suddenly drafted. "First Decimus," Jason said. "Then Marcus. And if you survive, you get me."

Reyna folded her arms. "Win, and the Senate will consider your proposal. Lose…" She let the pause hang.

Percy gave a short nod. "Then I'll make sure Othrys doesn't wait."

Jason exhaled through his nose, half a smirk curling at his lip. "You've got nerve, I'll give you that. All right, Graceus. Let's see if you can back it up."

The legionnaires erupted in murmurs as the group turned toward the Colosseum.
