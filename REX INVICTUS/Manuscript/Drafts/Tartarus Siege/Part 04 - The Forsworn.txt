THE SIEGE OF THE PYRIPHLEGETHON ARSENAL

PART FOUR. THE FORSWORN AND THE COHORT OF NEMESIS

"An oath is a debt. Nemesis collects."
Inscription over the gate of the Nemesis cohort-house at Rhamnous Primaris

A NEW THREAT. ROTATION SIX, CYCLE 1

By the start of Rotation Six, the Third had been on the Cinder Flats for seven rotations of front time, counting the build-up. It had transferred something over five million soldiers to take two belts of trenches and a few hundred metres of a third, and the Archflamen's army had lost more than a million of its own, permanently. Neither army was close to breaking.
Lord-General Castorre asked the Command at Watchpoint Hades for a new Line Phalanx. He told the Command that the Archflamen had been reduced to his second belt and that one more Line Phalanx, thrown in on the western Flats, would break it before the end of the rotation and put the Third back on the Répertoire's timetable. The Command sent the request down the Dionysian Logistics Directorate's channels, and the Directorate's clerks went looking in the depots for three Trench Regiments that no one else had a claim on.
They were still looking when the Fifth Line Phalanx's picket on the Mora Cut reported a column coming up out of the Cut on the old ore road, from the direction of the Styx.
The picket was a maniple of the Twenty-Sixth Trench Regiment, posted at the top of the ore road where it came up the face of the Cut below Ore Head. The ore road ran down into the dark for eleven kilometres to the floor of the Cut, and then east along the floor for three hundred to where the Cut opened on the Styx. Nothing had come up it in four rotations.
The column came up it at the change of the fourth watch. There were about four hundred of them, in the Empire's grey trench armour and the long black cloaks of the Styx Watch, marching in step, and their transponders answered the picket's challenge with the codes of a cohort of the Ninth Styx Legion, which had held the confluence at Depth 44 for a thousand years.
The maniple's optio asked them their business. Their commander, a tall woman with a centurion's crest and a face like a closed door, told him that they had been cut off at the confluence when the river rose in Rotation Two, and had fought their way up the Cut for four rotations, and had heard the guns on the Flats from the floor of the Cut, and come to them. She gave him the cohort's number and her own name and code. The optio checked them against the Répertoire. They were good. The Ninth Styx Legion had lost contact with a cohort at the confluence in Rotation Two, and the cohort had that number, and its centurion had that code.
The optio passed them through. He logged them on the picket's slate. They went on into the Fifth's lines, and the Fifth's staff sent them a guide and gave them a stretch of reserve trench on Leaf 52-33, behind the Forty-Fourth Trench Regiment, to rest in while the Command decided what to do with them.
They were gone by the next cycle. The Fifth's staff assumed they had been sent on up the Stair to rejoin their Legion. Nobody checked.

THE FORTY-FOURTH TRENCH REGIMENT, LEAF 52-33. ROTATION SIX, CYCLE 19

Eighteen cycles later, the Forty-Fourth made an attack on the second belt on Leaf 52-33 with Hoplon carriers.
It was the best attack the Third had made on the second belt since the Saturation. The Forty-Fourth had planned it for a month. The Hoplons went in under a short, heavy barrage at the second bell, thirty-one of them in a wedge, with fifty breachers in each one behind the armoured ramp. The barrage had been timed to lift as they reached the wire. They went through the wire on their tracks and across the forward trench of the second belt, and dropped their ramps on the support trench behind it, and put fifteen hundred breachers into the Archflamen's second belt in four minutes.
Centurion Ilsa Marrow's Sixth Cohort was in the Hoplons. By the end of the first hour it held four hundred metres of the support trench, and the Forty-Fourth's cohorts were coming across no-man's land behind it to widen the breach, and the Forty-Fourth's colonel was asking the Fifth for the Twentieth Armoured.
Then the Archflamen counter-attacked.
The counter-attack came along the support trench from the east, and Marrow, on the relay, heard her forward sections stop answering. She went along the trench to see. At the traverse she met her own soldiers coming back the other way, and behind them, in the bay beyond, she saw the counter-attack.
They were in the Empire's grey trench armour. They had the long black cloaks of the Styx Watch. Their transponders, on her own visor, showed blue. She had a second to think that they were reinforcements. Then one of them shot the soldier in front of her through the visor at six metres, without breaking step.
They went along the Forty-Fourth's breach like a scythe. They moved like nothing she had seen in nine years on the Flats. They did not take cover, and they did not seem to need it. Their armour was the Third's trench armour, but the Third's rounds did not go through it, and the rounds they fired went through the Third's like paper. They had swords, short and black, and in the bays they used them. Marrow saw one of her breachers go down with a black blade through the joint of his plate at the shoulder, and she saw his transponder go grey on her visor. She waited for it to show the upload. It did not show anything.
By the end of the second hour the breach was closed. All thirty-one Hoplons were burning in the Archflamen's second belt. The Forty-Fourth's cohorts in no-man's land had been stopped by the counter-barrage, and the fifteen hundred breachers in the support trench were dead or taken, all but sixty who got back over the wire on their bellies after dark.
Marrow was one of the sixty. She lay in the forward trench of the Forty-Fourth for an hour after she came back in, with her face on the duckboards, and did not speak to anyone. Then she stood up and went to the colonel's dugout to make her report. She told him that the counter-attack had worn the Empire's armour and shown the Empire's codes. She told him that it had used black swords, and that the soldiers killed by the swords had not uploaded.
Her report went to the Fifth Line Phalanx, and from the Fifth to the Third's staff on Quarterdeck Nineteen. The Third's staff sent it to the Répertoire. The Répertoire reported that the codes Marrow had read on the counter-attack's transponders were those of a cohort of the Ninth Styx Legion, which had entered the Third's lines at the Mora Cut eighteen cycles earlier, and been logged by a picket of the Twenty-Sixth.
Of the fifteen hundred breachers, four hundred and nine had been killed by the black swords. All four hundred and nine were null.

THE KATABASION. ROTATION SIX, CYCLE 2

They had come to the Archflamen a cycle after the picket passed them through, by the ore road under Ore Head, which ran on from the Cut into the second belt. The Arsenal's own sentries had challenged them in the Archflamen's name. They had answered that they had come to see him.
Their commander was waiting for him in the Katabasion when he came down for his evening prayers. She had taken off her centurion's crest. She was standing in front of the reliquary, looking at the bones of the First Descent through the glass, and she did not turn around when he came in. The Tabularium's record of the meeting notes that her first words were that she had known some of them, though not well. The Archflamen asked her who she was.
She said that her name was Hesper, and that she had once been a Legate of the Ninth Styx Legion, which had held the confluence at Depth 44 for a thousand years, and which still did. She had sworn its oath on the Styx when she was nineteen, as every soldier of the Ninth did, to hold the confluence until the Imperator came back up through it. She had held it for six hundred years. Then the Command had ordered the Ninth to give up a third of the confluence to save a hold upriver, and she had refused, and the Command had relieved her, and she had gone down into the Styx with the cohorts that would follow her, rather than break the oath.
Her account of what followed is the only one the Forsworn ever gave of themselves. The river had taken them. That, she said, was what the Styx did with an oath broken from above: it kept the oath and took the person. The Forsworn were what remained, and they alone still held the oath. The record notes that her eyes were the colour of the Styx, which is no colour.
The Archflamen observed that she had no implants. Hesper confirmed that the Forsworn had cut them out long before. The Empire's soldiers went up when they died and came back down and believed it a gift, and the Forsworn had seen where the uploads went on the way up. She told him that his Haruspex had asked the river for help, that the Forsworn had heard, that others had heard as well, and that the Forsworn had been closest.
Asked what the Forsworn wanted, Hesper answered that they wanted what the Archflamen wanted. The Empire's soldiers were to remember what dying was.
She offered him four hundred of her own, which was all she had brought, and two thousand more who would come up the Cut when she called them. She offered him their training for his thralls, whose fighting she had watched for a cycle from the ore road and thought poor, and her own experience, which was six hundred years of trench war on the Styx. She offered him something else, which she did not explain in front of his clerks, and which Dace afterwards took her to see the strays about.
The Archflamen accepted. He had been told by Dace for two rotations that the river would answer if it was asked, and he had not believed it, and now it had.
The Forsworn went straight to work on the second belt. They went into the trenches with the thrall cohorts and taught the thralls to fight in the bays, and to wait in the shelters until the barrage lifted, and to counter-attack at once, before the Third could bring its next wave across. They took the best of the Sworn and trained them as raiders, and sent them through the Third's lines at night to kill officers and signallers in their dugouts. They went out themselves through the Third's lines in the Empire's armour, with the Empire's codes, and walked through the Third's rear areas as Styx Watch, and nobody stopped them.
Then, on the Archflamen's authority, they went up the Echo Mast.
The Echo Mast had been dark since the Tabularium fell. Its relays were the Arsenal's link to the noosphere and through it to Watchpoint Hades, and the Archflamen's armourers had shut them down, since nothing that came down them could do him any good. Hesper's signallers did not open them to the noosphere. They turned the Mast around, down instead of up, and broadcast on the old bands the Arsenal had used to talk to the holds below Depth 40, before there were holds below Depth 40.
They broadcast for nine cycles. The Third's relay operators heard it on every set on the Flats, a long carrier tone with something under it, like a voice speaking a long way off. They could not read it. The Répertoire could not read it either. It logged it, and logged that it was being sent down the river.
Down the river, it was heard. Down the Phlegethon, and down the Lethe where it met the Phlegethon at Depth 43, and down the Cocytus where the Cocytus ran under the ice, the things that had been waiting in the deep for a war to come to them heard the Echo Mast calling them to the Pyriphlegethon Arsenal, to a fight against the Empire with a promise of slaughter and salvage at the end of it. They began to come up.

QUARTERDECK NINETEEN. ROTATION SIX, CYCLE 40

The Command at Watchpoint Hades read Centurion Marrow's report on the forty-first cycle of Rotation Six.
It had been passed up with three others: the picket's log of the Ninth Styx Legion cohort at the Mora Cut, the Répertoire's record of the carrier tone from the Echo Mast, and a note from the Répertoire's technical officers on the four hundred and nine null transfers from Leaf 52-33. The note said that the technical officers had not seen a weapon do that before. It said they were aware of one in the archive. They referred the Command to the Ninth Styx Legion's own records, eighteen hundred years old, of what had happened to the Ninth's soldiers who were cut by Styx-iron at the confluence.
The Command sent for the Ninth Styx Legion's records. They confirmed that the cohort the picket had passed through had been lost at the confluence in Rotation Two. They confirmed that its centurion's code had been reissued since. They had been issued, the records showed, eleven centuries before, to a Legate of the Ninth named Hesper Calloway, who had refused the Command's order at the confluence and been relieved, and had gone into the Styx with nine cohorts of the Legion, and been entered as null.
The Ninth's records called them the Forsworn.
Castorre was given the substance of it. He did not take it in at first. He asked his chief of staff whether it meant that the Archflamen had found the Ninth Styx Legion's codes.
Corvis corrected him. The Archflamen had the Ninth Styx Legion's dead, the soldiers who had gone into the river, and they had come back up. They knew the Third's codes because they had once been the Third's own. The picket had logged four hundred of them. Castorre's staff noted the comparison with the casualty leaf for Leaf 52-33, where four hundred had closed a breach held by fifteen hundred. Corvis's assessment was that the Forsworn carried swords whose wounds the vats could not undo, and brought six hundred years of trench war with them. They also had the Mast.
He sent the substance of it up to the Command the same cycle, with a request for the new Line Phalanx at once, and asked for something to be done about the Echo Mast.

THE COHORT OF NEMESIS. ROTATION SEVEN, CYCLE 30

At the start of Rotation Seven, the Third was given what it had asked for, more or less.
A convoy of the Tartarus Fleet came down through the ceiling to Landing Shelf Kappa on the thirtieth cycle, with the Eighth Line Phalanx in its holds, three Trench Regiments, the Fiftieth, the Fifty-Second and the Fifty-Fourth. They had been promised to a reformation peak further up the Phlegethon, and Castorre's family in the College had got them diverted. The Fifty-Fourth was a regiment of Lethe Hold drafts, raised from the ration rolls in Rotation Two, when the shells stopped coming. Most of its soldiers had never been to Depth 40.
The Command had also done something that it did not tell Castorre about until it was done.
The Répertoire did not believe that the Archflamen's war could be allowed to spread down the river. The carrier tone from the Echo Mast had been heard at Depth 43 and below, and things were coming up the river toward the Arsenal that the Third had not been built to fight. Pyrrhus Command, which commanded the Empire's pure-blood cohorts in the theatre, had not waited for the Third to ask. It had sent its own envoys to the Olympian orders on the ceiling worlds to ask for help, under its own seal.
The first to answer was Cohors Nemesis Prima.
The Cohort of Nemesis was a pure-blood order, the children of Nemesis and their own children, sworn to the goddess and recruited at the cohort-house on Rhamnous Primaris from those whose blood index ran above ninety. It did not hold ground. It had never held a line in its history. It went where an oath had been broken, and made the breaking cost. It kept a battle-carrier and two strike cruisers on permanent readiness at Rhamnous for the purpose, and it was able to answer inside a month. It sent half its strength, two thousand and forty of its sworn, under the Prefect of the Cohort himself, Lucan Ardent.
The battle-carrier Inexorable came down through the ceiling over the southern Flats on the thirtieth cycle, with the strike cruisers Adrasteia and Rhamnous and nine escorts, and held station outside the reach of the ceiling-lances.
Prefect Ardent did not signal the Third. He set about the mission he had given himself, which was to take the Phlegethon Docks and destroy them, so that the Arsenal could not be reinforced from the river. He did not need the Third's help, and he did not see any reason to tell it what he meant to do. While the Third's staff on Quarterdeck Nineteen were working out where to put the Eighth Line Phalanx, Ardent and his centurion-masters were in the low geometry over the southern Flats, putting the Cohort's strength down onto the ash.

THE PREFECT'S PLAN

The Cohort came down south of the docks, on the Gravel Strand.
The Phlegethon Docks lay at the south-eastern foot of the spur, where the Arsenal met the river. They were the Arsenal's river port, eleven kilometres of quays and slipways and warehouses on the bank of the Phlegethon, where the ore barges came up from the lower shelves and the shell barges went out to the holds downriver. They lay beyond the Demus Gorge from the Flats, out of reach of the Third's line, and the Third had never fought there. Three chain-bridges carried the docks' roads across the Gorge to the spur. The Gravel Strand was a shelf of river shingle at the foot of the Kerberos Hills, forty kilometres south of the docks along the bank, out of reach of the lances. The Cohort's Lantern gunships came down to it from the ships through the ceiling cracks to the south of the lance envelope, in a long loop, and unloaded the Cohort in a few hours.
Ardent chose the docks for three reasons.
They were the Arsenal's only way to the river. Whatever came up the Phlegethon to help the Archflamen would come to the docks.
An attack on the docks would also force the Archflamen to send troops to meet it. The docks had a small garrison. They had never been threatened by the Third, and the Archflamen had stripped them for the second belt. If he wanted to keep them, he would have to bring troops back from the second belt, and the Third would find the second belt thinner.
The third reason the Cohort kept to itself, as it kept most things. There were Forsworn on the Flats. The Cohort of Nemesis knew the name Hesper Calloway. It had been looking for her for eleven hundred years. A Legate of the Ninth Styx Legion who had broken her oath to the Command, and then sworn on the Styx that she had not, was the oldest debt on the Cohort's books. Ardent had brought his Castigator, Ennio Dask, whose work for the Cohort was getting answers out of oath-breakers, to get answers out of any of the Forsworn the Cohort could take.
His plan was a fast armoured column across the Strand and up the riverbank, to surround the docks and attack them where they were weakest. He could not afford a war of attrition. His ships would not come down inside the lance envelope, so he would be fighting in the docks without fire support from the ceiling. To keep the Archflamen from reinforcing the docks while he did it, he would take the three chain-bridges across the Demus Gorge and drop them into it. With the bridges gone, nothing could cross from the spur or the Flats to the docks. That task went to the Cohort's Third Century, under Centurion-Master Ilario Kett, who was to take the bridges and blow them and then join the attack on the docks.
The Cohort's arrival changed the war more than its numbers did. Two thousand pure-bloods in the Arsenal's rear could strike anywhere. They could fly over the second belt and land on the spur. Hesper understood this, and so did the Archflamen's commanders. They could not risk the Cohort coming down on the Acropolis with its garrison gone, so they had to pull troops out of the second belt to guard the Acropolis, the docks, the vaults and the lances. The second belt grew thinner by thirty thousand soldiers in a week. Two thousand of the Cohort of Nemesis had moved more of the Archflamen's army than the Third's general attack had in Rotation Four.

THE ATTACK ON THE PHLEGETHON DOCKS. ROTATION SEVEN, CYCLE 34

The Cohort moved on the docks on the thirty-fourth cycle of Rotation Seven.
The column went up the Strand along the riverbank, forty kilometres of shingle between the river and the hills. The Cohort's Areion striders went ahead of it, fast bipedal mounts ridden by the Cohort's scouts, keeping low along the shingle and looking for blocks on the road. Behind the striders came the armour: the Cohort's own heavy carriers and assault tanks, its Hoplons and its siege carriages, and the mobile mortar crawlers it called Furies, which fired keyed shells from the move and moved again before anything could fire back. The Lantern gunships flew cover over the column in the dark, in touch with the striders. The Third Century stayed at the landing ground on the Strand until the bridges were wanted.
The Arsenal sent its own scouts down the riverbank to find them, light crawlers and walker pickets of the dock garrison, hunting up and down the shingle. Some of them were caught by the striders and destroyed. Before long the Archflamen's commanders knew where the Cohort was going, and the docks' garrison was reinforced from the spur. Prefect Ardent rode at the head of the column himself in his command carrier, the Debt Unpaid. By the end of the first watch the column was in sight of the docks.

THE CHAIN-BRIDGES OF THE DEMUS GORGE

As Ardent's column closed on the docks, Centurion-Master Kett's Century went for the bridges.
The southern chain-bridge was the longest of the three, a kilometre and a half of iron deck hung on chains across the Demus Gorge, which went down beneath it for nine kilometres into the dark. Kett's assault sections went in by Lantern. They jumped from the gunships at low level onto the eastern end of the deck, at the Arsenal's checkpoint, and took it before the checkpoint's garrison could get to its guns. Most of the garrison ran. The ones who stayed were cut down. Two more Lanterns came over the bridge firing, and broke up the checkpoint, and set the far end of the deck burning.
Kett's line sections came down as the Lanterns touched. They went along the deck at a run with their rifles firing, and drove the bridge's garrison back toward the western end, into the assault sections that already held it. The garrison was caught between them and killed.
It took eleven minutes. Kett put a heavy weapons section on the western end to cover the approach from the spur. Soon after, an Arsenal column came along the road from the spur, heading for the docks: Thureos tanks, Hoplon carriers, walkers and a battery of divine mortars on crawlers. The heavy weapons section knocked out the lead tank with a lance-cannon. It burned across the road, and the carriers behind it swerved around it, and the heavy weapons section knocked them out one after another. In a few minutes the road from the spur was blocked by burning hulls.
While the heavy weapons section held the road, the assault sections set charges on the bridge's anchor towers and on the chains. Kett called his whole Century back across the bridge and the Lanterns came down and took them off. The first bridge had cost him three wounded. All three were fit to go on fighting. As the last Lantern lifted off and turned north, Kett set off the charges. The anchor towers went one after another, and the chains went, and the bridge fell into the Demus Gorge under its own weight. The Century's gunners counted forty-one seconds before the sound of it hitting anything came back up.
By the end of the cycle, Kett's Century had taken and dropped the other two bridges across the Gorge. The Arsenal's roads to the docks had been cut.

THE DOCKS. ROTATION SEVEN, CYCLES 34 TO 44

As Kett's Century dropped the bridges, Ardent closed on the docks.
His striders found an Arsenal armoured column moving south out of the docks to meet him on the riverbank, and he sent his Lanterns at it and his own column forward. By the time the Cohort's heavy carriers came up, the shingle was covered in the smoke of burning Arsenal hulls. The carriers went into the rest of the column with their lance-cannons, and cut through its tanks and carriers as it tried to get back to the docks. Ardent's column went through it and swung east around the docks.
Ardent had Kett's report of the first bridge by then. He meant to attack the docks from the east, draw the garrison onto him, and then put Kett's Century into the docks from the west, and crush the garrison between them.
The striders found him the way into the docks, and he sent in the siege carriages, with the Cohort's line centuries behind them in their carriers. The siege carriages knocked down the warehouses the garrison had fortified before the sworn got out of their carriers, and the sworn went into the rubble and killed whoever was left in it. The Cohort went through the streets of the docks, wrecking as it went. The Lanterns came back from the ships rearmed and bombed the garrison wherever the striders found it. The Furies fired salvo after salvo into the docks and moved after every salvo, and the Arsenal's gunners on the spur, firing back across the Gorge, could not catch them.
Ardent did not stop when the dark watch came. By the first bell of the second cycle, the Cohort had cleared the eastern approaches to the docks' northern slipways, and the garrison had fallen back. Kett's Century had come down to the west of the main slipways in the night, as a stop against the garrison running.
By the first bell, the garrison held the big buildings around the slipways: the shell sheds, the barge hangars and the repair shops. It had made strongpoints of them. On the second cycle, Ardent was content to shell them and probe them. He was not trying only to take the docks. He wanted the garrison destroyed with them, and he gave it the cycle to bring more of itself into the ring around the slipways.
By the third cycle, the docks were a ruin. Ardent knew where he would attack in strength. He gave the Cohort's Sixth Century to Castigator Dask, and gave Dask the honour of the Wheel, the Cohort's war-standard, to take into battle. He chose Signifer Thessaly Kord to carry it. The Sixth went south around the slipways and attacked to close the ring, with siege carriages and Furies behind it, and was soon in street fighting with the garrison in the rubble of the shell sheds. The Cohort's sworn fought through the ruins with flame and sword and keyed grenades. At the end of the cycle the streets were full of the dead. Dask reported fifteen of the Sixth transferred and the southern docks clear.
On the fourth cycle, Ardent called his centurion-masters to his carrier. They planned the end of it: three attacks on the slipways, one after another, from the east by Ardent, then from the south by Dask, then from the west by Kett. Over three cycles each attack squeezed the garrison tighter. In the course of Kett's attack, the Third Century overran the northern barge pens and found Forsworn there.
Ardent wanted them taken alive. Every one the Cohort could take. It was what the Cohort had come for.
On the tenth cycle, Ardent ordered the last assault. He led it himself, on foot, with the First, Second and Fifth Centuries, into the ruins of the slipways where the last of the dock garrison was holding out. He went into the garrison with his sword, the Scale, which was black iron with silver along the edge, and the garrison broke in front of him.
It was a trap. The Forsworn had been waiting in the slipways under the garrison, in the dry docks and the drainage culverts, and had let the garrison draw the Cohort onto them. They came up out of the ground in the middle of the Cohort's assault, in the Empire's grey armour, with their black swords.
Signifer Kord was killed in the first minute, shot a dozen times. The Wheel fell with her. Ardent saw it fall. The Wheel could not be left on the ground. He went for it across sixty metres of open slipway under fire. He reached it as the Forsworn did. The Cohort's sworn and the Forsworn fought over Kord's body, and neither side gave a metre. While the rest of the Cohort fought its way out of the trap, Ardent killed the last of the Forsworn around the standard and lifted it up.
Then Hesper came out of the smoke.
She was in the Ninth Styx Legion's old parade armour, black, with the Legion's eagle on the breast and the Styx-water still on it, never dry. A sworn of the Third Century went for her and she cut him in half. Ardent turned to meet her with the Wheel in his left hand and the Scale in his right.
They fought for a long time. Ardent was a son of Nemesis with a blood index of ninety-seven, and he had fought for the Cohort for three hundred years. He was not enough. Hesper had held the confluence of the Styx for six hundred years and had been in the river for eleven hundred more, and the river was in her. Her blows came down on his armour until it split, and the blood came out through the joints. She drove him back across the slipway. With her shoulder she knocked him off his feet, and with a low cut she took the Scale out of his hand. He lay in the rubble with the Wheel held against his chest. She lifted the black sword.
A heavy gun hit her in the side and threw her across the slipway.
It was Castigator Dask, coming up the slipway at a run with a section of the Cohort's heavy sworn in plate. He went into Hesper with his rod of office. He struck her down with a blow that split her breastplate, and she fell, dazed and bleeding grey Styx-water. Dask turned to see to the Prefect. When he turned back, she was gone. There was a culvert grating where she had fallen, and it was open.
The ambush cost the Cohort dearly. The Forsworn could not hold the slipways alone, though, and they did not try. When Hesper had gone, they went back into the ground after her. The Cohort hunted down and killed the rest of the dock garrison.
Two hundred and twelve of the Cohort's sworn were transferred in the battle for the docks. Eighty-eight of them had been killed by Styx-iron, and did not reach Watchpoint Hades. The Cohort had not taken one of the Forsworn alive. The Inexorable's holds held three thousand captured thralls of the dock garrison, for Dask's people to question at Rhamnous.
After ten cycles the docks were the Cohort's. Its engineers took them apart with charges. Two cycles later the Phlegethon Docks were wrecked beyond repair, the quays in the river and the slipways cracked through, and the barge hangars down. Then the Cohort of Nemesis went back up through the ceiling and left Tartarus.
Prefect Ardent sent the Third one signal before he went. It went to Quarterdeck Nineteen, under the Cohort's seal, and it said that the Phlegethon Docks had been destroyed and the Arsenal cut off from the river. It said that the Cohort would be back for the Legate.
Castorre read it in the command ring and asked Corvis what Legate.

THE VATS AT WATCHPOINT HADES. ROTATION SEVEN, CYCLE 50

The Third's medical officers had a room at Watchpoint Hades for the soldiers who came back wrong.
It had been a store for nectar casks once. Now it had forty beds, and a medicus-centurion named Hanno Ricci, who had been taken off the Flats by the Répertoire in Rotation Five for having been transferred nineteen times in two rotations, and had been given the room instead. The soldiers in it had come back slow, or strange, or with something missing. Most of them got better and went back down. Some did not.
Centurion Ilsa Marrow came to see him on the fiftieth cycle of Rotation Seven. She had been sent up for a month's rest after Leaf 52-33. She had not been transferred. The Forty-Fourth's medicus had sent her up anyway.
Ricci's notes of the conversation were later added to the Hunt's file on the nulls. Marrow asked about the null transfers, and Ricci told her what the technical officers knew. A null was an upload that reached the vats empty, or did not reach them at all. There had always been a few. A soldier taken by the river did not upload, a soldier whose implant failed did not, and the Phlegethon took a few uploads a rotation out of the air at Depth 40. There had always been a list. Marrow pointed out that it was longer now, and Ricci agreed that it was much longer.
The four hundred and nine killed by the black swords were all on it. Ricci explained them from the Ninth Styx Legion's old records. Styx-iron cut what the implant was trying to save. The soul went into the Styx with the blood, and the implant uploaded an empty room. In Ricci's view the swords were the easy part to understand.
The difficulty was the rest of the list, which was eleven metres long by the fiftieth cycle. The technical officers had marked every name with the place of death. Most of the marks were near relay pylons. After the swords, the river and the failed implants were taken off, more soldiers remained than the Third had ever lost in a rotation. They had been killed by ordinary shells in ordinary trenches. Their implants had fired, the relays had handed their uploads up into the noosphere and logged them going, and they had arrived at Watchpoint Hades empty. Ricci's conclusion was that something was taking them in the air, between the pylons and the ceiling, during the seven seconds they were in the open. The technical officers suspected the Echo Mast. It had been broadcasting down the river since Rotation Six, and they believed it was also listening.
Marrow went back down on the eightieth cycle. She asked to be posted to a cohort of the Forty-Fourth that held a stretch of trench as far as possible from the nearest relay pylon. The colonel did not ask why. By the end of the rotation, a third of the Forty-Fourth's centurions had asked for the same thing.

BREAKTHROUGH

"Forward. Forward. The ground keeps what you give it, so give it everything."
Colonel Damaris Lowe, Fifty-Second Trench Regiment, Eighth Line Phalanx

The Third did not know what the Cohort of Nemesis had done for it until the end of Rotation Seven, when its spotters counted the Archflamen's cohorts on the second belt and found thirty thousand fewer of them than at the start.
The Archflamen had needed them elsewhere. With the docks gone, he had no way to bring anything up the river, and anything that came up the river to help him would have to come up the Phlegethon's cliffs onto the spur itself. He put his best troops on the spur to wait for it. He put more around the Acropolis, and in the vaults, and in the lance silos, against the Cohort's return. To find them, he stripped the second belt.
The Third's centurions felt it before the Répertoire counted it. The counter-attacks on the second belt came later than they used to, and lighter. The barrage still came down on any trench the Third took, but the cohorts behind the barrage were thinner, and slower, and more of them were thralls and fewer were garrison. Twice in the last month of Rotation Seven, a regiment of the Third took a nest on the second belt and held it overnight, which no regiment had done since the Saturation.
The Third's Lord-General wanted a breakthrough. He had wanted one for three rotations. In the first cycle of Rotation Eight, with the second belt thinner than it had ever been and the Eighth Line Phalanx fresh on the western Flats, the Répertoire told him that he could have one.
