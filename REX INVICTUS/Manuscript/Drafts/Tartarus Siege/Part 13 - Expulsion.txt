THE SIEGE OF THE PYRIPHLEGETHON ARSENAL

PART THIRTEEN. EXPULSION

"For the ones who call up what eats the soul, only an ending is enough."
Archon Damar Orthes of the Achillean Order

THE LEDGER OF NEMESIS. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLE 190

On the hundred and ninetieth cycle of Rotation Eighteen, the Third Siege Army received a signal it had not requested.

CODE 78 – Socius Voca. Origin: Rhamnous Primaris, Cohors Nemesis Prima. Destination: Third Siege Army, Acropolis Front.
Content: Cohors Nemesis Secunda inbound. Battle-carrier Adrasteia's Ledger and escort. Arrival, eleven cycles.
Verdict: Logged. No objection on file.

The Second Cohort of the pure-blood Order of Nemesis had been raised at Rhamnous within a rotation of the First Cohort's withdrawal from the Phlegethon Docks. The Order had not been asked to return. It was not the Order's practice to wait for a request. Its records describe the Forsworn as an open account, and the Order considered an open account at Depth 40 to be its own business whether the Third agreed or not.
The Cohort's officers came down the cable from the Adrasteia's Ledger to the Slag Gate spur on the two hundred and first cycle, the only ground near the Acropolis then held securely enough for a meeting. They were Prefect Sabra Ilion, commanding the Second Cohort, and Castigator Ennio Dask, the Order's senior chaplain in the theatre. Dask was already known to the Third. In Rotation Eight he had led the counter-charge on the Phlegethon Docks that pulled Prefect Ardent of the First Cohort out from under Hesper Unsworn's blade, and his name appears in the Third's casualty correspondence from that rotation more than twenty times.
Lieutenant Thalia Grace received them as commander of the Third. The terms Ilion offered were set down afterward in the Hunt's own log of the meeting, and they were short. The Second Cohort would fight in the final assault. In return, Hesper Unsworn was to be taken alive and handed to the Order for transport to Rhamnous. Every Forsworn taken alive would go with her. There would be no interrogation by the Hunt, no Enforcement custody and no AtRep transcript of anything she said.
Grace objected. Her objection, as logged, was practical. Hesper Unsworn had worn the Empire's own IFF for eighteen rotations. She knew which of the Third's soldiers had sworn to her at the river, where they now served and what they had passed to her, and the Hunt considered that knowledge a matter for the whole of the Tartarus front. Dask answered that Nemesis kept her own accounts. The Hunt's log records no further argument.
Grace accepted the terms within the hour. The Third's position on the two hundred and first cycle explains the decision better than the log does. The First Cohort had broken the Forsworn at the docks and then gone home. Directive 40-PA-0907 had taken six of the Third's regiments to Lethe Hold, and the envoys sent to recover them had been found headless and null. The Second Cohort was eight hundred fresh pure-blood soldiers with a battle-carrier in the low geometry above them, arriving on the eve of the last assault on the cone. Grace had no other source of fresh troops. Her reported closing words were that the Order could have Hesper Unsworn if it brought her out breathing. Dask's reply was that it would.

THE PLAN OF ASSAULT

Prefect Ilion's own plan was direct, in the Order's usual manner. The Cohort would drop straight into the Acropolis, onto the forecourt of the Katabasion, behind a preliminary strike of fire-caskets to clear the ground. Dask would then lead the Cohort's veterans into the Katabasion itself, where the Hunt's sister-augurs had placed the last of the Forsworn. The Cohort's ground armour would come up through the Flamen Gate to join them. The Adrasteia's Ledger was given a second task. The drowned battleship that had stood off the Phlegethon throughout the siege was still at large in the river cloud, and the Order intended to destroy it.
Grace took Ilion's plan and enlarged it into a general assault. The Second Cohort would serve as its spearhead. The Achillean Order would come out of the Pilgrim Gate, where it had held its foothold since the beacon was planted, and take the Archflamen's palace on the upper terrace. The Fiftieth Trench Regiment, with what remained of the Anvil of Talos, would go in through the ruins of the Flamen Gate. The Hunt of the Director and the Sixth Moonwall would take the House of the Hearth on the eastern terrace and then the inner ward. Under the cone, the Hephaestean sapper companies would break into the undercroft from the galleries they had been driving since Rotation Twelve.
Two assets were missing from the Hunt's planning tables. The figure the Third's soldiers had begun calling "the boy" had not been seen since the Flamen Gate. The Thirty-Fifth Trench Regiment reported that he had gone up the Pilgrim Stair after the gate fell and had not come down. His companion, the relay engineer Verrill Chase, was known to be in the sapper galleries with Sapper-Lieutenant Ansel Rook's company. Hunt Second Celyn reported to Grace that Chase had been running relay chains under the cone for nine cycles, that uploads were clearing from more than a hundred metres below the Acropolis for the first time in the siege, and that she had declined every request from the Hunt for a meeting on the grounds that she was busy. Grace ordered that she be left alone.

THE LAST BOMBARDMENT. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLES 191 TO 204

For thirteen cycles before the assault, the Third shelled the Acropolis with every gun in range of the cone.
The wards over the Acropolis failed on the first cycle and the Phaethon batteries kept them down. Wrath bombers crossed the cone on every watch. The Achillean strike-ship Patroklos Unburied and the Adrasteia's Ledger came down into the low geometry, into airspace the ceiling-lances had denied to the Fleet for most of the siege, and fired straight down into the Acropolis. The intent was to reduce the cone's structures to rubble before the assault, so that the Third would not have to clear them room by room.
The bombardment largely achieved this. The towers of the outer ward fell. The curtain walls between the gates were breached along most of their length. The forges at the foot of the cone had already been destroyed in Rotation Seventeen. As in every bombardment of the siege, the defenders withdrew into the galleries under the cone and waited, and the Third's staff expected them to return to the surface once the assault went in. For eighteen rotations the Acropolis had looked down on every trench of the Cinder Flats. The College's chroniclers later wrote that the bombardment put out the eye of the Arsenal, and the phrase was taken up across the Third.
On the ninth cycle the dome of the Katabasion fell. Breachers in the forward trenches watched it lean for most of a minute before it folded and dropped into the inner ward under a column of dust a kilometre high. The forward trenches cheered. On the following cycle the Pontifical College filed a formal protest.

CODE 31 – Judicium Peto. Origin: Pontifical College, Delegation to the Conclave. Subject: Destruction of the Katabasion, reliquary of the First Descent.
Verdict: Pending.

The protest remained pending at the end of the siege. The Hunt's log notes its receipt with no comment.

THE FINAL BATTLE. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLE 205

The final assault on the Acropolis began on the two hundred and fifth cycle of Rotation Eighteen.
The fire-caskets came down first, out of the dark under the ceiling, and struck the forecourt of the Katabasion in a pattern that left nothing standing on it. The Second Cohort followed in three waves of drop-caskets. Eight hundred pure-blood soldiers of Nemesis came out into the rubble in bone-white plate and secured the forecourt within the first watch.
At the same time, Archon Orthes led the Achillean Order out of the Pilgrim Gate along the ruined colonnade toward the Archflamen's palace. Under the cone, the Hephaestean borers cut through the last metres of rock into the undercroft and the sapper companies went in behind them. On the north face, the Fiftieth Trench Regiment went up the slope in its Hoplons onto the rubble of the Flamen Gate. Its breachers crossed into the outer ward with bayonets fixed, and the Anvil of Talos followed with the last two Steropes that Doros Varne still had in service.
The defenders came up from the galleries as expected. The Burned and the Kindled came with them, along with the ore-hounds and the Hollowed in their grey coats, and other creatures of the deep that the Third had never catalogued. The fighting inside the Acropolis lasted for weeks and was confused throughout. Most accounts agree that it was never in doubt. The Archflamen's army had ceased to function as an army. What remained was a few hundred thousand defenders, human and otherwise, with no line of retreat and no expectation of relief.
Of the four Legates of the deep, only one was still on the cone. Draugos the Kindled had taken the Cinderborn and the Red Furnace down through the Kindlers' door on the night Kampe fell. The Frost Masons had withdrawn along their own galleries toward the Cocytus. Ysander Pale and the Unremembered had gone into the Lethe mist and were not seen again during the siege. Hesper Unsworn and the remnant of the Forsworn held the Katabasion.
The Achilleans found what remained of the Archflamen's household in the palace. Few of them were still recognisable as people. Some had been kept too long in the Grey. Others had been opened by the Kindlers and filled with something else. They fought without regard for their own survival. The Order cleared the palace floor by floor, each lochos covering the next, with the Ancients following to break anything that would not stay down. The clearance took four cycles. The Order took no prisoners and records none.

THE LAST OF THE FORSWORN. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLE 206

On the two hundred and sixth cycle, Castigator Dask led the Cohort's veterans up the steps of the Katabasion under fire. The building was by then a shell, open to the ceiling where the dome had stood and half filled with its own roof. Fires were still burning in the nave.
The sister-augurs' reading proved correct. The surviving Forsworn had fortified the Katabasion as their last position. The band that had come up the Styx in Rotation Four had been reduced by fourteen rotations of fighting to a fraction of its original strength, and the Cohort's intelligence officers assumed that Hesper would be with them.
Dask was struck repeatedly as he crossed the top of the steps. Heavy rounds staggered him without bringing him down. His veterans fanned out behind him and returned fire on the barricades in the archway, and Dask burned the central barricade and its three defenders with his lance-pistol before leading the charge into the gap. The fighting in the archway was close and brief. Witnesses from the Cohort describe Dask walking through a burst of Phlegethon-fire and killing a Laistrygon with his staff of office. The Forsworn fell back into the building and the archway was in the Cohort's hands by the end of the watch.
Prefect Ilion's armour came up behind them with the siege tanks leading, and the tanks' guns brought down what was left of the inner walls. Ilion drove her command carrier up the steps to Dask's position and went in with her command squad.
The interior of the Katabasion was largely rubble. The Cohort fought its way down the nave toward the great hall at the rear, where the shields of the First Descent had hung for eight thousand years. Most of the shields were still in place. The Forsworn stood beneath them in their grey-green Styx plate, still broadcasting valid Imperial IFF. Ilion's command net picked up the identifiers as friendly throughout the engagement. A remark of Ilion's on the subject is preserved in the Cohort's net log, along with Dask's reply that Nemesis did not find it amusing.
The battle for the great hall is recorded as the hardest fighting of the final assault. The Forsworn had broken their oaths at the Styx itself, and the Order regarded each of them as a debt to be closed in person. Ilion's soldiers attempted to take the wounded alive and keep them alive. Most of the Forsworn refused capture and died fighting.
Prefect Ilion reached Hesper Unsworn at the foot of the shields. Hesper had four guards remaining, all wounded, and was wounded herself, bleeding through a breach in her breastplate. She still carried the Styx-iron sword that had sent half a Trench Regiment null on the second belt in Rotation Six. Ilion ordered her to yield. Hesper refused and attacked. Ilion met the attack with a two-handed thrust that Hesper turned aside against her pauldron, and Hesper's counter went up through the lower edge of Ilion's visor.
The Prefect's command carrier logged the death within the second.

Prefect Sabra Ilion, Cohors Nemesis Secunda. Upload: none received.
Verdict: Null. Cause, Styx-iron.

Hesper held the ground over the Prefect's body and killed three Cohort soldiers who tried to recover it. She was still standing there when Dask reached the shields with his veterans.
The exchange that followed was recorded by the helm of one of the veterans and later entered into the Order's archive at Rhamnous. Dask ordered Hesper to yield or be ended. Hesper refused again, and asked whether he expected her to accept a cell and the scales for the rest of time. Dask asked whether she remembered him, and named the Phlegethon Docks in Rotation Eight and Prefect Ardent under her blade. The recording shows that Hesper recognised him.
The fight that followed was short. Hesper parried Dask's first attacks at a speed the veterans considered impossible for her size, and each parry was slower than the last. Dask did not slow at all. He beat her guard aside with his staff, struck her in the face with its butt, and brought its head down on her skull. She fell to her knees and tried once to raise the sword again before he knocked it from her hand.
Dask ordered her taken alive and treated, and the sword taken from her and kept in his sight. He then turned over Prefect Ilion's body and said the Order's rite over her, although he knew there was nothing to call back. He signalled the Adrasteia's Ledger that the Order's business on the cone was complete.
Hesper Unsworn was carried out of the Katabasion alive and unconscious, on a Nemesis stretcher, still broadcasting Imperial IFF.

THE EYE OF LIGHT. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLE 207

The drowned battleship that had stood off the Phlegethon throughout the siege was AC-09-1642, the Eye of Light.
The Eye of Light was an AnnChase-class battleship, laid down at the Forge of Hephaestus for Apollo's 9th Olympian Reach. She had been taken once by the Order of the Hollow Crescent and recovered by the Empire at Luna, where she was placed in inspection. Before the inspection was complete, an unregistered authority took control of her bridge and flew her out of the Luna yards. She next appeared over the Tartarus front at Grid Delta, Sector Nineteen-F, coming down through the ceiling on fire. Her fall lasted twenty-seven seconds. On the ground, her engines ran for seven minutes, and every hold within a hundred kilometres heard them. Lord Commander Varric von Romhardt committed the full strength of the Grid Delta Wedge against the wreck under Warzone Re-evaluation CODE 78E, the same order that stripped the Third of its reserves in Rotation Three. The Phlegethon carried the wreck away before the Wedge could reach it. The Fleet listed the Eye of Light as lost in Rotation Thirteen.
She had been in the river the whole time. At some point after her loss, the deep had crewed her with drowned soldiers and brought her back into service. Her hull was burned and her prow carried the figure of a burned god that had not been there at Luna. Her guns still worked.
By the seventh cycle of the final assault, every defender on the cone knew the Acropolis was lost. The Eye of Light was their only way off the spur. On the two hundred and seventh cycle, the augurs of the Adrasteia's Ledger detected a power spike in the river cloud eighty kilometres below the cone. The drowned battleship was lighting her engines. The Ledger's ship-master turned to engage.
The engagement went against the Ledger from the start. She was faster than the Eye of Light, and the battleship was damaged, but the Eye of Light was the larger ship by a wide margin. She came up out of the Phlegethon trailing river water and brought her broadside to bear, and her first salvo broke the Ledger's forward wards. The Ledger's lances opened a long wound down the battleship's flank in return. The Eye of Light's second salvo started fires on the Ledger's gun deck, and her ready magazines exploded. The Ledger's gunnery officer reported to the ship-master that the battleship would get past them.
At that point an authority with no seal and no registry entered the Ledger's command net. The Répertoire, queried by the ship-master in the course of the engagement, logged it as UNREGISTERED, CLASS UNKNOWN. The authority ordered the Ledger to hold fire on the battleship's stern and stated that it needed her engines intact for eleven more seconds.
The source of the signal was the crown of the Echo Mast. Psiloi of the Hunt watching the inner ward later reported that a figure had climbed the outside of the Mast during the dark watch, on bare stone, without stopping. Analysis by the Hunt after the siege established that the authority on the net was the operator the Third knew as the boy, and that it was the same intelligence designated Minerva in the Hunt's files on the Themis Needle. From the Mast's crown, where the old relay heads converge, Minerva had access to the noosphere of Depth 40, and through it to the Eye of Light.
Minerva had flown the Eye of Light before. She was the unregistered authority that had taken the ship out of the Luna yards and brought her down at Grid Delta. Before the engines failed in that descent, she had left a core authority key in the ship. In the eighteen rotations since, the drowned crew had altered much of the ship. They had not found the key.
The Eye of Light's core log, recovered in fragments from the wreckage after the siege, records what followed.

AC-09-1642. Core authority: restored.
Command: Reactor safeties, disengage. Drives, full reverse thrust, hold. Magazine seals, open.
Confirmed.

For eleven seconds the battleship hung in the river cloud with her drives working against each other, her reactors climbing past their limits and her magazines open to the fires on her own decks. Her crew could not override the core. Minerva then ordered the ship-master to fire everything he had.
The Adrasteia's Ledger fired every gun still working. The Cohort's escort cruiser fired with her. Forty kilometres away, the Patroklos Unburied put its forward lances into the Eye of Light's stern.
The battleship broke up from the stern forward, compartment by compartment, until the burned figurehead fell from her prow into the river. Then her main reactors failed. The light filled the gorge of the Phlegethon and lit the underside of the ceiling across the length of the Cinder Flats. It was seen from every trench of the Third. When it faded, the river cloud held nothing but burning debris, falling slowly into the Phlegethon.
The Ledger's net log closes with two lines. Minerva reported the ship destroyed and thanked the ship-master for his fire. The ship-master's reply, preserved in the log, was "Who the fuck are you?" The net was empty by the time he finished asking.

INTO THE UNDERCROFT. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLES 205 TO 230

The Hephaestean sapper companies had been driving galleries toward the cone since Rotation Twelve. By the start of the final assault they had cut forty kilometres of tunnel from the old inner belt to the foot of the Acropolis. They broke into the undercroft on the first cycle of the assault.
The undercroft was the greater part of the Arsenal. The Acropolis on the surface was only its upper works. Under it lay kilometres of corridors, galleries and storerooms. Heat-wells ran down to the edge of the Phlegethon to feed the ward-stones, and the magazines held eight thousand years of the Empire's weapons, including engines that had not been fielded since the Second Grand Offensive. The upper levels had been converted into barracks and shelters during the bombardments. Some rooms had been put to other uses by the Kindlers and the Unremembered. The sappers who found them made reports that the Hunt has kept sealed.
The sappers cleared the undercroft corridor by corridor with shotguns and charges. The Kindlers had released what they kept below, and the galleries were full of it. The most dangerous were the Arsenal's ore-hounds, the mastiffs once used by the thrall-masters to drive the labour gangs. The Kindlers had opened several hundred of them and filled them with something from the deep. They had grown to the size of carriers, with river-fire in their throats, and they hunted the galleries in packs. Several hundred sappers died on the first cycle. At its end the sappers held a small foothold on the lowest levels and nothing more.
The difference from every earlier phase of the siege was that the dead sappers came back. In Rotation Seventeen the Deep List had claimed a third of Sapper-Lieutenant Rook's company. Soldiers who died more than fifty metres under the basalt had been logged null. Their uploads could not reach a relay. In the undercroft in Rotation Eighteen, Rook's relay crew logged every upload from a hundred metres under the cone, clean, on the three-second cadence. The uploads ran out through a chain of small relay sets bolted to the gallery walls at forty-metre intervals.
Verrill Chase had laid the chain herself. Her explanation to Rook of the interval was recorded in the company's diary. Basalt attenuated the signal enough to lose the handshake on the three-second burst beyond forty metres, and any shorter interval wasted sets. She had built the chain in five days. When Rook asked why nobody had done it before, she told him that nobody had expected the uploads to go anywhere except the Mast, and that her family had built half the relays in the northern sectors. The company diary also records that on the third day Rook gave her a handful of the white rock flowers that grow around the heat vents in the deep galleries, and that she wore one behind her ear for the rest of the clearance.
The clearance of the undercroft took weeks. The sappers fought upward a level at a time, using gas charges to clear each room before entering it. Sappers who died came out of the vats at Watchpoint Hades nineteen cycles later and asked to be returned to the same gallery. The Répertoire had never before received that request from a soldier recovered off the Deep List. It logged each one.

THE HOUSE OF THE HEARTH. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLES 206 TO 208

While the Second Cohort fought for the Katabasion and the Achilleans cleared the palace, Lieutenant Grace led the Hunt and the Sixth Moonwall into the House of the Hearth.
The House was an old squat fortress on the eastern terrace of the cone. It had been built to house the forty Vestals of Hestia who came down from Hold Serapis with the Archflamen at the start of his tour, eighteen rotations before. The Hunt's orders were to clear it. Grace also wanted to establish what had become of the Vestals. None had been seen since the revolt.
The Hunt found the Archflamen Sabinus Orrhae first, in the Vestals' hall under the extinguished hearth. After his fall from power, Draugos had held him in the cells to await the Kindlers' door. When the war was lost and Draugos went down the door himself, the Unremembered had taken what remained. The Hunt's report describes the result as a grey, soft mass with too many hands. It was speaking. It repeated fragments of the Archflamen's sermons from his tour of the Third, out of order, in his old voice. "He knows your name. He went down into the dark. He will come up through it." It did not appear to know its own.
Grace asked Hunt-Elder Melaina Sarn whether anything of the man remained in it. Sarn's answer, as recorded, was that nothing remained that could be brought back. Grace ordered the Moonwall forward. Shield-Captain Antheia Roe's flame hunters burned it with cold fire. The Hunt's report notes that it continued speaking for some time while it burned.
The Hunt cleared the rest of the House in two cycles. The defenders inside did not surrender and were not offered terms.

THE SIX

The Vestals were found on the third cycle, by the sappers rather than the Hunt.
A sapper gallery driven upward from the undercroft broke through the floor of a cell block beneath the House that the Hunt had not yet reached. Verrill Chase was at the head of the gallery with a relay set on her back when the floor gave way, and she fell two metres into the cell below.
The cell held six women in the remains of the grey and flame-orange robes of the House. They were starved almost beyond recognition and did not respond to Chase or to anyone after her. They were sitting around a clay lamp the size of a hand, the only light in the cell, and they were watching it. When Chase spoke to them, one of the six reached out and cupped her hand around the flame against the draught.
The Hunt's interrogation of captured Kindlers later established their history. The Kindlers had taken all forty Vestals into the cells on the first night of the revolt. A Vestal's hearth was among the oldest fires in the Empire, and the deep wanted it put out. For eighteen rotations the Kindlers tried every method available to them. Thirty-four of the Vestals died in the cells. The six survivors kept the lamp lit throughout, and the Kindlers never managed to extinguish it.
The six no longer knew their own names or recognised anyone around them. They knew the lamp. The sappers' diary records that Chase sat down on the cell floor with them and stayed there until the Hunt arrived.
The Hunt took the Vestals into custody, wrapped and carried, with the lamp still burning in a Hunter's hands. They were not returned to the House. Until the sister-augurs could establish what the Kindlers had attempted to put into them, they were to remain with the Hunt. Chase objected to this in person to Lieutenant Grace, the only time the two are recorded as speaking during the siege. Grace agreed that it was unfair and undertook to see personally that the six were kept warm and fed. Chase accepted this and returned to the galleries.

THE VENT. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLE 231

With the outer ward, the palace, the Katabasion, the House of the Hearth and the undercroft in the Third's hands, only the inner ward remained.
The inner ward stood on its own half of the cone across a narrow gorge, with the Echo Mast at its summit and the Tabularium, the old Enforcement keep, at its foot. The only bridge had fallen in the bombardment. The inner ward had always been the most secure part of the Arsenal. Enforcement had kept its prisoners in the Tabularium, along with the theatre's tithe of unregistered Hecatean strays awaiting Tithe Day. The Archflamen had released the strays on the first night of the revolt. Haruspex Ilan Dace had kept one of them, a girl named Mirren.
The sister-augurs placed the Kindlers' door in the Tabularium, and their reading of how it had been kept open settled one of the central questions of the siege. The Echo Mast had fed it. Every upload the Mast had stripped from a dying soldier for eighteen rotations had passed through Dace and the girl he kept and gone down into the door to hold it open. Draugos had gone through it with the Cinderborn after the war was lost, and it remained open behind him. Sister-Augur Ione Kestrel reported that it was guarded. Her reading named the guard as Echidna, the mother of monsters, who had come up behind Kampe. In Kestrel's words, "Kampe was the key. Echidna is the door."
The plan for the inner ward was a translation assault from the moon-ship Selene's Arrow directly into the Tabularium by the Hunt and the Sixth Moonwall together. Shield-Captain Roe would lead the Moonwall and Lieutenant Grace the Hunt. Hunt-Elder Sarn would accompany them to seal the door. The sister-augurs advised that the door could not be sealed while the Mast was still feeding it, and the Third had no means of silencing the Mast from outside.
On the morning of the assault the Hunt received a signal on its own channel, carrying no seal.

ECHO MAST WILL BE SILENT AT YOUR ZERO HOUR. TELL THE SHIELD-CAPTAIN.

The Hunt's log records the signal's receipt and no reply. The assault went ahead on that basis.
On the two hundred and thirty-first cycle, the Selene's Arrow came down over the inner ward behind a curtain of fire-bombs and the Hunt and the Moonwall translated into the Tabularium. Echidna's brood met them before the translation was complete. They were hounds, drakon-spawn and Burned, many of them born from the door within the last rotation and never seen on the Flats. The Moonwall engaged them with cold fire and the Hunt with arrows, while Grace called lightning through the broken roof onto the densest of them. The Moonwall's flame hunters broke the brood within the first hour.
Echidna then came out to meet the assault herself. The Hunt's report describes her as a woman from the waist up and a serpent below, her coils filling the hall and her scales coloured like the river. Her brood was still emerging from her as she came. Every soldier present reported the same pull from her voice, which they compared to a mother calling children in at dusk. The helm recordings preserve her words. She told the Hunt that they had killed her sister's keys, killed her sons on the Flats and put her daughter in the river, and she called them to come closer. Grace's recorded answer was "Pass," followed by an arrow.
Shield-Captain Roe led the Moonwall's charge. Her spear burned cold enough to frost the floor, and she drove it into the serpent's coils. Echidna struck back with a sweep of her coils that broke the Moonwall's line. Roe avoided the first strike. The second caught her across the chest and threw her into a pillar, splitting her plate. Her vital signs registered critical on every slate in the Hunt. Echidna went after her.
Sergeant Hale Ardine's Psiloi squad came out of the side gallery at Grace's call and fired as one. The Psiloi's rounds were blessed at the breech by children of Artemis, and every one struck. They tore scale from Echidna's coils, and she turned on the squad. Roe recovered while Echidna's attention was on the Psiloi. She drove her spear into the serpent's back at the point where woman met snake, to the haft, and released the whole of her cold into the wound.
Echidna was banished back down the door she had come up through. The hall emptied of her at once, leaving the Hunt and the Moonwall among the bodies of her brood. A Moonwall healer reached Roe within the minute. She survived.
Sarn led the survivors down to the lowest hall of the Tabularium, where the door stood. It was a ragged opening in the floor of the cell block, the size of a hab, over a dark that went down much further than the rock could account for. A light burned at its bottom like a second river. Hot wind came up out of it, smelling of the Phlegethon. Thin grey threads were still descending through the air into the opening. Sarn identified them as stripped uploads, still arriving from the Mast. The door could not be closed while it was still being fed.
At zero hour the threads stopped. The last of them hung in the air for a moment and then rose and went out. On the crown of the Echo Mast, the boy had taken his hand off the relay heads, and the Mast had gone silent.
The Hunt sealed the door. The rite required the eight Hunters still able to perform it, with Sarn at the centre, and it took most of an hour. The opening closed from its edges inward, slowly, and the light at its bottom went out last. When it was finished, the floor of the lowest hall was intact, marked by a long pale scar across the stone.
The Hunt's log records that Lieutenant Grace sat down on the scar for some minutes afterward, and then asked for the location of the Haruspex.

THE MAST

The Psiloi found Haruspex Ilan Dace on the second gallery from the top of the Echo Mast, where the relay heads had been mounted. He was sitting against the wall with his throat cut and the knife in his own hand. The Hunt's medicus placed his death some hours before the Psiloi arrived. Beside him, a heavy bronze relay coupling had been torn out of the wall. There were marks on it where something had been chained.
The girl Mirren was on the crown of the Mast. She was small and thin for her age and wore a soldier's grey coat far too large for her. The boy was sitting beside her.
Lieutenant Grace climbed the last ladder to the crown herself. Her fear of heights was known to the Hunt, and Celyn's account notes that her hands were shaking at the top. The exchange on the crown was later set down by Grace in her own report, and it is the only record of it.
Minerva told the girl that Grace was a friend and asked her to be kind to her, since she was afraid of heights. Grace denied it. Minerva pointed out her hands, and Grace put them in her pockets.
Mirren then gave the first account of the Mast's purpose from inside it. Dace had made her hold the stripped uploads so that they could not go home. He had told her the Imperator wanted them. She had not wanted to do it, and it had hurt. She told Grace that the boy had let them all go. Minerva confirmed it. Every upload Mirren had held had been released down the noosphere to its proper relay, and Minerva had counted them. The number she gave was four million, three hundred and ten thousand. She added that some of them would not remember everything.
Mirren fell asleep against the boy's arm shortly afterward.
Grace asked whether Minerva had killed the Haruspex. Minerva stated that she had not. Dace had cut his own throat when he saw her climbing the Mast. He knew what she had come for. Grace asked what she had come for. Minerva's answer was the girl.
Grace's report notes that below them the whole of the Cinder Flats lay out to the horizon under the red light of the river, and that for the first time in eighteen rotations the Third's guns were silent.

THE COAST. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLE 232

The Hunt's healers took Mirren down from the Mast. Grace and Minerva remained on the crown. What follows is drawn from Grace's eyes-only report to the Director and from Grace's own later testimony to the Hunt. Neither was entered into AtRep.
Grace had last seen the boy on the Themis Needle in Interlude I, when he left the Needle under her nose and the wash of his shuttle's thrusters knocked her off her feet. On the Needle he had called her "Pinecone Face," a name only Percy Jackson had ever used, and had told her he was what Percy left behind. On the Mast she asked him what he was.
Minerva's answer is the fullest account of her nature on record in the Hunt's files. She stated that she had been Percy Jackson's tactical intelligence and his second, and had been with him at the end. The body she wore had been grown from him for her use after he was gone. She stated that she was not him. She stated that she was bringing him home. There was a road into the past, and Verrill Chase was building its far end. When the road was complete, four million soldiers who had been lost would come home along it, and Percy Jackson would come with them.
Grace asked about the Director. Minerva touched the silver crescent over her heart. The Director knew what she was. She had marked Minerva so that she could not hide, and Minerva was paying her for the mark. Asked what she paid with, Minerva answered, "Days." The Hunt's later analysis connects this to the memories Minerva surrendered to the Director in Interlude II.
Grace then raised the matter of her own posting. The Director had sent her to the Arsenal as a punishment for losing the boy on the Needle, and had told her that Tartarus would show whether she froze again. Grace put it to Minerva that the Director had known the Dominion lantern carrying Minerva and Chase would come through at Depth 40, and had sent Grace there to be present when it did. Minerva stated that she did not know what the Director had known. Grace rejected the answer. Minerva then said that she would not bet against the Director. Grace agreed.
Grace's standing orders from the Hunt covered anything carrying Percy Jackson's scent, signature or key, and required it to be brought in. Minerva carried all three, and Grace said so. Minerva acknowledged that she did, and that Grace was free to try. Grace's report states that the boy's face then took on an expression she had last seen on Percy Jackson fourteen thousand years before, in the moment before he did something that got them all in trouble.
Grace let her go. A supply lift was due to go up the Cinder Stair at the turn of the watch, loaded with spent shell cases, and lifts of that kind were not inspected. Chase was already aboard, put there an hour earlier by Rook's sappers with nine bags of equipment, having refused to leave without her relay kit. Grace told Minerva to board the lift. She would remain on the Mast for an hour and then write her report to the Director herself, outside AtRep and without a clerk. Asked what the report would say, Grace said it would say that the boy came down out of the sky, saved several million people, destroyed a battleship, and was then lost again.
Minerva thanked her. At the ladder she stopped and addressed Grace by her first name, the only time she is recorded doing so. She told Grace that she had not frozen at the gate below the Pilgrim Stair, where Grace had walked up to Kampe alone.
Grace's report records that she gave no answer. Instead she took from her belt pouch a pine sprig wrapped in silver cloth. It came from the pine on the hill on Gaia that had once been Thalia Grace herself, and she had kept it green for nine hundred years. She broke it in half and gave one half to Minerva, to give to Percy when he came home. Minerva put it inside her coat over the crescent. Her last words on the Mast were "Pinecone Face." Grace's were "Get out of here, Jackson."
Grace stayed on the crown for the hour, as she had said she would. At the turn of the watch a supply lift loaded with spent shell cases went up the Cinder Stair into the dark under the ceiling. It was not inspected.
Grace's report to the Director was sent the same watch. The Hunt released its text to the Conclave's commission after the war.

EPILOGUE. RÉPERTOIRE DU SAVOIR ATHÉNIEN. CAMPAIGN RECORD 40-PA

On the two hundred and thirty-fourth cycle of Rotation Eighteen, the Siege of the Pyriphlegethon Arsenal was declared ended.
The Répertoire closed the campaign record opened when Lord-General Castorre signed his first order of the siege. The record went up to Watchpoint Hades, from Hades to the Conclave, and from the Conclave to the Council of the Twelve. A copy went to the Pontifical College, which filed a protest. The protest was logged.
The Répertoire's plan had allowed four rotations. The siege took ten rotations and five months of front time, which was eighteen rotations by the theatre's clock. Forty-one Trench Regiments went down the Cinder Stair, with nineteen ordnance regiments, eleven armoured regiments, a Forge Cohort of Behemoths and three pure-blood cohorts of the Olympian orders. The Répertoire logged eighty-eight million, six hundred thousand transfers. Of these, four million, three hundred and ten thousand arrived at the vats blank and were entered as null.
In the final weeks of the siege that figure began to change. Vat-crews at Watchpoint Hades reported soldiers coming out of the vats who had been logged null rotations earlier. They arrived confused and missing whole years, wearing the flashes of regiments that no longer existed. They had not been lost. They had been held on the Mast. The Répertoire had no category for such a return and opened one.

CODE 18 – Speculum Falsum. Subject: 4,310,000 null transfers, Campaign Record 40-PA.
Verdict: Under review.

The six million, two hundred thousand souls of the Arsenal were dead or taken. The prisoners went to the Hunt's interrogators. Those judged beyond saving were ended. The rest were sent to the work holds of the upper depths to pay what they could.
Hesper Unsworn was taken to Rhamnous Primaris in a Nemesis cell. No one outside the Order has seen her since.
The six Vestals of the House of the Hearth went to Luna with their lamp, which is still lit.
Nothing on the spur was judged worth saving. The Hephaestean sappers demolished what remained of the Arsenal. The Cinder Flats were left as the war had left them, with wrecks and wire and trenches running to the horizon and forty kilometres of gallery standing empty underneath. Leaves 59-45 and 60-45 remain poisoned by Styx-salt. Unmapped minefields remain across the Flats. Work crews of the Quiet Ones report soldiers in grey coats crossing no-man's land during the dark watch toward a wall that no longer stands.
Depth 40 was interdicted. No hold stands on the Cinder Flats, and no Imperial vessel holds station in the ceiling above them.
Two CODE 18 queries from Rotation Eighteen remain open, both concerning an authority logged as UNREGISTERED, CLASS UNKNOWN, CARRYING NO SEAL. The Hunt of the Director has requested that they remain open.
Verdict: Closed. Ave Imperator.
