THE SIEGE OF THE PYRIPHLEGETHON ARSENAL

PART TWELVE. THE GATES

"Better broken in body than emptied in mind."
Hunt-Elder Melaina Sarn

THE CENTURION'S REPORT. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLE 92

Two cycles after the battle in the Gully of Ash, Centurion Liane Marsh of the Thirty-Fifth Trench Regiment's Ninth Cohort was brought to the Hunt's command bunker on the Slag Gate spur to report in person. Lieutenant Thalia Grace had asked for a witness who had seen the lantern come down on the Scree, and Marsh had been closest.
Her account survives in the Hunt's interview log. She described the Dominion lantern burning gold as it fell onto the Scree, and a boy climbing out of the wreck. She described rounds stopping in the air in front of him, and the moment he put his hand on a dead signaller's relay set and the Thirty-Fifth's fire net came back up. Asked for a description, she gave him as about sixteen, black-haired and sea-green-eyed, with a small silver crescent showing through his coat over the chest, which she compared to the Lieutenant's own. His companion was a blonde girl with a pencil in her hair who swore freely and addressed him as "mister hero."
The Hunt's log records Grace's response to the description in full: "Oh, you have got to be kidding me."
The silver crescent was the Director's own mark. The Hunt's officers in the bunker understood at once that the pair on the Scree were the boy and girl who had escaped Grace on the Themis Needle, and Hunt Second Celyn reminded the Lieutenant that the Director's standing order applied. Anything carrying Percy Jackson's scent or signature was to be brought in. Grace's ruling was entered into the log the same watch. The pair were to be located and watched but not approached. If the boy was running the Thirty-Fifth's fire, the Hunt would not pull him off it in the middle of the assault on the cone, and nobody was to touch him until the gates were down. Grace reserved the approach to herself.

THE RETURN OF THE ACHILLEANS. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLE 159

On the hundred and fifty-ninth cycle of Rotation Eighteen, the Hunt's embassy to the Achillean Order bore fruit. Hunter Naomi had gone up to the Order's Hearth on the ceiling, as instructed, and put the Lieutenant's request directly into the hands of the Order's Archon, Damar Orthes. Orthes took it to his council.
No order of the Empire committed itself to a fight lightly, and the Achilleans least of all. They carried no implants. Every warrior they lost was lost for good, and they were few, and they had to be spent with care. Orthes already knew the Arsenal, however. Polemarch Avel's company had come back from Operation Scaean Gate half destroyed and victorious, and the Hunt was now asking the Order to finish what Avel had begun. The Hunt could have ordered it. The Director's seal reached that far. Grace had chosen to ask, and the Order took note. The Achilleans were bound to the Empire by their own oath and nothing else, and they regarded those who broke their oaths as the worst of enemies. The Forsworn were on the Flats. It was this argument, put by Naomi, that carried the council.
Orthes assembled the whole Order in the Hearth before the shield and announced his decision. He would lead a force back to the Arsenal himself, large enough to finish the work Avel had started. Four lochoi would go, four hundred warriors in all, and the Order's great ship, the Patroklos Unburied, was made ready to carry them.
The Order prepared for war as it had done thousands of times before. The Archon's ship-master took command of the Patroklos Unburied. The Order's priests warned every warrior that the Arsenal was now a haunt of the deep, and that the things there would want more than their lives. They would face the Archflamen's guns, and they would face the river, and the river would offer them things. The Order's teaching held that a fight against the deep had to be won inside each warrior as well as on the field.
The arming took days. The Ancients were woken in their coffins, Ancient Aias among them with a new arm, along with Ancient Rhesos and the broken warrior who now walked in Dolon's rebuilt shell. The Order's smiths blessed every engine in its armoury. Polemarch Avel came out of the smiths' hall on his own feet, with a new chest of bronze and iron under his plate, and took his place at the head of the First Lochos. The Patroklos Unburied came down through the high geometry toward Depth 40.
She arrived over the Flats on the hundred and fifty-ninth cycle and put the whole force down on the Slag Gate spur by cable and lander. Orthes met Grace in her bunker and placed his force at the Hunt's disposal. After a briefing the two went into council alone. Orthes studied the war-table model of the cone at length. The Acropolis was a terrible objective, but the Order's pride required it to take the hardest task the Lieutenant could give it, and Orthes asked for the honour of the first breach into the cone itself.
They agreed that the Achilleans would attack up the Pilgrim Stair from the ruins of the Slag Gate spur and take and hold the Pilgrim Gate. The attack would go straight up the stair, under the guns of the cone's southern walls for every step of the climb. The Pilgrim Gate was a bastion of cut stone with its own guard towers. Its outer gate opened onto a long tunnel, certain to be a killing ground, which led to an inner gate and then to a colonnade running all the way to the Katabasion. The wards had so far kept the Phaethons from doing more than scar the face of the gatehouse. Its interior defences were intact.
Orthes identified the central difficulty. The Polemarch's First Lochos was the Order's translation lochos, and it could not translate into a warded gatehouse without a beacon inside the wards. There was no means of carrying one there.
While the council was still sitting, a signal appeared on the war table on the Hunt's own channel, carrying no seal.

BEACON WILL BE IN THE OUTER TUNNEL OF THE PILGRIM GATE AT YOUR ZERO HOUR. TELL THE POLEMARCH.

The Hunt's log records that Orthes asked who had sent it, and that Grace replied that she was working on it. The plan went ahead with the beacon included.

THE PILGRIM STAIR. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLE 171

The assault on the Pilgrim Gate began on the hundred and seventy-first cycle with an attack by Lochagos Ithra Vell's leap-squads on the bunkers covering the stair. Vell watched from the top of a rubble heap three storeys high that had once been part of the Slag Gate's wall, with the leap-squads of three lochoi behind her. Her target was a cluster of low bunkers cut deep into the cliff below the stair, where it turned at the first landing. They were cast basalt, built to shrug off plunging shells, and every leap-squad carried breaching charges to open them.
A strike craft of the Patroklos Unburied came in low and dropped its load of fire-bombs onto the bunkers. When the fire burned out Vell gave the word, and her squads went off the rubble in one long bound, dropping out of the smoke onto the bunkers with their leap-rigs blazing.
As Vell's force went in, Archon Orthes ordered the Order's armoured column forward to its start line in the ruins of the Slag Gate. A second strike craft fired a salvo of missiles into the gatehouse. The Order's rocket batteries fired along the walls of the cone, where the wards flared as they absorbed hit after hit. The road that had once led up to the Pilgrim Stair had been broken to pieces by rotations of shelling, and the carriers churned through the paste and over the stones to the foot of the stair. On the helm feeds, Orthes could see Vell's squads clearing the bunkers hand to hand.
With the bunkers engaged and falling, the main column moved, three heavy assault carriers leading. Orthes rode in the first with his warriors and ordered the driver forward at full speed.
The advance was fast. The leading carriers raced past the bunkers with every gun firing ahead and up the face of the cone at the walls and the bastion. The route was a single stair that pilgrims had climbed in file for eight thousand years, with the cliffs rising to the ramparts on the left and a sheer drop to the Flats on the right. It climbed in long ramps with flights of broad steps between them, and at each flight the drivers drove the carriers into the steps until the tracks bit and heaved them up, breaking the old stone. Cannon rounds came down on the hulls like hail.
Less than halfway up, the leading carrier was hit and slewed to a stop across the stair with fire coming out of its engine deck. Its assault ramp had been damaged and would not release. Orthes broke the locks by hand and forced the ramp down with two of his warriors, and led them out of the smoke and on up the stair on foot. The second carrier shoved the wreck aside and struck a hidden mine that took off its right track. The column could go no further. Squad after squad of the Order came out of the carriers behind and followed the Archon's standard up the stair through the fire.
The Archflamen's soldiers came pouring down out of the Pilgrim Gate to meet them, led again by the Brethren of the Black Shale in their dark plate. The fighting on the ramps and steps was savage. Orthes is recorded throwing one of the Brethren over the edge of the stair and cutting a second in half. The Order's strike craft dived on the crowd on the stair, and the Ancients came up behind the warriors, climbing where no carrier could, and drove the Archflamen's soldiers back up the stair with their heavy guns. Within minutes hundreds of bodies lay along the steps. The Order halted under the walls of the gate-towers to regroup. The defenders had pulled back inside and barred the outer gate. The Achilleans had reached their objective and now had to storm it.

THE BEACON

At zero hour, the beacon was in the outer tunnel of the Pilgrim Gate, as the signal had promised.
How it got there was never established by the Order. Psiloi of the Hunt watching the cliff below the gatehouse reported a figure climbing the bare rock under the walls during the fighting on the stair. They took it at first for a lizard of the deep. It climbed two hundred metres of cliff in four minutes and went in through a gun slit the width of a hand, cracked by the Phaethons and never repaired. Survivors of the gate's garrison, taken later, reported that a boy in a burned coat came out of the gun gallery into the outer tunnel carrying an Achillean translation beacon, set it on the floor and opened it. The garrison fired on him. Every round stopped in the air a hand's breadth from his body and fell.
The Order's net log records a single transmission, informing the Polemarch that he had a lock. In the translation chamber of the Patroklos Unburied, Polemarch Avel and the First Lochos translated into the gatehouse.

THE BASTION

The Archflamen's soldiers had been driven back from the stair but they were not beaten. Inside the bastion more of the deep's warriors were waiting, and at their call was everything the Kindlers had raised. The outer gate opened again and the deep came out of the tunnel mouth. There were the Burned, the soldiers of the reformation, walking fire in the shape of men. There were hounds of the deep as big as carriers, with river-fire in their mouths, and khalkotauroi, the bronze bulls of the old forges, charging with fire coming from their nostrils. With them came the Kindled, the Lictors who had gone down in the Gully of Ash and come back changed beyond recognition, along with drakon-spawn and mad thralls.
The fighting went back and forth across the wide steps under the bastion, and the Achilleans were hard pressed. When the Archon called for the First Lochos, it was already engaged. Avel's warriors had come out of the dark onto the floor of the outer tunnel around the beacon and were fighting their way toward the steps, into the backs of the creatures coming down them. Many of the Order fell. Their plate gave them little protection against what they faced. The Order's standard-bearer was trampled by a bronze bull until a lance-pistol shot burst the bull's furnace. Ancient Aias went down backward on the steps with three hounds of the deep on his coffin. The priest of the Second Lochos chanted the Order's oath throughout with her staff raised. The Order did not give way.
Orthes was wounded and dragged clear by the Order's healer, his helm and blade lost. For a time it appeared that the Achilleans would have to fall back or be destroyed. Then Lochagos Vell, her work at the bunkers done, fired her leap-rig and came off the first landing with what was left of her squad. They struck the Burned from behind, where nobody had been watching for them. Vell's charge broke the Burned and the thralls behind them ran. Her squad's last breaching charges blew a hole in the outer gate, and the Achilleans went in through it with Vell leading.
In the outer tunnel, Vell found the First Lochos fighting with their backs to the beacon and the boy among them, with one hand on the tunnel wall. Vell's helm recorded the exchange. He informed her that the inner gate ran on a counterweight and that he would have it open in thirty seconds. Vell demanded to know who he was. He gave her the remaining time, twenty-eight seconds, and nothing else.
The inner gate went up on its counterweight twenty-eight seconds later and the Order went through. Orthes, having pushed his healer away and taken up a fallen blade, rejoined the fighting inside. The Achilleans cleared the bastion floor by floor and showed no mercy to anything left in it. By the end of the cycle the gatehouse was theirs. They sealed its entrances and set up their defence. Most of the warriors who had begun the attack were dead, their bodies lying along the Pilgrim Stair and against the gatehouse walls. The survivors swore to hold the gate against anything that came. Orthes climbed to the roof of the bastion and planted the Order's torn standard there, in view of every soldier on the Flats. As he did so, the first salvoes of the Patroklos Unburied began to land on the wards of the Acropolis from the low geometry, now that the ceiling-lances were mostly destroyed.
When Vell looked for the boy afterward, he was gone.
While the main force fought for the Pilgrim Gate, the rest of the Order made a diversionary attack on the Founders' Gate. Their siege tanks and battle tanks went up the lower switchbacks, engaged the Archflamen's walkers and furnace-engines on the road and kept them occupied for the duration of the assault.

THE FLAMEN GATE. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLE 171

While the Achilleans fought up the Pilgrim Stair, the Hunt's own assault force gathered for its attack on the Flamen Gate on the north face of the cone. To the west, the rolling barrage ahead of the Fiftieth Trench Regiment's renewed attack on the Founders' Gate was already pounding the slope and wrapping the Acropolis in grey dust.
Lieutenant Grace's force was the strongest the Hunt could assemble. It comprised eight Hunters of the Director's household with their full retinues, the Psiloi squads, and companies of shock breachers from the Forty-Third and Fiftieth Trench Regiments. The Sixth Moonwall's two companies went up in the Hunt's silver carriers under Shield-Captain Antheia Roe and Flame-Captain Dione Tharn. Hunt-Elder Sarn walked with them on her metal legs. Wrath wings stood by in the air to cover the final approach.
Verrill Chase accompanied the assault as a relay engineer. She had come over from the Thirty-Fifth the cycle before and warned the Forty-Third's signal officer that his relay chain would fail as soon as the assault entered the gate tunnels. The tunnels ran sixty metres into the rock. Below eighty metres no uploads reached a relay, and between sixty and eighty they were unreliable. Chase proposed a chain of relay sets laid up the slope and into the tunnels behind the assault at forty-metre intervals, so that the three-second cadence could find its way out. When the signal officer asked who she was, she identified herself as the one who had fixed the masks. He gave her two signallers. She spent the assault in a trench at the foot of the slope with a stack of relay sets.
The Hunt's chronicle records the Lieutenant walking among the breachers before the advance, her priests following to give every soldier the old vow against what they were about to face. Grace's armour was wired from shoulder to heel, and her spear was at full length with a light running along it. A signaller in Chase's trench later reported that Chase recognised Grace on sight as the Thalia Grace of the old stories and began describing her aloud with some enthusiasm, until every relay set in the trench spoke at once in a single voice, ordering "Chase. Quiet." She stopped.
On the Lieutenant's word the whole force rose and advanced up the slope beside its carriers at walking pace. As it climbed, the ceiling over the Flats went black and a storm broke under it. There is no rain on the Cinder Flats. There was only lightning, crossing the geometry under the ceiling from one horizon of the Flats to the other, wave after wave, as the Hunt went up toward the Flamen Gate. Every soldier on the slope reported a weight in the air.
The defenders came out of the gate to meet them in a mass of red and bronze, the Burned and the Kindled with the bronze bulls and the hounds, and furnace-engines walking among them spitting molten iron from their cannon. They chanted as they came. The breachers opened fire and the defenders kept coming through it. The two forces met on the slope. The Moonwall came out of their carriers straight into the fighting, their spears burning cold and their flame hunters colder, and the Burned went out in plumes of steam where the spears went in. Lightning came down out of the ceiling onto the slope wherever Grace pointed her spear. The hounds ran across the slope in packs and dragged down many of the Hunt's force, but the Moonwall cut their own path up after the Lieutenant. A furnace-engine fell with a Moonwall spear in its workings. When the last of the Burned went out, the Flamen Gate lay open in front of them.
Then Kampe came.

THE WARDEN. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLE 171

Kampe came up out of the gorge that splits the cone, out of the Phlegethon far below, on black wings and in a column of river-fire that rose past the Echo Mast and lit the whole of the Flats red. She was the warden of Tartarus, the jailer of the Hundred-Handed, who had kept the pit for the Titans before the Olympians were born. The Kindlers' door had let her up, as Sister-Augur Ione Kestrel's reading had predicted in Rotation Fifteen.
She came down on the ramparts of the Flamen Gate and crouched there to look at the battle. From the waist up she was a woman, as tall as a Behemoth's command deck, with a head of black snakes. Around her waist grew the heads of fifty beasts, bears and boars, jackals and lions, all alive and snarling. Below the waist she was a dragon, and a scorpion's tail rose behind her with a sting as long as a lance. She carried a curved blade in each hand, smoking with green poison. Shells burst around her without visible effect.
She came down off the parapet onto the slope. Her first target was the Steropes-pattern Bellows of the Forge, which had walked up the lower slope behind the Hunt. Her first blow cut through its wards and plate, her second broke it open, and its reactor exploded. She went on into the Hunt's force, sweeping aside everything in reach. A Hunt carrier that could not reverse out of her path fast enough was cut open and burst into flames around her. Kampe rose on her wings out of the fire, came down again and killed two Psiloi with a single stroke of her tail.
Flame-Captain Tharn charged her with her company. The Moonwall's cold fire was the first thing in the battle to hurt the warden, and Tharn's company poured it into her until a storm of cold light surrounded the fight. Kampe struck back blindly, throwing one shieldmaiden through the air and cutting another in half. Tharn held her for some time. The cold fire was draining her strength, and the rest of her company were cut down or driven back one by one. At the end Tharn gathered the last of her strength into her spear and met a blow from Kampe's blade with it. The spear broke in a burst of light that lit the whole slope. Tharn fell, disarmed and badly hurt. The last two shieldmaidens stepped in front of her. Kampe threw one down the slope and seized the other and carried her up into the storm. The Flame-Captain's company had been broken.
While Kampe fought the Moonwall, Grace had been leading every survivor she could gather up toward the gate. Most of the gate and its two towers had been reduced to rubble by the Phaethons, and the fire-shells had cleared the walls on either side of defenders. Kampe came down out of the storm and landed in the ruined gateway with her wings spread, blocking it. She dropped the captive shieldmaiden on the stones and screamed a challenge at the slope. The breachers broke and fell back.
Grace went up the last of the slope into the gateway alone.
The Hunt's chronicle describes the duel that followed. Grace fought with speed and lightning and Kampe with strength. The warden's blows split the stone of the gateway, and Grace turned them repeatedly while the bolts she called came down onto Kampe wherever her spear touched and burned black marks across the scales. Both were bleeding. Kampe had done this for longer than there had been gods, and she did not tire. One of her blades caught Grace across the left arm and the poison numbed it to the shoulder. Grace threw herself flat under a stroke of the tail and rolled onto her back with the warden standing over her, both blades raised.
The blow was stopped by a shield that no one on the slope could see. Witnesses describe a shimmer in the air a hand's breadth thick, like heat over the Flats, on which both of Kampe's blades stopped dead. The shock of it went out through the gateway in a ring and threw the dust off every stone. The boy was standing over Grace with one hand raised. This was the first direct use by Minerva in the siege of the Aegis, the shield the Hunt's files record from the Themis Needle.
Helm recordings from the gateway preserve what followed. Kampe recognised him, or believed she did, and named him as the boy who had stolen her prisoner. The prisoner meant was Briares the Hundred-Handed, freed from Kampe's keeping by Percy Jackson in the Titan War fourteen thousand years before. Minerva's answer was that Kampe had known someone, and she ordered the Lieutenant up. Grace recognised the face she had chased up the Themis Needle. Minerva directed her to the warden's left side.
They attacked together. Kampe struck at each of them and the Aegis turned each blow. Grace went in under the warden's left arm where the shield had opened her guard, drove her spear into the dragon's flank below the ring of heads and called the whole of the ceiling's storm down through it. Kampe reeled back across the gateway, burning along every scale, and lashed out with her tail. Minerva caught the sting on her raised forearm against the Aegis and drove it down into the stone. At her word Grace put the spear into the same wound and released everything she had left.
Kampe went over backward off the edge of the gateway, where the Flamen Gate looks down into the gorge. She fell burning, with the lightning still on her and Grace's spear still in her side, into the Phlegethon. The river took her. A column of steam rose out of the gorge as high as the Echo Mast and hung there for most of a cycle. Grace's spear was lost with her.
When Grace turned from the edge, the boy was already walking back down the slope toward the stretcher teams. She called after him by the name Jackson. The helm recordings show him stopping and replying that this was not the time, that her gate was open and that she should take it. He then continued down the slope and was not seen again by the Hunt until the Mast.
Hunter Atlanta reached the gateway shortly afterward. Grace took up a fallen Moonwall spear from the stones and led the Hunt through the Flamen Gate into the Acropolis.
The signallers in Chase's trench, who had watched the duel on a relay feed, recorded that once it was over Chase remarked several times that the Lieutenant had called him Jackson.
