THE SIEGE OF THE PYRIPHLEGETHON ARSENAL

PART NINE. INTO THE BREACH

"Every one of them that does not come back brings the Imperator nearer. There is nothing in the Empire that can stop what we began to call down so many rotations ago. Soon it will come up at our asking."
Haruspex Ilan Dace, entered under CODE 31 by the Conclave at Watchpoint Hades, Rotation Twelve

THE HOPLON ASSAULT. LEAF 57-44. ROTATION TWELVE, CYCLE 230

The assault on the crater would be led by Hoplon carriers. Two cohorts of the Thirty-Fifth Trench Regiment would ride to the breach in thirty of them, with Thureos tanks and a company of Colossi in support. At the breach the Hoplons would drop their ramps and put eight hundred breachers onto the rubble at once, enough to swamp whatever the mine had left alive. Behind them would come more of the Thirty-Fifth on foot, and the shock breachers of the regiment's grenadier cohort in Kentauros carriers. The Phaethons of the Tenth Ordnance Phalanx were already firing over their heads to box the breach off from reinforcement, and the Wrath bombers would come in low to hit anything moving up behind the Wall.
The Cinder Wall was the last of the Archflamen's rings and the oldest. It circled the foot of the Acropolis in a single unbroken line twenty-two kilometres long. It was cast basalt, poured by the Arsenal's first founders in courses the height of a hab-deck, six courses high and thirty metres thick at the footing, and capped by a parapet of the same stone. There were towers along it every two hundred metres, more than a hundred of them, each with its slits for heavy guns and its plunging fire onto the ground below. Inside the Wall were bunkers and magazines and shelters and long corridors, so that the garrison could move along it under cover from one end to the other. Along the parapet sat the Arsenal's old automatic guns, the ones the founders had built to hold off the swarms. In front of it the Archflamen had dug more trenches and pillboxes and wire, and sown it with mines, and cut ditches across it to stop tanks. The Wall had one weakness. It had no depth. Behind it there was nothing but the forges and the cone. Once a long enough stretch of it was down, the Third could pour through the gap faster than the Archflamen could fill it.
Before the dust from the Great Mine had settled, the Hoplons' drivers started their engines and let off their brakes. The carriers ground forward, slowly at first and then faster, the air turning blue with their exhaust. They went out across the cratered ground toward no-man's land in a ragged line, pitching and rolling like boats in a swell. Inside each one stood fifty soldiers of the Thirty-Fifth, packed shoulder to shoulder, thrown about by every hole the carrier crossed. They could see nothing. They heard the shells going out over their heads and waited for the ramp. Every Hoplon's dozer blade cut a track through the ash, and every track ran straight at the breach.
To the garrison on the Wall it looked as though the ground itself was coming at them. Most of the soldiers left on that stretch were still dazed from the mine. Some had been thrown out of their bunks two hundred metres away. The survivors pulled themselves up and went to their pillboxes and fire trenches.
The Archflamen's counter-fire began to land among the Hoplons. It was a few mortar rounds at first, bursting among the carriers and throwing ash over them and doing little harm. Heavier shells followed as more batteries were given the target. Soon divine mortar rounds were falling among them, and then the Arsenal's own siege guns, firing from the pits around the cone. Through all of it the Hoplons drove on. One took a shell straight into its open troop bay. Fifty soldiers in a steel box took the blast together, and the carrier slewed to a stop with smoke coming off it and its deck awash. The relay at the Thirty-Fifth's command bunker logged forty-four uploads off that one Hoplon inside two seconds. Six were logged null.
From the Wall the Archflamen's gunners opened fire with lance-cannon. The beams hit the first wave and spat off the Hoplons' heavy front plates, which had been built to take exactly that. The Thureos tanks fired back, their guns recoiling, the shells breaking chunks off the parapet. A second Hoplon was hit and burned, its fuel going up. The soldiers inside came out of the back of it on fire and ran a few metres in the ash and fell. A third carrier was stopped with a broken track. A fourth blew up.
The leading Hoplons were close now. The order went down to fix bayonets, and in each carrier fifty blades came out together and clicked home. The first ramps dropped and hit the ash. The centurions and optios gave the word, and the Thirty-Fifth went down the ramps and out into the fire, boots ringing on the steel.
The first officer out of a carrier on that front was Optio Danel Ruark of the Second Century, Seventh Cohort. He was shot through the head on the ramp and his soldiers jumped over him. He transferred clean. Many of those behind him did not get so far. The heavy guns on the Wall had them in grazing fire before they had cleared the ramps, and whole sections went down in the ash together. Across the sector the Archflamen poured fire into the breach. Century after century came out into it and met it.
At the lip of the crater the Thirty-Fifth found a wall of broken stone in front of them. The mine had thrown up its own rampart. Blocks of the old Wall and its footings lay piled around the rim, some of them the size of a Hoplon, and they gave the breachers cover and stopped the carriers and the tanks dead. The Hoplons had done their job. Their drivers threw them into reverse and backed out with their guns firing over the breachers' heads. From here on, into the crater and up the other side, the fight was for infantry. The Archflamen's guns moved their fire onto the point of the attack. The Thirty-Fifth went forward through the rubble under plunging fire from the towers on either side of the gap.
Centurion Abel Strand of the Nineteenth Cohort was already down among the blocks, shouting his soldiers on, when a mortar round landed at his feet. It took his left leg off below the knee and filled him with fragments. He went down. He did not die, which was the worst thing for him, since a soldier who did not die did not upload. His optio dragged him behind a block and put a tourniquet on him, and he lay there for nine hours.
Provost Edrin Solk of the Thirty-Fifth's provost section saw the centurion go down and took his cohort. Solk had his rod of office in one hand and his pistol in the other. He ordered every section near him to follow and went over the rim and down into the crater. A round took the cap off his head on the way down. He reached the foot of the far slope and threw himself flat, and the sections behind him caught him up and did the same. They were the furthest forward of anyone in the Thirty-Fifth. The broken ends of the Wall stood up on either side of them, swept clear of defenders by the fire of the Thureos tanks. Solk began to climb the far slope. The stone under him was still hot from the mine. He was nearly at the top when the Archflamen counter-attacked.
The Archflamen's commanders had known the attack on the breach was coming and had pulled in everything they could reach to stop it, marching soldiers in from the neighbouring stretches of the Wall through the shelling. Waiting at the top of the crater, in the broken stone of the rim, were the Laistrygons. They had been dosed with furnace-draught, the heat-well stimulant the Arsenal's overseers had once used to get a double shift out of them, and they had been held there until the last moment. They came down the slope at a run onto the Thirty-Fifth as it climbed. Their weight carried them through the first sections, swinging as they came. A Laistrygon stood three metres tall and weighed as much as five soldiers, and the furnace-draught had burned out anything in it that could be afraid. The Thirty-Fifth, caught on a slope by something twice its height, had no chance. The Laistrygons went through them into the crater.
Solk was knocked aside by the first of them and lost his rod. He emptied his pistol into the next and it kept coming. He was reloading when a third came at him with a casting-line rock borer, and he had nothing left to stop it with. The borer cut him in half at the waist. His implant uploaded. The relay logged it as clean, and nineteen cycles later Solk woke in a vat at Watchpoint Hades and asked for his rod.
More of the Thirty-Fifth were climbing down into the crater, and the fight at the bottom turned into a scrum in the ash, the Laistrygons swinging into it with no thought of anything. It was butchery. A Laistrygon went down for every dozen soldiers it killed, and the crater floor was covered in bodies from both sides.
The Archflamen's guns shelled the crater itself, onto the scrum, killing everyone in it, his and the Third's. The relay at the command bunker filled with uploads faster than the crews could count them.
In the breach the Thirty-Fifth's tanks could not get forward. The rubble had blocked them for good. Without them, the breachers could not fight their way up the slope against the Archflamen's soldiers, who were fighting as though the Wall itself would fall on them if they gave a metre.
Behind the fighting the second wave was already moving, the Kentauros carriers in front with the grenadier cohort in their heavy plate, and more centuries on foot behind them. The garrison fought as hard as anything the Third had met on the Flats. Both sides had lost terribly. Of the two cohorts that went in first, most of the soldiers had transferred. Some centuries had gone in their entirety. The second wave did no better, and the fighting went on into the dark watch. The Thirty-Fifth could not take the breach and hold it. Time after time the breachers went over the rim into the crater, and time after time the Archflamen moved guns to sweep it, or counter-charged, and cut them down. In the dark watch the Thirty-Fifth pulled back to its own trenches, most of the survivors dragging someone. Centurion Strand was carried out on a door. The dead lay three and four deep in places. The wrecks of tanks and Hoplons lay across the sector. When the firing died down the reclaimers of the Répertoire came out with the Quiet Ones behind them, and went over the ground the way carrion birds would have, if the Flats had had any, cutting implants out of the dead who had not uploaded so that the Répertoire could check them and log them.
The Thirty-Fifth's command admitted that the first attempt on the breach had failed. Colonel Vidar Osk asked for a second. He would have time to prepare it properly. This time he would lead it himself, and he told his staff he would rather go null than be beaten twice.

THE SECOND ASSAULT. ROTATION TWELVE, CYCLE 246

On the two hundred and forty-sixth cycle of Rotation Twelve, the Thirty-Fifth went back into the breach. This time it had been planned with great care. The staff had decided the first attack had failed on the rubble. No armour had been able to cross it. So for fourteen cycles the Phaethon batteries of the Tenth Ordnance Phalanx, firing from their pits south of the Demus Gorge, shelled the breach itself every watch with their heaviest rounds, to break the blocks small enough for tanks to cross. The rest of the Tenth shelled the Wall and the towers either side of the gap, to break up the parapet and the old automatic guns along it. The softening went on for two weeks. It used up a large share of the Seventh Line Phalanx's shells.
Meanwhile Osk made his new assault cohorts ready. The Hoplons had worked and they would carry his soldiers again, with more tanks behind them this time, and more Colossi.
The staff had also decided the first attack had been too narrow, a single thrust with two cohorts at the point. The second would go in wider, against the Wall along the whole of the regiment's front. It looked like waste. The staff did not think so. In the first attack the Archflamen had held the breach by moving soldiers along the Wall's inner corridors from the stretches either side, out of reach of the shelling. This time those soldiers would be held in place by attacks on their own stretches. The Colossi would go in with those attacks and try to blow new breaches with their guns. With one breach to hold, the Archflamen had been able to mass enough soldiers in it to stop the attack. With several breaches to hold, his soldiers would be spread out, and less able to stop the blow when it came.
This time the Archflamen was better prepared. The Hoplons met a storm of lance-fire and missiles on the way in, and many were knocked out and joined the wrecks of the first attack in no-man's land. The Archflamen's gunners had had two weeks to range the ground and answered fast and heavily. Colonel Osk led his soldiers into the crater himself, and found it held again, and held hard. He was killed there, fighting hand to hand among the blocks with his signifer beside him. A Laistrygon's hammer came down on him and on the signifer together. The relay logged him null. His body was not found. It was somewhere under the soldiers who had followed him into the crater and died there too.
North and south of the breach the wider attack had some success. The Colossi blew two new, smaller breaches in the Wall and gave the Third more places to come through, but neither could be taken and held. In one of them the gap changed hands eight times in a single cycle before the Thirty-Fifth was forced out of it. The second assault had been beaten back like the first. There would have to be a third.

THE MEAT-GRINDER. ROTATION TWELVE, CYCLE 300 TO ROTATION THIRTEEN, CYCLE 340

This time Kastellan brought the Ninth Assault Phalanx into the line to lead the attack on the Wall. Once again the Third went up into the breaches in waves, and once again it was thrown back. The Archflamen was fighting with everything he had left. Over the next rotation there were four more assaults on Leaf 57-44, and each of them ended in a bloody retreat. The Behemoths of Talos joined the fighting, and the drowned Behemoths came across the Flats to meet them, and the ground around the crater became the bloodiest of the whole siege. It was a grinder. Both armies fed regiments into it and it ate them.
The fourth assault ended when the Frost Masons fired a mine of their own under the original crater. They had been digging under it since the second assault, in silence, at the Cocytus pace that the Hephaestean sappers had learned to dread. The mine went off under eleven centuries of the Ninth Assault Phalanx in the bottom of the crater. Some of them were caught so deep in the rock that their implants never found a relay. The Third's sappers had learned in the war beneath that a soldier killed eighty metres down went onto the Deep List, and nine hundred names went onto it in a single second. The crater was deeper and wider when the dust settled, and there was more rubble in it than ever, and the attack was over.
Kastellan had not yet found a way to take the breach, and he was worried that it was eating the army. He asked the Forge for more Behemoths, and Cradle-Lord Varne passed on the Forge's refusal. Talos had lost eleven engines on the Flats already and would not risk the rest in a crater. The fighting at Leaf 57-44 was the fiercest on the Flats, but there was still a siege to run. The whole of the Flats was one battlefield. Attacks and counter-attacks, raids and counter-raids went in along the whole front every watch, as each side tried to break the other's line, and stretch after stretch of the front was swallowed by it. The Forsworn kept up their attacks. The Unremembered were found raiding far behind the Third's lines, coming out of the mist off the Lethe Cut to blow up gun pits and shell dumps and going back into it before anyone could follow. The First Line Phalanx on the northern front was fully taken up with the Grey Seventh shelling, which still came over in every Arsenal barrage, and which still sent soldiers back to Hades blank or did not send them back at all. The drowned Behemoths were still out on the Flats, adding their weight to any big attack the Archflamen made, and the Talos engines had to go wherever they appeared, until Varne's cohort was spread across the Flats in twos and threes. Every watch the front was on fire somewhere. The reports came up to Quarterdeck Nineteen faster than Kastellan's staff could read them. Under the ground the sappers kept digging.
Perhaps the war beneath could give him another breach, or a second one beside the first. It did not. The Frost Masons had learned from the Great Mine and were counter-mining better than ever. Under the ground the war had become a deadlock as complete as the one above. The two sides were well matched. Each blow was met by another, and neither had the strength to finish it. After a rotation of savage fighting, and something over eleven million more transfers on the Third's books, the tempo of the war slackened. Kastellan's plans for a new push had run their course. The Archflamen had matched them and, at enormous cost, held. In places the Third's regiments could see the Cinder Wall from their trenches. In others they were still stuck in the ruins of the inner belt.
The Archflamen was no longer surrounded, either. The Third's southern ring had been given up after the Demus Rim, and from the south the Archflamen could send columns around the Third's flank, out across the open Flats to the east. Far from the trenches, the Third's outrider squadrons fought their own hard war, riding long patrols across the ash to find the columns before they reached anything that mattered. Running fights between outriders and the Archflamen's scouts were part of every cycle out there.
So another rotation of the siege went by, with heavy losses, a little ground, and no end in sight for the soldiers who lived through it. Kastellan did have one piece of good news. The Fleet's patrol sweeps down the river and through the low geometry had found no trace of the Eye of Light. The drowned battleship had once been the Empire's, an AnnChase-class warship built at the Forge of Hephaestus for Apollo's fleet, registry AC-09-1642. The thing that had stolen her had driven her down through the ceiling into the Tartarus front at Grid Delta, Sector Nineteen-F, rotations before the Archflamen ever revolted, and the river had kept her. She had come back up the Phlegethon with a drowned crew and a burned face, and had fought the Fleet in the low geometry and ambushed Kastellan's own relief convoy. Now she was gone from the augurs. Most of the Fleet's officers thought she had gone back down the river too badly damaged to risk another fight. The cables and the convoys down to Landing Shelf Kappa were safe again.

THE LONG WAR. ROTATION THIRTEEN, CYCLE 340 TO ROTATION FIFTEEN

After the fighting at the crater the long war of attrition began again. Both sides husbanded their strength and reorganised and refitted. The barrage went on every watch, but for the time being neither side could do much more. Kastellan accepted what his offensive had won and drew up new plans to take back the ground that had been lost, but he knew now that the attrition would go on, and if it had to go on forever, it would. The Archflamen's army had been joined by the deepest things in Tartarus and had given itself to them, and it would not break or surrender. It would have to be destroyed to the last soldier.
The Répertoire tried to count what was left of it. There had been six million, two hundred thousand souls on the spur at the start of the siege, and none of them had implants worth the name. Every one the Third killed was gone for good. If the Third had killed one Arsenal soldier for every five transfers it had suffered, which was the rate the Répertoire's first plan had assumed, then something over seven million had died since the siege began. That was more than the Arsenal had ever held. It could not be true, and it was not. The Répertoire's kill counts from the Flats were inflated by a factor nobody could pin down. The truth was worse. The Arsenal had been reinforced, by the Forsworn, by the Cinderborn and the Frost Masons and the Unremembered, by the drowned, and by whatever came up the river every reformation. The Archflamen was nowhere near the end of his soldiers.
The Archflamen's soldiers had been thralls and casting-line gangs and a garrison militia that drilled once a rotation. Now all of them were veterans, hardened by the siege into good soldiers, and they believed something below them was on their side and that victory would be theirs, whatever happened at the front. Archflamen Sabinus Orrhae had not been seen or heard from in a long time. Nobody in the Third knew whether he was still alive in his rooms in the Acropolis. Some of Kastellan's staff talked of sending an Enforcement killer into the cone after him. Kastellan did not think it would make any difference. He thought it more likely the Legates of the deep rivers had taken the war out of the Archflamen's hands long ago, and that the siege was now being fought to their plans.
So the two armies were deadlocked again. The long war would have to go on. The Répertoire's revised end-date for the siege had passed most of two rotations before. The Dionysian Logistics Directorate had accepted the delay and found the shells for it, but now it was carrying out another long review of the campaign. The Third had been given twelve rotations. It had used them, and more, and the victory had not come.
Kastellan argued against it, but the Directorate could not go on meeting the Third's demands. Other fronts wanted the shells and the soldiers, and the reduction of the Arsenal would lose its priority. It would not be given up. The Archflamen still had to be beaten and the Arsenal given back to the Empire. It would simply take longer, and cost more, in the end. Kastellan and his staff argued it out with the Directorate's delegates on Quarterdeck Nineteen. It did him no good. The new plan had already been sealed on the ceiling worlds by the Directorate's own masters. He would have fewer replacements and fewer shells and he must plan accordingly. There would not be another review for five rotations.
In answer, Kastellan sent a delegation of his own staff officers up the cables to find help, fast, before the Directorate's new order began to bite.

THE ACHILLEAN ORDER

Kastellan's delegates found it at the relay station of Erebus Nine, a day's flight above the Flats. The Achillean strike-ship Thetis Mourning was there, taking on fuel and water on her way back from a sweep through the Erebus shallows, where she had burned out a nest of drakon-breeders in the old flood-tunnels.
She carried ninety of the Achillean Order, drawn from four of its lochoi, under Polemarch Teukros Avel of the Third Lochos. The Order was small and old. It had been founded on a vow made over a shield, and its members were legacies of Achilles and of Thetis, and of the Myrmidons who had followed him, and they took the short life. No Achillean carried a Hades implant. When an Achillean died, the Order burned the body and said the name and that was the end of it. Their founder had been offered a long life and an obscure one, and he had chosen the other, and the Order had chosen the same thing every generation since. They were hard to kill, and they knew exactly what it cost when it happened.
Avel heard Kastellan's delegates on the strike-ship's deck with the courtesy the Order showed to everyone. The Marshal needed help. His army was locked in a deadlock in front of the last wall, and attempt after attempt on it had failed. Now he was asking the Order. Avel said he would look at the situation, and put the request to the Order's Hearth on the ceiling. He would not commit the strike-ship himself, though the Order's customs allowed him to.
Some months later the answer came down to Quarterdeck Nineteen. The Hearth had agreed. Avel's company would come down to the Flats and look at the siege on the ground before deciding where it could do the most good. There was one condition. Avel would be under no one's command. No Achillean would take an order from the Third. The Polemarch alone would decide where and how his warriors fought.
Kastellan agreed at once. He was glad that anyone would help him while the Directorate was cutting him off. He had Avel and his officers briefed, and they held their own council to choose a target. They chose the breach at Leaf 57-44. It was the one place on the Flats where ninety warriors could change the outcome of the siege. They would take the crater and open the Cinder Wall. They named the operation Scaean Gate. Kastellan's staff looked the name up afterward and wished they had not.

OPERATION SCAEAN GATE. ROTATION FIFTEEN, CYCLE 189

The plan used everything the Order had, including the strike-ship. It would be a single fast blow in the dark watch, and it would depend on its timing. The point of it was the Order's First Lochos, the oldest warriors Avel had, in Myrmidon plate, the heavy powered armour that the Order's own smiths forged for each of them and that took a lifetime to earn. Avel would lead them into the crater himself, with the Order's seer, Mantis Orrin Kaledos, beside him. They would not walk. They would translate, through the Thetis Mourning's translation chamber, straight from the ship's deck to the crater floor. It had to be exact. A translation that missed by twenty metres would put them inside the rubble. The strike-ship would have to come down as close to the Flats as she could, and someone would have to put a beacon on the crater floor for the chamber to lock onto.
No Achillean could get to the crater floor without being seen. Avel asked Kastellan for a sapper.
Sapper-Lieutenant Ansel Rook of the Sixty-Sixth Sapper Company had been underground for most of three rotations. He knew the old galleries under Leaf 57-44, the ones the Sixty-First had dug to lay the Great Mine, which the mine itself had half-collapsed and the Frost Masons had half-filled. He said there was still a way through. It came up in a cleft under one of the big blocks on the crater floor, unless it had fallen in since he last looked. He asked for four volunteers and got eleven, and took four.
While Rook went up the gallery, a second force would be ready to drive for the crater as soon as the First Lochos was in it. Lochagos Ithra Vell of the Sixth Lochos would command it, in the Order's own armour, with Kriophoros siege tanks to clear the rubble and three of the Order's Ancients. The Ancients were warriors who had been too badly broken to fight and too stubborn to be burned, and had chosen to go on inside walker-coffins of bronze and iron. They were Ancient Aias, who had been a priest of the Order before he was broken, Ancient Rhesos, and Ancient Dolon, who would go in with a rock-breaking drill and a fire-lance for siege work.
When the First Lochos had the crater, Vell's force would come up and hold it against the counter-attacks that were bound to come. When the Order had secured it, Avel would signal the codeword, and a battle group of the Ninth Assault Phalanx, chosen for the job, would go through the breach first and exploit it. The battle group would have the Behemoths of Talos with it. Cradle-Lord Varne had agreed to the plan, and would walk with it himself in the Anvil of Talos. If everything went well, the Order would hand the crater to the Ninth and go back up to its ship. For the first time in the siege, the Archflamen would face the Empire's war machine all at once.
Aboard the Thetis Mourning the Order made ready. Avel spent a watch alone in the ship's shrine, where the Order kept the shield it had been founded over. Then he put on his plate, and so did Kaledos and the eleven warriors of the First Lochos. Vell's force went down the cables to the Flats.
The captain of the strike-ship was briefed. He would be taking his ship under the ceiling into range of the ceiling-lances, and the lances would find him as soon as he came down, and fire. It was a risk to the ship. She only had to live through the lances long enough for the translation. Then he was to break off and run for the high geometry as fast as she would go.

THE CRATER. ROTATION FIFTEEN, CYCLE 189. THE DARK WATCH

Rook went up the gallery on his belly. It was half a metre high in places. The Frost Masons had packed the side galleries with ice, and the ice had sweated in the heat of the Flats and frozen again, and the four sappers behind him went through it in the dark by touch. At the eleventh hour of the dark watch he found the cleft. It was smaller than he remembered. He took his pack off and pushed it in front of him and went through on his side.
He came out under a block of cast Wall the size of a hab and lay still. The crater floor stank of the dead of seven assaults. Above him, on the rim, the Archflamen's sentries walked with their lamps.
Above the Flats the Thetis Mourning came down out of the high geometry with her running lights dark. The ceiling-lances found her in four minutes. The beams came up out of the dark under her and broke on her wards, and her own guns answered them, firing down along the beams at the lances' mounts. Two beams got through her wards and burned along her flank. She held her course.
In the crater, a sentry's lamp swung across the foot of the block and stopped on a boot. Rook heard the shout go up. He had the beacon out of his pack. He set it on the stone and opened it and began to turn it on, by hand, the way the Order's techs had shown him. Rounds came off the block over his head. Two of his sappers fired back up the slope. A third took the spare beacon and went to the other side of the block, in case.
The beacon's light went green. In the strike-ship's chamber, the translation officer called the lock.
Rook's sappers fought from the foot of the block with their carbines. On the rim the alarm had gone up, and the Archflamen's soldiers were coming down into the crater toward the lamp. A missile came out of the dark and hit Sapper Iolo Brenn in the chest and killed him, and his implant uploaded. Sapper Kara Nysse took a round through the face and did not die, and lay behind the block holding it. There were five of them, and then four, and then three in a fight, against hundreds. Rook did not expect to see the end of it.
In the Thetis Mourning's chamber the light came up around Avel and his warriors and filled the room. When it went out, the chamber was empty.
They came out of the dark onto the crater floor in a crack of displaced air, exactly on the beacon, close enough to Rook that the rush of it knocked him down. Thirteen figures in Myrmidon plate stood up among the dead. Avel's gun was firing before his feet had settled. The Arsenal soldiers coming down the slope fired into them, and the rounds came off the plate like hail off a roof. The First Lochos went up the slope with their spears and their hammers and their great shields, and they killed everything on it. In the dark nobody in the crater could tell what was happening except the Achilleans, who knew the plan and kept to it, moving together up the slope and clearing it in turn. Kaledos fought at Avel's shoulder. Where he pointed, the dark came apart. Soldiers who had been about to fire found their guns already empty, or their feet already turned the other way. The ones who did not run did not live.
On the Flats behind them, as soon as the First Lochos went in, Vell's force was moving across no-man's land, the warriors dismounting into a line to finish the crossing on foot. Among them walked the three Ancients. The dark did not hide them for long. The searchlights on the Wall came round and found them, and the Archflamen's guns opened up.
Vell's warriors went forward through the fire, their own guns answering in a steady stream. From behind them the missiles and lance-beams of the Order's heavy weapons went over their heads into the parapet. The Phaethons' pre-ranged fire came down on the towers. A shell landed close enough to Vell to throw her into the air, and she landed in a rain of stone and got up and went on. The First Lochos was in front of her, fighting for the crater, and she meant to get to it. Somewhere in the dark a Kriophoros fired and hit what was left of a pillbox that the Archflamen's soldiers had rebuilt with sandbags and blocks of Wall, and the pillbox came apart.
Vell's leading squad reached the rim of the crater and stopped there under heavy fire to reload, firing over the edge. Ancient Aias was with them, the barrels of his lance glowing. Rhesos and Dolon came up behind, Dolon's drill breaking the biggest of the blocks to open a road for the tanks. Behind him came the first Kriophoros. It fired again, a high lobbing shot onto the parapet above the crater, and broke a piece off it the size of a carrier.
Vell's main body was at the crater now. With Aias in front, they went down into the bottom of it where so much of the Third had died before them, through the plunging fire and the thickening barrage, and up the far side. Their plate carried them up the slope without a stop.
Ahead of them Avel's warriors met the Laistrygons head on. They came down off the top of the rim the way they had come down on the Thirty-Fifth. The furnace-draught had them howling. Avel's blade took the leg off the first one and it went down, still swinging its hammer. The rest of the charge broke on the First Lochos's shields. These were not trench soldiers. The Achilleans' hammers knocked the Laistrygons backward, and their spears went through the Arsenal's crude plate. Beside Avel stood Ansel Rook with a dead sentry's rifle and a sapper's entrenching blade, his carbine long since empty. When the Order's chroniclers wrote down that night, they wrote his name in the same column as their own.
Vell and Aias reached their Polemarch. They were both scorched and dented, but the Order held the crater. The dead of the Archflamen lay across it on top of the dead of seven assaults. The Achilleans had done in a single dark watch what the Third had failed to do in two rotations. The garrison had not been able to stop the translation, or the speed of the First Lochos's attack, or whatever it was that Kaledos did with his hand. Avel formed Vell's warriors into a perimeter around the crater as they came up. They had it. The codeword went out. Now they had to hold it until the Ninth Assault Phalanx and the Behemoths arrived. Avel's warriors knew that the battle was not over.
Rook sat down against a block and found that Kara Nysse was still alive behind it. He put his own dressing on her face, and waited for the Ninth.

HERE WE STAND. ROTATION FIFTEEN, CYCLE 190

The Archflamen had planned for a breach in the Wall. He kept a reaction force of tanks and carrier-borne infantry behind it, ready to move to any gap. As soon as the news of the crater reached his commanders they sent it in, to stop any breakthrough and take the gap back. The drowned Behemoths would follow it.
Before the turn of the watch the two forces were on their way to meet. The Order held the wreckage of the Wall and the broken ground in front of it. Avel put his heavy weapons in the gap, and pulled the First Lochos back to wait behind them, ready to throw them into any part of the line the Archflamen came close to breaking. This was where the Order would stand. The Archflamen's guns were still shelling the crater accurately, and the Achilleans had to dig in among the blocks and throw up barricades as fast as they could. Behind them, help was on the way.
The Ninth Assault Phalanx's battle group sent its engineers in first. They set to work clearing the last of the obstacles around the breach with charges and dozers. Behind them came the tanks, the spearhead of the breakthrough, each company with a Colossus. Behind the tanks came the breachers and the field guns, which were soon in place and stocked with shells and ready to fire.
At the turn of the watch, when the light off the Phlegethon brightened over the cone the way it did every cycle, the Archflamen's counter-attack began. The drowned Behemoths opened fire from long range and raked the breach. More of the Wall came down. A transport of the Order took a beam from a Phlegethon-cannon and was gone, leaving nothing but black scorched stone where it had been. Ancient Aias lost his lance arm to a white-hot fragment. The Third's siege guns answered from the back of the Flats, their shells going over the Achilleans' heads and bursting around the feet of the drowned engines. Avel watched them through his scope. He could see the Archflamen's armour coming on, heavy tanks with carriers behind them. Some of the carriers were already burning in the barrage. More Behemoth fire came down on his positions and left new craters among the blocks. Varne's engines had not yet arrived. Avel needed something that could answer the drowned engines before they took his warriors apart. Myrmidon plate would not stop a Phlegethon-cannon. He turned to look back toward the Third's lines, and through the smoke he saw the Anvil of Talos coming.
Doros Varne's Behemoth came for the breach at its full stride, stepping over and around the tanks still grinding forward. Its augurs had found the drowned Behemoths long before, and its gunnery crew was already working out the long shots for every weapon it had. Two Steropes followed it. The Anvil of Talos went through the breach first, its wards flaring under the first hits. Avel looked up as it stepped over him. Its command deck rode eighty metres above the Flats.
Avel smelled ozone and felt the air hum as the Behemoth's three lance-batteries came up to power, and fired together, a triple beam so bright it left a line across his sight. He followed the shot out and saw the wards of a drowned Behemoth flare white where it hit. The Anvil walked on past his warriors with the two Steropes behind it, light-footed beside it. They fired too. Avel closed his helm. With the Behemoths fighting their own duel, his warriors could deal with the tanks. The fire was getting heavier as the Archflamen's armour came closer, the lances cracking, the missiles screaming in. A carrier blew up in an orange fireball. Avel pulled back to where he could see the whole fight. His own turn would come later. For now it was a gunnery duel. Looking back, he saw the first of the Ninth's Cyclopean lance-tanks crawling across the crater, coming up the slope and pushing the last of the rubble aside. Soon it would be firing on the drowned Behemoths too.
The Archflamen's counter-attack pressed hard all through the cycle, but more of the Third poured through the breach behind the Order and out onto the ground beyond it. Several times the Archflamen's breachers reached the crater, and each time Avel's First Lochos threw them out. Avel himself was hit by a jet of river-fire from a Phlegethon-lance. His plate saved his life, but the jet burned through the breastplate and through the Polemarch's chest behind it. Rook and the Order's healer dragged him out of the line. He was barely conscious and still giving orders. The healer packed the wound and dosed him and said he would live, if the Order's smiths could rebuild what the fire had taken.
Command of the crater passed to Lochagos Vell and Ancient Aias. All cycle the Order fought until its ammunition was gone. By then the Ninth had fought the Archflamen's reaction force to a standstill. Varne's engines had fought their duel with the drowned. One of the Steropes was destroyed by a Phlegethon-cannon, its reactor going up in a flash that lit the whole of the Flats like a second Phlegethon, and when the light faded its two legs were still standing in the ash with nothing on top of them. But the drowned engines had been badly hurt in the exchange and pulled back rather than be destroyed. Varne could add another victory over the drowned to the Anvil's tally.
When Vell judged that the Ninth was in the breach in such strength that nothing the Archflamen had could move it, she ordered the Order out. The Achilleans came back across no-man's land on foot, or riding on the hulls of the few transports they had left. Ninety of the Order had gone into Operation Scaean Gate. Thirty-one walked out, and many of those were badly hurt. Avel was carried. Ancient Aias had lost his arm. Ancient Dolon was destroyed, an armour-piercing shell from a Lance-Hunter having gone through his coffin, and his wreck was towed back so that the Order's smiths could put another broken warrior in it.
The fifty-nine dead were carried out with the living. Not one of them would come back. The Order had no vats and wanted none. The survivors loaded the bodies onto their transports to take them up to the Hearth, where they would be burned and their names said in front of the shield. Ansel Rook walked back with them. Somewhere on the way across no-man's land Lochagos Vell asked him whether he wanted to join the Order. He said he liked coming back too much. She said that was a pity, and meant it. Above the Flats the Thetis Mourning had lived through the ceiling-lances. She was burned along one side, but she had got away.
The Order's part was done. It had paid for the breach with fifty-nine names. Few in the Empire could have done what it did, and the Third knew it. On Leaf 57-44, the Third was through the Cinder Wall at last.
Only the Acropolis lay in front of it.

QUARTERDECK NINETEEN. ROTATION FIFTEEN, CYCLE 197

On Quarterdeck Nineteen, Kastellan was working through the supply calculations for the breach when his adjutant brought him a sealed packet. The news from Leaf 57-44 had changed everything. The Ninth needed shells and fuel and replacements on the far side of the Wall, and the Répertoire had produced new figures overnight. Kastellan's plan to bring in the Achillean Order had worked, and for the first time the Third could see its last objective in front of it. The crisis that had begun on the Demus Rim had been contained.
The packet bore the crescent seal of the Director of Anti-Espionage and Celestial Surveillance. It told him that the Hunt of the Director had taken readings on the Tartarean theatre, and that its sister-augurs had searched the probable futures of the Flats, and that every reading was the same. The Arsenal was about to break open from below.
The Hunt had known for a long time that the Archflamen's soldiers were praying to the deep rivers and that the Legates of those rivers had come up to fight beside him. Now the readings said the Archflamen's people would soon be giving themselves to the things below as hosts, and calling up more of them, and if they succeeded the Arsenal might not be recoverable at all. The deep geometry under Depth 40 was already restless. The Third was in danger that no map in Kastellan's war room showed and no gun in his army could stop. The calling-up was nearly done.
The Director held the Tartarean containment lines jointly with the House of Hades, and had done for longer than the Third had existed. Under that charge she was bringing a motion before the Conclave at Watchpoint Hades, to take the Third Siege Army under her seal.
Kastellan read the packet twice, and then put it in his desk and went back to the shell figures.
On the Flats the signs were bad, and nobody there knew how to read them. Wrath crews flying over the cone reported a light under the Phlegethon, deep down in the river cloud, like a second river burning underneath the first. The ceiling geometry over the Flats had always been restless. Now its storms flickered without stopping. The Echo Mast had started to hum on frequencies that the Third's signallers could not filter out. Some sentries on the northern front swore they had seen soldiers walking in no-man's land in the dark watch, soldiers in the Third's grey with their helmets off, who did not answer a challenge and walked toward the Wall. When the sentries looked again, there was nobody. Little notice was taken of any of it. Soon the Third would have a new enemy on the Flats.

LUNA. ROTATION FIFTEEN, CYCLE 199

Two cycles later the Director received Lieutenant Thalia Grace in the long gallery under the Sixth Moonwall on Luna. No record of the meeting was entered into AtRep. What is known of it comes from Grace's own later testimony to the Hunt.
The Director informed Grace that she would present the Hunt's motion to the Conclave in four cycles. Hunt-Elder Sarn would speak for it, but the motion would be Grace's. When it passed, Grace would take command of the Third at the Arsenal and hold it until the Arsenal was closed, with her own contingent under her. Grace objected that she was no politician, and that a siege of twenty million soldiers in its fifteenth rotation was a task for a Marshal. The Director's answer was that the Third already had a Marshal and needed the crescent.
Grace then put it to the Director that the posting was about the Themis Needle, where she had come within a metre of the boy carrying Percy Jackson's signature and failed to take him. The Director confirmed it. In Grace's account, the Director said that Grace had frozen on the Needle, that she had never frozen before, and that both of them needed to know whether she would do it again. Tartarus would answer that question. The Hunt has always regarded the posting as a punishment, and Grace described it the same way. The Director's closing remark, as Grace recalled it, was that there were no heights in Tartarus. Grace's fear of heights was well known in the Hunt.

THE CONCLAVE AT WATCHPOINT HADES. ROTATION FIFTEEN, CYCLE 203

The forecourt of the old audience hall at Watchpoint Hades was full. Hundreds of clerks and aides and servants and attendants waited in front of the black bronze doors to be let into the galleries above the floor. The Conclave of the Tartarean theatre sat in full session that cycle, and its list was long, from the removal of a dozen hold quartermasters for theft to the trial of a Haruspex of the Lethe Hold for heresy. First on the list was the Hunt's motion to take the Third Siege Army under the Director's seal.
The Hunt was asking the Conclave for command of the war at the Arsenal. The siege had gone on for fifteen rotations. By the Répertoire's count the Third had transferred more than forty million soldiers on the Cinder Flats, and close to two million of them had come back blank or not at all. Each metre of the Flats had been bought at a price the Répertoire could state to the gram of slurry. On its own, none of that would have moved the Hunt. Two things had. The Legates of the deep rivers were on the Flats, and the Hunt's sister-augurs had read what was coming up behind them.
Grace knew the Conclave would have to act. If it did not, the Third would be lost, and after the Third the holds around Depth 40, and after the holds the lines themselves. If the door under the Arsenal opened from below, the containment of Tartarus would have a hole in it that ran up to the ceiling worlds.
She also knew the Conclave was split. The Pontifical College had been asking for the war since the first rotation. The Archflamen was a Flamen of the College. He had been confirmed by it, and he had preached in its name, and he had poured his heresy into twenty-seven million soldiers under its seal. The College's argument was simple. Its apostate was its own to judge. The Enforcement delegates had sided with the College, as they usually did, since the College would let Enforcement run the trials. Between them they had nearly half the votes. The Hunt's people had sounded out the rest and found them evenly divided.
The hall was older than the Third. Its floor was black basalt from the first holds, worn into hollows in front of the benches by ten thousand years of feet. There were no windows at Depth 3. The servo-lamps that hung under the vault lit the benches poorly, and the floor not at all, except at the centre, where a single beam fell on whoever had the floor. Around the upper tier ran the galleries, where the clerks recorded everything said below them.
The Conclave came in to the old oath of the theatre, the one every soldier of the Third had sworn at the vats: to hold the line, to keep the door, and to come back up. The Lord Marshal of the Tartarean Theatre led them, with her staff. Behind her came the delegates of the College in their red, and of Enforcement in black, and the Dionysian Logistics Directorate, with Logothete Irene Vasska at its head, and the Forge, and Pyrrhus Command, and the House of Hades. The Hunt came in last. Hunt-Elder Melaina Sarn walked in front of it. She was older than anyone in the hall except the Lord Marshal, and more of her was silver alloy than was flesh. She had fought in the theatre's first Grand Offensive, and lost both legs and an arm and most of her face in it, and had gone on fighting. Behind her came Grace, in silver-grey, with the Director's seal on her pauldron.
When the Conclave was seated and the oath read, the Lord Marshal gave the floor to the Hunt. Grace walked out into the beam of light. She had the motion in her hand and a speech in her head. She looked at the speech for a moment and put it away.
Her address, as recorded by the clerks in the galleries, was short. She told the Conclave that something was coming up under the Arsenal. The Hunt's augurs had read it, and the delegates had held the readings for two cycles. They could vote for the motion and give the Third to the Director so that the Hunt could close the door, or they could send it for another review and argue over whose apostate the Archflamen was. In that case, she told them, nobody in the hall would be left to judge anybody. She then sat down.
The hall erupted. Delegates of the College rose to accuse the Hunt of threatening the Conclave, and others shouted back. For several minutes nobody could be heard. The clerks' record includes a single remark from Hunt-Elder Sarn to Grace on the bench beside her, to the effect that it had not been a good speech.
The debate went on for most of the cycle. Sister-Augur Kestrel was brought before the Conclave and questioned for three hours. The reports from the Flats were read in. The College's delegates spoke against the motion one after another, and the Hunt's friends spoke for it. Twice the Lord Marshal had to call for order, the second time when Hunt-Elder Sarn took the floor on her metal legs and told the College's senior delegate that she had been holding the door of Tartarus since before his House had been founded and that she would not hand it to a man who had confirmed the Archflamen in his office. Grace, who had not been allowed to speak again, sat on her hands.
When the debate was over the motion went to the vote. It was close, as everyone had known it would be. The College's delegates voted against it to a member, and Enforcement with them. At the end it came down to the Directorate. Logothete Vasska had argued for the removal of the Third's command a dozen rotations before, and had been overruled. She stood up when her name was called and abstained. Three of her delegation abstained with her. It was enough. The motion passed by two votes.
The Hunt had its command. If anyone had hoped the Conclave would come out of the hall united behind it, they were disappointed. The delegates left in an angry, suspicious mood. The College had not been persuaded of anything except that the Hunt had taken a war that belonged to it. The Archflamen was the College's apostate and the College would not forget who had taken him away from it. The arguments went on out into the forecourt and down the corridors of the Watchpoint.
Grace left the hall without speaking to anyone. On the way to the lifts, Logothete Vasska joined her and explained her abstention. She had not done it for the Hunt. She had done it for the Third, which somebody had to finish, and she wanted that somebody to be able to count. Vasska advised the Lieutenant to begin with the shells.

PREPARATIONS FOR A NEW WAR. ROTATION FIFTEEN, CYCLE 203 TO 240

The Director's order went out the same cycle, under her seal, placing the Third Siege Army under the Hunt of the Director, with Thalia Grace, Lieutenant of the Hunt, in command of it on the Flats. With her came her own contingent, Atlanta and Celyn and Naomi and Josephine and Hemithea and Phoebe, with the Hounds Columbina and Scaramucius. Behind them came thirty-eight more Hunters of the Director's household, each with her own small retinue, who would be placed in the command bunker of every regiment on the Flats to watch, and when the time came, to lead from the front. Each of them could call on the Director's other weapons if she needed them. There were the Psiloi, the Director's oath-sworn marksmen, who had fought in every war the Hunt had fought for ten thousand years. There were Enforcement's assassins, whom nobody liked to call on. And there were the flame hunters and shieldmaidens of the Sixth Moonwall, the oldest fighting companies of the Hunt, who had been trained for nothing except fighting the things that came out of the pit.
The plan had been made long before the vote. A priority signal had gone up to Luna before the Conclave left the hall. A fast moon-ship was already loading the Sixth Moonwall's best companies, with orders to come down to the Flats and put themselves under the Lieutenant's command. Hemithea had their roster and was not letting anyone else see it.
The Psiloi would come too. Their squads would stand guard on the Hunters in the regimental bunkers. They had served the Hunt for so long that most of the Third thought they were part of it, and they swore their oath to the Director's hunt every morning, and they could put a round through a Laistrygon's eye at two kilometres.
Grace sent envoys of her own. The Achillean Order had already bled for the Arsenal. She sent Naomi up to the Order's Hearth on the ceiling to ask them to come back, in strength this time, and finish what they had started. She did not order it. The Order took no orders. She also sent a request to the Cohort of Nemesis at Rhamnous Primaris, which had fought on the docks of the Phlegethon in the eighth rotation and gone home. She had little hope of that one. The Cohort answered to Nemesis and to nobody else, and Nemesis kept her own accounts.
When the order was sealed, the Hunt's people went down to Quarterdeck Nineteen to tell the Third's command. Kastellan was to give up the command of the army at once. He could take a post under the Lieutenant, or he could resign and wait for a new appointment. There would be no blame and no charge, though the Directorate would review his conduct of the siege in the usual way. From the Hunt's point of view, the Marshal had done well. He had saved the Third from defeat, and broken the inner belt, and opened the Cinder Wall. Nobody could have asked much more of him.
Kastellan heard the order standing. Then he asked to be posted to the Flats. He would take a regiment, or a cohort if there was no regiment. He had fought the siege from Quarterdeck Nineteen for six rotations and he wanted to see the Wall from the ground. The Thirty-Fifth had no colonel. Osk had gone null in the crater. Grace gave it to him the same cycle, and a dozen of his staff officers asked to go down with him and were allowed.
Hunt-Elder Sarn went down to the Flats too. She had asked for it the cycle the vote passed. She was old even for the Hunt, and more alloy now than woman, but she put on her armour again and took up her spear. Nobody on the theatre's staff knew more than she did about the things that came out of the pit, or how to send them back. The Arsenal would be her last war against them. She seemed pleased about that.
Across the Flats, the colonel of every regiment and every unit attached to the Third, down to the Quiet Ones and the cable crews and the reclaimers, received the order that the Hunt of the Director had taken command of the siege. Orders would come from the crescent now. Orders without its seal were to be ignored. In the trenches, little changed. The barrage still went over every watch and came down in no-man's land. The Archflamen's soldiers still fought for every metre. But far behind the front, the Third was being made ready for a different war.

LANDING SHELF KAPPA. ROTATION FIFTEEN, CYCLE 241

Grace came down the cable to Landing Shelf Kappa in a supply car, with her contingent and the Hounds and forty tonnes of shells. The car took nine minutes to come down. She kept her eyes shut for all nine of them.
From Kappa, the cone of the Acropolis stood twenty kilometres off, rising into the dark under the ceiling with the Echo Mast on its summit and the red river cloud behind it. Between the shelf and the cone the trenches of fifteen rotations crossed the ash from one horizon to the other. A signals officer of the Third met the Hunt on the shelf with the compliments of Colonel Kastellan of the Thirty-Fifth, and with a request from Commander Thaleia of Lethe Hold. Signals for the two commanders had already begun going astray, and Thaleia asked that the Hunt's traffic carry the Lieutenant's full name and seal to tell them apart. Grace agreed.
