## Chapter 6: K-19

*"Struck nineteen times by the Phaethons without being cracked. Anchors eleven kilometres of the outer belt. Recommend it be left alone."*

*Survey note on Redoubt K-19, 2nd Line Phalanx intelligence section, Rotation Two*

---

**THE FORWARD LINE OF THE 44TH TRENCH REGIMENT, NORTHERN FLATS. ROTATION TWO, CYCLE 117**

The rat was the size of a hound, and it was eating Trooper Hollis's breakfast, and Ilsa Marrow had to admire its nerve.

"Don't move," she said quietly.

Hollis didn't move. He was standing on the fire-step of Bay 14 with his rifle in one hand and his mess tin in the other, and the rat had come up over the back of the parados while he was looking out through the periscope and was now sitting on the duckboards at his feet with its front paws in his tin, eating his ration-paste with long, deliberate, slurping licks. It was grey-black and hairless along its back and tail, and its hide was mottled pink and red where the heat had cooked it, and it smelled of the Phlegethon, sulphur and something rotten underneath, and its eyes were small and black and entirely unafraid.

"Centurion," Hollis said, without moving his lips. "It's eating my breakfast."

"I can see that."

"Can I shoot it?"

"If you shoot it in the bay, the round goes through it and through the duckboards and into the dugout, where the Second Section is sleeping, and I'll have to fill in a transfer form for whichever one of them you hit." She drew her trench knife. "Hold still."

She went down the bay toward it, slowly, the knife low. The rat looked up from the mess tin at her and showed its teeth, which were yellow and long and far too numerous, and made a low wet hissing sound in its throat. Then it went back to eating.

She killed it with the knife, a single quick thrust down through the back of the neck that she'd learned from a sergeant on Tamaris, who'd learned it on rats much smaller than this, and it thrashed for a while on the duckboards and then lay still. Hollis looked down at it, and then at his tin, which was empty.

"Fuck's sake," he said.

"Get another ration from the dugout. And throw that thing over the parapet." Marrow wiped the knife on the rat's hide. "If the cooks see it, they'll try to put it in the stew."

"They already do, Centurion. That's what the stew is."

He threw it over the parapet. It landed on the ash a few metres out, in front of the wire, and within the hour there were three more rats eating it.

---

The rats had come up the Phlegethon in the second month of the siege, with the heat, and nobody knew where they'd come from before that. The Third's quartermasters called them Phlegethon rats, and so did everyone else. They fed on the dead in no-man's land and on the rations in the dugouts, and they fed on the living if the living were wounded and couldn't move, and in the six months since the first assault on the Drum they had become as much a part of the line as the ash and the seep and the shells. Nobody shot them anymore. There were too many, and the rounds were needed for other things.

In six months the line hadn't moved, only dug itself deeper.

Along the whole of the arc, from the Mora Cut to the Demus Gorge, both armies had gone into the ground, and over the six months a maze of trenches had spread across the Cinder Flats in a band that grew from three kilometres deep to eleven. For months at a time nothing moved in it. The Third sat in its trenches, and the Archflamen's thralls sat in theirs, and the guns fired at each other over their heads, and the Phlegethon rats went back and forth between the two lines eating what they found.

It looked as if nothing was happening, and that was false. Every regiment took its turn at patrolling and raiding, and at night the ground between the lines was full of people. Patrols went out from the Third's line every night to lift the enemy's mines and lay their own, or to find gaps in the wire for the raiders. Raids went out to take prisoners twice a week from every regiment on the line, a section or a maniple at a time, with blackened faces and trench knives and clubs, and came back with one or two thralls, or none, or fewer soldiers than had gone. And the Archflamen's raiders came the other way. They came most often from the Sworn of Orrhae, with the ash-mark on their foreheads, and they came silently, over the parapet in the dark, and they took the heads of the sentries they killed.

The *44th* had lost eleven sentries to them since the Drum. They'd all come back from the vats, eventually. Their heads hadn't. The Sworn took them back across no-man's land and put them up on stakes along the parapet of the outer belt, facing the Third's line, and they were still there, looking out over the ash, eleven of them in a row opposite the *44th*'s front, slowly drying in the heat.

Without any regiment making an attack, the Third lost an average of two thousand three hundred transfers a cycle along the arc. Marrow had seen the number on a staff summary. It had been planned for. The vats at Watchpoint Hades ran without pause, and the cables brought replacements down every cycle, a third of them soldiers coming back from their own transfers, and the line stayed at strength.

That was the deal, still. The ground kept whatever you gave it. You gave it your body, and got a new one, and went back.

---

She went down the bays at the end of the third watch, as she always did, with Brandt behind her.

Bay 14, Hollis with his new breakfast, eating it fast in case something else came for it. Bay 15, two troopers asleep on the fire-step with their rifles across their knees, which she let pass because it was the end of the watch and they'd been on the wire all night. Bay 16, a trooper she didn't know, new from the vats, sitting on the fire-step staring at nothing with his hands shaking.

She stopped beside him.

"Name?"

He looked up. It took a moment. He was older than most of them, thirty perhaps, with a grey stubble on his jaw and pale, washed-out eyes.

"Cresk, Centurion. Trooper. *2nd* Maniple."

"How many?"

He knew what she meant. Everybody on the line knew what that question meant.

"Forty-three," he said.

She looked at his hands. They were shaking, steadily, all the time, a fine tremor that ran from his fingers up into his wrists, and he wasn't trying to stop it.

"Old Ladder," Brandt said quietly, behind her.

Marrow nodded. She'd seen them before. Everybody on the line had. The vats restored a soldier's body perfectly every time, and left him everything he remembered of his death. Most soldiers came back from a first transfer fit for duty, and a second, and a third. By the fourth or fifth, some of them came back slowed. Some came back with tremors. Some came back unable to sleep without a light, or unable to sleep at all, or waking every night at the same hour screaming the same thing. By forty, most of them were like Cresk. The line called them Old Ladders. Forty rungs, the joke went. Forty times up the ladder to the ceiling and back down again.

"How long have you been in the *6th*?" she asked.

"Eleven years, Centurion. Since before you came." He looked down at his hands as if he'd only just noticed them. "I was in the *4th* Maniple when we went up the Mora Cut in the Hundred-and-Twelfth Reformation. I was killed nine times that rotation. Nine. One after the other. The river kept coming up at us and coming up and we kept dying." He paused. "I don't remember most of it. I remember the river."

"All right," Marrow said. She put a hand on his shoulder for a moment. "You're off the fire-step, Cresk. Wire team, from tonight. Brandt'll sort it."

"Centurion."

They walked on. When they were out of earshot, Brandt said, "That's the fourth Old Ladder you've put on the wire this month."

"They're steady on the wire. You've seen them. Nothing frightens them out there anymore. They're the best wiremen we've got."

"I know. I'm saying it's the fourth this month." He was quiet for a while. "There's more of them coming back every rotation, Centurion. You've seen the returns. The medici have been sending it up the line since the Drum. Every transfer, a few more of them come back like Cresk."

"I know."

"Command's taken note of it, they say."

"I know that too." Marrow stopped at the end of Bay 20 and looked out through the periscope at the outer belt, at the eleven heads on their stakes, at the long black line of the parapet and the nests behind it. "Taken note of it. That means nobody up there's going to do a fucking thing about it, Saul. Not till it gets worse."

---

The night before the order came, the *44th*'s relay pylon went down.

She was in her dugout, asleep, when it happened. The shell came in at the third bell, a heavy keyed round from somewhere on the second belt, and it came down squarely on the pylon behind the *44th*'s support line, a tall black spire of steel humming in the heat that served four kilometres of the regiment's front. It took the pylon down in a single long shriek of tearing metal that Marrow heard in her sleep and woke to, and for the rest of the night the *44th* uploaded through the pylons of its neighbours, at longer range, through more of the Phlegethon's interference.

The signallers had a new pylon up by the end of the next watch. They always did. They'd replaced forty in a single cycle once, the signallers said, on the southern Flats, when the Archflamen's gunners had found the range of a whole stretch of the line at once. The pylons were the Third's most important targets after its guns, and the Archflamen's gunners had learned to find them early, and the Third's signallers had learned to put them back up faster than the gunners could knock them down.

Brandt brought her the figures that morning, from the regiment's signals office, because she'd asked for them.

"Four transfers in the *44th* last night while the pylon was down," he said. "One null."

"Who?"

"Trooper Allis. *5th* Maniple. Shell in the support trench."

Marrow took the slate and looked at it. The null was there at the bottom, in amber, in the Répertoire's unhurried script. *ALLIS, DEVRA. TROOPER. TRANSFER: NULL.*

"The signals officer says it's the pylon," Brandt said. "The nulls cluster where the pylons are down. They always do. The uploads have to go further, through more of the river. The technical office has asked for more pylons."

"Have they got them?"

"The Command approved it last rotation. The Quiet Ones laid the cable." Brandt shrugged. "The null rate didn't fall."

**HEADQUARTERS OF THE 2ND LINE PHALANX, NORTHERN FLATS. ROTATION TWO, CYCLE 300**

Phalanx-General Oren Callix counted his nulls personally at the end of every rotation, and he had been doing it for twenty-two years, and he had never once found the number going down.

He did it alone, in his quarters, at the end of the last watch of the last cycle, with a lamp and a slate and a pot of the black Flats coffee the cooks of the *2nd* Line Phalanx made by boiling the ash out of the water and then boiling the water again. He went through the Répertoire's transfer list for every regiment of the *2nd* one name at a time, and when he came to a null, he wrote the name down in a book. It was a long book now. It was the fifth he had filled.

He was sixty-one. He'd been born on a ceiling world called Varrow's Rest, a mining world, and drafted at thirteen into a Trench Regiment with a pick and a rifle and a body that had never had enough to eat, and he'd risen through the ranks of the Third by being better at trench work than anybody around him, for forty-eight years, until he was the senior Phalanx-General of the army, which no mortal-line officer had been in two hundred years. He wasn't an Athenian. He'd never been near the Academy on Callistus. He'd read the Tactica Olympica on his own, at night, by a trench lamp, as he had read everything, and he had come away from it with a firm belief that the people who wrote it had never been under a shell.

He went down the list for Rotation Two. There were a great many more names than there had been in Rotation One.

When he had finished, he sat back and drank his coffee, which was cold, and looked at the page.

It was the three hundredth cycle. Six months since the Drum. In those six months the *2nd* Line Phalanx hadn't made a single attack. It had sat in its trenches on the northern Flats and been shelled and raided, and patrolled and raided back, and taken its two thousand three hundred transfers a cycle along with the rest of the arc, and the null rate on its stretch of the line had gone from a tenth of a percent at the Drum to half of one percent.

Half of one percent of a lot of transfers was a lot of people.

He looked at the order on his desk. It had come down from Quarterdeck Nineteen at the start of the watch, from the Répertoire, countersigned by Castorre, and it ordered the *2nd* Line Phalanx to make a probing attack along its entire front on the three hundred and second cycle.

He'd read it twice already. It didn't take long. The Répertoire's orders never did. It ordered each regiment of the *2nd* to select a few points on its front and send its breachers against them under heavy bombardment and smoke. It gave the attack a cost, eleven thousand transfers, which was cheap by the standard of the Drum. It didn't give the attack an objective.

Callix knew what the order was for. He'd seen enough of them. The Third's engineers were about to begin a new sap in the north, toward the end of the outer belt along the Mora Cut, and they needed the Archflamen's thralls kept in their shelters while they did it, and a probing attack along the whole of the *2nd*'s front would do that very nicely. Eleven thousand transfers to buy the engineers a cycle of quiet. And half of one percent of eleven thousand was fifty-five.

He didn't expect anything of it. Neither, he suspected, did the Répertoire.

He picked up his stylus and wrote his orders to the regiments of the *2nd*, and signed them, and sent them down the line. Then he picked up the book again and turned to a fresh page and wrote at the top of it, *Rotation Two, cycle 302. Probing attack.* He'd fill the page in on the three hundred and fifth.

**THE FORWARD TRENCH OF THE 44TH. ROTATION TWO, CYCLE 302, SECOND BELL**

The *44th* chose K-19, and Marrow wanted to know which bastard at regiment had done it.

"Because they hate us," Brandt said. "Obviously."

"Because they hate us," Marrow agreed.

She was standing in the forward trench of the *44th* with her helmet off and her optio beside her and the six maniple leaders of the *6th* Cohort crowded into the fire bay around them, looking at the map on her slate. The order had come down from regiment at the end of the last watch. The *2nd* Line Phalanx would make a probing attack along its entire front. Each regiment would select a few points. The *44th* had selected three, and two of them were small nests on the southern end of its front that would be attacked by the *2nd* and *8th* Cohorts. The third was K-19.

Everyone on the northern Flats knew K-19.

It was a resistance nest four kilometres north of the Drum, where the outer belt went down into a slag gully and climbed out the other side, and it had been built of the Arsenal's best concrete, poured six metres thick over a gun deck cut into the basalt of the gully wall. It looked out over the gully and the ground on both sides of it, and it anchored eleven kilometres of the outer belt, and the Phaethons of the *10th* had struck it nineteen times since the siege began without cracking it. The intelligence section of the *2nd* had surveyed it in the first rotation and recommended that it be left alone.

"Nineteen times," said Dalla Venn of the *3rd* Maniple, a hard, dark, sharp-faced woman who had been a section leader in the Lethe holds before she was posted to the *44th*. "The Phaethons. Nineteen. And they want us to knock on the front door."

"They want us to probe it," Marrow said. "It's a probing attack. Nobody expects us to take it. We go forward under the smoke, we get into the wire, we see what's there, we come back. That's all." She looked around at them. "That's what the order says."

"And what do you say, Centurion?"

Marrow looked down at the map again, at the gully, and the long black shape of K-19 on the far wall of it, and the forward trench of the outer belt running along the foot of the wall below it.

"I say the order's got smoke in it," she said. "I say there'll be a lot of it, and the bombardment will put it down on the gully at the start of the second watch, and that gully runs right up to the forward trench under the redoubt. I say anybody in the redoubt is going to be looking out over the smoke at the ground, not down into it." She tapped the gully. "And I say if we go down the gully at a run, in the smoke, and not at the fucking walk, we might be in the wire before anybody up there knows we're coming."

They looked at her.

"At a run, Centurion?" Venn said.

"At a run. The manual says walk. The manual got the *37th* killed on the Drum." She put her helmet back on. "Brief your maniples. Rifles slung, grenades, entrenching tools, bayonets. We're not going to stand off and shoot. We're going in."

---

The bombardment came down at the start of the second watch, and so did the smoke.

It was a heavy bombardment, though nothing would ever be the Drum again, and the *2nd* Ordnance Phalanx had lent the *44th* three batteries for the cycle, and the regiment's own mortars had been stockpiling for a week, and for an hour the shells came down on the outer belt from one end of the *44th*'s front to the other, and then the smoke-shells came down on the gully below K-19 and filled it from wall to wall with a thick grey-white fog that rolled slowly up the slope toward the redoubt and lay over the forward trench of the outer belt like a blanket.

Marrow went over the parapet with the first maniple.

She went over at a run, and the *6th* Cohort went over behind her, six hundred and forty soldiers coming up out of the forward trench of the *44th* at a run with their rifles slung and their bayonets fixed and their entrenching tools in their hands, and they ran down the slope of the near side of the gully into the smoke, and the smoke took them.

She couldn't see her hand in front of her face. She couldn't see Brandt, a metre behind her. She could hear him, and the cohort around her, boots in the ash and the rattle of kit and somebody swearing steadily under his breath, and she could hear the shells going over and bursting on the outer belt ahead, and she could hear the guns of K-19 open up somewhere above and ahead of her, firing out over the smoke at the ground they could see, at the edges of the gully, at nothing.

She ran for nine minutes, down into the bottom of the gully and across it and up the far side, with the ash sliding under her boots and the smoke burning her eyes and her lungs, by the compass on her wrist and the slope of the ground under her feet. At the end of the nine minutes she ran into the wire.

It was there before she saw it. She was running, and then she was caught, the razor-wire wrapping around her legs and her arms and her chest in a great tangled coil that hadn't been thinned by the bombardment at all, and she went down into it and felt it cut through her trousers and into the skin of her thighs and her hands, and she was shouting, "Wire! Wire! Cutters forward! Cutters!"

The cutters came forward. They came up through the smoke on their bellies with their long-handled shears and began to cut, and the cohort came up behind them and went down into the wire alongside her, and for a few minutes, a few long terrible minutes, the *6th* Cohort lay in the wire under K-19 cutting itself free while the guns of the redoubt fired out over their heads into the smoke and didn't see them.

Then the Archflamen's gunners on the second belt began to fire into the gully.

They were firing blind. They couldn't see into the smoke any better than the redoubt could. They knew the Third was attacking somewhere along the outer belt, and they knew there was smoke in the gully below K-19, and they had orders to fire on any smoke, and so they did. Their shells came down into the gully in their long careful patterns, from the Third's wire to the outer belt and back, and they came down on the *6th* Cohort lying in the wire, and on the slope behind them, and on the forward trench of the outer belt in front of them, without distinction.

The *2nd* Maniple was caught in the wire when the shells came down. Marrow heard it. She heard the shells walk across them, one after another, close, and she heard the *2nd* Maniple scream.

"Through!" she was shouting. "Through the wire! Through it! Go, go, go, through it, into the trench, now!"

She went through it. She tore herself through the last of the wire with her hands, and felt it open her palms to the bone, and fell down the far side of it into a trench.

---

The forward trench under K-19 was the Arsenal's work, and it was better than anything the Third had dug on the Flats in six hundred years. It was two and a half metres deep and revetted in steel, with fire bays cut every six metres and duckboards that were dry, and it had been shelled for an hour and was almost intact. There were thralls in it.

There were four of them in the bay she fell into, crouched against the front wall with their rifles, looking out over the parapet into the smoke, and they didn't see her come down behind them. She came up off the duckboards with her entrenching tool in her hand and hit the nearest of them across the back of the neck with the blade of it, and he went down without a sound, and the second turned toward her with his rifle coming up and Brandt came down off the parapet behind her and shot him in the face.

The other two ran. They ran down the trench toward the traverse at the end of the bay, and the *6th* Cohort came down into the trench on top of them and killed them before they reached it.

After that, it was the bays.

The *6th* Cohort went down the forward trench of the outer belt under K-19 bay by bay, in the smoke, with grenades and bayonets and entrenching tools, and the thralls in the bays fought them for every one. They fought hard. They were forge-workers who had first held a rifle the year before, and they fought in the bays with their rifles and their knives and their hands, and they had no implants, and they knew it, and they died where they stood. Marrow lost twenty soldiers in the first ten bays. She killed six thralls herself, she found out later. She didn't remember any of them.

At the end of the fourteenth bay she found the shelter.

It was an entrance in the back wall of the trench, a low doorway of poured concrete with a steel door standing half-open and a stair going down behind it into the dark. Above it, the wall of the trench went up into the side of the gully, and above that, she knew, was the gun deck of K-19. The shelter went down under the redoubt.

"Venn," she said. "Grenades."

Venn came up with her maniple. They put four grenades down the stair, one after another, and waited for them to go, and then went down after them into the dark.

---

The shelter was empty. Whatever had been in it had gone deeper, or up into the redoubt, or out the other side. It was a long low chamber cut into the basalt, with bunks along the walls and a water tank and a stove, and at the far end of it a second stair going down further, and a narrow passage leading off to the north, into the rock.

Marrow stood in the middle of it, breathing hard, with her entrenching tool in one hand and her rifle in the other and her palms bleeding down onto the floor, and looked around.

"Centurion," Brandt said. "We're in."

"We're in." She looked at the stair going down, and the passage going north. "Saul, how many have we got?"

He went back up the stair to count. It took him a while.

"Four hundred and eleven," he said, when he came back. "In the trench and the shelter. The *2nd* Maniple's gone. Lost it in the wire. Most of the rest we lost in the bays." He paused. "Four hundred and eleven, Centurion. Out of six hundred and forty."

"And the relay?"

"Nothing. The jammers have been on the Second's bands since the bombardment started. Can't raise regiment, can't raise anybody."

Marrow thought about it. She was standing in a shelter under K-19, the redoubt the Phaethons had struck nineteen times without cracking, which anchored eleven kilometres of the outer belt, with four hundred and eleven soldiers of the *6th* Cohort, and nobody in the whole of the *3rd* Siege Army knew she was here.

"Paper," she said.

Brandt gave her a field-message pad. She wrote on it, kneeling on the floor of the shelter with the slate on her knee and her bleeding hand smearing the paper.

*From Marrow, 6th Cohort, 44th. Hold forward trench under K-19, bays 1 to 14, and entrance to personnel shelter under redoubt. 411 effective. Need breachers and engineers. Need them now. Relay jammed.*

She tore it off and folded it and looked around the shelter for a runner. They were all looking back at her. A young trooper of the *1st* Maniple named Tamsin Ord, a girl of eighteen who'd come down the Stair with the *44th* and been killed once, on the Drum, stepped forward.

"Centurion. I'll go."

Marrow gave her the message.

"Eleven hundred metres back to our line," she said. "Across the gully. The smoke'll be gone by the time you're halfway. Keep low, keep moving, don't stop for anything. If you get hit, keep crawling. If you get killed..." She stopped.

"If I get killed, I'll be back in a fortnight, Centurion," Ord said, "and you'll be dead, because nobody will have come."

"That's about the size of it."

Ord went up the stair.

**THE CAT'S CRADLE, THE GULLY UNDER K-19. ROTATION TWO, CYCLE 302**

Tamsin Ord took four hours to cross eleven hundred metres of ash, and she was wounded twice on the way.

She went up out of the trench and through the wire where the *6th* Cohort had cut it, crawling, and down into the gully in the last of the smoke. Halfway across the bottom of the gully the smoke thinned and blew away, and the guns of K-19 saw the gully for the first time since the bombardment started, and saw her.

She went to ground in a shell crater. They fired at her for an hour. They fired at the crater with the heavy guns of the redoubt, and with rifles from the outer belt, and the Archflamen's gunners on the second belt joined in, and she lay at the bottom of the crater on her face with her hands over her head and the message folded in the breast pocket of her plate, and listened to the rounds hitting the lip of the crater above her, and waited for them to stop.

When they stopped, she crawled on.

The first round hit her an hour later, in the left leg, above the knee, while she was crawling between two craters on the near slope of the gully. It went through the meat of her thigh and out the other side, and she lay in the ash for a while biting her own hand until she could move again, and then she tied her belt around her leg above the wound, and crawled on.

The second round hit her in the third hour, in the back, as she crawled up the last of the slope toward the *44th*'s wire. It went in under her shoulder blade and stayed there, and it knocked her flat, and after it she couldn't breathe properly and there was blood in her mouth. She crawled on. She didn't remember much of the last part. She remembered the wire, and a voice shouting a challenge at her from the parapet, and herself trying to shout back the reply and being unable to, and then hands pulling her down into the trench.

She remembered giving the message to somebody. She didn't know who.

She died in the regimental aid post at the end of the fifth hour, and woke up nine days later in a vat at the Watchpoint, and was back in the *44th* within the month, and when she came down the Stair, she found out that somebody had come.

**HEADQUARTERS OF THE 2ND LINE PHALANX. ROTATION TWO, CYCLE 302, NINTH WATCH**

The message reached the *44th*'s command post at the eighth hour of the watch, and Phalanx-General Callix at the start of the ninth.

He read it standing at the map table in the operations dugout of the *2nd* Line Phalanx, with his staff around him and the reports of the probing attack coming in on the panes along the walls. They were what he had expected. The *8th* Cohort of the *44th* had gone forward against a small nest at the south end of the regiment's front and been stopped in the wire. The *2nd* Cohort had got into the forward trench of the outer belt for an hour and been shelled out of it. All along the *2nd*'s front it was the same story, regiment after regiment, cohort after cohort: forward under the smoke, into the wire, stopped, shelled, back. The engineers on the Mora Cut had begun their sap, unseen, as the Répertoire had planned. The attack had done what it was for.

And in the middle of the reports was a sheet of field-message paper with blood on it.

*From Marrow, 6th Cohort, 44th. Hold forward trench under K-19, bays 1 to 14, and entrance to personnel shelter under redoubt. 411 effective. Need breachers and engineers. Need them now. Relay jammed.*

He read it three times. Then he went to the overlay and looked at K-19.

"Get me the colonel of the *44th*," he said.

He got the colonel of the *44th*, on the relay, which was working again on the regimental bands although the jammers were still on the forward ones. The colonel was a nervous man named Durrant who had been in command of the *44th* for two years and had never been in a battle. He confirmed the message. He confirmed that the runner had come in through the regiment's wire at the eighth hour, wounded twice, and had died in the aid post. He confirmed that nobody at the regiment had been able to raise the *6th* Cohort since.

"What's Marrow like?" Callix asked.

"Sir?"

"Centurion Marrow. What's she like? Is she the sort of officer who'd send that message if she didn't have the trench?"

There was a pause.

"No, sir," Durrant said. "She's the sort who'd have the trench and not bother to send the message."

Callix looked at the overlay for a long time. K-19 sat on the far wall of the gully in a black square, and around it the outer belt ran north and south, eleven kilometres of it, anchored on the redoubt. The Phaethons had struck it nineteen times without cracking it. His own intelligence section had recommended it be left alone.

And a cohort of the *44th* was sitting under it, in its own forward trench, at the head of its own shelter.

"Colonel," he said. "Pull every heavy maniple in the *44th* off the line and get them into your forward trench by the end of the watch. And the engineers. All of them." He turned to his chief of staff. "Signal the *41st* and the *46th*. I want every breacher they can spare. And get me Quarterdeck Nineteen. I want to talk to the Lord-General about the *4th* Assault Phalanx."

"Sir," his chief of staff said carefully. "This is a probing attack. The Répertoire's order..."

"The Répertoire's order was written to keep the thralls in their shelters while the engineers cut a sap," Callix said. "Marrow has just put four hundred soldiers into one of the shelters. I think the thralls are going to be very busy."

---

**BENEATH K-19. ROTATION TWO, CYCLES 302 TO 303**

The Archflamen's gunners found the trench at the eleventh hour. They had orders to shell any part of the outer belt taken by the Third, with their own soldiers in it if need be, and they had found out at last which part had been taken.

They shelled it for nine hours.

Marrow took the cohort down into the shelter before the second salvo landed. There wasn't room for all of them, and so she took the rest down the second stair at the far end of the shelter, into the dark, and down the second stair the *6th* Cohort found the galleries.

They were cut into the basalt under K-19, a warren of narrow passages a metre wide and less than two high, lit by tin lamps hanging from hooks in the rock, running away from the shelter in every direction. Some of them went up toward the gun decks of the redoubt. Some of them went north and south, along the line of the outer belt, toward the next nests. And all of them were full of thralls.

It was the worst fighting of her life. She would think so afterward, through everything that came later, the inner belt and the drowned and the Wall. It was the worst because it was the closest. The passages were too narrow for two soldiers to stand abreast, and too low to stand upright in, and the lamps were few, so that most of the fighting happened in the dark, or the half-dark, at a range of a metre or less. The thralls came down the passages at them out of the dark with knives and bars and rifles they couldn't swing, and the *6th* Cohort met them with grenades and bayonets and entrenching tools, and in the first passage Marrow killed a thrall by putting her bayonet through his throat while he was trying to strangle Venn, and then she had to pull it out to use it again, and his blood went into her eyes.

They fought for every metre. They fought for every junction. At the end of the third hour the *6th* Cohort held the first gallery under K-19, a long passage running east from the foot of the shelter stair, and the junction where it met a second gallery running north, and they held it with grenades thrown around the corner and bayonets in the dark, and they held it for nine hours while the Archflamen's shells came down on the trench overhead and shook the rock around them and brought the dust down from the roof, and the thralls came at them again and again out of the dark.

Venn died in the fifth hour, at the junction. Brandt in the seventh, in the gallery, going back for a wounded trooper. Marrow didn't see either of them go. She heard about them afterward. She was at the junction for most of the nine hours, with her back to the wall and her rifle and her entrenching tool and a sack of grenades, and she was killed twice that day in her imagination for every hour she wasn't.

And then, at the start of the second cycle, the thralls stopped coming.

---

**THE GULLY UNDER K-19. ROTATION TWO, CYCLES 303 TO 304**

Callix committed his breachers at the first bell of the second cycle, the heavy maniples of the *44th* together with every breacher the *41st* and the *46th* could spare, and they worked their way up the gully toward K-19 trench by trench against the redoubt's guns.

He watched it from the *44th*'s forward observation post through a periscope. It was slow, bloody, careful work. The breachers came up the gully in their plate in short rushes from crater to crater, with the engineers behind them, and the redoubt's heavy guns knocked them down one at a time, and they got up again, or they didn't. At the end of the cycle they held the near slope of the gully and the bottom of it, and the redoubt's guns still held the far slope, and between them was the anti-tank ditch at the foot of the outer belt, eight metres wide and five deep, and Marrow and her cohort were somewhere under the far side of it.

At the end of the cycle the Lord-General answered.

"You'll have the *4th* Assault Phalanx," Castorre said on the relay. His voice was soft and precise and tired. "Three companies of the *16th* Armoured. Forty-two Thureos. And the *Lady Hestia*, if you can get her over the ditch."

"I'll get her over the ditch, sir."

"Callix." A pause. "The Répertoire has priced this. It wants to know why a probing attack has become an assault on a redoubt it recommended be left alone, and it wants to know what you expect to gain."

"Eleven kilometres of the outer belt, sir. The anchor of it."

"It's not in the plan."

"No, sir," Callix said. "Neither was Marrow."

There was a longer pause.

"You'll have them by the third cycle," the Lord-General said, and the relay went dead.

---

They came up on the third cycle.

The *4th* Assault Phalanx came forward from its revetments behind the *44th*'s line in a long column across the ash, the Thureos tanks of the *16th* Armoured Regiment in the van, squat grey shapes with the Third's crest on their glacis and their main guns traversed forward, forty-two of them, and the Hoplon assault carriers behind them, and behind the Hoplons, eleven Mattock engineering crawlers with their dozer blades lowered. And in the middle of the column, taller than everything around her, came the *Lady Hestia*.

She was a Colossus super-heavy. She'd come down the Cinder Stair on rail in Rotation Zero, on a flatbed of the Quiet Ones' track, in three pieces, and been assembled at the foot of the Stair by the engineers of the *4th* Assault Phalanx over a month, and since then she had sat in a revetment for nine months waiting for a target worthy of her. She was forty metres long and eleven high. She had a main gun the size of a Phaethon tube, and four secondary turrets, and armour so thick that the Archflamen's divine shells had burst on her glacis in Rotation One during a counter-battery exchange and left nothing but scorch marks. Her crew called her the Old Lady. Callix had served beside her on the Mora Cut in the Hundred-and-Twelfth Reformation, and seen her put a shell through the skull of a thing out of the river the size of a hold, and he was glad to see her come.

The Mattocks went to the ditch first.

They had to. The anti-tank ditch at the foot of the outer belt below K-19 was eight metres wide and five deep, and no tank could cross it, not even the *Lady Hestia*. The Mattocks went forward to it under the fire of the redoubt, eleven broad-tracked machines with dozer blades the width of a road, and began to fill it, pushing the slag of the redoubt's own glacis down into it, blade-load after blade-load, under the guns.

The redoubt's heavy guns knocked out four of them in the ditch. The first went in the first hour, a heavy round through its cab that killed its crew and left it burning on the lip of the ditch with its blade still lowered. The second went in the third hour. The third and fourth went together in the sixth, when the Archflamen's gunners on the second belt found them and walked a pattern of forge-fire across the ditch that set the slag alight. The other seven kept going. They filled the ditch in eight hours, pushing their own burning dead in with the slag when they had to, and at the end of the eighth hour the commander of the last of them came up on the relay and reported that the ditch was filled and could be crossed, and Callix sent the tanks.

They crossed behind the Mattocks, two at a time, over the filled ditch, under fire. Two of them threw tracks on the slag and stopped. The rest got across, and spread out at the foot of the far slope below K-19, and began to fire into the embrasures of the redoubt.

Then the *Lady Hestia* came over.

She came over slowly. She was too big to come over any other way. She ground forward across the filled ditch on her vast tracks with the slag crumbling and smoking under her, and the redoubt's guns hit her eleven times while she crossed, and Callix watched the shells burst on her glacis and her turrets in bursts of gold and red and orange and leave nothing but scorch marks. On the far side, at the foot of the slope, she stopped. Her main gun traversed slowly round and up, toward the long black shape of K-19 on the wall of the gully four hundred metres above her.

She fired once, and the shell went into the forward embrasure of K-19, through the main gun deck, and out through the rear wall of the redoubt in a gout of black smoke and flame that went up into the dark over the gully a hundred metres high. Nineteen strikes by the Phaethons hadn't cracked it. The *Lady Hestia* had put a hole through it from front to back, at four hundred metres, with a single round.

The guns of K-19 stopped firing.

"Breachers," Callix said into the relay. "Go."

---

**BENEATH K-19. ROTATION TWO, CYCLE 304**

Underground, in the galleries, Marrow didn't see any of it.

She felt the *Lady Hestia*'s round go through the redoubt above her as a single enormous blow that shook the whole of the rock around the junction, harder than any shell in nine hours, and brought down a section of the gallery roof thirty metres to the east in a roar of falling basalt, and for a long while after it she couldn't hear anything at all. She found out what it had been afterward.

It hadn't been quiet in the galleries before that. The thralls had stopped coming at the junction at the start of the second cycle, and for a few hours she thought they had given up. They hadn't. They had gone back to the heat-wells.

They came back in the third cycle with flame lances.

The lances were tools. They'd been made for the casting lines, to clear slag from the heat-wells with jets of river-fire drawn up through a hose from a tank on the thrall's back, and they had never been meant for war. In the galleries under K-19, in passages a metre wide, they were the most terrible weapon Marrow had ever seen. The thralls came down the gallery from the north in the dark with the lances held out in front of them, and the river-fire went down the passage ahead of them in long roaring jets of orange flame that filled the gallery from wall to wall, and there was nowhere to go. Forty soldiers of the *6th* Cohort died in the first hour of it. They died in the passage, burning. They died at the junction. Marrow saw two of them run back past her down the first gallery with their plate glowing red and their hair on fire, screaming, and fall, and burn.

She held the junction anyway. She held it with grenades, rolled around the corner into the north gallery, one after another, until there were no more grenades. She held it with rifle fire into the dark at the flame. She held it with the bodies of her own soldiers, piled across the mouth of the north gallery, which the river-fire couldn't burn through. She held it for six hours, until she had eleven soldiers left and four rounds and her entrenching tool, and then she heard, above and behind her, in the shelter at the foot of the stair, a great many heavy feet.

She turned, with her rifle, toward the sound.

They were coming down the shelter stair and along the first gallery toward her in the dark, big, slow, heavy shapes, too big for thralls, filling the passage from wall to wall, with the steady grinding clank of metal on stone. She raised the rifle. The eleven around her raised theirs. Someone had told her once, early in the siege, that the Archflamen's garrison had plate too, from the vaults, the same pattern as the Third's. She thought, *the garrison's come down from the redoubt*, and her finger went to the trigger.

"*Falling*," she shouted. It was the challenge of the *44th*, the first half of the regiment's motto. Some animal part of her said it before the rest of her could fire.

There was a pause, in the dark.

"*Rising*," a voice said back, from behind a visor. "Who the fuck is that?"

Marrow lowered the rifle.

"Marrow," she said. "*6th* Cohort. Who the fuck are you?"

"Breachers. *44th*. Heavy *2nd* Maniple." The leading shape came forward into the light of the last tin lamp at the junction, and it was a breacher, in two hundred kilograms of the Third's plate, black with smoke and running with someone else's blood, with a heavy rifle in one hand and an entrenching blade in the other, and it pushed its visor up. Under it was a broad, tired, astonished face. "Centurion, we came down the main shaft from the gun decks. We've been fighting our way down through the redoubt for three hours. We nearly fucking shot you."

"I nearly fucking shot you," Marrow said.

The breacher looked past her at the junction, and at the bodies piled across the mouth of the north gallery, and at the eleven soldiers of the *6th* Cohort standing around her with their rifles and their four rounds.

"Is this all of you?" he asked quietly.

"This is all of us." She leaned back against the wall. Her legs weren't working properly anymore. "The rest went up."

---

They cleared the galleries passage by passage in the next six hours, the breachers going ahead with their heavy rifles and their plate and the flame lances spending themselves on the plate in roaring sheets of fire that didn't burn through it, and by the end of the cycle there was nothing alive under K-19 but the Third.

Marrow didn't go with them. She sat at the junction with her back to the wall and her rifle across her knees and let a medicus of the breachers bind her hands, which had been cut to the bone on the wire and had been bleeding for three cycles, and she let him give her water, and she let him go away again. Then the breachers' centurion came down the first gallery to find her, a tall heavy man named Caddock in his plate with his visor up, and squatted down in front of her.

"Centurion Marrow," he said. "The Phalanx-General's compliments, and he'd like to know how you are."

"Tell the Phalanx-General I'd like a bath."

Caddock laughed. Then he stopped laughing.

"I have to make a report," he said. "Anything I should put in it? Anything I need to know?"

Marrow thought about it. She thought about the galleries, and the flame lances, and the junction. She thought about the passages running north and south along the line of the outer belt, toward the other nests.

"There's a gallery," she said. "Under the second gun deck. It goes east. We found the mouth of it in the second cycle, when we were pushing up toward the decks. It's bigger than the rest. Taller. Cut better. It goes east, away from the outer belt, toward the spur." She looked up at him. "We followed it for about two hundred metres before the flame lances came. It just kept going. Straight, east, sloping down. I've seen the Arsenal's plans of the outer belt, Centurion. The Third's had them since the beginning. That gallery's not on them."

Caddock looked at her for a long moment.

"East," he said. "Toward the spur."

"Toward the spur."

He wrote it down.

---

**THE OUTER BELT. ROTATION TWO, CYCLES 305 TO 381**

With more soldiers and tanks pushed into the breach at K-19, the Archflamen's army couldn't hold the outer belt any longer, and Callix had known it would be so from the moment the *Lady Hestia* fired.

The outer belt had been built around its nests. Each nest covered the ground in front of its neighbours, and the trenches ran between them, and an attacker coming at the belt from the front would be under the fire of two nests at any point. It had never been built to be attacked from the inside. With its anchor taken, the Third's breachers could work along its trenches from K-19 north and south, taking each nest in turn from the rear, where its guns didn't bear.

Ostorian saw it too. Callix watched him see it, through the reports. Within a cycle the Archflamen's Prefect was sending his reserves north to stop the breachers, drawing them out of their shelters on the Drum and in the Lethe Cut sector and marching them up the inside of the outer belt toward K-19, and the sectors they left behind were thinly held. The *4th* Assault Phalanx went through those sectors.

Over the next seventy-five cycles, the *4th* rolled up the outer belt from K-19 to the Lethe Cut. Callix watched it from the forward observation posts of the *2nd* Line Phalanx, and later from the outer belt itself, and it was the best thing he had seen the Third do in forty-eight years. The Thureos tanks of the *16th* Armoured went along the trench lines firing down their length. The Hoplon carriers came up behind them and put breachers onto the parapets of the nests behind their ramps, fifty at a time. The Kyklops siege carriages came up last, the twelve-wheeled crawlers with their single short howitzers, and reduced the nests that held out by firing into their embrasures at fifty metres. One after another the nests fell, and the trenches between them, and the belt came apart.

The Archflamen's army began to abandon it. It did it well. Callix gave Ostorian that. It fell back in good order, the thrall cohorts covered by the garrison, toward the second belt thirty kilometres in, and the garrison brought most of its guns back with it. The Third's outrider squadrons went after them across the ash on their armoured hounds, the great grey dogs of the ceiling worlds as tall at the shoulder as a man, and caught the slower cohorts in the open, and Callix heard their reports on the relay and found that he didn't want to hear them.

Several cohorts were left behind in the nests on the Drum, cut off by the roll-up. The *7th* Line Phalanx took nine cycles to clear them. They fought to the last, every one, and none of them came back from any vat.

On the three hundred and eighty-first cycle of Rotation Two, the Répertoire logged the outer belt of the Pyriphlegethon Arsenal as reduced.

---

**HEADQUARTERS OF THE 2ND LINE PHALANX. ROTATION TWO, CYCLE 381**

At the end of the last watch of the last cycle of Rotation Two, Oren Callix sat in his quarters with a lamp and a slate and a pot of black Flats coffee, and counted his nulls.

The reduction of the outer belt had cost nine hundred and sixty thousand transfers, against the Répertoire's estimate of nine hundred thousand. It had taken eighteen months of front time, against an estimate of eight. Those figures were in the Répertoire's report, which had arrived that watch and which credited the victory to the breakthrough at K-19 and to the speed with which the *4th* Assault Phalanx had exploited it. They were reasonable figures. They were, by the standard of the Third, a success.

He went down the transfer list name by name. When he had finished, he had filled eleven pages of his book.

Four thousand one hundred and forty of the transfers of the outer belt had been null. It was nearly twenty times the rate of the first assault on the Drum.

He wrote the figure at the bottom of the last page and looked at it for a long time. Then he took a fresh sheet and wrote a letter to the Répertoire's technical office on Quarterdeck Nineteen, in his own hand, asking it to explain why. He'd written that letter at the end of every rotation of the siege so far. He'd receive the same answer as before, he knew. Jammers, and pylons, and the Phlegethon. The Command would note it.

He sealed the letter. Then he took a second sheet and wrote a shorter letter, to Archmagister Cael Varros on the same Quarterdeck, whom he had never met.

*Archmagister. I'm told you keep a file. I'd be grateful if you would add this to it. O. Callix, Phalanx-General, 2nd Line Phalanx.*

He folded his nulls book open at Rotation Two, wrapped it in oilcloth, and put it in the packet with the letter.

---

**QUARTERDECK NINETEEN. ROTATION THREE, CYCLE 4**

The Corona Muralis was awarded in the conference hall of Quarterdeck Nineteen on the fourth cycle of Rotation Three, with the Lord-General present and the staff of the Third standing along the walls and the Arsenal burning on its spur nine hundred metres below the crystal floor.

It was the first Corona Muralis of the siege. It was a small gold crown in the shape of a city wall, with battlements, on a ribbon of red and black, and it had been awarded in the Third perhaps forty times in six hundred years, to the first soldier over the wall of a fortified place. Castorre pinned it to Ilsa Marrow's dress tunic himself. He did it carefully, with his small neat hands, and when it was done he stepped back and looked at her for a moment.

"Centurion," he said.

"Lord-General."

"The Répertoire did not plan for you," he said. "I want you to know that I'm aware of it."

Marrow didn't know what to say to that. She stood at attention in her dress tunic, with her hands bandaged at her sides and the little gold crown on her chest and the staff of the *3rd* Siege Army along the walls, and said nothing at all.

Castorre nodded, as if that were an answer, and moved on.

Afterward, in the corridor outside the conference hall, a tall grey man in a Phalanx-General's coat stopped her. He had a broken-looking face and big scarred hands, and he stood in front of her in the corridor and looked at her, not at the crown, for a long moment.

"Callix," he said. "*2nd* Line Phalanx."

"Sir."

"I sent you the breachers."

"Yes, sir. Thank you, sir."

"Don't thank me. You'd taken the trench. All I did was believe you." He looked at her bandaged hands. "Your optio. Brandt. And the leader of your *3rd* Maniple. Venn."

"They came back, sir. Last rotation. They're on the line."

"I know they came back. I count them." He was quiet for a moment. "Four hundred and twenty-nine of the *6th* Cohort went up from the galleries under K-19, Centurion. Thirty-one of them didn't come back. They were null. Did you know that?"

She hadn't. She stood in the corridor and felt it go into her like a blade.

"Thirty-one," she said.

"Thirty-one." Callix reached into his coat and took out a small slip of paper, folded, and gave it to her. It was a list of names, in his own handwriting. "I thought you'd want them. Most officers don't. I thought you might."

She unfolded it. The first name on the list was Tamsin Ord.

"She came back," Marrow said. "Ord. She was my runner. She took the message. She came back, sir, I saw her, she came down the Stair with the *44th* last month..."

"She came back the first time," Callix said gently. "From the aid post. She went up again in the roll-up, on the Drum, on cycle three hundred and sixty. She didn't come back from that one."

Marrow looked at the name for a long time. The corridor was very quiet.

"What's doing it, sir?" she said at last. "The nulls. What's taking them?"

Callix looked down the corridor toward the conference hall, where the staff of the *3rd* Siege Army were filing out past the crystal floor, and the Arsenal burning beneath it.

"The Répertoire says jammers," he said. "And the river. The technical office says pylons." He turned back to her. "I don't know, Centurion. I've been counting them for twenty-two years and I don't know. But I've started to think it's the same thing that dug your gallery."
