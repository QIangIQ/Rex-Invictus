## Chapter 34: Ledger of Nemesis

*"For the ones who call up what eats the soul, only an ending is enough."*

Archon Damar Orthes of the Achillean Order

---

**THE BATTLE-CARRIER *ADRASTEIA'S LEDGER*, DESCENDING. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLE 190**

The signal went down the cables ahead of them, eleven cycles ahead. The Order of Nemesis did not arrive anywhere unannounced and did not ask anyone's leave to arrive.

CODE 78. SOCIUS VOCA. ORIGIN: RHAMNOUS PRIMARIS, COHORS NEMESIS PRIMA. DESTINATION: THIRD SIEGE ARMY, ACROPOLIS FRONT. CONTENT: COHORS NEMESIS SECUNDA INBOUND. BATTLE-CARRIER *ADRASTEIA'S LEDGER* AND ESCORT. ARRIVAL, ELEVEN CYCLES. VERDICT: LOGGED. NO OBJECTION ON FILE.

Castigator Ennio Dask read it on the slate in his cabin aboard the *Ledger*, the day it went, and thought that the last line was the only honest thing the Répertoire had ever written about the Order. There was never any objection on file. Nobody had ever thought to file one.

He was sixty-three. He had served the Order of Nemesis as a chaplain for forty-one years, and as its senior chaplain in the Tartarean theatre for nine, and he had a broad plain face, a shaved head with the Order's mark tattooed in white across the back of it, and the hands of a stonemason, which he had been before the Order took him. He carried a staff of office of black iron, forged from the chains of a perjurer the Order had hanged at Rhamnous a long time ago, and he carried it everywhere. In the eighth rotation of the siege he had led the counter-charge on the Phlegethon Docks that pulled Prefect Ardent of the First Cohort out from under Hesper Calloway's blade, and the First Cohort had broken the Forsworn at the docks and then gone home. Dask had gone home with it. He had not wanted to.

The Second Cohort had been raised at Rhamnous within a rotation of the First Cohort's withdrawal. The Order had not been asked to come back. It was not the Order's practice to wait to be asked. Its records described the Forsworn as an open account, and an open account at Depth 40, in the Order's view, was the Order's business, whether the Third agreed with it or not.

Prefect Sabra Ilion commanded the Second Cohort. She came to Dask's cabin on the hundred and ninety-sixth cycle, while the *Ledger* was still descending through the long dark of the middle depths, and sat on his bunk with her helm on her knees.

She was forty, tall and spare, a pure-blood daughter of the Order's oldest line, with grey eyes and a long jaw, and she had commanded the Second Cohort for the whole of its short life. She had never fought in Tartarus. She had read everything the Order had on the Arsenal, twice, and she had read Dask's own account of the docks three times, and she wanted, she said, to hear about Hesper Calloway from somebody who had stood in front of her.

"She's fast," Dask said. "Faster than she ought to be, for her size. She's eleven hundred years old and she was a Prefect of the Styx War before your great-great-grandmother was born, and she's fought every kind of soldier there is. She'll read you in the first exchange." He turned the iron staff slowly in his hands. "And the sword. Styx-iron. Anything it kills goes null. I watched it take half a Trench Regiment null on the second belt in the sixth rotation, Prefect, and I've seen the files on what it did before that. If she touches you with it, there's nothing for Rhamnous to call back."

"And at the docks?"

"At the docks she had Ardent down, and the sword at his throat, and I came in from the side with forty veterans and she let him go and stepped back into the river rather than be taken." Dask was quiet for a moment. "She'll do the same again if she can. The Forsworn would rather go back into the Styx than stand in front of the scales."

"Then we'll make sure she can't," said Ilion.

---

**THE SLAG GATE SPUR. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLE 201**

The Cohort's officers came down the cable from the *Adrasteia's Ledger* to the Slag Gate spur on the two hundred and first cycle. It was the only ground near the Acropolis held securely enough for a meeting.

Thalia received them as commander of the Third in the old guardroom of the southern bastion, at her plank table, with Celyn and Atlanta and Dray behind her. Her left arm was in a sling. The Hunt's healers had drawn most of Kampe's poison out of it, and the rest was going slowly, and the hand had no feeling yet.

Ilion's terms were short.

"The Second Cohort fights in your final assault, Lieutenant," she said. "Wherever you want us. We're eight hundred, pure-blood, with our own caskets and our own armour and a battle-carrier in the low geometry. In return, Hesper Calloway is taken alive, and handed to the Order for transport to Rhamnous. With her, every Forsworn taken alive. There will be no interrogation by the Hunt. No Enforcement custody. No Répertoire transcript of anything she says."

Thalia looked at her for a while.

"Prefect," she said. "Hesper Calloway has worn the Empire's own IFF for fourteen rotations. Her Forsworn are still broadcasting it. She knows which of my soldiers swore to her at the river. She knows where they serve now. She knows what they passed to her. The Hunt considers that knowledge to be a matter for the whole of the Tartarus front, and so do I."

Dask answered before Ilion could.

"Nemesis keeps her own accounts, Lieutenant," he said.

The Hunt's log records no further argument. It records that the Lieutenant was silent for some time after that, and that she accepted the terms within the hour.

She had little choice, and everyone at the table knew it. She was being offered a fresh force of the first quality on the eve of the last assault, at the price of the most valuable intelligence the siege could still yield. The First Cohort had broken the Forsworn at the docks and gone home. The Directorate had taken six of the Third's regiments to Lethe Hold, and the envoys sent to recover them had been found headless and null on a supply car. The Second Cohort was eight hundred pure-blood soldiers with a battle-carrier above them, and Thalia had no other source of fresh troops anywhere.

"One condition," she said, at the end of the hour.

"Name it," said Ilion.

"You bring her out breathing. I don't want her ending herself at the river at the last moment. I don't want her taking a dozen of yours with her when she does it. If the Hunt can't have her, I want to know at least that she's in a cell somewhere and not back in the Styx, waiting."

"The Order will bring her out breathing," said Dask.

---

Ilion's plan was direct, in the Order's usual manner. The Cohort would drop straight into the Acropolis, onto the forecourt of the Katabasion, behind a strike of fire-caskets to clear the ground. Dask would lead the Cohort's veterans into the Katabasion itself, where the Hunt's sister-augurs had placed the last of the Forsworn, while the Cohort's ground armour came up through the Flamen Gate to join them. The *Adrasteia's Ledger* was given a second task. The drowned battleship that had stood off the Phlegethon throughout the siege was still at large somewhere in the river cloud, and the Order meant to destroy it.

Thalia took Ilion's plan and made it a general assault, with the Second Cohort as its spearhead.

The Achilleans would come out of the Pilgrim Gate, where they had held their foothold since the beacon, and take the Archflamen's palace on the upper terrace. The *50th* Trench Regiment, with what remained of the *Anvil* battle group, would go in through the rubble of the Flamen Gate. The Hunt and the *6th* Moonwall would take the House of the Hearth on the eastern terrace, and then the inner ward. Under the cone, the Hephaestean sapper companies would break into the undercroft from the galleries they had been driving since the twelfth rotation.

"It's right," Dray said, that night, looking at the finished plan on the war-table. "After everything. The Trench Regiments ought to be in it at the end."

"They've been in it since the beginning," Thalia said.

---

It was the last force the Third could field.

The Second Cohort of Nemesis brought eight hundred soldiers in bone-white plate, with its own drop-caskets and a company of siege tanks, and the *Adrasteia's Ledger* and her escort cruiser in the low geometry. The Achillean Order had a hundred and twelve warriors fit to fight at the Pilgrim Gate, with Ancient Rhesos and Ancient Aias and the new warrior in Dolon's shell. The *50th* had been refilled from the vats to a strength of a hundred and forty thousand, and the *Anvil* battle group had its last two Steropes. The Hunt had six Hunters of the household left, and the Psiloi, and the *6th* Moonwall, reduced now to Roe's company and the survivors of Tharn's. Beneath the cone, the Hephaestean sapper companies had eleven thousand soldiers in the galleries. Behind them all stood the guns of the Third and the *Patroklos Unburied* and every remaining regiment of the ring, which would hold the foot of the cone and take no part in the assault unless it failed.

Against them, the Hunt's staff estimated between two and three hundred thousand defenders on the cone, most of them on the upper terraces, and an unknown number of creatures of the deep.

Two assets were missing from the Hunt's planning tables.

The boy had not been seen since the Flamen Gate. The *35th* reported that he had gone up the Pilgrim Stair after the gate fell and had not come down, and the Psiloi had lost him somewhere in the ruins of the colonnade. His companion was easier to find. Celyn reported that Verrill Chase was in the sapper galleries under the cone with Sapper-Lieutenant Ansel Rook's company, that she had been running relay chains under the cone for nine cycles, and that uploads were clearing from more than a hundred metres below the Acropolis for the first time in the siege. She had declined every request from the Hunt for a meeting, on the grounds that she was busy.

"How did she put it?" Thalia said.

Celyn read from the slate. "'Tell the Lieutenant I'm terribly sorry, and I'd love to, honestly, but I'm a hundred and ten metres under her feet with a bag of relay sets and a sapper company that keeps dying, and they keep coming back now, so I'd rather keep doing that, if it's all the same to her.'"

Atlanta laughed out loud. Thalia didn't, quite.

"Leave her alone," she said.

---

**THE ACROPOLIS. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLES 191 TO 204**

Before the final assault, every gun in range of the cone fired on the Acropolis for thirteen cycles.

The wards failed on the first cycle. The *10th*'s Phaethons had been wearing at them since the Scree, and with Kampe in the river and the heat-wells beneath the cone no longer tended by anyone, they went down one after another in the first watch, all round the summit, and the Phaethon batteries kept them down. Wrath bombers crossed the cone on every watch. The *Patroklos Unburied* and the *Adrasteia's Ledger* came down into the low geometry, into the airspace the ceiling-lances had denied the Fleet for most of the siege, and fired straight down into the Acropolis from above.

The aim was to reduce the cone's structures to rubble, so that the soldiers of the final assault would be spared the task of clearing them room by room, and the bombardment largely achieved it. The towers of the outer ward fell. The curtain walls between the gates were breached along most of their length. The defenders, as they had in every bombardment of the siege, withdrew into the galleries under the cone and waited, and the Third's staff expected them to come back up to the surface when the assault went in.

For eighteen rotations the Acropolis had looked down on every trench on the Cinder Flats. The College's chroniclers wrote afterward that the bombardment put out the eye of the Arsenal, and the phrase went round the Third and stayed.

On the ninth cycle of the bombardment, the dome of the Katabasion fell.

Dask watched it from the bridge of the *Ledger*, through the forward viewports, from four kilometres above the cone. The breachers in the Third's forward trenches watched it from below. They saw the great dome lean, for most of a minute, slowly, its ribs giving way one after another, the eight-thousand-year-old stone grinding and cracking in a sound that came across the Flats to every trench. Then it folded down into the inner ward under a column of dust a kilometre high.

The forward trenches cheered. Dask, on the bridge of the *Ledger*, made the sign of the scales over his breast and said nothing.

On the next cycle the Pontifical College filed a formal protest.

CODE 31. JUDICIUM PETO. ORIGIN: PONTIFICAL COLLEGE, DELEGATION TO THE CONCLAVE. SUBJECT: DESTRUCTION OF THE KATABASION, RELIQUARY OF THE FIRST DESCENT. VERDICT: PENDING.

It was still pending at the end of the siege. The Hunt's log notes its receipt and nothing else.

---

**THE FORECOURT OF THE KATABASION. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLE 205**

The final assault on the Acropolis began on the two hundred and fifth cycle of Rotation Eighteen.

The fire-caskets came down first, out of the dark under the ceiling, and struck the forecourt of the Katabasion in a pattern that left nothing standing on it, and the Second Cohort followed in three waves of drop-caskets. Dask came down in the second wave. The casket hit the rubble of the forecourt hard enough to crack two of his ribs, and its doors blew open, and he came out of it into the smoke with the iron staff in one hand and his lance-pistol in the other and the veterans of the Cohort coming out of their own caskets all round him in their bone-white plate. Eight hundred pure-blood soldiers of Nemesis secured the forecourt within the first watch.

At the same hour, Archon Orthes led the Achillean Order out of the Pilgrim Gate along the ruined colonnade toward the Archflamen's palace. Under the cone, the Hephaestean borers cut through the last metres of rock into the undercroft, and the sapper companies went in behind them. On the north face, the *50th* went up the slope in its Hoplons onto the rubble of the Flamen Gate, and its breachers crossed into the outer ward with bayonets fixed, with the *Anvil*'s last two Steropes walking behind them.

The defenders came up out of the galleries, as the Third's staff had expected. With them came the Burned and the Kindled, and the ore-hounds, and the Hollowed in their grey coats, and other creatures of the deep that the Third had never catalogued and never would. The fighting inside the Acropolis would go on for weeks, and it was confused from beginning to end, and its outcome was never in doubt. The Archflamen's army had ceased to be an army. What remained of it was a few hundred thousand defenders, human and otherwise, with no line of retreat and no hope of relief. Of the four Legates of the deep, only one was still on the cone.

In the palace, the Achilleans found what remained of the Archflamen's household.

Few of them were still recognisable as people. Some had been kept too long in the Grey. Others had been opened by the Kindlers and filled with something else. All of them fought with no regard for their own survival, and the Order cleared the palace floor by floor against them, each lochos covering the next, with the Ancients following behind to break anything that would not stay down. It took four cycles. The Order took no prisoners in the palace, and its chronicle records none.

---

**THE KATABASION. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLE 206**

On the two hundred and sixth cycle, Dask led the Cohort's veterans up the steps of the Katabasion under fire.

It was a shell by then. The dome had gone, and the great reliquary hall was open to the ceiling above it and half filled with its own fallen roof, and fires were still burning in the nave among the rubble. The sister-augurs' reading had been correct. The surviving Forsworn had fortified the Katabasion as their last position. They had built barricades of fallen stone and broken shrine-furniture across the great archway at the head of the steps, and they were firing down the steps from behind them.

The band that had come up the Styx in the fifth rotation had been a thousand strong. Thirteen rotations of fighting had worn it down to a fraction of that, and the Cohort's intelligence officers assumed that Hesper would be with what was left.

Dask went up the steps at the head of his veterans. He was hit repeatedly crossing the top of them. Heavy rounds struck his plate and staggered him, one after another, and he kept walking, and his veterans returned fire on the barricades in the archway all round him. At the central barricade he raised his lance-pistol and burned it, and the three Forsworn behind it, in a single long shot, and walked into the gap he had made with the veterans coming in behind him. The Cohort's witnesses describe him walking straight through a burst of Phlegethon-fire on the far side of the gap with his plate glowing, and meeting a Laistrygon that came at him out of the smoke, and killing it with his staff of office in two blows.

By the end of the watch, the archway was the Cohort's.

Ilion's armour came up behind them with the siege tanks in the lead, grinding up the steps of the Katabasion, and their guns brought down what was left of the inner walls of the nave. Then Ilion drove her command carrier up the steps and through the archway and went in herself, with her command squad around her.

---

The Cohort fought its way down the nave toward the great hall at the rear.

That was where the shields of the First Descent had hung for eight thousand years, round the walls of the great hall, under the dome that was gone now, the shields of the first legion to go down into Tartarus and come back. Most of them still hung there. Pilgrims had touched them for eight thousand years, and their bronze was worn smooth and bright where the pilgrims' hands had been, and the light of the fires in the nave shone on them.

The Forsworn stood beneath the shields in their grey-green plate of Styx-work, still broadcasting valid Imperial IFF, so that Ilion's command net marked every one of them as friendly for the whole of the engagement, a row of green sigils on every slate in the Cohort, and the Cohort fought them anyway.

The battle for the great hall was the hardest fighting of the final assault.

The Forsworn had broken their oaths at the Styx itself, every one of them, at the river by which the gods themselves swore. The Order of Nemesis regarded each of them as a debt to be closed in person, face to face, and its soldiers went in against them in the great hall in pairs and threes, with blades. Ilion had ordered that they try to take the wounded alive and keep them alive, and they tried. Most of the Forsworn would not be taken. They fought until they fell, and some of them, falling, put their hands to the backs of their necks where the oath-scar lay, and went still.

Ilion reached Hesper at the foot of the shields.

Hesper Calloway had four wounded guards left round her, and she was bleeding through a breach in her own breastplate, a long black wound below the ribs. She still carried the Styx-iron sword, long and dark and dull, that had sent half a Trench Regiment null on the second belt in the sixth rotation. She stood under the shields of the First Descent with the sword in her hand and the fire of the nave behind her, and watched the Prefect of the Second Cohort come toward her across the hall.

"Hesper Calloway," Ilion said. "Yield to the scales."

Hesper looked at her for a moment, the pure-blood Prefect in her bone-white plate with her two-handed blade, forty years old, who had never fought in Tartarus.

Then she attacked.

She turned Ilion's first thrust aside against her own pauldron, the two-handed blade glancing off the old grey-green plate, and drove her counter up in the same motion, under the Prefect's guard, through the lower edge of her visor.

The Prefect's command carrier, at the archway, logged the death within the second.

PREFECT SABRA ILION, COHORS NEMESIS SECUNDA. UPLOAD: NONE RECEIVED. VERDICT: NULL. CAUSE, STYX-IRON.

Hesper stood over the Prefect's body. Three soldiers of the Cohort came at her, trying to reach it, to drag the Prefect clear, and she killed all three of them. She was still standing there over the body, with the Styx-iron sword in her hand and the three dead soldiers at her feet and the blood running down her leg from the wound under her ribs, when Dask came to the foot of the shields with his veterans.

---

The helm recording of what followed was entered afterwards in the Order's archive at Rhamnous. It shows the great hall of the Katabasion lit by fire, and the shields of the First Descent high on the walls, and the woman in grey-green plate standing under them over the Prefect's body.

"Yield," said Dask, "or be ended."

Hesper laughed. It was a short tired sound.

"To what?" she said. "A cell at Rhamnous? The scales of Nemesis for the rest of time?" She shifted her grip on the sword. "I've been in a cell before, Castigator. I've been weighed before. I broke my oath to everyone who ever weighed me. I'll take the river."

"Not this time," said Dask. "There's no river here, Prefect." He came forward a step. "The Phlegethon Docks. Rotation Eight. You had Prefect Ardent of the First Cohort on his back with your sword at his throat, and a stonemason's son came in from the side with forty veterans and an iron staff."

The recording shows her face change. She had recognised him.

"You," she said.

"Me," said Dask.

---

The fight was short.

Hesper parried his first attacks at a speed the veterans watching considered impossible for anyone her size, with the wound in her side, after the fighting she had already done that watch. The Styx-iron sword met the iron staff again and again, and rang, and each time Dask came on. And each parry was a fraction slower than the one before. Dask did not slow at all. He had the strength of the stonemason he had been, and he had the patience of forty-one years as a chaplain of the Order, and he came on steadily, never hurrying, until he beat her guard aside with the staff and struck her full in the face with the butt of it, and brought the iron head of it down on her skull.

She went to her knees. She tried, from her knees, to raise the sword again.

Dask knocked it out of her hand. It went across the floor of the great hall, spinning, and stopped against the base of the wall under the shields.

"Take her alive," Dask said. "Treat her. Keep her breathing. Don't let her hands near her neck." He looked at the sword lying against the wall. "And nobody touches that. Keep it where I can see it."

His veterans took her. Then Dask went to Ilion, lying on her back at the foot of the shields, and knelt beside her, and took off his helm, and said the Order's rite over her, all of it, in the old words, though he knew there was nothing left of her to call back. When he had finished he stayed kneeling for a while with his hand on her breastplate.

Then he stood up and signalled the *Adrasteia's Ledger* that the Order's business on the cone was complete.

Hesper Calloway was carried out of the Katabasion alive and unconscious on a Nemesis stretcher, still broadcasting Imperial IFF.

---

The Second Cohort had paid for its prize with its commander. Its terms had been met in full. The Hunt was left with no means of learning what Hesper Calloway knew about the Forsworn's agents in the Third, and the question of which soldiers of the Tartarus front had sworn to her at the river remained open at the end of the siege.

The Hunt has not closed it.

---

**THE ECHO MAST. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLE 206, THE DARK WATCH**

Minerva climbed the Mast in the dark watch, on the outer face, on bare stone, without stopping.

She had been in the inner ward for nine cycles by then. She had crossed the gorge on the night Kampe fell, on the stump of the fallen bridge and a cable she had strung across from it, while the Hunt was clearing the outer ward and the Psiloi were searching the colonnade for her. She had gone to ground in the ruins at the foot of the Tabularium among the rubble of the dome, and listened, with the whole of her attention, to the Mast.

It hummed. It had hummed for eighteen rotations over the Flats, and from the inner ward, close up, she could hear what the hum was made of. It was voices. Millions of them. Every upload the Mast had ever taken from a dying soldier of the Third was still up there, somewhere in the relay heads at its crown, held, and she could hear them, faint and all at once, with the same sense that heard the noosphere of a whole depth when she reached for it. She could hear something else, below them, under the Tabularium, a long thin pull of threads going down from the Mast into the dark under the rock. The voices were being fed into something.

And she could hear a child. On the second gallery from the top. Crying in her sleep.

She had planned it for nine cycles. On the two hundred and sixth, the Psiloi of the Hunt watching the inner ward from the outer ward's ruined towers reported a figure climbing the Echo Mast during the dark watch, on the outer face, on bare stone, without stopping. It went past the second gallery from the top on the far side of the spire, where nobody on the gallery could see it, and reached the crown before the watch changed.

The crown of the Mast was where the old relay heads converged, a ring of bronze and crystal round the top of the spire, eight thousand years old, the place where every upload on the Cinder Flats had once begun its long climb to Watchpoint Hades. The wind at the top came straight off the burning Flats, hot and full of ash. From the crown, Minerva could see everything. The whole of the Flats lay spread out below her to the horizon under the red light of the river, the trenches and the wire and the wrecks and the dead, eighteen rotations of them.

She put the boy's hands on the relay heads.

*There you are,* she said, to the quiet, and nobody answered, and she did not expect anyone to.

---

She did not touch the voices yet. It was not the time. She found the edge of the noosphere of Depth 40 through the relay heads instead, the whole of it, every relay and every implant and every machine on the Flats and under them, and lay in it, and listened, and waited.

She had expected the Eye of Light to move before the end. She had not known when.

At the second bell of the two hundred and seventh cycle, the augurs of the *Adrasteia's Ledger* detected a power spike in the river cloud eighty kilometres below the cone, where the drowned battleship had lit her engines.

---

**THE RIVER CLOUD. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLE 207**

By the seventh cycle of the final assault every defender on the cone knew the Acropolis was lost, and the *Eye of Light* was the only way off it.

The *Ledger* turned to engage. Her ship-master, a big profane veteran of the Order's fleet called Lucan Merrow, had been waiting the whole siege for the drowned battleship to come up out of the river, and he took the *Ledger* down into the river cloud toward the power spike at her best speed, with her escort cruiser on her flank.

The engagement went against the *Ledger* from the beginning. She was faster than the *Eye of Light*, and the battleship was damaged. But the *Eye of Light* was by far the larger ship. She came up out of the Phlegethon trailing river water from every seam, burned black from stem to stern, with a figure of a burned god on her prow that had not been there when the Fleet lost her, and she brought her broadside to bear on the *Ledger*, and her guns still worked.

Her first salvo broke the *Ledger*'s forward wards. The *Ledger*'s lances opened a long wound down the battleship's flank in return. Her second salvo started fires on the *Ledger*'s gun deck and set off her ready magazines, and the *Ledger* shuddered from end to end, and her gunnery officer reported to Merrow, in a flat voice, that the battleship was going to get past them.

That was when an authority with no seal and no registry entered the *Ledger*'s command net.

The Répertoire, queried during the engagement, logged it as UNREGISTERED, CLASS UNKNOWN. It spoke on the *Ledger*'s bridge from every speaker at once, in a boy's voice, flat and even.

"Ship-Master. Hold fire on the battleship's stern. I require her engines intact for eleven more seconds."

Merrow stared at the speakers. "Who in the name of the pit is this?"

"Eleven seconds, Ship-Master."

---

Minerva had flown the *Eye of Light* before.

She had flown her out of the Luna yards from under the inspectors' noses, a long time ago, before the siege began, and brought her down at Grid Delta, Sector Nineteen-F, on the third cycle of Rotation Four, as the waypoint for an order that the Fleet had called Warzone Re-evaluation CODE 78E. Lord Commander Varric von Romhardt had committed the whole of the Grid Delta Wedge against the wreck. The Phlegethon had carried the wreck away before the Wedge could reach it, and the Fleet had listed her as lost. She had been in the river the whole time. At some point the deep had crewed her with drowned soldiers and brought her back into service, and the drowned crew had changed a great deal of her in the rotations since.

They had never found the key.

Before the engines failed on that first descent to Grid Delta, Minerva had left a core authority key in the ship, deep in the old core, under everything, where a drowned crew would never think to look. From the crown of the Mast, where the old relay heads converged, she reached down through the noosphere of Depth 40 into the river cloud, into the *Eye of Light*, and found it, still there, exactly where she had left it.

The battleship's core log was recovered in fragments from the wreckage after the siege. It records what followed.

AC-09-1642. CORE AUTHORITY: RESTORED.

COMMAND: REACTOR SAFETIES, DISENGAGE. DRIVES, FULL REVERSE THRUST, HOLD. MAGAZINE SEALS, OPEN.

CONFIRMED.

For eleven seconds, the *Eye of Light* hung in the river cloud with her drives working against each other, her reactors climbing past their limits, and her magazines standing open to the fires on her own decks, while her drowned crew tried and failed to override the core.

On the tenth second, Minerva spoke on the *Ledger*'s net again.

"Ship-Master. Fire everything you have."

Merrow fired everything he had. The *Ledger* fired every gun she still had working, and the Cohort's escort cruiser fired with her, and forty kilometres away, above the cone, the *Patroklos Unburied* put its forward lances into the *Eye of Light*'s stern.

The battleship broke up from the stern forward, compartment by compartment, one after another, the fires running forward along her decks into the open magazines. The burned figurehead fell from her prow into the river. Then her main reactors failed.

The light of it filled the gorge of the Phlegethon and lit the underside of the ceiling across the whole length of the Cinder Flats, white, and every soldier of the Third in every trench on the Flats saw it. On the crown of the Echo Mast, it lit the boy's face from below, for a long moment, as he crouched there with his hands on the relay heads.

When it faded, nothing remained in the river cloud but burning debris, falling slowly down into the Phlegethon.

"Battleship destroyed," Minerva said, on the *Ledger*'s net. "Thank you for your fire, Ship-Master."

Merrow's reply is preserved in the *Ledger*'s net log in full. It is long and profane, and it ends with a demand to know who in the name of every god in the pit was on his net. It went unanswered. The net was empty before he had finished asking.

On the crown of the Mast, Minerva took her hands off the relay heads for a moment and sat back on her heels in the hot wind.

Below her, on the second gallery from the top, the child had woken. Minerva could hear her moving about. She could hear a man's voice too, low, speaking to the child, and the clink of a chain against bronze.

*The Hunt will need the Mast silent before it can close the door,* she told the quiet. *It will take them three or four weeks to reach it, Varlet. I will need every cycle of that.*

She put her hands back on the relay heads, and began, slowly and with great care, to listen to the voices.
