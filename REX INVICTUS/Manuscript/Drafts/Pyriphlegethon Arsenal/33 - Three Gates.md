## Chapter 33: Three Gates

*"An oath is the only armour that cannot be taken from you. It can only be put down."*

Teaching of the Achillean Order, said at the Hearth before the shield

---

**THE PILGRIM STAIR. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLE 171**

Lochagos Ithra Vell had stood on the rubble of the Slag Gate's wall for an hour before first bell, looking up at the stair.

It went up the southern face of the cone in a single line, eight thousand years old, cut into the black basalt by the Arsenal's founders for the pilgrims who would come to the Katabasion. On one side of it the cliff rose sheer to the ramparts of the outer ward. On the other, there was nothing at all, a drop to the Flats that grew with every flight, until near the top a pilgrim who looked over the edge would see the whole of the Cinder Flats spread out beneath her, burning, from the Lethe Cut to the Demus Gorge. The steps had been worn into hollows by the feet of the pilgrims. In places the hollows were a hand deep. Halfway up, where the stair turned at its first landing, the Archflamen's people had cut bunkers into the cliff, three of them, with gun slits that looked straight down the line of the steps. Above the bunkers the stair climbed on to the Pilgrim Gate, a bastion of cut stone between two guard towers, black against the red glow of the summit.

She had led the leap-squads up the crater rim at the Scaean Gate. She had come back from it with her helm split and a third of her squad in the urns, and the Order had given her the bunkers on the Pilgrim Stair for having done that, and she understood it as an honour, and she understood what kind of honour it was.

"Lochagos." Her second, a young warrior called Phaidra Kos with a burn scar across her jaw, came up the rubble beside her. "The strike craft are on their way in."

"Squads?"

"Ready." Kos looked up at the stair. "It's a long way to jump."

"It's two hundred and forty metres," said Vell. "The rigs will do three hundred. We've jumped further."

"Not uphill, Lochagos. Not into bunkers."

"No," Vell agreed, and put on her helm.

---

The bunkers were cast basalt, built to take plunging shells, and every leap-squad carried breaching charges to open them.

A strike craft of the *Patroklos Unburied* came in low over the spur at the first bell, under the guns of the southern walls, and dropped fire-bombs on the first landing in a long line, and the landing vanished in a sheet of flame. When the fire burned down, Vell's leap-squads went off the rubble heap that had once been part of the Slag Gate's wall in a single long bound. Thirty-six warriors of three lochoi rose into the air together with their leap-rigs blazing white and blue behind them, two hundred and forty metres up the face of the cone, and came down out of the smoke onto the first landing among the bunkers.

Vell landed four metres from the slit of the middle bunker. Something inside it was already firing. She threw herself flat beneath the slit, under the line of fire, and rolled, and came up against the basalt wall with her back to it and a breaching charge in her hand, and slapped the charge onto the wall beside the slit and rolled away again. It blew. The slit became a hole the size of a door, and Kos went through it with her blade drawn before the smoke had cleared, and Vell went in after her.

As they went in, Orthes ordered the armoured column forward to its start line at the foot of the stair. A second strike craft fired a salvo of missiles into the gatehouse above, and the Order's rocket batteries on the spur began to fire along the walls of the cone, and the wards flared up gold and white all along the southern face under the impacts.

---

With the bunkers falling, the main column moved off.

Three heavy assault carriers led it, great squat bronze-plated machines of the Order's own pattern, built in the forges of the Hearth for exactly such work as this, and Damar Orthes rode in the first of them with his helm on and the Order's standard furled beside him. The road to the foot of the stair had been broken by rotations of shelling into a grey paste of cinder and ash, and the carriers churned through the paste to the foot of the stair and then up it, with every gun on every carrier firing at the walls above.

The drivers forced the carriers up the steps flight by flight, driving the tracks into the old stone until they bit, grinding up through the hollows the pilgrims had worn, with the cliff on one side and the drop on the other and fire from the walls falling on the hulls the whole way. Less than halfway up, below the first landing, the leading carrier was hit. It slewed sideways across the stair and caught fire, and its assault ramp jammed half open.

Orthes forced the ramp down with his own hands. Two of his warriors helped him, the three of them with their shoulders to the jammed bronze, and it gave, and fell, and he led them out onto the stair on foot through the smoke. The second carrier came up behind and pushed the burning wreck aside, off the edge of the stair, and it went over and fell, turning, for a long time. Then the second carrier struck a mine hidden under the hollows of the next flight, and the blast took off its right track, and the column could go no further.

The rest of the Order dismounted and followed the Archon's standard up the stair on foot, through the fire.

---

The Archflamen's soldiers came down out of the Pilgrim Gate to meet them.

The Brethren of the Black Shale led them, once again, in their dark plate of river-stone with the sheets of it clicking at every step, and behind them came the garrison of the gate and its thralls. They came down the stair in a mass. The Order met them on the steps, between the first landing and the second, on a stair that was never more than six metres wide, with a sheer drop on one side, and the fighting on the ramps and steps there was the most savage the Order had seen in three centuries.

Orthes is recorded in the Order's chronicle throwing one of the Brethren bodily from the stair into the void and cutting a second in half with a single stroke. The chronicle also records the Order's strike craft diving on the defenders crowding the steps above, so low that their wingtips cleared the cliff by metres. And it records the Ancients, who had come up the cliff beside the stair on their great iron legs, climbing where no carrier could, coming up behind the warriors and driving the Archflamen's soldiers back up the stair with their heavy guns. Within minutes, hundreds of bodies lay along the steps.

The Order halted under the gate-towers to regroup. The defenders withdrew inside the gatehouse and barred the outer gate behind them.

The Achilleans had reached their objective. Now they had to storm it.

---

**THE SOUTHERN CLIFF. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLE 171**

Minerva had been on the cliff for nine minutes by then.

She had come up from the spur during the fighting on the stair, from the foot of the cliff two hundred metres east of the steps, where nobody was looking. The Order's translation beacon was strapped to the boy's back in a canvas sling. It was the size of a ration crate and weighed thirty-one kilograms. She had taken it from the *Patroklos Unburied*'s armoury on the hundred and sixty-ninth cycle by the simple means of walking in and picking it up, with the Order's armourers busy in the next bay, and nobody had missed it, since the Order did not believe that anyone could steal from the Order.

*Two hundred metres of bare basalt,* Minerva noted, to the quiet she still talked to. *Average incline eighty-one degrees. The Phaethons have cracked the face in a great many places.* He would have called it insane. He would have meant the whole thing, and she would have known what he meant.

She was climbing fast. She had the body's strength at her disposal, all of it, with nothing held back, and she was using all of it, finding the cracks the Phaethons had made in the face with the boy's fingers and pulling up, finding the next, pulling up, spread flat against the black rock, with the noise of the fighting on the stair to her left and the drop to the Flats beneath her boots. The Hunt's Psiloi, watching the cliff below the gatehouse from the spur, saw something climbing the bare rock under the walls and took it at first for a lizard of the deep, one of the long pale things that lived in the cracks of the cone. It climbed two hundred metres in four minutes. They watched it reach the foot of the gatehouse wall and stop under a gun slit, and then they watched it go in.

The gun slit was the width of a hand. The Phaethons had cracked it in the fourteenth rotation and nobody had ever repaired it, and the crack ran down from it through the stone for a metre and a half and was as wide as a man's shoulders at the bottom. Minerva went through it sideways, with the beacon held in front of her, and came down into the gun gallery of the Pilgrim Gate's eastern tower on the other side.

There were nine soldiers of the gate's garrison in the gallery. They were crouched at the slits, firing down at the stair. Minerva walked through them. They turned, and stared, and two of them fired at her from three metres away, and the rounds stopped in the air a hand's breadth from the boy's body and hung there glowing, and dropped. She did not stop walking. She went down the gallery stair into the outer tunnel of the gatehouse, where the garrison had massed to meet the Order behind the barred gate, two hundred of them, and walked out into the middle of the tunnel floor in front of all of them, and set the beacon down on the stone and opened it.

*Now would be the time to be frightened, Varlet,* she said, to nobody, and found that the body's heart was going fast, and let it. She was reading the beacon's settings and changing them at speed. Every round the garrison fired at them stopped in the air and fell. *Brace.*

The beacon lit. It glowed blue, the Order's blue, in the dark of the tunnel, and began to sound, a deep pulsing note that went up through the stone of the gatehouse and through the wards.

Minerva sent a single transmission on the Order's net.

POLEMARCH. YOU HAVE A LOCK.

---

**THE TRANSLATION CHAMBER, *PATROKLOS UNBURIED*. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLE 171**

Avel had been standing on the plate in the translation chamber for forty minutes, with the First Lochos around him in their ranks, waiting.

He had not believed it. He had told the Archon so, when the Archon told him about the signal. A beacon, inside the wards of the Pilgrim Gate, placed by nobody knew whom, at an hour nobody could guarantee. He had said that the Order did not plan battles around miracles. The Archon had looked at him and said that he did not think it was a miracle, and that he wanted the First Lochos on the plate at zero hour in any case.

So he had stood on the plate, with the new bronze of his chest heavy under his plate and the First Lochos around him, eighty-eight warriors, all that remained of it after the Scaean Gate and the replacements from the Hearth, and listened to the fighting on the stair coming over the net, and waited.

At the thirty-ninth minute, the lock came up on the chamber's board, blue and steady. A moment after it, a single line of text.

POLEMARCH. YOU HAVE A LOCK.

Avel looked at it for perhaps a second.

"First Lochos," he said. "Translate."

The chamber filled with light.

---

**THE PILGRIM GATE. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLE 171**

The Archflamen's soldiers had been driven off the stair, and they were not beaten. Behind them in the bastion waited what the Kindlers had raised.

Vell saw it come out. She was on the first landing among the broken bunkers with what remained of her squads, eighteen of thirty-six, getting her breath, when the outer gate of the Pilgrim Gate swung open again above the Order on the steps, and the deep came out of the mouth of the tunnel.

The Burned came first. They were soldiers of the reformation who walked as fire in the shape of men, white-hot at the core and red at the edges, with nothing left inside the fire but the shape. Behind them came the hounds of the deep, the size of carriers, black and long-jawed, with river-fire running from their mouths. Then came the khalkotauroi, the bronze bulls of the old forges, three times the height of a warrior, with furnaces burning in their bellies behind grilles of bronze ribs and smoke pouring from their nostrils. With them came drakon-spawn, and mad thralls, and the Kindled, who had been Lictors in the Gully of Ash ten cycles before. They had come back changed beyond knowing, burning in the remains of their black armour.

They came down the stair onto the Order.

The fighting surged back and forth across the steps beneath the bastion, and the Achilleans were hard pressed. The First Lochos could not come to their aid. It had translated into the outer tunnel among the gate's garrison and was already fighting its way out of the tunnel into the backs of the things coming down the steps. The Order's plate gave little protection against what it faced. Its bronze and iron had been forged against blades and bullets, and the Burned went through it, and the bulls trampled it, and many warriors fell on the steps under the bastion. The Order's standard-bearer, a big quiet warrior of the Third Lochos called Menon, went down under a bronze bull that trampled him for a long time, until a warrior put a lance-pistol shot through the grille of its ribs and burst its furnace, and it fell burning off the stair. Ancient Aias went down on the steps with three hounds of the deep on his coffin, tearing at the bronze. The priest of the Second Lochos, an old woman called Theano, stood on the steps throughout with her staff raised and chanted the Order's oath aloud into the noise, over and over, the same words, the old words the Order said before the shield, and the Order held.

Orthes was wounded. A Burned took him across the body and set his plate alight, and the Order's healer dragged him clear down the stair with his helm and blade lost, and for a while it seemed that the Achilleans would have to fall back down the stair or be destroyed on it.

Vell looked up at it from the first landing, with eighteen warriors behind her and her breaching charges almost gone.

"Kos," she said.

"Lochagos."

"Rigs."

"We've barely enough charge for one jump, Lochagos."

"Then we'll make it count," said Vell. "Up."

They came off the first landing with what remained of the squads, eighteen warriors on failing rigs, in a single bound up the stair over the heads of the Order on the steps, and came down on the Burned from behind.

It broke them. The Burned had not expected anything to come at them from behind and above, and Vell's squad went through them with blades and the last breaching charges, and the thralls behind the Burned saw it, and broke, and fled back up the stair toward the gate. Vell's squad's last two charges blew a hole in the outer gate, where it had swung half shut again behind the deep, and the Achilleans went through the hole after the fleeing thralls into the outer tunnel.

---

In the outer tunnel Vell found the First Lochos, fighting with their backs to the beacon.

The beacon was still glowing blue on the floor in the middle of the tunnel, among the dead of the gate's garrison, and the First Lochos had closed round it in a ring with Avel at its head, and the garrison's survivors and the deep were coming at the ring from the inner gate end of the tunnel. And among the First Lochos, standing beside the beacon with one hand flat against the tunnel wall, there was a boy.

He was slight and dark-haired, in a long coat burned at the hem. He had no helm and no plate. The rounds fired at the ring stopped in the air around him and dropped. He was not fighting. He had his palm flat on the stone of the tunnel wall and his eyes half closed.

Vell went straight to him through the fighting.

"Who are you?" she said.

The boy opened his eyes and looked at her. They were green. Her helm recorded what he said next, word for word, and the Order's chronicle preserves it.

"Lochagos. The inner gate runs on a counterweight. The mechanism is behind this wall. I will have it open in thirty seconds."

"I asked you who you are."

He said nothing. He closed his eyes again and pressed his palm flatter against the stone.

Twenty-eight seconds later, the inner gate of the Pilgrim Gate rose into the rock with a long groaning of chains, and the Order went through it.

---

Orthes came back into the fight inside. He had refused his healer, thrown off the burning plate on the stair, and taken up a fallen blade from the steps, and he came up through the outer tunnel and the inner gate behind the Order in his under-armour with his scarred face bare. The Achilleans cleared the bastion floor by floor and showed no mercy to anything they found in it, and by the end of the cycle the gatehouse of the Pilgrim Gate was theirs. They sealed its entrances. They swore, before the Order's priest, to hold it against whatever came.

Then Orthes climbed to the roof of the bastion and planted the Order's torn standard there, in view of every soldier on the Cinder Flats. As he did it, the first salvoes of the *Patroklos Unburied* began to fall on the wards of the Acropolis from the low geometry overhead, the first time any ship of any fleet had fired on the cone since the siege began.

When Vell looked for the boy afterward, he was gone.

The gate had cost the Order most of the force it had committed. Of the four hundred warriors who had come down from the *Patroklos Unburied*, two hundred and thirty-one lay dead along the Pilgrim Stair and against the gatehouse walls. Of the rest, only a hundred and twelve were judged fit to fight. Ancient Aias was recovered from the steps and returned to his coffin, the third time in his long service that he had been carried off a field. Menon was carried down to the spur on his shield. The Achilleans had no vats to go back to. Every one of the dead was gone for good.

The Order did not ask to be relieved. Thalia did not offer it. For the remaining cycles of the siege the Achilleans held the Pilgrim Gate with what they had left, against six counter-attacks from the colonnade, and they were still holding it when the final assault began.

---

**THE FOUNDERS' GATE ROAD. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLES 171 TO 174**

On the western face of the cone, the attack on the Founders' Gate went much as the Hunt's staff had expected it to.

Colonel Silvan Thrale took the *50th* up the lower switchbacks behind its rolling barrage at the first bell, with the Order's siege tanks and battle tanks on the road beside his breachers and the *Anvil of Talos* walking below. He was a quiet precise man, fifty-eight, who had held the *50th* on the right of the *52nd* in the worst rotations of the siege and been passed over for a Line Phalanx twice since, and he had read the Hunt's orders for the Founders' Gate with great care and understood exactly what they meant. The gate stood at the top of eleven switchbacks, each of them commanded by a tower of the outer ward, and the road had been mined and cratered so often in the course of the siege that only its lower half could still carry armour. The *50th* was to go up it as far as it could, and draw the enemy's armour down onto it, and hold it there, and if the *50th* took the gate as well, so much the better.

The *50th* took the first four switchbacks in the opening watch, at a cost of nine thousand transfers. Then it met the counter-attack the plan had been made to provoke.

The Archflamen's walkers and furnace-engines came down the road from the Founders' Gate in strength. It was the largest concentration of the enemy's remaining armour seen since the Scree, the long-legged furnace-crabs with their flame-throwers and the heavy siege walkers of the casting lines and, among them, the iron machines into which the Cinderborn had sealed living people. The Behemoths of the *Anvil* battle group engaged them on the open ground of the lower switchbacks, as Thalia had intended, and over the following two cycles destroyed thirty-eight walkers and furnace-engines on the road. The Order's diversionary force held the fourth switchback against everything that came down it. The *50th* could make no further progress, and on the third cycle Thrale halted the attack below the sixth switchback, with the gate itself untouched.

It had cost the *50th* thirty-one thousand transfers, the *Anvil* battle group one Behemoth, and the Order eleven of its tanks, and it had failed to take its objective. It had also done exactly what was required of it. For the whole of the assault on the cone, the enemy's walkers and furnace-engines were held on the western face, and not one of them reached the Pilgrim Stair or the north slope, where the decisive fighting took place.

---

**THE NORTH SLOPE. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLE 171**

While the Achilleans fought up the Pilgrim Stair, the Hunt's own assault force gathered for its attack on the Flamen Gate.

It was the strongest force the Hunt could put together on the Flats. There were eight Hunters of the Director's household with their full retinues, and the Psiloi squads, and companies of shock breachers from the *43rd* and the *50th*. The *6th* Moonwall's two companies went up in the Hunt's silver carriers, under Shield-Captain Antheia Roe, with her arm still in a sling from the Pilgrim Stair, and Flame-Captain Dione Tharn. Hunt-Elder Sarn walked with them on her metal legs, with her staff in her hand. Wrath wings stood by overhead to cover the final approach. To the west, the rolling barrage in front of the *50th* was already pounding the slope and wrapping the Acropolis in grey dust.

The assault's communications were Verrill Chase's work.

She had come over from the *35th* the cycle before, on foot, through the ring at the foot of the cone, with a sack of relay sets over her shoulder, and walked into the *43rd*'s signal dugout on the north slope and told its signal officer, a harassed tribune called Owyn Bracegirdle, that his relay chain would fail the moment the assault entered the gate tunnels.

"The Flamen Gate tunnels run sixty metres into the rock," she said. "Below eighty metres, no upload reaches a relay. Between sixty and eighty, they're unreliable. Your chain ends at the foot of the slope. The moment your breachers are in the tunnels, every one of them who dies in there goes up into the rock with nowhere to go, and the cadence can't find its way out, and the Mast is two kilometres away across the gorge and it's waiting."

Bracegirdle stared at her. "Who are you?"

"The engineer who fixed the masks," said Chase. "Give me two signallers. I'll lay you a chain of sets up the slope and into the tunnels behind the assault at forty-metre intervals. Every forty metres, all the way in. The cadence will find its way out along it."

He gave her two signallers. She spent the assault in a trench at the foot of the slope with them, with a stack of relay sets as tall as she was, sending the sets up the slope behind the assault one after another on the backs of runners as fast as they could carry them.

---

Before the advance, Thalia walked among the breachers while her priests gave every soldier the old vow against what they were about to face, the vow the Hunt said before it went up against the deep.

In the trench at the foot of the slope, Chase saw her go by, and stopped with a relay set in her hands.

"That's her," she said. "That's Thalia Grace."

"The Lieutenant," said the signaller beside her, a boy called Daffyd Pruett, who was eighteen. "Yes, miss."

"No, you don't understand. That's Thalia Grace. The Thalia Grace. From the old stories, the real ones." Chase set the relay set down on the parapet and stared up the trench after the Lieutenant with her spectacles pushed up and her face alight. "Daughter of Zeus. She was a pine tree, you know, for years, on a hill, she was a tree, and then she wasn't, and she joined the Hunt, and she's been the Hunt's Lieutenant longer than anyone. She fought at Manhattan, in the old war, the first one. She fought the Titans. Somebody wrote a whole book about her, my grandmother had it, I read it eleven times. And she's standing there, look at her, she's actually standing right there, and she's..."

Every relay set in the trench spoke at once, all of them together, in a single flat voice.

"Chase. Be quiet."

Chase was quiet.

Pruett stared at the relay sets, and then at her. Chase picked her relay set up off the parapet, rather pink, and handed it to the next runner without another word.

---

On Thalia's word, the whole force rose and went up the slope beside its carriers at a walk.

As it climbed, the ceiling over the Flats went black and a storm broke beneath it.

There is no rain on the Cinder Flats. The storm was lightning alone. It came crossing the geometry under the ceiling from one horizon of the Flats to the other, in wave after wave, white and violet and blinding, as the Hunt went up toward the gate, and the thunder rolled across the whole of the Flats and drowned out the guns. Every soldier on the slope said afterward that there had been a weight in the air, a pressure on the chest and on the backs of the eyes, and that the hair had stood up on their arms under their sleeves. Chase, in the trench at the foot of the slope, watched the lightning crossing the ceiling with her mouth open, and did not say anything at all.

The defenders came out of the Flamen Gate to meet the assault in a mass of red and bronze. There were the Burned and the Kindled, and the bronze bulls and the hounds, and furnace-engines walking among them, spitting molten iron from their cannon. They chanted as they came, in the old tongue of the deep, and they kept coming through the breachers' fire.

The two forces met on the slope.

The Moonwall came out of their carriers straight into the fighting with their spears burning cold, a silver-blue fire that steamed in the hot air, and the Burned went out in plumes of steam wherever the spears went into them. Lightning came down out of the ceiling wherever Thalia pointed her spear. It struck the bulls and burst their furnaces, and struck the furnace-engines and split them open, and struck the ground among the Kindled and threw them down the slope. The hounds dragged down many of the Hunt's soldiers. But the Moonwall cut its way up the slope after the Lieutenant, and a furnace-engine fell with a Moonwall spear driven through its workings, and when the last of the Burned went out in a hiss of steam on the slope below the gate, the Flamen Gate lay open in front of the Hunt.

---

**THE WARDEN OF TARTARUS**

Kampe came up out of the gorge.

She rose out of the Phlegethon far below on black wings, in a column of river-fire that climbed straight up out of the gorge past the Echo Mast and lit the whole of the Cinder Flats red, as far as the Lethe Cut, as far as the Demus Gorge. Every soldier on the Flats saw it. In the trench at the foot of the north slope, the relay sets went silent all at once. On the Pilgrim Gate, the Achilleans on the bastion roof stopped fighting and turned to look. At Kappa, eleven kilometres away, Sister-Augur Kestrel, who had been reading the deep all through the assault with her hands flat on the floor of the augurs' station, took her hands off the floor and sat back and said, to nobody, that it had come.

She was the warden of Tartarus. She had kept the Hundred-Handed in the pit for the Titans before the Olympians were born. The Kindlers' door had let her up.

She settled on the ramparts of the Flamen Gate and crouched there, looking at the battle on the slope below her.

From the waist up she was a woman as tall as a Behemoth's command deck, with a head of black snakes that hissed and coiled and spat, and round her waist grew the living heads of fifty beasts, wolves and bears and lions and boars and things that had no names, all of them alive, all of them snarling. Below the waist she was a dragon, scaled black and green, with a scorpion's tail rising up behind her over the ramparts and a sting at the end of it as long as a lance. She carried a curved blade in each hand, and the blades smoked with green poison. Shells burst round her on the ramparts without any visible effect.

She came down off the parapet onto the slope.

Her first target was the *Bellows of the Forge*, which had walked up the lower slope behind the Hunt to cover it. Kampe went down the slope to the Behemoth in three great bounds with her wings half spread, and her first blow cut through its wards and its plate, and her second broke it open, and its reactor exploded in a white flash that threw her back up the slope. She came on again. She went on into the Hunt's force, sweeping aside everything within reach of her blades and her tail. A Hunt carrier that could not reverse out of her way was cut open and burned. Two Psiloi were killed by a single stroke of her tail.

Flame-Captain Tharn charged her with her company.

The Moonwall's cold fire was the first thing in the battle to hurt the warden. Tharn's company poured it into her from every side, thirty spears of silver-blue fire, until a storm of cold light surrounded the fight on the slope and Kampe was screaming inside it, and the green poison on her blades was steaming. She struck back blindly through the light and cut down the shieldmaidens one by one. Tharn held her for a long time, while the cold fire drained her own strength out of her, and at the end she gathered what remained of it into her spear and met a blow from Kampe's blade with it.

The spear broke in a burst of light that lit the whole slope white. Tharn fell, disarmed and badly hurt. Her last two shieldmaidens stood over her. Kampe threw one of them down the slope, and caught up the other in her fist and carried her up into the storm.

The Flame-Captain's company had been broken.

---

While Kampe fought the Moonwall, Thalia had gathered every survivor she could find on the slope and led them toward the gate.

Most of the gate and both its towers had been reduced to rubble by the Phaethons, and the fire-shells had cleared the walls on either side of defenders. The Hunt went up toward it through the rubble. Kampe came down out of the storm into the ruined gateway in front of them with her wings spread wide, and dropped the captive shieldmaiden on the stones at her feet, and screamed her challenge at the slope.

The breachers broke. They fell back down the slope, all of them, the shock breachers of the *43rd* and the *50th*, who had gone up the Scree and the Wall and the Ore Gate and never broken anywhere, broke and ran from the gateway. Nobody blamed them for it afterward.

Thalia went up the last of the slope into the gateway alone.

---

**THE FLAMEN GATE**

The Hunt's chronicle describes the duel that followed as a contest between speed and lightning on one side and strength on the other, and that is how the soldiers on the slope remembered it.

Kampe's blows split the stone of the gateway. Thalia turned them, again and again, with the shield on her arm and the shaft of her spear, slipping aside from the blades and under the sweep of the tail, never still. The bolts she called came down wherever her spear touched the warden and burned black marks across the scales. Both of them were wounded within the first minute. Kampe had fought for longer than there had been gods, and she did not tire, and Thalia did. One of Kampe's blades caught her across the left arm, the hand already burned in the lance chamber, and the poison numbed the arm to the shoulder, and the shield dropped. Thalia threw herself flat under a stroke of the tail and came onto her back on the stones of the gateway with the warden standing over her, both blades raised.

The blow was stopped by a shield nobody on the slope could see.

The witnesses described a shimmer in the air a hand's breadth thick, a ripple of heat over the Flats, on which both of Kampe's blades stopped dead, a metre above the Lieutenant's face. A shock went out from it through the gateway in a ring, and threw the dust off every stone in the rubble, and knocked the nearest of the breachers on the slope off their feet.

The boy was standing over Thalia with one hand raised.

---

Minerva had come up the north slope through the rout. She had come from the Pilgrim Gate, along the outer face of the cone, on the ledges below the outer ward, at a run, from the moment Kampe rose out of the gorge, and she had reached the gateway four seconds before the blades came down. The Aegis took both blows. The shock of them came back through it into the boy's arm and shoulder and spine, and Minerva absorbed it and put it out again through the ring, and the Aegis held.

*Your cousin, Varlet,* Minerva noted, to the quiet. *On her back. You would have had something to say about it.*

Kampe looked down at the boy.

The helmet recordings from the gateway show it plainly. The warden looked at the boy standing over the Lieutenant with his hand raised, and her snakes went still, and the fifty heads at her waist fell silent all together. She had known him, or believed she had. She spoke in the old tongue of the pit, and the Hunt's scribes translated it afterward from the recordings.

"*You,*" said Kampe. "*Thief. You are the one who stole my prisoner.*"

Her prisoner had been Briares, the Hundred-Handed, freed from her keeping in the old war by a son of Poseidon called Percy Jackson, fourteen thousand years before.

"You knew someone," said Minerva, in the boy's voice, flat and even. Without looking down, she added: "Lieutenant. On your feet. Her left side."

Thalia stared up at the boy standing over her. She saw his face for the first time. It was the face she had chased up the Themis Needle, the face she had come within a metre of, with her hand out, and frozen.

She got to her feet. She went to the warden's left side.

---

They attacked together.

Kampe struck at each of them in turn, the blades coming down at the boy and at the Lieutenant one after another, and the Aegis turned every blow. Where the shield turned a blade, it opened the warden's guard. Thalia went in under Kampe's left arm through the opening the shield had made and drove her spear into the dragon's flank, below the ring of heads, and called the whole of the ceiling's storm down through it.

Every bolt of lightning crossing the ceiling over the Flats came down into the Flamen Gate at once. The soldiers on the slope were blinded by it for minutes. Kampe reeled back across the gateway, burning along every scale, and lashed out with her tail, and the sting came down at the boy as long as a lance. Minerva caught it on Percy's raised forearm against the Aegis and drove it down, the whole weight of it, into the stone of the gateway, and pinned it there.

"Lieutenant," she said. "Again. The same wound."

Thalia put the spear into the same wound and let go of everything she had left.

Kampe went over backward off the edge of the gateway, where the Flamen Gate looks down into the gorge, and fell, burning, with the lightning still on her and Thalia's spear still in her side. She fell for a long time. The Phlegethon took her at the bottom of the gorge. A column of steam rose out of the gorge as high as the Echo Mast, and hung there over the summit of the cone for most of a cycle.

Thalia's spear went into the river with her.

---

When Thalia turned from the edge, the boy was already walking away, back down the slope toward the stretcher teams.

"Jackson," she said.

He stopped. The helmet recordings show it. He stopped on the slope below the gateway with his back to her, and turned his head, and he spoke in the same flat even voice.

"This is not the time, Lieutenant. Your gate is open. Take it."

Then he went on down the slope, and Thalia stood in the gateway of the Flamen Gate with her left arm hanging numb at her side and watched him go, and didn't follow. The Hunt did not see him again until the Mast.

Atlanta reached the gateway a few minutes later, running, with the first of the Hunters behind her. She found the Lieutenant still standing at the edge, looking down the slope.

"Thalia," she said. "Are you hurt?"

Thalia turned round. She bent and took up a fallen Moonwall spear from the stones of the gateway, and tested its weight in her right hand.

"Bring them up," she said. "All of them. We're going in."

She led the Hunt through the Flamen Gate into the Acropolis.

In the trench at the foot of the slope, the signallers who had watched the duel on a relay feed recorded that Verrill Chase said, several times, to nobody in particular, staring at the screen, "She called him Jackson."

**THE ACROPOLIS. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLES 172 TO 189**

The fall of Kampe was the turning point of the assault on the cone.

With two gates in the Third's hands and the warden of Tartarus in the river, the Archflamen's commanders could no longer hold the outer ward. The Hunt and the breachers of the *43rd* and *50th* went through the Flamen Gate on the evening of the hundred and seventy-first cycle, and over the next eight cycles they cleared the outer ward's northern towers and the curtain walls on either side of the gate, while the Achilleans held the Pilgrim Gate and the colonnade below it against everything that was sent against them. The defenders of the outer ward fell back on the upper terraces, into the Katabasion and the palace and the House of the Hearth. On the hundred and seventy-ninth cycle, the garrison of the Founders' Gate abandoned it, and the *50th* walked up the last seven switchbacks and took it without a fight.

The Third held every gate of the Acropolis and most of its outer ward.

The three attacks and the clearance of the outer ward cost the Third a hundred and four thousand transfers over nineteen cycles, against the Répertoire's estimate of four hundred thousand for the three gates alone. Fewer than nine hundred of them were null. It was the lowest rate of any major engagement since the first barrage of Rotation One, and the Répertoire's technical officers recorded, with some surprise, that most of the uploads from the gate tunnels had cleared along Chase's relay chain from sixty metres inside the rock.

The Hunt's losses were heavier, in proportion. Tharn's company had been broken by Kampe, and Tharn was carried off the slope on a stretcher and took no further part in the siege. Of the eight Hunters of the household who had gone up the north slope with Thalia, two had been killed, and the Hunt did not replace them. The Achilleans had lost more than half their strength. The *Anvil* battle group had lost the *Bellows of the Forge* on the north slope and a second Behemoth on the western switchbacks, and Cradle-Lord Varne reported to the Hunt that he could field two Steropes for the final assault, and nothing more.

---

The collapse of the outer ward came with the departure of the deep.

On the night Kampe fell, Draugos the Kindled went down through the Kindlers' door in the Tabularium on the inner ward, with the Cinderborn and the Red Furnace behind him, and did not come back. The Frost Masons withdrew down their own galleries toward the Cocytus. Ysander Pale and the Unremembered went into the Lethe mist, taking whatever they had taken from the Arsenal with them, and were gone. The Legates had come to the Arsenal for what the war could give them, and with the war lost, they left it to its fate.

Only Hesper Calloway and the remnant of the Forsworn remained, holding the Katabasion. The Hunt's sister-augurs reported that the door behind Draugos had been left open, and that it was now guarded by something else that had come up after Kampe, something they could not read. The Archflamen's surviving soldiers, a few hundred thousand starving defenders on the upper terraces with no line of retreat, were left to fight on for an Acropolis whose masters had gone.

At Watchpoint Hades, the news of the gates reached a Command that had largely given up expecting it. The Directorate's review of the siege, opened in Rotation Sixteen, had been preparing recommendations for the Arsenal's abandonment and the interdiction of Depth 40 without its capture, and the fall of the gates overturned them in a cycle. The Conclave received the Hunt's report of the duel in the Flamen Gate on the hundred and seventy-fourth cycle, and asked for the identity of the operator described in it. The Hunt declined to provide it, on the authority of the Director's seal. The Conclave's delegates recorded their objection. The Pontifical College petitioned the Conclave to spare the Katabasion in the final assault, on the grounds that the reliquary of the First Descent was the holiest shrine in the theatre. The Hunt replied that the Forsworn were inside it. The petition was logged and not granted.

---

For Thalia, sent down to Depth 40 to learn whether she would freeze again, the duel in the Flamen Gate answered the Director's question.

She knew it, and she suspected the Director knew it too. It also confirmed what her officers had suspected since the Scree: that the Third was being helped by an intelligence of the first rank that answered to no seal in the Empire, and that the Hunt's standing orders required her to take it.

She made no move to take it.

Over the cycles that followed, the Hunt's staff prepared the final assault on the Acropolis, and the Psiloi reported that the boy had gone up the Pilgrim Stair after the gate fell and had not come down. Thalia read the report and put it aside.

The Third had the gates. What it lacked was the strength to clear the cone quickly. On the hundred and ninetieth cycle, that strength arrived, from a quarter the Third had not asked.
