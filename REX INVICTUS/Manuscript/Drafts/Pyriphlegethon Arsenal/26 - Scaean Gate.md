## Chapter 26: Scaean Gate

*"Our founder was offered a long life and an obscure one, or a short life and a name. He chose. We have chosen the same in every generation since. You will forgive us if we do not find your vats as comforting as you do."*

*Polemarch Teukros Avel, Third Lochos of the Achillean Order*

---

**QUARTERDECK NINETEEN. ROTATION FOURTEEN, CYCLE 310**

The delegates of the Dionysian Logistics Directorate came down the cables at Kappa on the three hundred and tenth cycle of Rotation Fourteen, four of them, in grey travelling coats with the vine-and-ledger seal of the Directorate on their collars, and sat down at the long table in the briefing room on Quarterdeck Nineteen across from Kastellan and his staff, and laid out their slates in front of them in a neat row, and Kastellan knew before any of them had said a word what they had come to tell him.

He'd seen that row of slates before. Everybody who'd ever commanded a siege had seen it.

"Marshal," said the senior delegate. His name was Lysias Corde, and he was a soft-voiced, round-faced, courteous man of about sixty with immaculately clean hands. "Thank you for receiving us. You'll know why we're here."

"I can guess."

"The Répertoire's revised end-date for the reduction of the Arsenal passed nearly two rotations ago," Corde said. "The Directorate accepted the delay at the time, and found the shells for it. It has now completed a further review of the campaign." He touched the first slate. "The Third was given twelve rotations to retake the Arsenal. It has used them, Marshal, and more, without victory."

"I'm aware of it."

"The Directorate has concluded that it can no longer meet the Third's demands while other fronts want the same shells and the same soldiers. The Cocytus front. The reformation peaks at Depth 12." Corde's voice didn't change. "The reduction of the Arsenal will lose its priority. It will not be abandoned. The Archflamen still has to be defeated and the Arsenal still has to be returned to the Empire. But it will take longer, and in the end it will cost more."

Nobody on Kastellan's side of the table moved.

"The figures," Kastellan said.

Corde gave them. He gave them in the same soft courteous voice, one slate at a time, and Dray wrote them down beside Kastellan though they all had copies. Under the new plan, the Third's replacements from the ceiling worlds would be cut from about a hundred and ninety thousand soldiers a rotation to a hundred and ten thousand. Its allocation of shells would be cut by two fifths, the balance going to the Cocytus and Depth 12. The Ordnance Phalanxes would be placed on a ration of twelve shells per gun per watch outside the preparation of an attack. There would be no further review for five rotations.

"Twelve shells," said the commander of the *10th* Ordnance Phalanx, at the end of the table, quietly. "Per gun. Per watch."

"Outside the preparation of an attack," Corde said.

"And how often does the Directorate imagine we'll be preparing attacks, on two fifths less?"

Corde looked at him courteously and didn't answer.

Kastellan argued it. He'd known he would, and he'd known it would make no difference, and he argued it anyway for most of the cycle, with Dray's figures and the Répertoire's figures and the *10th*'s figures spread across the table, point by point. The Third was through the inner belt. It had a breach in the Cinder Wall. It was closer to the Acropolis than it had been at any time in fourteen rotations. The Archflamen's losses were final and the Third's weren't. A siege of attrition always favoured the besieger who was properly supplied, and the Directorate was proposing to stop supplying it at the precise moment when the attrition had begun to tell.

The delegates listened to all of it, politely, and wrote some of it down. At the end of the cycle Corde gathered his slates into a neat pile and squared their edges with his clean hands.

"Marshal," he said, "I understand your position. I'll report it fully. But I should be honest with you. The new plan has already been sealed on the ceiling worlds by the Directorate's masters. It isn't ours to change." He paused. "I would also observe, if you'll forgive me, that a siege army which still numbers more than twenty million soldiers can hardly plead poverty."

Kastellan looked at him for a long moment.

"An army of the Third's size," he said, "can't be fed with shells at that rate. If you cut it to twelve a gun a watch, it'll have to stop attacking. Write that down, Delegate. In the minutes. In those words."

Corde wrote it down.

---

Kastellan sent for his staff that night, after the delegates had gone back up to Kappa.

"We need help," he said. "From somewhere that isn't the Directorate. Somewhere that doesn't draw on its stores and doesn't answer to its review and doesn't care what the Directorate's masters sealed on the ceiling worlds."

"Marshal," said Dray, "there isn't anywhere."

"There's always somewhere. Find it." Kastellan looked round the table. "Tribune Holloway. You'll take a delegation up the cables. Four officers. Go where you like, ask who you like. I don't care who it is or what they want. Find me somebody who can fight and will come."

Tribune Marius Holloway was the youngest of Kastellan's senior staff, thirty-eight, a long dry clever man from the Cocytus front with a habit of looking at the ceiling while he thought. He looked at it now.

"Anyone, Marshal?"

"Anyone."

Holloway went up the cables at Kappa two cycles later. He was gone for most of a rotation.

---

**THE RELAY STATION AT EREBUS NINE. ROTATION FIFTEEN, CYCLE 12**

He found them at Erebus Nine, a day's flight above the Flats, on the twelfth cycle of Rotation Fifteen, and afterward he could never decide whether it had been luck or something else.

Erebus Nine was a relay station on a shelf of black rock above the Erebus shallows, a cluster of fuel tanks and water cisterns and a docking cradle for ships passing up and down between the deep and the ceiling, and when Holloway's cable car came into it on the twelfth cycle, there was a ship in the cradle he'd never seen before.

She was small. Smaller than a Lantern frigate. Long and lean and dark, built for speed, with a sharp prow and swept-back fins and a hull of dull grey plate scarred and pitted along its whole length, and painted on her bow, in faded bronze, a shield. Under the shield, a name. *Thetis Mourning.*

"What is she?" Holloway asked the station master.

The station master looked at the ship, and at him, and seemed surprised he didn't know.

"Achillean," he said. "Strike-ship of the Order. Came in yesterday from the shallows. They've been down in the old flood-tunnels under Erebus, burning out a nest of drakon-breeders. Taking on fuel and water." He paused. "You'll not want to bother them, Tribune."

Holloway went to bother them.

---

Polemarch Teukros Avel of the Third Lochos of the Achillean Order received the Third's delegation in the strike-ship's wardroom, a narrow low cabin with a table of dark wood and a shield on the wall, at the end of the dark watch, and offered them wine, and listened to them for two hours without interrupting once.

He was a big man, though not as big as Holloway had expected, broad and deep-chested, with close-cut grey hair and a short grey beard and a face that had been handsome once and was now a map of scars, and he sat at the head of the table in a plain dark tunic with his large scarred hands folded in front of him and his grey eyes on whichever of them was speaking. Two of his officers sat with him. One of them was a tall thin silent woman with a fresh burn along her jaw, whom he introduced as Lochagos Ithra Vell of the Sixth Lochos. The other was an old man, ancient, with a long white beard and milky blind-looking eyes, who sat at Avel's shoulder and said nothing at all and seemed to be looking at something on the far side of the wardroom wall. Avel introduced him as Mantis Orrin Kaledos, the Order's seer.

Holloway knew something about the Order. Everyone on the Tartarus front knew something about it, and most of what they knew was wrong. He'd read the file at Erebus Nine overnight, in the station master's office, and learned the rest.

It was small and old. Fewer than nine hundred warriors, in nine lochoi, founded upon a vow made over a shield, so long ago that the Répertoire had no record of the date. Its warriors were legacies of Achilles, and of Thetis, and of the Myrmidons who had followed him to Troy. And they took the short life. No warrior of the Achillean Order carried a Hades implant. When one of them died, the Order burned the body and said the name before the shield at the Order's Hearth on the ceiling, and that was all. There was no more. Their founder had been offered a long and obscure life and had chosen the other, and the Order had made the same choice in every generation since.

The Tartarus front's commanders had never wanted them. Soldiers who couldn't be returned were an extravagance the theatre couldn't afford. So the Order fought where it chose, on the ceiling worlds and in the shallows of the deep, and its strike-ships went wherever its Hearth judged that a few warriors who didn't fear their own deaths could do what larger forces had not. It drew nothing from the Directorate's stores. It accepted no Flamen and kept its own rites, and the Pontifical College disliked it intensely.

Holloway had thought, reading the file, that all of that was in its favour.

At the end of the two hours Avel unfolded his hands.

"You've been honest with me, Tribune," he said. His voice was deep and slow and courteous. "I'll be honest with you. The Order doesn't fight in the deep in strength. It never has. Your theatre's commanders have made it plain for a great many years that they don't want us, and the Hearth has been content to oblige them." He looked at Vell. Vell looked back at him, expressionless. "And I have ninety warriors aboard this ship, from four lochoi, and a ship that needs a refit, and a Hearth on the ceiling that will want to know why I've taken them down to Depth 40 to die in a crater for an army that thinks of death as an inconvenience."

"I understand, Polemarch."

"I don't think you do." Avel's voice was quite gentle. "Your Marshal sends soldiers into that crater by the hundred thousand, and they die, and they come back through your vats in a few weeks, and he sends them in again. That's your war. I don't criticise it. But every one of my warriors who dies in that crater won't come back. Ever. I'd burn them at the Hearth and say their names before the shield, and that would be the end of them." He paused. "You see why I can't commit this ship on my own authority, Tribune. Though the Order's customs would allow it."

Holloway was silent for a moment.

"Polemarch," he said, "I've seen the crater. I was there for the second assault. And I can tell you that the soldiers who go into it now don't think of death as an inconvenience. They come back through the vats slower, every time. They come back with less in their heads. Some of them don't come back at all. One in five, on the rim, they say now. The Third has transferred more than forty million soldiers on the Cinder Flats and close to two million have come back blank or not at all." He held Avel's eyes. "We're not as different as you think, Polemarch. Not any more."

Avel looked at him for a long time.

Then he turned his head, slightly, toward the old seer at his shoulder.

Kaledos hadn't moved. He was still looking at the wardroom wall, or through it, with his milky eyes. But he said, after a moment, in a thin dry papery voice, "The gate."

Avel had gone utterly still.

"What gate?" Holloway asked.

Nobody answered him.

"I'll put your Marshal's request to the Hearth," Avel said at last. "That's all I can promise. I'll put it to them myself, as strongly as I can, and they'll answer as they choose." He stood. "Thank you for the wine, Tribune. Forgive me. It was yours."

---

**QUARTERDECK NINETEEN. ROTATION FIFTEEN, CYCLE 140**

The Hearth's answer came down to Quarterdeck Nineteen on the hundred and fortieth cycle of Rotation Fifteen, a hundred and twenty-eight cycles after Holloway's meeting at Erebus Nine, written in an old formal hand on a single sheet of thick cream paper sealed with the shield.

The Order would come down to the Flats. Avel's company would come, the ninety warriors of the *Thetis Mourning*, and assess the siege on the ground before deciding where it could do the most good. On one condition. The Polemarch would be under no one's command, and he alone would decide where and how his warriors fought.

Kastellan read it once, and signed his acceptance on the back of it, and sent it back up the cables the same watch.

"Anyone," Dray said, watching him. "You said anyone, Marshal."

"I did," Kastellan said. "And I'm glad of them. I'd be glad of a company of Frost Masons if they'd fight for us, Dray, with the Directorate cutting us off." He put down the stylus. "Find them quarters. The best we've got. And send me everything we have on the crater."

**THE CRATER, LEAF 57-44. ROTATION FIFTEEN, CYCLES 160 TO 176**

Avel came down the cables at Kappa on the hundred and sixtieth cycle with Vell and Kaledos and nine of his officers, and spent sixteen cycles on the Flats.

He went everywhere. He walked the line from the Mora Cut to the Lethe Cut, on foot, in a plain grey field coat without insignia, with Vell beside him and the old seer leaning on Vell's arm, and the soldiers of the Third who saw him pass didn't know who he was. He went into the bays and talked to the centurions. He went to the cradle-yard and talked to Varne for a whole watch. He went down the galleries under Leaf 56-46 with Ansel Rook, barefoot, ninety metres down, and stood in the dark at the face with his hand on the rock for a long time. He went to the *35th*'s trenches on Leaf 57-44 and stood at the parapet beside Abel Strand on his steel leg and looked at the crater through a periscope for most of a cycle.

On the hundred and seventy-sixth cycle, he and his officers held their own council in the quarters Kastellan had given them, and nobody from the Third was present. They sat all night. At the first bell Avel came to Quarterdeck Nineteen and stood in front of Kastellan's desk.

"The crater," he said.

Kastellan looked up at him.

"Leaf 57-44. The breach at the Wall. It's the one place on the Cinder Flats where ninety warriors can change the outcome of your siege, Marshal. Everywhere else, you need millions. There, you need ninety, if they're the right ninety, and if they strike at the right moment, and if they don't stop." Avel put his scarred hands flat on the desk. "We'll take the crater for you. We'll hold it until your armour comes through. Then we'll give it to you and go home."

Kastellan was silent for a long moment.

"What will it cost you?" he asked.

"A great deal."

"How much?"

"Kaledos says two in three," Avel said. "He's usually right."

Kastellan looked at him.

"Polemarch," he said quietly, "I've sent a great many soldiers into that crater. I've never once been told, before I did it, that two in three of them wouldn't come back. Not ever. Not one of them."

"No," Avel said. "That's the difference between us, Marshal. We know." He straightened. "We've named the operation. It's the custom. Scaean Gate."

Kastellan nodded, and wrote it down.

His staff looked the name up afterward, in the archive, and found what it meant. The Scaean Gate of Troy. The gate where Achilles fell. Dray brought the entry to Kastellan in his office that night, on a slate, and Kastellan read it, and put the slate face down on the desk, and didn't say anything.

The staff wished they hadn't looked it up.

**THE SHAFT HEAD BEHIND LEAF 57-44. ROTATION FIFTEEN, CYCLE 180**

The plan committed everything the Order had, including the *Thetis Mourning* herself, to a single fast blow in the dark watch, and everything depended on its timing.

Its point would be the Order's First Lochos. Thirteen warriors, the oldest in Avel's company, in Myrmidon plate forged by the Order's own smiths for each wearer and earned over a lifetime. Avel would lead them himself, with Kaledos at his side. And they wouldn't cross no-man's land at all. They would translate. The *Thetis Mourning* carried a translation chamber, an old and rare thing, which could move a warrior from the ship's deck to a point on the ground below her in a fraction of a second, if the ship could come close enough, and if there was a beacon on the ground for the chamber to lock onto. The translation had to be exact. A translation that missed by twenty metres would put the First Lochos inside the rubble of the crater rim, inside solid rock, and that would be the end of them.

So the strike-ship would have to come down as close to the Flats as she could, within reach of the ceiling-lances on the cone. And somebody would have to put a beacon on the crater floor, in the middle of the Archflamen's defences, in the dark, before she came.

No Achillean could reach the crater floor unseen. Avel said so to Kastellan, at the briefing, plainly.

"I need a sapper," he said.

---

Ansel Rook got the task because he'd been down the galleries with Avel on the hundred and sixty-eighth cycle, and because Avel had asked for him by name.

He felt far older than the five rotations since he had come down the cables. He was a sapper-captain of the *66th* now, with two hundred and eleven sappers left of the company he'd taken into the diversion and three hundred new ones from the Forge's drafts, and a long pink scar under his ribs, and a letter from his mother in the pocket of his tunic. He came to the briefing in the *35th*'s command bunker and stood at the back and listened to Avel describe what he wanted, and at the end of it he said, before anyone else could speak, "The *61st*'s galleries."

Avel looked at him.

"Under Leaf 57-44," Rook said. "The ones Gault's company dug to lay the Great Mine. Nine hundred metres from the shaft to the footings, eighty-six metres down. The mine went off at the end of them and brought down the last three hundred metres, and the Frost Masons have packed most of the side galleries with ice since. But not all of them." He came forward to the map table. "There's a branch. Here. A listening gallery the *61st* drove off the main gallery to the south, under the crater rim, before the mine was fired. I've been in it. The mine cracked the rock above it and opened a cleft. I think the cleft comes out under a block on the crater floor." He put his finger on the map. "Here. Under the south slope. If it's still open, a party could come up through it onto the crater floor in the dark, right under the Archflamen's noses."

"Can you get there?"

"I think so. It'll be tight. Half a metre, in places, where the rock's come down." Rook looked at the map. "And there'll be ice."

Avel was quiet a moment.

"How many will you need?"

"Four," Rook said. "Volunteers."

He asked the *66th* for four volunteers that afternoon, at the shaft head. He got eleven. He took four: Sapper Iolo Brenn, a quiet broad-faced veteran of the diversion who had come out of the ninth counter-mine alive when Cole hadn't; Sapper Kara Nysse, small and fast and fearless, the best climber in the company; and from the *61st*, who had heard what was happening and come down the line to volunteer though nobody had asked them, Wynn Tallis and Meriel Toft, who had made the carry under Leaf 57-44 for a hundred and twelve cycles and knew every metre of the *61st*'s galleries by touch in the dark.

"You'll be the last of the carry," Toft said to Tallis, at the shaft head, the night before.

"What?"

"The *61st*'s galleries. We carried the mine down them. Now we're carrying the beacon." Toft smiled at her. "Last load."

---

**THE *61st*'S GALLERIES, BENEATH LEAF 57-44. ROTATION FIFTEEN, CYCLE 189, DARK WATCH**

They went down the *61st*'s old shaft at the fourth hour of the dark watch on the hundred and eighty-ninth cycle, five of them, barefoot, with the beacon in its padded case on Toft's back.

It was the size of a ration crate and weighed thirty kilograms, a squat drum of bronze and black glass with a ring of crystal lenses round its crown and a firing stud on its side under a hinged cover, and an Achillean artificer had spent an hour that afternoon showing Rook how to set it going. It had to be on the crater floor. It had to be standing upright. It had to be uncovered and switched on and left alone for ninety seconds while it reached out into the high geometry and found the *Thetis Mourning*'s translation chamber and locked, and in those ninety seconds, the artificer said, it would glow.

"Glow how much?" Rook had asked.

The artificer had looked at him with something close to sympathy.

"Enough," he'd said.

The main gallery was as Rook remembered it, for the first six hundred metres. Two sappers wide. A metre and a half high. Black basalt, smooth and dry and hot, cut by the *61st*'s wrapped picks a flake at a time. Tallis led. She walked it in the dark without a lamp, one hand on the wall, counting under her breath, and Rook realised after a hundred metres that she was counting her steps from the shaft, as she had on the carry, and that she knew exactly where she was.

At six hundred and forty metres the gallery ended in a wall of ice.

It filled the gallery from floor to roof, grey and cloudy and cold enough to burn, and their lamps went into it a hand's breadth and stopped. The Frost Masons had packed the main gallery from here to the footings. Rook had known they would. He'd found the edge of the ice on the hundred and sixty-eighth cycle, with Avel.

"Here," Tallis whispered. It was the first word anyone had spoken. She put her hand on the right-hand wall of the gallery, a metre short of the ice. "The listening gallery. The branch. It went off here."

There was no branch. There was a wall of basalt, rough and broken, where the roof had come down in the shock of the Great Mine.

"It's behind," Tallis said. "It's behind the fall. I know it is. It went off at step one thousand six hundred and twelve and there was a listener's niche at step eight and I carried past it four hundred times."

Rook put his palm on the rock, and closed his eyes, and listened, as Voss had taught him.

"Picks," he said.

They dug for two hours. Brenn and Nysse at the face, kneeling, with picks wrapped in three layers of cloth, taking the fallen basalt down a fragment at a time and passing it back hand to hand in silence, and Toft holding the beacon, and Tallis sitting against the gallery wall with her eyes shut, and Rook listening at the ice for the Frost Masons. At the end of the second hour Nysse's pick went through into empty dark.

The listening gallery was half a metre high.

Rook went in first. He went in on his belly, with his lamp hooded and his pistol in his hand and the beacon's case dragged behind him on a cord, and the roof pressed down on his back and the rock on either side pressed against his shoulders, and he crawled. The mine had cracked and shifted the whole of the rock round the listening gallery, so that it had sagged and twisted and in places almost closed, and he wormed through the narrowest places with his face pressed to the stone and the cold of the Frost Masons' ice coming through the rock from the main gallery beside him. There was ice in the side galleries off the listening gallery, too, where they branched away to the listening niches. He felt it on his face as he passed them. In one place it had crept into the listening gallery itself and half filled it, and they cut through it with their knives a sliver at a time, and the slivers closed behind them as they went.

It took three hours. At the end of the third hour the gallery tilted upward under him and opened into a cleft.

He felt the air change first. Hot, and dry, and full of the smell of the Flats: burnt stone, cordite, rot. He felt it on his face, coming down the cleft from above. And then he heard the guns. Faint, far off, the Third's barrage coming down on the Wall to the north. And closer, much closer, voices. Two of them, talking low in the grunt. Footsteps on rubble.

He lay in the cleft and looked up. Twenty metres above him, through a gap between two great tilted blocks of the Wall's footings, he could see the brown dark of the sky over the crater, lit red from below.

He signed back down the gallery. *Here.*

They came up the cleft one after another in the dark, Tallis and Toft and Brenn and Nysse, and lay in the bottom of it, under the block, on the floor of the crater the Great Mine had made, among the dead of seven assaults.

---

**THE *THETIS MOURNING*. ROTATION FIFTEEN, CYCLE 189, ELEVENTH HOUR**

At the eleventh hour of the dark watch, the *Thetis Mourning* came down out of the high geometry over the Cinder Flats with her running lights dark.

Teukros Avel stood in the translation chamber amidships with the thirteen warriors of the First Lochos round him and felt her fall.

The chamber was a round bronze room at the heart of the ship, with a floor of black glass inlaid with a great silver ring, and the First Lochos stood within the ring in their plate. Myrmidon plate. Every piece of it forged for the warrior who wore it, by the Order's smiths at the Hearth, over a lifetime, and earned a piece at a time. It was dark bronze, old and scarred and polished, with the shield of the Order on every breastplate, and every warrior of the First Lochos had worn theirs for longer than most soldiers of the Third had been alive. They carried spears, and hammers, and the great round shields of the Order. Avel's own shield had been his teacher's, and his teacher's teacher's, back eleven generations. He'd counted.

Kaledos stood beside him in a plain grey robe, without armour, leaning on a staff, with his milky eyes half closed.

"Four minutes," said the ship's captain over the band, from the bridge. Her voice was steady. "The lances will find us in four minutes. Maybe less."

"Hold your course," Avel said.

"I'll hold it, Polemarch."

He felt the ship shudder. He felt her wards come up, and her guns run out, and the long scream of the hull as she came down through the ceiling geometry toward the Flats, closer, closer, inside the envelope of the lances. He felt the Lochos round him in the ring, still and silent in their plate.

At the third minute, the first lance found her.

He felt it strike. A long blow along the flank, through the hull, and the ship lurched and heeled over, and the wards screamed, and on the band the captain was calling damage in the same steady voice. The second lance came a moment later. He felt it burn along the flank behind the first. He smelled it, even in the chamber: hot metal, scorched paint, ozone. The ship's guns were firing. The wards were fighting the beams, flaring and burning and failing and coming back.

The captain held her course.

"The beacon," Avel said. "Kaledos."

The old seer said nothing. His eyes were closed now. His lips were moving.

---

**THE CRATER FLOOR. ROTATION FIFTEEN, CYCLE 189, ELEVENTH HOUR**

The sentry found them before the beacon was ready.

Rook never knew how. They'd been lying in the cleft under the block for an hour in the dark, without a sound, waiting for the eleventh hour, while the Archflamen's soldiers moved on the slope above them and talked and coughed and cleaned their rifles. Then at the eleventh hour Toft had brought the beacon up out of the cleft onto the crater floor and stood it upright in the shadow of the block, and Rook had lifted the hinged cover on its side and pressed the stud, and the ring of crystal lenses round its crown had begun, faintly, to glow.

It was blue, a soft clear cold moonlit blue, and it grew brighter.

"Enough," the artificer had said. It was enough. Within thirty seconds it was lighting up the underside of the block above them and the rubble round them and their faces, and somewhere on the slope above, a voice called out sharply in the grunt.

Then a lamp. Swinging. Coming down the slope toward the light.

"Ninety seconds," Rook whispered. "We need ninety seconds. Hold them off."

They held them off.

The sentry came down the rubble with his lamp and his rifle and saw the beacon glowing under the block and the five sappers crouched round it, and opened his mouth to shout, and Brenn shot him. The shot was enormous in the silence of the crater. The whole slope above them woke at once. Rook heard voices shouting, all along it, and boots on rubble, and then the first rounds came down out of the dark, smacking into the block and the stones round them and whining away, and a flare went up, a long way off, and burst, and hung over the crater, and in its hard white light he saw the Archflamen's soldiers coming down the slope toward them. Twenty of them. Thirty. Thralls in grey and Sworn in black, with rifles, running and sliding down the rubble.

"Sixty seconds," Rook said.

They fought from the shadow of the block. Brenn and Nysse and Tallis on the right, Rook and Toft on the left with the beacon between them, firing up the slope with their shotguns and pistols, and the beacon glowing brighter and brighter under the block behind them, blue and clear and cold, until it lit up the whole floor of the crater round them and the Archflamen's soldiers could see them as plainly as by day. The first thralls reached the foot of the slope. Tallis shot one at five metres. Toft shot another.

The beacon flickered.

Rook looked round. A round had struck it, high on its bronze drum, and gouged a long bright scar in the metal, and the ring of lenses had gone dim and was flickering, on and off, uncertain.

"It's not locking," Toft said. "Captain. It's not locking. It's been hit."

Rook dropped his shotgun and went to it on his knees. He didn't know what to do. The artificer had told him nothing about this. He put his hands on the drum, on either side of the scar, and held it, willing it steady, and felt it hum under his palms.

Brenn was hit at the seventy-fifth second. Rook heard him grunt, and turned his head, and saw him fall back against the block with his hands on his chest, and then slide down it. He lay in the rubble at the foot of the block with his eyes open. Rook heard his implant tick.

He had died on the surface, within reach of a relay, and his upload went to the relay at the *35th*'s command bunker eight hundred metres away in less than a second, and came in clean, Rook learned afterward. He woke at Watchpoint Hades twenty-two cycles later and asked first of all whether the beacon had locked.

Nysse was hit at the eightieth second, in the shoulder and then in the thigh, and went down on the rubble beside Tallis and didn't get up, and Tallis knelt over her firing.

The beacon steadied under Rook's hands. The lenses brightened. Blue, clear, cold.

It locked.

He felt it lock. A sudden deep stillness in the drum under his palms, and a sound, high and pure and ringing, a struck-glass note, that went up out of the beacon into the dark sky over the crater and away. The three of them still standing, Rook and Toft and Tallis, round the beacon at the foot of the block, with Brenn dead and Nysse down, and the Archflamen's soldiers at the foot of the slope twenty metres away coming at them in the light of the flare. Rook stood up from the beacon and drew his pistol.

The air above the crater floor, ten metres away, folded.

---

**THE CRATER. ROTATION FIFTEEN, CYCLE 189, ELEVENTH HOUR**

The First Lochos arrived precisely on the beacon.

Rook saw it happen, and could never describe it afterward. The air folded, and opened, and there were thirteen warriors standing in the rubble of the crater floor where there had been nothing, in dark bronze plate, with great round shields and spears and hammers, among the dead of seven assaults. They stood quite still for perhaps a second. Then the one in the centre, who carried a shield older than the others, turned his head, and looked at the beacon, and at Rook, and at Brenn lying at the foot of the block, and at Nysse, and nodded once.

The Archflamen's soldiers on the slope fired on them.

The rounds struck the Myrmidon plate and the great shields and fell away. Rook saw it. He saw a burst from a Sworn's rifle at ten metres go into the breastplate of the warrior nearest him and simply stop, and the flattened rounds fall into the rubble at the warrior's feet, and the warrior didn't even turn his head. Then the First Lochos went up the slope.

They went up it methodically. That was the word Rook used for it afterward, when the Order's chroniclers came to him to ask, and it was the right word, though it wasn't enough. Thirteen warriors in a line, shields locked, going up the rubble of the crater's south slope at a steady walk, with their spears out over the rim of the shields and their hammers in their other hands, clearing it. The Archflamen's soldiers came down at them and died. Rook saw thralls and Sworn throw themselves at the line of shields and be thrown back off it, broken, and the spears going in and out over the shields, and the hammers coming down. Nobody else in the crater could have told what was happening. It was dark, and the flare had burned out, and the beacon's blue light was fading, and there were only the shapes of the Lochos going up the slope in the dark and the sounds.

Kaledos walked at Avel's shoulder.

He had no armour. He had no weapon but his staff. He walked up the slope beside the Polemarch in his plain grey robe, leaning on the staff, with his milky eyes half shut, and now and then he lifted his free hand and pointed, at a thrall, at a Sworn, at a rifle pit in the rubble above them. And where he pointed, Rook saw, the defenders' weapons were found to be already empty. Their rifles clicked on nothing. Their grenades didn't go off. Or their feet were found to be already turned, and they were running, without knowing why, back up the slope away from the old man in the grey robe. Those who didn't run didn't survive.

Rook went up the slope after them. He didn't decide to. He found himself doing it, with Toft's shotgun and a dead Sworn's rifle and his sapper's entrenching blade, and Tallis came with him, and Toft stayed with Nysse at the beacon.

---

Behind them, Vell's force crossed no-man's land.

Rook didn't see that either. He learned it afterward. The second force of the Order, under Lochagos Ithra Vell of the Sixth Lochos, came out of the *35th*'s trenches the moment the First Lochos was in the crater, and drove for the crater across the open ground under the Wall's searchlights and guns. They came with Kriophoros siege tanks, the Order's own, squat and heavy, with great ram-headed prows built for breaking walls. They came with heavy weapons firing into the parapet of the Wall on either side of the gap, and the Phaethons of the *10th* Ordnance Phalanx dropping their pre-ranged fire on the towers. They came with three of the Order's Ancients.

The Ancients were warriors too badly broken to fight and too stubborn to be burned. Rook had seen them, at the Order's quarters on the outer belt, the cycle before: three great walker-coffins of bronze and iron, five metres tall, on legs built to a Behemoth's pattern in miniature, with the shield of the Order on their fronts, and inside each of them, sealed in the dark in a cradle of fluid, what was left of a warrior of the Order who had been dying and had chosen not to. Ancient Aias, who had been a priest of the Order once, with a lance arm and a great hammer. Ancient Rhesos. Ancient Dolon, who carried a rock-breaking drill and a fire-lance for siege work.

A shell threw Vell into the air on the open ground in front of the crater rim. Strand saw it from the *35th*'s parapet, and told the story for the rest of his life. He saw the thin tall woman go up in the burst of a shell, five metres into the air, turning over, and come down in the ash, and lie there. And then get up. And go on, walking, toward the crater, with her spear.

Her leading squad reached the rim with Ancient Aias. Dolon came up behind them and set his drill against the largest of the blocks on the rim, the ones the *10th*'s Phaethons had shelled for fourteen cycles and failed to break, and the drill broke them. It went into them with a long grinding shriek and split them open, one after another, and Dolon walked into the gaps and broke them wider, and opened a road through the rubble for the Kriophoros tanks. And the main body of Vell's force went down through the crater and up its far side under plunging fire from the towers without halting once.

---

At the top of the slope, the First Lochos met the Laistrygons.

They came down off the rim in the dark as they had come down onto the *35th* in Rotation Twelve: forty of them, giants, dosed with furnace-draught, with their eyes burning and their casting-line tools in their hands, roaring. Rook was behind the Lochos, twenty metres down the slope, with Tallis. He saw them come over the rim in the light of the burning towers, and he thought of Edrin Solk, and of the *21st* Cohort, and his heart turned over in his chest.

The charge broke on the Achilleans' shields.

He saw it break. He saw forty Laistrygons, four metres tall, come down the slope at a run onto thirteen warriors in a line, and strike the line, and the line didn't move. It took the shock on its shields and held, all thirteen together, and then went forward into them. The spears went up into the Laistrygons' bellies and chests over the shields. The hammers came round. Rook saw a Laistrygon bring a slag hammer down on Avel's shield, two-handed, with the whole weight of its body behind it, and the shield take it, and the Polemarch step forward under the blow and put his spear up through the giant's jaw into its skull, and step back, and the giant fall.

The hammers and spears of the First Lochos did to the Laistrygons what no trench soldier of the Third had ever managed in fifteen rotations of siege.

Rook fought beside Avel at the top of the slope. He didn't remember deciding to do that either. He was there, somehow, at the Polemarch's left shoulder, where a gap had opened in the line when a warrior of the Lochos went down, with the dead Sworn's rifle firing into the Laistrygons until it was empty, and then with the entrenching blade. He killed a thrall with it who came over the rim behind the giants. He never knew whether he'd killed anything else.

The Order's chroniclers asked him about it afterward, at length, and wrote down what he told them, and later he learned that they had entered his name in the Order's chronicle of Scaean Gate in the same column as the First Lochos's own. He never saw the chronicle. Vell told him.

When Vell and Ancient Aias reached their Polemarch at the top of the slope, the Order held the crater.

In a single dark watch it had done what the Third had failed to do in two rotations. The garrison had been unable to stop the translation, or the speed of the attack, or whatever it was that Kaledos did. Avel formed Vell's warriors into a perimeter round the crater and among the broken ends of the Wall, with the Kriophoros tanks hull-down in the gaps and the Ancients between them, and turned to his signaller and gave her the codeword.

Rook heard it. It was a single word, in the old tongue. He didn't know what it meant. Tallis, who had grown up in a hold where the old tongue was still spoken in the sapper hall, told him later that it meant *here*.

---

**HERE WE STAND. ROTATION FIFTEEN, CYCLE 190**

The Archflamen had planned for a breach in the Wall. He'd kept a reaction force of tanks and carrier-borne infantry behind it for two rotations against exactly this, and his commanders sent it in the moment word of the crater reached them, and the drowned Behemoths came after it.

They had to hold until the Ninth and the Behemoths arrived.

Avel put his heavy weapons in the gap and held the First Lochos back behind them as a reserve. Under accurate shellfire, the Achilleans dug in among the blocks and threw up barricades of rubble and wreckage and the bodies of Laistrygons. Rook dug with them. He was a sapper. It was the one thing he was sure he knew how to do.

Behind them, through the road Dolon had broken in the rim, came the *9th* Assault Phalanx's battle group. Its engineers came first, to clear the remaining obstacles with charges and dozers, and then its tanks, each company with a Colossus, and then its breachers and its field guns, grinding up out of the *35th*'s lines and across no-man's land in the dark toward the crater in a long column.

At the turn of the watch, the Archflamen's counter-attack began.

The drowned Behemoths raked the breach from long range. Rook saw them, far off, through the smoke beyond the Wall: three of them, black and scaled and burning, standing on the open ground at the cone's foot among the ruins of the shanty-town, and their guns firing into the crater. They brought down more of the Wall. A shell from one of them struck one of the Order's transports in the gap and it went up in a long sheet of flame. A fragment from another took off Ancient Aias's lance arm at the shoulder, and Rook saw the great bronze coffin rock back on its legs with a long screech of torn metal and then steady, and Aias's voice came out of the speaker grille in its front, deep and slow and calm, saying something in the old tongue that made the warriors round him laugh.

The Third's siege guns replied from the rear of the Flats, and it was too little. Myrmidon plate wouldn't stop a Phlegethon-cannon, and the Order's position depended on Varne's engines arriving before the drowned engines took it apart.

The *Anvil of Talos* came through the breach first, at full stride.

Rook felt it before he saw it, through the ground, the long slow booming tread through the rubble, and then it came up out of the dark behind them and over the rim of the crater in a single stride, eighty metres tall, bronze-black, with its honour banners burned to rags and its wards blazing, and two Steropes behind it. Its augurs had already found the drowned Behemoths. Its gunnery crews had already worked out their solutions. Its three lance-batteries fired together as it came over the rim, on the nearest drowned engine, and the drowned engine's wards lit up red and failed, and it staggered.

The Behemoths fought their own duel at the cone's foot while the Order and the Ninth dealt with the Archflamen's armour, and the first of the Ninth's Cyclopean lance-tanks came crawling across the crater floor to join the engagement.

The counter-attack pressed hard through the whole of the cycle. Several times the Archflamen's breachers reached the crater, and each time the First Lochos threw them out.

Avel was struck at the ninth hour.

Rook was ten metres from him. He saw it. A Phlegethon-lance, fired from a carrier at the foot of the cone, put a jet of river-fire into the line of shields at the top of the slope, and it struck the Polemarch full in the chest, and burned through his breastplate and into his chest behind it. Rook saw the bronze glow red and then white and then run. He saw Avel stand there in the jet with his shield still raised for what seemed a long time and then go down on one knee.

He didn't fall. He went down on one knee among the blocks and stayed there, upright, with his shield, and when the warriors of the Lochos reached him he was still giving orders.

They carried him from the line still giving them. The Order's healer, a small fierce old woman who had come in with Vell, looked at the wound and said that he would live, if the Order's smiths could rebuild what the fire had destroyed. Command of the crater passed to Lochagos Vell, and to Ancient Aias with his lance arm gone.

The Order fought on until its ammunition was gone, and then it fought on with its spears and hammers. By then the Ninth had fought the Archflamen's reaction force to a standstill on the open ground beyond the Wall. One of the Steropes was destroyed by a Phlegethon-cannon at the twelfth hour, and its reactor lit the Flats with the glare of a second river. But the drowned engines had been badly damaged in the exchange with the *Anvil*, and at the end of the cycle they withdrew, rather than be destroyed. Varne could add another victory over the drowned to the *Anvil*'s tally. He did, Rook heard. He had it painted on the *Anvil*'s carapace in the cradle-yard, small, beside the others, under the name of the *Steadfast*.

---

When Vell judged that the Ninth held the breach in a strength nothing the Archflamen had could shift, she withdrew the Order.

They went back across no-man's land on foot, and on the hulls of the few transports they had left, in the grey light at the end of the cycle. Ninety warriors of the Achillean Order had gone into Operation Scaean Gate. Thirty-one walked out, many of them badly hurt. Avel was carried. Ancient Aias walked out on his own legs with his lance arm gone. Ancient Dolon had been destroyed by an armour-piercing shell from a Lance-Hunter at the eleventh hour, and his coffin was towed back across no-man's land behind a Kriophoros, so that the Order's smiths could place another broken warrior in it.

The fifty-nine dead were carried out with the living. Every one. The Order went back for them into the crater, under fire, and carried them out across no-man's land on their shields, to be burned at the Hearth on the ceiling and their names said before the shield. None of them would return. The Order had no vats, and wanted none.

Rook came back with the Order. He walked beside Vell across the ash with Tallis behind him and Nysse on Toft's back, and at the *35th*'s parapet, as the warriors of the Order went down into the trench one by one with their dead on their shields, Vell stopped and turned to him.

"Sapper," she said. It was the first time she had spoken to him directly. Her voice was low and rough. "The Order would have you. If you want it. The Hearth takes few who aren't born to it. But it takes some. And it would take you."

Rook looked at her for a long time.

"Lochagos," he said at last. "I'm honoured. I am. But I value coming back too highly."

Vell looked at him. Then she smiled, slightly, the first time he had seen any of them smile.

"So did he," she said, and nodded toward the dead on their shields going down into the trench. "At the start. All of us do, at the start." And she went down into the trench after them.

The *Thetis Mourning* had survived the ceiling-lances, burned along one flank, and was waiting at Kappa.

---

**QUARTERDECK NINETEEN. ROTATION FIFTEEN, CYCLE 192**

Kastellan had the Order's dead entered on the roll of the Third Siege Army as soldiers of the siege. All fifty-nine of them, by name, with the note that they had not been transferred and would not be. He wrote the note himself. Then he sent the roll to the Order's Hearth on the ceiling with his thanks.

The Order acknowledged it.

It didn't reply to his request that the *Thetis Mourning* remain over the Flats. She left for the ceiling with her wounded on the hundred and ninety-second cycle, with Avel aboard her in the Order's healing-cradle, and the thirty-one, and the fifty-nine on their shields in her hold.

In the cycles that followed, the *9th* Assault Phalanx widened the lodgement through the Wall to a frontage of eleven hundred metres and dug it in against counter-attacks that went on for nine cycles and failed. The lodgement cost the Ninth a further forty thousand transfers. The Répertoire's figures for the whole of the fighting at Leaf 57-44 since the Great Mine came to more than one million two hundred thousand transfers and sixty-one thousand nulls, besides the fifty-nine names of the Order and the two thousand names of the Deep List.

It was the most expensive stretch of ground on the Cinder Flats.

And it was the first piece of the Arsenal's last ring the Empire had held in fifteen rotations of siege. On Leaf 57-44, the Third was through the Cinder Wall at last, and only the Acropolis lay before it.

Kastellan stood at the window of his office on Quarterdeck Nineteen that night and looked out at the cone, twenty kilometres away across the Flats, with the Acropolis on its summit under its wards and the Mast above it, and the long red glow of the river behind it.

Dray came in with a slate.

"Marshal," he said. "Sapper-Captain Rook's report on the beacon. And a request from the *66th*. They want to know if the Marshal would put the names of Sapper Brenn and Sapper Nysse forward for the Order's chronicle as well as Rook's. Brenn's transferred, and Nysse will live. They say it was the five of them, not one."

Kastellan didn't turn round.

"Yes," he said. "Send it up. And send it to the Hearth with my compliments. And Dray."

"Marshal?"

"The names of Sapper Tallis and Sapper Toft of the *61st*. Them too. The carry." He was quiet a moment. "Last load."

Dray hadn't read that far in Rook's report. He wrote it down anyway.
