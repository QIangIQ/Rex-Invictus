## Chapter 30: The Scree

*"My eye to your aim. My hand to your will. My life to your hunt."*

Oath of the Psiloi, said on the Flats before the first assault on the Acropolis

---

**THE ACROPOLIS. ROTATION EIGHTEEN**

All the old ore roads of the Cinder Flats ended at the foot of the Acropolis.

They had once linked the casting lines and heat-wells and magazines that lay wrecked now behind the Third, and they came in from every point of the compass across the ash and met at the foot of the cone, the last and tallest of the old cinder vents, which rose out of the Flats on a pillar of black basalt two kilometres high. The main road climbed its southern face in switchbacks to the Founders' Gate, a gate of cut basalt blocks worn smooth and glassy by eight thousand years of heat. Above the walls and parapets of the cone rose the great dome of the Katabasion. It had begun as a single shrine over the bones of the first legion to go down into Tartarus and come back, and over the centuries it had grown into a reliquary hall carved out of the cone's own stone, where pilgrims from the ceiling worlds had come in their thousands to lay their hands on the legion's shields. And higher still, on the cone's inner ward, separated from the rest of the summit by a sheer gorge that split the top of the Acropolis in two, stood the Echo Mast.

It had been the Arsenal's noosphere relay once. Every upload on the Flats had gone up through it to Watchpoint Hades, for eight thousand years, and from its gallery a watcher could see the whole of the Cinder Flats laid out below, from the Lethe Cut to the Demus Gorge. It hummed. Every soldier of the Third had heard it hum by now, a low steady note under the noise of the guns that never changed, by day or in the dark watch.

By the eighteenth rotation, the Flats below the cone had become a field of smoke and fire. The guns of the Third, which had been a distant thunder for so long, had closed in until they made a single continuous sound around the cone, and their shells struck its slopes or burst against the wards of the Acropolis every minute of every watch. Inside its walls, the Acropolis was crammed with soldiers and guns and stores. Outside them, the slopes were covered with gun pits and observation posts. Every face of the cone was pocked with covered trenches and marksmen's holes, and the ground around its foot was scarred with bunkers and pillboxes and dugouts, layer on layer of them, the work of three million people over eighteen rotations. The Archflamen's people, and now Draugos's, had worked hard to make the Acropolis a rock on which the Third would break itself, again and again. After rotations of bombing by the Wrath wings and fire from the heaviest guns on the Flats, it was badly damaged, and it was still standing.

The end of the siege would be fought out on it.

---

**QUARTERDECK NINETEEN. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLE 12**

There had been a plan for the assault on the cone almost since the siege began.

The Arsenal was an Imperial work, and the Répertoire held every plan and drawing of every change and strengthening the founders and the Directorate had ever made to it, eight thousand years of them, in the archives at Watchpoint Hades. Castorre had planned the assault in detail in the first rotations of the siege. Kastellan had revised the plan when he took the Third. Now Thalia was the third commander to go through the slates and the war-table models and the supply figures and the regimental briefings and change the plan again, and she did it at the long table on Quarterdeck Nineteen over eleven cycles, with Dray and Celyn and the senior Hunters around her and the model of the cone in the middle of the table under the lamps.

It was enormous. A million soldiers would attack the cone in phases, to wear the defenders down a step at a time and then take and hold the key points. The ground was the worst of it. No armour could be used in the final assaults, since the slopes of the cone were too steep for tanks and barely passable on foot, and every approach funnelled the attackers into killing grounds the defenders had spent eighteen rotations preparing. However the Hunt's planners came at it, the final push into the Acropolis would be the bloodiest phase of the bloodiest siege in the theatre, and for a great many of the million it would be the last battle of any kind. The Echo Mast stood at the top of the cone, and the closer to it a soldier died, the likelier he was to go null.

"The wards first," Thalia said, on the first cycle, with her hands flat on the table either side of the model. "While they stand, nothing we have will touch the stone."

The wards of the Acropolis were fed by heat-wells sunk down through the basalt to the edge of the Phlegethon beneath the cone, and they wrapped every wall and tower and buttress on the summit. They could only be overloaded if enough hits fell on each one, fast enough, for the heat-wells feeding it to cut out. Keeping each ward down once it fell was the task of the Phaethons the Directorate had left to the *10th* Ordnance Phalanx, and to do it they needed shells, more shells than the Third had ever fired at a single target. The *10th* was given first call on every shell that came down the cables. The regiments' own guns would go short, which meant fewer shells for the barrages and the smoke and the counter-battery work, and fewer for meeting counter-attacks, and that would leave the breachers more exposed on the slopes than in any assault of the siege.

"The Directorate will complain about the shells," Dray said.

"Let it."

"The College will protest about the Katabasion, Lieutenant. If we bring down the wards and the guns get through, there won't be anything left of it."

"I know," said Thalia. She looked down at the model, at the small carved dome on the summit, and the smaller spire beyond the gorge. "Something is growing up there, Iason. Under the dome, and on the Mast. I'm going to burn it out, and if the reliquary of the First Descent goes with it, the College can send its protest to the Director, and she can send it to me."

The next objectives were the three main gates. With the wards down and the guns turning the interior of the Acropolis to rubble, the breachers would climb the slopes and take the gates, the Founders' and the Pilgrim and the Flamen Gates, as stepping stones for the waves that would come after them. It would be the hardest work of the siege, an advance up open ground under fire against the strongest defences the Archflamen possessed, dug in behind walls so thick that only direct hits from the heaviest shells could touch them, and everyone at the long table knew that the cohorts would be ground up on the slopes doing it. None of them could see any other way. Thalia had to decide who would be given it.

---

The forces assembled for the assault were the largest the Third had ever concentrated at a single point. They were still smaller than the plan had once assumed. The Directorate's withdrawal of six regiments and three Phaethon regiments in Rotation Seventeen had taken away the margin that Castorre and Kastellan had both built into their versions of the plan, and Thalia's revision had to do with eighteen Trench Regiments what the original had given to twenty-five.

The first wave would be carried by four of them. The *22nd* would take the slopes and the gates on the eastern and northern faces of the cone. The *35th* would take the Gully of Ash and the great vault doors on the western flank. The *26th*, rebuilt after the Grey and the hook, would take the Slag Gate spur to the south-west, and the *43rd*, rebuilt after the Ore Gate, would hold the Wall breaches behind all three and feed soldiers forward as they were needed. Behind them stood the regiments of the *7th* and *11th* Line Phalanxes, to be committed one by one as the first wave was used up.

The armour was the remnant of five armoured regiments, some six hundred tanks in all, of which no more than a third could be used on the cone at any one time. The Behemoths of Forge Cohort Talos, under Cradle-Lord Varne, would support the attacks on the Slag Gate spur and the Gully of Ash, where the ground at the foot of the cone was broad enough for them to walk. Enforcement had lent the Hunt a Lictor cohort, a hundred and fifty strong, for a drop into the gully. The *6th* Moonwall, with both its companies now on the Flats, would wait aboard the *Selene's Arrow* as Thalia's reserve. The Wrath wings would bomb the cone every watch until the assault began, and the *10th* Ordnance Phalanx would fire on the wards throughout.

The Répertoire priced the operation at four hundred thousand transfers for the three gates. It put the null rate at one in six.

That was the figure that troubled Thalia's staff most. The Mast stood at the summit, and every soldier who died on the slopes would die within its reach, and the Répertoire's estimate proposed no means of reducing the figure, for none existed. The Third's technical officers had been searching for one since the Lethe Cut, and had found nothing. The regiments that would make the assault were told the figure in their briefings, cohort by cohort, in plain words.

None of them asked to be relieved.

**THE *22ND* TRENCH REGIMENT, LEAF 58-46. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLE 30**

She gave the Founders' Gate to Saldane.

He had risen through the *22nd* over the whole of the siege, from centurion to colonel, and his name had become something of a legend on the Flats. As a centurion he had led the attack that broke the outer belt sixteen rotations before, and had been cut off on the far side of it and fought there for three cycles without help, wounded five times. He had transferred twice since. In the last cycles of the Kastellan Offensive, driving the *22nd*'s salient into the inner belt on Leaf 54-45, he had lost his left arm to a Laistrygon's slag-rake and come back from Hades with a bronze one, and he had led the regiment through the Cinder Wall with it. He still talked to his soldiers with his hands in his pockets. The bronze one fitted in the pocket, more or less.

Thalia came to his headquarters herself, on the thirtieth cycle, in a dugout under the inner face of the Wall on Leaf 58-46 where the *22nd*'s staff had pinned the photographs of the cone along every wall. She stood in front of the photographs with him for a long time before she said anything.

"I'm going to tell you plainly what I'm asking," she said at last. "The Founders' Gate. The eastern and northern faces. The *22nd* goes first, up the slope, before anyone else, into everything they've got. I don't expect the *22nd* to come back down, Colonel. I expect it to get as far up as it can, and hold, and I expect another regiment to go through it. One in six of the soldiers you lose on that slope will go null. I can't change that. Nobody can."

Saldane looked at the photographs for a while longer, with his hands in his pockets.

"The *22nd* will take the Founders' Gate, Lieutenant," he said.

"I didn't ask you to promise that."

"No. I'm offering it." He turned round. "I was at the outer belt, Lieutenant. In Rotation Two. I was on the far side of it for three cycles, and I knew then that nobody was coming, and the *22nd* came anyway. It always comes. It'll take the gate."

---

Saldane built the regiment up for the assault over the next fifty cycles. He drew shells and ammunition and fuel and soldiers forward from the dumps behind the Wall, and studied the models and the plans with his staff, night after night, at a war-table in the dugout under the photographs. Corso Penn, his adjutant, drew up the plan. Rosamund Tey, his signifer, sat in a corner of the dugout every night with the regimental standard on its Thureos-mast staff across her knees, sewing the battle honours back onto it where the Cinderborn had torn them in the Kastellan Offensive, and listened.

From the forward trenches that now ringed the cone, the *22nd* would attack from three directions at once. From the north it would go against the Flamen Gate. From the west it would go against the Slag Gate and the works outside it, including the ceiling-lance silo that guarded the approach to the Pilgrim Gate above. And from the south-east, with its heaviest blow, it would go against the Founders' Gate. Each attack would be led by a whole cohort in fifteen Hoplons, the *1st* Cohort from the north, the *7th* from the west, the *15th* from the south-east.

The attack would go in when the Ordnance Phalanx's barrage had done its work and lifted onto the cone itself. The Hoplons would carry the leading cohorts through the last of the outer works and set them down where the slope grew too steep to drive. From there the breachers would go on up on foot under the fire of any tanks that could find a position at the foot of the cone. When the Hoplon cohorts stalled, a second wave on foot would pass through them, and a third through the second. The fourth wave would contain most of the regiment's shock breachers, in their heavy plate, who would carry the attack to the gates and fight for the gate-towers.

On the switchback road to the Founders' Gate, Hephaestean dozer-crawlers would clear the mines so that a company of Thureos could climb the road in single file to support the shock breachers. Fresh cohorts would wait at the foot of the road to go straight up and hold the gate once it was taken. The attacks on the other two gates would be strong feints, with enough soldiers and guns to hold their defenders in place, and perhaps to take the gates outright if the defence wavered.

"It's the Founders' Gate," Saldane said, on the last night, standing at the war-table with the bronze hand flat on the model. "If we get a foothold there, somebody else takes over. By then we'll be finished."

"Yes, Colonel," Penn said.

"Good." Saldane took his hand off the model. "Rosamund. How are the honours?"

"Sewn, sir," said Tey, from her corner. She held up the standard so that he could see. The new thread was bright against the old, scorched cloth. *Leaf 50-27*, the newest of them, near the top.

"Good," said Saldane again. "Bring it."

**THE SCREE. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLE 81**

The barrage opened at the first bell of the eighty-first cycle.

The *10th* Ordnance Phalanx, dug in south of the Demus Gorge, had spent many watches preparing the fire mission, and every gun on the Flats that could be found had been trained on the cone and the Acropolis. The divine mortar rounds landed first, the heavier rounds of the aedificant batteries a few seconds behind them, and then the Phaethon shells, and a thousand shells fell on the cone in the first minute, and the Acropolis and its slopes vanished into fire and smoke.

Saldane watched it from the forward observation post on Leaf 58-46 with Penn beside him and the band headset on, and when the barrage lifted onto the cone at the time on the slate in front of him, he gave the order. The Hoplons moved off with the leading cohorts aboard. Behind them came more cohorts on foot, in ordered lines, and then the Thureos and the field guns and the Kentauros carriers, and last of all the shock breachers in their plate. He committed everything he had. He kept back three cohorts and the regiment's outriders as a reserve, to be thrown in if a breakthrough came.

All three attacks ran into the hard resistance everyone had expected.

The Hoplons drew heavy fire even through the smoke. In the west, the leading carrier of the *7th* Cohort was struck by a hidden gun in a pit nobody had found, and the shell went in through the front of its troop bay and out through the back and killed everyone inside it. In the north the *1st* Cohort ran into a minefield and left several Hoplons burning in it, and from high on the cone a ceiling-lance fired flat down the slope and destroyed three more. On the *15th* Cohort's front, in the south-east, the carriers were disabled by tank rounds and shredded tracks, and the soldiers who climbed out of them scattered under heavy fire on the open slope.

When the ramps came down, every century met the same reception. Heavy guns swept the ground from emplacements on the slope above. There were minefields hidden under the loose cinder everywhere, and mortar fire fell on pre-ranged targets with a terrible accuracy, and the breachers went forward into all of it.

Centurion Petra Saye was leading the *7th* Cohort, on the western slope, toward the road to the Slag Gate. She had been a centurion of the *22nd* for nine rotations and had taken the cohort through the Cinder Wall on Leaf 57-46, and she went up the slope at the head of her command section, through the smoke, past a bunker that had been logged destroyed by the *10th*'s spotters on the seventy-ninth cycle. The bunker was not destroyed. It opened fire on her command section at point-blank range, from twenty metres, and killed Saye and every soldier around her in the first burst. The *7th* Cohort's soldiers crawled on up the slope toward the Slag Gate road over their own dead, and a breacher put a satchel charge through the bunker's slit, and they went on.

In the north, the Archflamen counter-attacked the *1st* Cohort before it had even begun to climb, out of covered trenches on the lower slope that the barrage had passed over. They came out of the ground among the Hoplons in hundreds, and threw back the *1st*'s first wave in hand-to-hand fighting in the smoke. The second wave turned it again with fixed bayonets. The third won a few hundred metres in a rush toward the Flamen Gate, and the guns and mortars on the upper slope broke it there, well short of the gate.

Centurion Dagny Ferrant of the *19th* Cohort tried to press on uphill through the wreck of the third wave, and the *19th* went the same way as the waves in front of it. Fewer than a hundred of its seven hundred and forty soldiers came back. Ferrant was not among them. Her implant had been within reach of a Third relay throughout the fight, and the relay crew on Leaf 59-47 saw her signal on their screen right up to the moment she died, strong and clear. They logged her null. The Mast had been closer.

---

The main attack on the Founders' Gate went no better.

Eight of the *15th* Cohort's fifteen Hoplons were wrecked on the lower slope. The rest pulled back, firing up the slope with every gun they had until the guns were empty, while the breachers climbed on into fire that came down on them from above and lost all order in the fighting among the cinder and the rocks. On the switchback road, the Hephaestean dozer-crawlers went up the first and second bends with their blades down, scraping a track through the mines, until lance-fire from the walls caught them one after another and left every one of them burning on the road.

Behind them, the twelve Thureos of the *22nd*'s *5th* Armoured Company climbed in line astern, firing on the gate-towers as they went, and were knocked out one by one. The last of them reached the fourth switchback. Its crew abandoned it there and came back down the slope on foot, under fire, carrying their wounded gunner between them. The burning wrecks of the dozer-crawlers and the Thureos lay all the way up the road, and blocked it to any attack that came after them.

Ahead of the assault, the barrage had closed on the cone and was hammering the wards as the plan intended, and the wards were flaring up gold and white under the Phaethon shells, all along the walls of the Acropolis. But the heat-wells that fed them were buried far down beneath the vaults, and every ward came back up the moment the shells stopped falling on it. Any harm done to them lasted only as long as the shells kept coming.

Saldane watched it from the observation post through most of the first cycle, in silence, with his hands in his pockets.

The *22nd*'s soldiers called the slopes of the cone the Scree, after the loose black cinder that slid away under their boots as they climbed and carried them back down a metre for every two they went up. By the dark watch of the first cycle the Scree was covered with the *22nd*'s dead from top to bottom.

---

He renewed the attack on the second cycle with his three reserve cohorts.

He went up with them himself, as far as the middle slope, with Penn and Tey and the standard beside him, and the reserve cohorts got further than the first wave had, almost to the third switchback on the Founders' Gate road, before the fire from the walls stopped them there. They dug in among the rocks on the middle slope and held for most of the cycle, and the Archflamen's counter-attack came down on them out of the Founders' Gate at the end of the second watch and drove them back off it. Tey carried the standard down the Scree with a splinter wound in her side. On the third cycle, Saldane sent the attack in again with the soldiers who had come back from the vats overnight, still pale and stiff from the vats, some of them sent back up the slope where they had died the cycle before. It was thrown back on the middle slopes too.

By the end of the third cycle the *22nd* had lost fifty-eight thousand soldiers, and the Répertoire had logged more than eleven thousand of them null. It was nearly twice the rate it had predicted.

The relay crews on the Flats saw how it happened. They watched it on their screens through all three cycles, and on the third cycle a signals optio on Leaf 59-47 plotted the null transfers on a map of the cone, by height, and sent it up the line to Quarterdeck Nineteen. The null rate rose with altitude. Soldiers killed at the foot of the Scree generally reached Watchpoint Hades. Soldiers killed within a kilometre of the gates generally did not.

On the fourth cycle, Thalia ordered the *22nd* to hold the lower Scree and stop attacking until the wards were down. She shifted the weight of the Ordnance Phalanx's fire to the eastern face to help it.

The wards would not come down.

The heat-wells beneath the cone were deeper than the Répertoire's plans showed. The Archflamen's engineers had sunk new ones during the siege, in secret, down toward the river, and the Phaethons could not keep enough shells on any single ward for long enough to overload it. The *10th*'s fire planners tried for two cycles, concentrating the whole of the Ordnance Phalanx's fire on one ward at a time, the great ward on the Founders' Gate, and twice brought it down for a few seconds, and each time it came back up before the next salvo arrived.

Three cycles into the assault on the Acropolis, the Hunt's plan had failed at its first step.

Saldane sat in the dugout under the photographs on the dark watch of the fourth cycle with the casualty returns on the table in front of him, and read every name on them, as Kastellan had done with the Deep List. It took him most of the watch. When he had finished, he sent for Tey, and asked her to bring the standard, and sat for a long time with it across the table in front of him, looking at the honours she had sewn back on.

"We'll take it, Rosamund," he said at last. "The gate. We said we'd take it."

"Yes, sir," Tey said.

He wasn't sure she believed him, and he wasn't sure he did.

**THE GULLY OF ASH, THE WESTERN FLANK. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLE 81**

West of the *22nd*, the *35th* Trench Regiment went at the Gully of Ash.

The gully was a deep ravine that cut into the western flank of the cone and ran up into it for a kilometre and a half, narrowing as it climbed, to the Arsenal's great vault doors, the armoured gates of the magazines that lay under the Acropolis. Kastellan had studied it for forty cycles on the war-table at the *35th*'s headquarters, and he had gone forward to look at it with his own eyes through a periscope on the Wall eleven times, and he still disliked it. He had taken the *35th* in the fifteenth rotation, when the Hunt removed him from the Third, and he had commanded it since then exactly as he had once commanded the Third, from close up and in person, and the regiment had come to trust him more than it had ever trusted its previous colonel. Vidar Osk had been a good officer. He had gone null at the firing station in the great mine. The *35th* had spent its first cycles under Kastellan waiting to find out whether the Hunt had sent them a disgraced army commander to sulk in their headquarters, and had found instead an old man who walked their trenches at the second bell every dark watch and knew most of their names.

The plan for the gully was intricate. The first objective was a natural trench across the line of advance at the foot of the slope, which the Répertoire's maps called the Seam. From the Seam, two forces would attack the twin strongpoints at the mouth of the gully, while a third swung right to storm the ruins of a ceiling-lance silo on the flank, a work the Phaethons had wrecked months before whose broken walls still gave the defenders cover to fire along the line of advance. A fourth force would then push into the gully itself and attack its walls and the vault doors, at the same moment as Enforcement's Lictor cohort dropped into the gully from above. Once the walls were broken, a final attack would clear the rest of the gully, and the Hephaestean sappers would blow the vault doors and begin clearing the magazines beyond. Thalia expected it to take three or four cycles of hard fighting.

The planners had misjudged the Seam.

On the war-table it had appeared as a single line, and the augurs had believed it lightly held. In fact it was a deep crack in the basalt, with a steep northern wall that tanks could not climb in most places, and the defenders had dug deep into the wall where the guns could not reach them. The *35th*'s fire plan had been designed for the gully and for the plunging fire from the cone above it, and it had given the Seam only a brief shelling by a few batteries of light mortars. The defenders had also hidden tanks in the bottom of the crack, with firing positions dug at its lip, from which they could roll up hull-down, fire and drop back before anyone could reply.

They held their fire until the leading centuries of the *35th* were at short range. Then they tore them apart.

Five Thureos were burning within seconds, hit by flanking shots from tanks that nobody could see, and the attack stopped dead under the mortar fire that came after. The commander of the *3rd* Cohort, Centurion Bram Esk, was killed in the first volleys with six of his eleven centurions. There was no way round the Seam. It ran across the whole front of the gully from wall to wall, and the attack had to go straight at it, and made little headway.

Kastellan brought the guns down on it in haste, and the Seam's defenders took shelter under its lip from the shells. The regiment's three Colossi went forward to cross it and found no way over, and two of them were destroyed at the edge of the crack by demolition charges thrown up out of it by hand. It was not until the second cycle that the *35th* forced its way into the Seam by sheer weight of numbers and began to clear it with grenades and bayonets, and found in it thralls and Laistrygons and garrison soldiers of the Arsenal and Frost Masons in their white masks, side by side, fighting together in the crack. The *16th* Cohort, whose task was the ruined silo on the right, had already been badly hurt in the fight for the Seam and lacked the guns to subdue the silo, and its attacks on the silo failed.

The Seam was meant to fall in the first hours. It was the end of the third cycle before it was entirely in the *35th*'s hands. With the attack so far behind its timetable there was no time for the long barrage the twin strongpoints at the mouth of the gully needed, and the assault on them went in under the fire of the Phaethons and of the tanks in the Seam, and Kastellan watched the strongpoints take most of the fourth cycle to fall.

The assault planned for three cycles was in its fifth before any soldier of the Third set foot in the gully itself. The *16th* had tried the silo twice more and failed both times. Even the arrival of the *Hammer of Lemnos* for a third attempt, the Steropes walking up out of the ash behind the *16th* to put its forge-lance into the silo's walls, could not get the breachers onto it.

**THE KILN. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLE 85**

Centurion Liane Marsh took the *9th* Cohort into the gully at the start of the fifth cycle.

She was thirty-two. She had joined the *35th* as a trooper in Rotation Three and had transferred six times, and she had been a centurion for a rotation and a half, since the great mine, when Osk had promoted her on the firing station a cycle before he died. She went into the gully at the head of four hundred and twelve soldiers, behind a barrage that was already thinning, and the first thing she saw when she came round the corner of the northern strongpoint was a forest.

It was a forest of iron. The Frost Masons had filled the bottom of the gully with tank traps set closer together than the trees of any wood she had ever seen in the ceiling worlds, great crossed beams of black steel a man's height high, and between them were trenches and wire and minefields, layer on layer of them, all the way up the gully as far as she could see through the smoke. Above them on both sides the walls of the gully rose sheer for two hundred metres, and the defenders were on top of them, and in the towers at the head of the gully, and in loopholes cut into the walls themselves, firing down.

The *9th* went into the iron forest and began to fight its way up it a few metres at a time.

It went on for most of three cycles. Behind the regiment, the shells were running short. The assault had outrun the stocks allocated to it, and the *10th* Ordnance Phalanx had first call on every shell that came down the cables, so the fire meant to suppress the defenders on the gully walls slackened and then almost stopped, and the plunging fire from the top of the gully became murderous. There was no cover anywhere from fire that came straight down. Marsh's soldiers huddled against the iron beams of the tank traps, and the beams kept off nothing, and cohort after cohort of the *35th* was used up in the gully beside the *9th* for nothing at all, a few metres of iron forest at a time.

The *35th*'s soldiers began calling the gully the Kiln on the sixth cycle. By the seventh, nobody in the regiment called it anything else.

On the sixth cycle the regiment's relay bunker at the foot of the gully took a direct hit from a heavy shell, and the bunker collapsed with the regiment's whole signals section inside it. The *35th*'s fire net went down with it. From then on, every battery still able to fire into the Kiln fired blind, by the map, from the old firing tables, with no spotters and no corrections, and Marsh lost eleven of her own soldiers on the seventh cycle to a battery of the *35th*'s own guns that had laid its fire twenty metres short.

She sent a runner back to the regiment to report it. The runner didn't return.

---

**THE GULLY OF ASH. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLE 87**

It was the seventh cycle of the battle before Thalia ordered the Lictor drop.

It had been planned for the second cycle, to go in with the attack on the walls. It had been postponed on the second, and again on the fourth, and again on the sixth, each time with the *35th* still too far from the vault doors to join it. On the seventh, with the regiment still five hundred metres short of the doors and the Kiln eating cohorts faster than Kastellan could send them in, Thalia ordered it anyway. There was nothing else left to send.

Enforcement's Lictor cohort, a hundred and forty-eight strong, went down into the head of the gully in drop-caskets from a Wrath at the start of the second watch, behind a wave of fire-caskets that sprayed the landing ground with flame. With them went four Hunters of the Director's own household, who had volunteered to go.

Marsh saw the caskets come down. She was lying in a shell-hole among the iron beams four hundred metres below the vault doors with her signaller, and she saw them coming in over the lip of the gully through the smoke, black and fast, with the fire-caskets bursting in front of them, and heard them hit. The low smoke and the lip of the gully between hid the landing itself. Nobody in the *35th* could see what was happening at the head of the gully, and the fire net was down, and the moment for the combined attack on the walls and doors from both sides at once, which was the whole point of the drop, came and went without anyone in the regiment being able to tell that it had come.

Then the vault doors opened.

They were a hundred metres high and forty across, and they swung back slowly on hinges the size of Hoplons, both of them at once, with a groaning that Marsh heard over the guns from four hundred metres away. What came out of them was a Behemoth.

It was a drowned one. It came walking up out of the dark of the magazines into the smoke of the Kiln, a Forge engine of an old pattern, eighty metres tall, crusted black from head to foot with growth from the river, with weed hanging from its joints and its armour green and rotten under the crust. Its great cannon on its right arm fired as it came out, and the shell went down the gully in a streak of red fire and burst among the iron beams a hundred metres in front of Marsh and set the floor of the Kiln burning, and the fire that burned there was the river's, and it did not go out. Around the drowned engine's feet came a swarm. Thralls and Hollowed and things from the river that had no names, and after them, in their furnace-plate, the Cinderborn and the warriors of the Red Furnace, and at their head, his champions around him, Draugos.

The Lictors had come down into a trap.

---

Kastellan ordered the *35th* forward to reach them. It was a desperate order and he knew it, and he gave it anyway, over every runner and every field telephone the regiment had left, and the *35th* went forward up the Kiln into the fire from the drowned engine's cannon and the walls, cohort by cohort.

A Cyclopean lance-tank of the *63rd* Armoured, the only one that had got into the gully, crawled up to within three hundred metres of the vault doors through the tank traps, scraping between the beams, and put its lance into the left-hand door again and again until it burned through it in three places, but the regiment still could not force its way inside. Kastellan sent in his last reserve, the *35th*'s entire outrider force, two hundred and ten riders on ash-bikes, up the one clear lane through the iron forest toward the doors, and every one of them was shot down before they had gone halfway.

With that, the last reserve for the assault on the gully was gone.

Without relief, the Lictor cohort was destroyed at the head of the gully, fighting, and the four Hunters of the Director's household with them. Marsh heard it go on for a long time in the smoke above her, and then she heard it stop. Those of the Lictors who had been wounded or taken alive were carried back through the vault doors by the Red Furnace, and handed to the Kindlers on the casting floor beneath the cone, who opened them up as hosts for the river-fire. Within a few cycles they would come back out onto the Flats as the Kindled, in Lictor black, burning.

---

The setbacks did not end in the gully.

To the north-east, in one of the many smaller attacks meant to support the main effort, the *35th*'s *17th* Cohort was working through the ruins of the old forges at the foot of the cone when the Unremembered came up out of the ground among them. They came out of passages beneath the forges that appeared on none of the Répertoire's plans, bareheaded, silent, in the Third's own grey, and surrounded the *17th* in the ruins before anyone in the regiment knew they were there. The cohort was destroyed before help could be spared from the other attacks. More than seven hundred soldiers were lost. Most of them were taken alive, down into the passages under the forges and up into the Acropolis, to be made into Hollowed.

By the eighth cycle the *35th* was coming apart in the Kiln.

Two-thirds of its breachers were dead. The relays at the mouth of the gully were logging one in four of its dead as null. The Grey came down into the gully with every Arsenal barrage now, rolling in over the lip in thick slow banks, and the soldiers who breathed it went quiet and still and lay down in the iron forest and stopped. The drowned Behemoth was walking down the gully toward the Seam, a few metres at a time, firing its cannon every forty seconds, with the Red Furnace following in its wake. On the Scree, what remained of the *22nd* was being pushed off the lower slope.

Kastellan ordered the *35th* to fall back.

He gave the order at the end of the second watch of the eighty-eighth cycle, from his forward headquarters in the Seam, and it went out over the runners and the field telephones to every cohort in the Kiln. Then he sat in his dugout in the wall of the crack for a while, with his hands on his knees. Dray had told him once, on Quarterdeck Nineteen, that he was the only commander in the Third who could look at the casualty returns without flinching. He had never been certain of it.

The assault on the Acropolis was on the point of failure.

---

**QUARTERDECK NINETEEN. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLE 88**

On Quarterdeck Nineteen, the scale of the failure was clear by the end of the eighth cycle.

In eight cycles the Third had taken none of the three gates. It had not overloaded a single ward for longer than a few seconds. It had lost a hundred and sixty thousand soldiers, and thirty-nine thousand of them were null, almost one in four against the Répertoire's estimate of one in six. Two of the four regiments of the first wave were no longer fit to attack. The *26th*'s assault on the Slag Gate spur, the last of the three, had not yet begun.

Dray drew up two sets of orders that night, and put them in front of Thalia at the long table under the lamps, side by side.

The first committed the *26th* to the Slag Gate spur on the following cycle, as planned.

The second suspended the assault on the Acropolis. It withdrew the first wave to the Wall, and reported to the Director that the cone could not be taken by the means now available to the Third.

Thalia read them both, twice, and then sat looking at them without moving. Celyn stood on the far side of the table and said nothing. The duty staff worked on at their desks, quietly, without looking up. Outside the bunker, eleven kilometres to the north, the guns went on firing at the cone.

She held both orders until the dark watch.

The Hunt's inquiry, rotations later, could find no evidence of which of them she meant to sign. Dray, asked, said that he didn't know. Celyn, asked, said that she didn't either, and that she had never asked. Events in the Gully of Ash made the choice for Thalia before she had signed either one.

---

**THE GULLY OF ASH. ROTATION EIGHTEEN, CYCLE 88, THE DARK WATCH**

Marsh was lying in her shell-hole in the iron forest when she saw the light.

The order to fall back had reached the *9th* half an hour before, by a runner from the Seam, and the *9th* had a hundred and sixty soldiers left out of its four hundred and twelve and was trying to obey it, a few at a time, back through the tank traps toward the strongpoints at the mouth of the gully with the drowned Behemoth's cannon firing behind them. Marsh had stayed in the shell-hole with her signaller to cover the last of them out. Her signaller was dead. He had been dead for an hour, lying on his back in the cinder beside her with his relay set still strapped on.

She looked up, and there was a light under the ceiling.

It was the only light beneath the ceiling that was not on fire. Everything else on the Flats burned, the river and the cone and the gully and the vents, and everything that burned threw up the same red-brown glow. This light was gold, and it was clean, and it was moving, low and fast, across the darkness two kilometres above the Flats, from the east toward the west, and as she watched it the ceiling-lances on the cone above her swung round toward it, one after another.

The first beam missed.

The second took off its tail. She saw the gold light lurch and fall, and burst into flame as it fell, and come down onto the upper Scree above the head of the gully with a sound she felt through the ground, and slide. It slid down the black cinder of the Scree for two hundred metres, burning, throwing up a long plume of sparks and ash behind it, and came to rest against the wreck of a Hoplon at the head of the Gully of Ash, above the vault doors, and lay there on its side, still burning, gold under the fire.

Marsh stared at it from her shell-hole for a long time.

Then something climbed out of it.
