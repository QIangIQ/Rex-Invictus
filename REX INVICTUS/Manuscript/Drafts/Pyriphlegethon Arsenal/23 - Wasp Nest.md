## Chapter 23: Wasp Nest

*"The trench soldiers think we have it soft. We sleep in beds. We eat hot food. And then twice a watch we fly into the Nest and count who comes back."*

*Flight-Lieutenant Senna Halloran,* 4th *Interceptor Squadron, Tartarus Fleet*

---

**THE FOOT OF THE CINDER STAIR, CABLE HEAD TWO. ROTATION NINE, CYCLE 118**

The swarm came out of the river cloud over the Stair at the fourth hour of the dark watch, and Loadmaster Ennis Brodie of Cable Head Two heard it before he saw it, a high, thin, shrill whine, rising, coming down the Stair from somewhere up in the dark under the ceiling.

He'd heard it twice before. He knew what it was. He dropped his slate and ran.

"Swarm," he shouted across the cable yard. "Swarm. Get under. Get under."

The cable yard at the foot of the Cinder Stair was the size of a parade ground, a broad flat apron of cut basalt at the foot of the long climb up to Kappa, with the cables of Head Two running up from it into the dark in a long taut fan, forty of them, each as thick as a man's waist, humming. Cargo cars came down them out of the dark every few minutes, slowing on the brakes with a long scream of steel, and settled into the cradles at the yard's edge, and the cargo gangs swarmed over them and unloaded them onto the rail. Shells, mostly. Food, fuel, spare barrels, rifles, uniforms. Everything the Third had came down these cables or the cables of Head One beside them, and went out across the Flats by rail to the front.

The cargo gangs ran for the shelters. Brodie ran with them. He was fifty and heavy and his knees were bad, and he was the last into the shelter at the edge of the yard, and he turned in its doorway and looked back and up.

The swarm came down out of the dark.

They were Stymphalians. Everybody on the Flats knew the name by now. Things of the deep that had always come up the rivers, and for six hundred years the ceiling-lances had burned them out of the dark over the Flats as fast as they appeared, so that nobody in the Third had ever really seen one until the Archflamen took the lances. Now there were thousands of them. They came down over the cable yard in a long dense shrieking cloud, each the size of a large dog, with bronze-black wings and long hooked beaks and claws, and the light of the yard's lamps flashed off them as they turned, and they came down on the cables.

They went for the cables. That was what they'd been sent for. Brodie watched them do it from the shelter doorway with his heart in his mouth. They settled on the cables in their hundreds, clinging, shrieking, and tore at them with their beaks and claws, and where they tore the cables' armoured sheathing came away in long bright strips and the strands beneath it parted. He heard the first cable go. A long whip-crack, high up in the dark, and then the scream of a cargo car coming down the cable out of control with nothing to hold it, and the crash as it hit the yard.

Behind the swarm, out of the river cloud, came the Lanterns.

He'd seen those before too. Drowned ships' craft. Old Lantern-pattern fighter-bombers of the Fleet, lost in the river in wars nobody remembered, flying again for something else, with their hulls black and scaled and running with river-fire and their drowned crews at the controls. Four of them came down over the yard in a long dive, and their bombs went into the cable cradles at the yard's edge, one after another, and the cradles went up.

Brodie threw himself down in the doorway of the shelter.

When he got up again, eleven of the forty cables of Head Two were down. Cargo cars lay smashed across the yard, burning. The cradles were wreckage. And the swarm had gone back up into the dark, shrieking, and the Lanterns with it, and the yard was quiet except for the fires and the shouting and the long slow creak of the remaining cables swaying in the hot wind from the burning cars.

Nothing had fired back. Nothing at all. The Third had almost nothing to fire back with. Its plan of the siege had never thought it would need anti-air guns, since the lances had kept the air over the Flats clear for six centuries, and the few it had brought had long since been turned on ground targets, or lost to shellfire. Brodie had heard the Third's gunners in the yard's canteen joke about it, before the drowned came. They didn't joke about it now.

The Quiet Ones had the cables of Head Two running again in nine cycles. For nine cycles, the Third's supply ran on Head One alone, and the front went short.

It was the second time the swarms had cut the cables at the Stair. In the yard's canteen that night, somebody chalked the number on the wall.

**THE WRATH FIELDS, THE OLD OUTER BELT. ROTATION NINE, CYCLE 330**

Flight-Lieutenant Senna Halloran of the *4th* Interceptor Squadron of the Tartarus Fleet had flown in the deep for nine years, over the Acheron stairs and the ice-caves of the Cocytus and the long black gulfs below the Styx, and had never in all that time flown anywhere without a sky.

The Flats had no sky. She stood on the edge of the new landing field on the old outer belt at the end of the dark watch on the three hundred and thirtieth cycle, in her flight suit with her helmet under her arm, and looked up, and saw nothing. Ash. Brown, low, thick, rolling ash cloud under the vast dark roof of the cavern at Depth 40, lit red from beneath by the glow of the Phlegethon away to the south and by the flashes of the guns along the front, hanging two thousand metres over the Flats from horizon to horizon, and above the ash, in the dark, somewhere, the ceiling geometry, where the drowned ships lay.

"Cheerful, isn't it," said Kit Darrow, beside her.

He was her wingman, twenty-two, a long gangling cheerful young man from the upper Acheron holds with a big nose and a laugh that could be heard across a hangar, and he'd been flying with her for eleven months and had never once stopped talking.

"I've seen worse," Halloran said.

"Where?"

She thought about it.

"No," she said. "I haven't."

The *4th*'s Wraths stood in their revetments along the edge of the field behind them, twenty of them, low and blunt and heavy, with the Fleet's grey and white on their wings. They had come down in the convoy's holds thirty cycles earlier, broken down into sections and crated, and the Fleet's ground crews had spent the thirty cycles reassembling them, while the Third's engineers built the landing fields round them on the old outer belt and the Fleet's quartermasters brought down fuel and munitions and stacked them. It had all taken longer than anyone wanted. The Third's colonels had been sending messages to the fields every watch since the convoy landed, asking when the Wraths would fly.

They were going to fly now. The first sortie of the Tartarus Fleet's air war over the Cinder Flats. Eight interceptors of the *4th* and *7th* squadrons, to patrol the front from the Mora Cut to the Lethe Cut and show the Third's soldiers what they looked like.

"Do you think the swarms will come up?" Darrow asked.

"I think they'll come up."

"Good," Darrow said. "I'm bored."

They went out to the Wraths together and climbed into their cockpits, and the ground crews pulled the ladders away, and Halloran ran the engines up, one after the other, and felt the Wrath come alive round her. She'd flown the standard Wrath for six years. It was the workhorse of the Fleet's strike wings, well armed and well armoured, not fast and not graceful, a heavy blunt square-shouldered machine with four autocannon in the wings and a lascannon under the nose and enough plate on it to take a burst from a Lantern and keep flying. She loved it. She'd tried to explain why to her mother once and failed.

The flight controller's voice came on the band. "*4th* and *7th*, you're clear. Good hunting."

She took the Wrath down the field and up into the ash.

It closed round her at once, a thick brown roaring dark, and she flew on instruments through it for forty seconds with the Wrath bucking in the turbulence, climbing, and then broke out of the top of it into the open air between the ash cloud and the ceiling. She looked down. She saw the top of the ash below her lit red from beneath, rolling away to the horizon in a brown sea, and the flashes of the guns lighting it from inside along a curve a hundred kilometres long, and to the south, the long red glow of the river. She looked up. Two thousand metres above her, in the dark, the roof of the cavern. And somewhere up there, invisible, the drowned ships.

She brought the Wrath round in a long turn and led the flight north along the line of the front, just above the ash, and dropped down through the ash to fly beneath it, low, where the Third's soldiers could see them.

Down there, they were heard before they were seen.

Senna Halloran never knew what it sounded like on the ground. She learned afterward from the Third's soldiers, from the letters they wrote and the things they said in the canteens on the outer belt, and the regimental diaries that recorded it, and she came to understand that it had been the first time in six hundred years that the Flats, which had never had a sky, had heard engines in the dark overhead. Soldiers came up out of the trenches all along the front to see them. They stood on the parapets and fire-steps in their thousands, looking up into the ash, and when the eight Wraths of the *4th* and *7th* squadrons came over them low out of the dark from the south with their engines roaring and their running lights on, they cheered.

The swarms came up at the Mora Cut. Darrow got two Stymphalians and a Lantern. He talked about it for a week.

**THE WRATH FIELDS. ROTATION NINE, CYCLES 352 TO 355**

The drowned answered the Wraths in the first rotation of the air war, and the answer came from the lances.

The Archflamen's gunners had learned in the first rotation of the siege that the ceiling-lances could be fired flat across the Flats as well as up. Every soldier of the Third knew that; it was why the inner belt had been the inner belt. The Fleet's air officers had known it too, when they sited the fields. They had sited them on the old outer belt anyway, inside the lance envelope, since it was the nearest flat ground to the front, and the nearest flat ground meant the shortest sorties and the most sorties a watch.

On the three hundred and fifty-second cycle, at the second hour of the dark watch, a lance on the spur fired flat across the Flats at the *4th* Squadron's field, and Halloran woke in the barracks to a light so white it came through the walls.

She ran out into it. The field was burning. The lance had come across the Flats from the spur twenty kilometres away in a long white beam and walked down the line of the *4th*'s revetments in less than a second, and where it had touched them the Wraths in the revetments were molten. Six of them. Their fuel and munitions were going up in the heat, one after another, in long rolling bursts of fire, and the ground crews were running across the field toward them with extinguishers and being driven back by the heat. She stood on the barracks steps in her shirt and bare feet and watched her squadron burn.

It happened again on the next night. And the night after.

Thirty Wraths were destroyed on the ground in three nights, across the fields on the old outer belt, before the Fleet's air officers gave up and moved the fields back beyond the lance envelope. The ground crews and the Third's engineers built new revetments for the remaining aircraft out of cut basalt, deep and thick and roofed, at the new fields on the far side of the Drum, and the Wraths went into them.

It added forty minutes to every sortie over the cone. It reduced the number the wings could fly in a watch by a third.

The Third's *10th* Ordnance Phalanx was ordered to give the lances priority in its counter-battery fire. Over the next two rotations it destroyed three of them in their silos round the spur, with Phaethon fire, at enormous cost in shells. Halloran watched the third one go, from the cockpit, flying escort for a Vigil photographing the silo for the gunners: a long white flash in the dark at the foot of the cone, and a column of fire going up into the ash, and the Vigil's crew cheering on the band. There were fifty-seven left.

Darrow had lost his Wrath in the first night's fire. He flew a replacement for the rest of the war, and he painted the old one's name on its nose, *Gangly Bastard*, and under it the date of the fire, and said that it was a memorial.

"A memorial to what?" Halloran asked.

"To my first Wrath," Darrow said. "She was a good girl. Shot down two Stymphalians and a Lantern and then got melted in her bed by a ceiling-lance. It's not right, Senna. A Wrath should go in the air."

**CABLE HEAD TWO. ROTATION TEN, CYCLE 79**

Until the Wraths could hold the air over the Flats, Kastellan's staff improvised what defence they could, and Loadmaster Ennis Brodie watched it built round his yard.

The Forge sent forty pairs of old Hephaestean flak guns from its stores in Rotation Nine. They came down the cables at Kappa in crates, ancient long-barrelled things of bronze and steel, with the Forge's hammer stamped on their breeches and dates on their mountings from wars five hundred years gone, and the Third's engineers built towers for them on the old outer belt and round the cable heads at the foot of the Stair. Brodie had four of them on towers at the corners of his yard. Their crews were Forge men, old and quiet, who spoke to nobody and cleaned their guns every watch.

And the Third's gunners found something better.

They found it by accident, in the first rotation of the swarms. A divine battery of the *6th* Ordnance Phalanx, keyed by moon-sworn keyers of Artemis, was firing on the inner belt when a swarm came over it, and one of its keyers, a big slow-spoken woman named Iolanthe Brede, fused her lunar shard for an air burst on her own initiative and fired it straight up into the swarm. It burst among them in a long silver flash, a sphere of cold white moonlight thirty metres across, and the Stymphalians in it fell out of the air in their dozens, burning silver, shrieking. Brede was put on a charge for firing at an unauthorised target. The charge was withdrawn the next cycle, when Kastellan heard about it.

Four of the moon-sworn batteries were pulled out of the gun line in Rotation Ten and sited round the cable heads at the foot of the Stair and round the main shell dumps behind the front. Brede's was one of them. She came to Brodie's yard with her battery on the sixtieth cycle of Rotation Ten and set up her guns at the yard's southern edge, and Brodie went to talk to her.

"Will it work?" he asked her.

Brede looked at him. She had a broad plain face and steady grey eyes, and a crescent moon tattooed in silver on the back of each hand, and she'd been a keyer of the *6th* for eleven years.

"Some," she said. "Not enough. There's too many of them. But some."

"Some'll do," Brodie said. "Some's more than we've had."

It was. On the seventy-ninth cycle of Rotation Ten, the swarm came down the Stair again, at the fourth hour of the dark watch, a long shrieking cloud with six drowned Lanterns behind it, and this time something fired back.

The four Hephaestean flak towers at the corners of the yard opened up first, their old long barrels pumping shells up into the dark in long bright streams, and the shells burst among the swarm in black puffs of smoke and fire. Then Brede's battery fired. Brodie saw it from the doorway of the shelter. He saw her guns fire straight up into the dark, one after another, and the lunar shards burst among the swarm in great silver spheres of moonlight, cold and white and dazzling, so bright that for a moment he could see every cable of Head Two running up into the dark and every Stymphalian clinging to them, and in the silver light the Stymphalians fell. They fell out of the dark in their hundreds, burning silver, and hit the yard round him with soft heavy thuds. One of them fell five metres from the shelter door and lay on the basalt flapping and shrieking with silver fire running along its wings, and a cargo-hand ran out and beat it to death with a crowbar.

Then the Wraths came.

Halloran's flight came down the Stair out of the ash at the seventh minute of the attack, four of them, from the new fields beyond the Drum, and went into the Lanterns. Brodie saw them come. He saw them come down over the yard out of the dark in a long screaming dive with their autocannon firing, and saw the first Lantern take a long burst through its scaled black hull and come apart in the air and fall burning into the Stair. He saw a second turn away. He saw the swarm break.

Three cables went, that night. Three, out of forty. The Quiet Ones had them running again inside a cycle.

After the eightieth cycle of Rotation Ten the swarms never cut the cables at the Stair again.

Somebody in the yard's canteen chalked a line under the number on the wall, and after a while, when it was clear the line was staying there, somebody else chalked Brede's name underneath, and then the *4th* Squadron's.

**THE WRATH FIELDS BEYOND THE DRUM. ROTATION TEN, CYCLE 241**

The Tartarus Fleet's commitment to the Cinder Flats was two wings.

The interceptor wing, of ten squadrons, about twenty Wraths in each, defended the Flats. It flew patrols every watch along the front and escorted the bombers wherever they went. The bomber wing, of seven squadrons of a dozen heavy Wraths each, with four Vigil-pattern craft for reconnaissance and command, could bomb the Archflamen every watch: strongpoints at low level, gun lines, roads, and whole stretches of the front carpeted before an attack. It gave the Third a reach well beyond its guns.

The Wrath wings were directed from the Fleet's standing squadron over Kappa, and remained the Fleet's own. Kastellan could request targets. He could set the priority of each. He couldn't order a single Wrath into the air. The arrangement worked better than the one with the Forge's sappers, everybody agreed, since the Fleet kept a liaison staff of air officers on Quarterdeck Nineteen and accepted most of the Third's requests. But the Fleet reserved the right to take the wings away to its other charges in the deep whenever it chose.

On the second cycle of the Kastellan Offensive, Halloran flew escort for two waves of heavy Wraths over Leaf 57-48.

She'd flown escort before. She hadn't flown it for anything like this. The heavy Wraths of the bomber wing came up out of the fields beyond the Drum at the first bell in two long waves, a dozen to a wave, big and slow and heavy-laden, with long range and a large bomb load and almost nothing to defend themselves with, which was why they never flew without escort. Halloran's squadron flew above and ahead of them through the ash, eight Wraths, weaving, watching the dark for swarms. The swarms came up at the inner belt. They came up out of the river cloud over the cone in a long dense cloud, shrieking, and went for the bombers, and Halloran took the *4th* down into them.

She never remembered much of it afterward. You didn't, in a turning fight with the swarms. It was too fast and too close and too dark, and the Stymphalians came at the Wraths from every direction at once, small and fast and able to turn inside anything the Fleet flew, so that she was always turning, always firing, always pulling the Wrath round in long grinding turns that pressed her down into her seat and greyed her vision at the edges, with Stymphalians going past the cockpit glass a metre away, shrieking, and the autocannon hammering in the wings, and the band full of voices. She remembered Darrow's voice. *Got one. Got one. Senna, on your left, on your left.* She remembered pulling round, and a Stymphalian coming apart in her sights in a spray of bronze-black feathers.

She remembered looking down through a gap in the swarm, once, and seeing the bombs go into the inner belt on Leaf 57-48.

A long wall of fire, rolling. And then a second, behind it. And under the smoke, tiny with distance, the soldiers of the *52nd* coming up out of their trenches and going forward into it.

The *52nd* gained more ground on Leaf 57-48 that cycle than in a whole rotation, Halloran learned afterward. Its colonel sent a message to the Wrath fields at the end of the cycle. It was read out in the mess hall at the *4th*'s field that night by the squadron commander, a dry tired old pilot named Aurel Kesh who'd been flying since before Halloran was born.

*52nd Trench Regiment, Leaf 57-48, to the Wrath wings,* Kesh read. *My compliments and the regiment's. If you can get me two more waves of those I'll get the Marshal the next trench as well. Lowe, Colonel.*

The *4th* cheered. Darrow climbed onto a table.

That evening, Kesh told them later, Kastellan asked the Fleet for more bombers.

**THE VIGIL *QUIET EYE*. ROTATION TEN, CYCLE 290**

The bombers' principal target was the Acropolis, and the first Vigil missions over it in Rotation Ten found something the Third hadn't known was there.

Halloran flew escort for the Vigil *Quiet Eye* on the two hundred and ninetieth cycle, on its second pass over the cone. The Vigils were few and precious, four of them on the whole of the Flats, Wrath airframes stripped of their bombs and their guns and fitted with augurs and imagers in their place, and the Fleet guarded them jealously. Halloran's flight of four flew close round the *Quiet Eye* the whole way in from the Drum, above and below and to either side, and when the swarms came up over the Cinder Wall to meet them, they fought the swarms off it while the Vigil flew straight and level over the foot of the cone at two thousand metres, with its imagers running, through the flak.

There was a great deal of flak. Halloran had never seen anything like it.

The Arsenal had been building its own air defence for eight thousand years, against the swarms, and it was all still there. Guns on the Cinder Wall. Guns on the cone, in tier after tier, all the way up to the Acropolis. Guns in the shanty-town at the cone's foot. They put up a wall of fire round the *Quiet Eye* and its escort that turned the ash cloud orange and black from end to end, and shook the Wraths in the air with the shock of the bursts, and rattled their plate with fragments. And above the guns, on top of it all, the ceiling-lances. Two of them fired at the Vigil as it went over, upward this time, two long white beams lancing up through the ash past the *Quiet Eye*'s wing, so close that Halloran saw the paint on its wing blister and run.

The Vigil's crew got their pictures.

The *4th* got them home. Halloran sat in the mess hall afterward with her hands shaking round a cup of something hot, and Darrow sat beside her and, for once, didn't talk.

"They call it the Nest," he said at last.

"What?"

"The cone. The Acropolis. The bomber crews call it the Wasp Nest." He turned his cup round in his hands. "I asked one. In the canteen. He said, *you go up there and poke it and they all come out and sting you.*"

Halloran said nothing.

"Senna," Darrow said. "Did you see the flak over the cone?"

"I saw it."

"I've never seen anything like it."

"No," Halloran said. "Nor have I."

The *Quiet Eye*'s pictures went to Quarterdeck Nineteen that night, and the Third's intelligence officers spent three cycles poring over them, and what they found in them went into a report that Kastellan read on the two hundred and ninety-fourth cycle, and read again, and sent for the Fleet's air liaison officer to read it with him.

The Archflamen hadn't been living off the stores of his vaults alone.

Round the foot of the cone, inside the Cinder Wall, a shanty-town had grown up over the rotations of the siege: foundries and machine shops and casting pits, hundreds of them, crowded together in the narrow ground between the Wall and the cone's lowest tier, roofed with slag-iron and canvas and smoking every watch. The *Quiet Eye*'s pictures showed them working. They were repairing tanks there, and making shells, and rifles, every watch, and sending them out through the gates of the Wall to the front. Beyond the reach of the Third's guns. Out of sight of every observer on the Flats. They'd been there, the intelligence officers believed, since Rotation Four.

The Acropolis itself was too well defended beneath its wards to be attacked directly. Every officer on Quarterdeck Nineteen knew it. The forges were defended nearly as strongly, but they had no wards.

"Those," Kastellan said to the Fleet's air liaison officer, with his finger on the picture. "First."

**THE WASP NEST. ROTATION TEN, CYCLES 300 TO 400**

Other missions were flown, over the next rotation. Against strongpoints at the front, at low level, for the Line Phalanxes. Against the Archflamen's roads between the Wall and the inner belt, by night, to cut his supply. Against gun lines, against tank parks, against the gates of the Cinder Wall. But the cone came first.

To the trench soldiers on the Flats, the Wrath crews seemed to have the easiest war of anyone. Halloran knew it. Everybody on the fields knew it. They lived in hardened barracks far back beyond the Drum, in beds, with hot food and hot water, spared the shelling and the walk into the guns, and the soldiers who came back to the outer belt on rotation from the line looked at them in the canteens with an expression Halloran learned to recognise. She didn't blame them. She'd have looked at herself the same way.

The crews knew otherwise.

Over the Acropolis, a Wrath crewman had less chance of coming back than a trench soldier did. Kesh said so in the mess hall, on the three hundred and fortieth cycle, reading out the wing's figures for the rotation from a slate. And when a crewman didn't come back from the Nest, he was more likely to be null than a trench soldier, for the noosphere over the cone lay nearest the Echo Mast.

The relay crews at the Wrath fields worked it out first. They sat in their relay huts at the edges of the fields through every sortie, logging the uploads of crews lost in the air as they came in, and in the first rotation of the war over the cone they began to see a pattern. Uploads from crews lost over the Flats came in whole, nearly always. Uploads from crews lost over the cone, within a few kilometres of the Mast, came in empty. Half the time. Sometimes more.

Kesh read that out too.

The *4th*'s mess hall stayed quiet for a long time after he'd finished. Then somebody at the back, one of the new pilots who'd come in on the replacement draft from the Acheron, said, in a small voice, "Half?"

"Half," Kesh said.

"Sir. What do we do about it?"

Kesh looked at the slate for a while.

"Fly fast," he said, "over the cone."

**THE BOMBER FIELD BEYOND THE DRUM. ROTATION ELEVEN, CYCLE 149**

Flight-Captain Dorian Tavish of the *2nd* Bomber Squadron sat on the steps of the briefing hut at the end of the dark watch on the hundred and forty-ninth cycle of Rotation Eleven with his crew round him and a cup of cold tea going colder in his hands, and listened to the wing commander tell them what they would be doing for the next nine cycles.

"Every serviceable heavy Wrath," the wing commander said. "Every watch. For nine cycles. Against the forges at the foot of the cone."

Nobody said anything. Tavish looked at his tea.

"The whole of the interceptor wing will escort," the wing commander said. "All ten squadrons. The *10th* Ordnance Phalanx will shell the flak positions on the Cinder Wall during every raid to draw their fire. The Marshal has requested it and the Fleet has granted it. The Third's intelligence officers estimate that the forges at the foot of the cone are supplying a third of the Archflamen's shells and most of his repaired armour. If we can burn them out, the Third will feel the difference on every leaf of the front within a rotation." He looked round at the crews. "It'll be expensive. I won't pretend otherwise. You all know what the Nest is like."

They knew. Tavish had flown over it eleven times. He'd come back eleven times, which put him, he'd been told, in the luckiest tenth of the bomber wing.

His crew was four: Tavish at the controls, a navigator named Wenda Pryor who was fifty and had flown in the deep for thirty years and never been sick in an aircraft once, which she mentioned often; a bomb-aimer named Joss Merriwether, twenty-four, who prayed before every sortie to a god he wouldn't name; and a tail-gunner named Aiden Gryce, who was nineteen and hadn't been sick in an aircraft once either, which nobody believed. Their Wrath was the *Patience of Stone*, a heavy of the *2nd* Squadron, eleven years old, patched in forty places, with a crude painting of a tortoise on her nose that some long-ago crew had done and nobody had painted over.

"Nine cycles," said Merriwether quietly, beside him.

"Nine cycles," Tavish said.

"Captain. Do you think we'll..."

"Don't," Pryor said. "Don't ask him that, Joss. Nobody ever answers it right."

Merriwether was quiet.

The raids on the forges began at the first bell of the hundred and fiftieth cycle, and went on for nine cycles, every watch, and were the largest air operation of the siege.

**OVER THE CINDER WALL. ROTATION ELEVEN, CYCLE 150**

Halloran flew top cover for the *2nd* Bomber Squadron on the first raid, and on the second, and on the fourth, and on the seventh.

She didn't remember most of them separately afterward. They ran together in her mind, nine cycles of them, into a single long roaring nightmare of ash and fire and the swarms. The take-off from the fields beyond the Drum in the dark, the forty-minute flight across the Flats under the ash to the Cinder Wall, the *10th* Ordnance Phalanx's shells going over the Wall below them in long red arcs onto the flak positions, and then the cone, rising out of the ash ahead of them, vast and dark, with the Acropolis on top of it under its wards, glowing faintly gold, and the thin black needle of the Mast above that. And the forges at the foot of it, smoking, hundreds of them, in the narrow crowded ground between the Wall and the cone's lowest tier.

And the Nest waking.

The flak came up first, every time. From the Wall, from the cone, from the shanty-town itself, a wall of fire that turned the ash orange and black and shook the Wraths in the air. The lances fired upward through it. And then out of the river cloud over the cone the swarms came, in their thousands, shrieking, with the drowned Lanterns among them, and went for the bombers with no regard at all for their own losses.

She saw the first heavy Wrath go down on the first raid. A lance caught it, straight through the wing root, and the wing came off and went spinning away into the ash, and the bomber rolled over slowly onto its back and went down into the shanty-town with its bombs still aboard, and blew up among the forges with a long rolling flash that lit the whole foot of the cone. She saw the second go down to the swarms. They settled on it in a cloud, a hundred of them, clinging to its wings and its hull and tearing at it with their beaks, until it was a black shrieking mass in the air with fire coming out of it, and then it fell.

She went into the swarms. All the interceptors did. They went into them every time, at full throttle, firing, and tore through them and turned and tore through them again, and fought them off the bombers as best they could. It was never enough. There were always more.

On the fourth raid, the Wrath Seconds came.

Halloran had heard about them. Everybody on the fields had. The Fleet had been building them for two years at the yards above the Acheron, a lighter, faster Wrath, with less plate and fewer guns but a turning circle that could stay with a Stymphalian in a fight, and two squadrons of them had come down on the convoy in Rotation Ten and been held back at the fields since, working up. They flew for the first time over the Nest on the fourth raid. She saw them come past her in the ash, small and quick, and go into the swarm round the bombers, and turn inside the Stymphalians, tighter than any standard Wrath could manage, and kill them.

"Oh, I want one," Darrow said on the band. "I want one of those, Senna."

"Shut up and watch the bombers, Kit."

"I'm watching. I'm watching. I still want one."

---

**THE *PATIENCE OF STONE*. ROTATION ELEVEN, CYCLE 154**

The *Patience of Stone* went over the forges at the foot of the cone on the fifth cycle of the raids at two thousand metres, in the middle of the second wave, with her bomb doors open and Merriwether lying in the nose over his sight, and Tavish holding her straight and level through the flak.

That was the worst of it, always. Holding straight and level. On the run in, over the target, a heavy Wrath had to fly straight and level for ninety seconds while the bomb-aimer lined up his sight, and in those ninety seconds she couldn't turn, couldn't climb, couldn't dive, couldn't do anything at all but fly straight through whatever the Nest threw at her. Tavish had done it eleven times. He'd never got used to it.

"Steady," Merriwether said, in the nose. His voice was quite calm. It always was, at this point. "Steady. Left a little. Steady."

A flak burst went off under the *Patience*'s right wing, close, and the whole aircraft jumped, and something rattled along the hull plate in a spatter of gravel-sharp blows. Tavish held her.

"Steady."

He could see the forges below through the cockpit glass, through the smoke. Hundreds of them. Long roofed sheds of slag-iron and canvas, crowded together, with the glow of casting pits inside them and the chimneys of foundries above. Some of them were burning from earlier raids. Most of them weren't. He could see figures moving between them. Thralls, he supposed. Hundreds of them, too.

"Steady," Merriwether said. "Bombs gone."

The *Patience* lifted as the bombs went, and Tavish felt it through the controls, the whole aircraft rising a few metres as the weight fell away, and he put her into a long climbing turn away from the cone at once, as hard as she'd go.

"Hits," said Gryce from the tail, a moment later. His voice was high and shaky and excited. "Hits, Captain, I see hits, three of them, right on the... oh. Oh, fuck."

"Gryce?"

"Captain. Lance."

Tavish saw it come up past the cockpit, a long white beam rising out of the cone through the ash, close enough to touch, and felt the heat of it through the glass, and felt the *Patience* shudder.

It had missed. It had gone past the *Patience*'s tail by less than ten metres, Gryce said afterward, so close that he'd felt the paint on his turret blister. It had gone on up past her into the ash and hit the heavy Wrath flying behind her in the second wave, the *Iron Psalm*, squarely, and the *Iron Psalm* had gone.

Tavish knew its captain. A tall red-haired woman named Hilde Varga who'd flown with the *2nd* Squadron for six years, and played the fiddle in the mess hall on the nights there was no flying, badly, and laughed at herself for it.

The relay hut at the bomber field logged the *Iron Psalm*'s crew that night. Four uploads. Two of them were empty. Varga's was one.

---

**OVER THE CONE. ROTATION ELEVEN, CYCLE 157**

On the eighth cycle of the raids, over the cone, Kit Darrow went down.

Halloran saw it happen. She'd seen him from the corner of her eye through the whole of the fight, as she always did, his Wrath on her left and a little behind, where a wingman should be, with *Gangly Bastard* painted on its nose under the name of its replacement and the date of the fire. They were flying top cover for the third wave of the *2nd* Bomber Squadron over the foot of the cone, at two thousand five hundred metres, in the flak, and the swarms were coming up out of the river cloud round the bombers below them, and she took the flight down into the swarms, and Darrow came with her.

A drowned Lantern came out of the swarm at them head on.

She saw it at the last moment. Black and scaled and burning, its old hull running with river-fire, coming straight at them out of the dark of the swarm with its guns firing, and she broke right, hard, and felt the Wrath's plate ring with hits along the left wing, and pulled round, and looked for Darrow.

He'd broken left. He'd broken left as she broke right, as they'd always done, as they'd trained to do, so that the Lantern would have to choose, and the Lantern had chosen him.

She saw its rounds go into his Wrath. She saw the cockpit glass go white. She saw his Wrath roll over onto its back, slow and almost lazy, and go down into the flak over the cone, trailing smoke, toward the Acropolis and the Mast, and she saw no canopy come off it.

"Kit," she said on the band. "Kit. Get out. Kit, get out."

The band was silent.

She watched his Wrath go down into the ash over the cone until she couldn't see it anymore, and then she went after the Lantern that had killed him, and killed it, and went after another, and killed that too. She didn't remember any of it. Her gun cameras showed it afterward. Kesh showed her the pictures, on the next cycle, in his office, without saying anything.

The relay hut logged Darrow's upload at the twelfth minute of the raid.

It came in empty.

Halloran went to the relay hut that night, after the debrief, and asked to see the log. The relay crew didn't want to show it to her. She stood in the doorway until they did. It was a single line on the slate, in the relay crew's neat cramped hand, among the others from the raid.

*Darrow, K., Pilot Officer, 4th Interceptor Squadron. Lost over the cone, c157. Upload received 12th minute. Pattern empty. Null.*

She read it twice. Then she gave the slate back, and went out, and sat on the steps of the relay hut in the dark for a long time.

---

**THE FIELDS. ROTATION ELEVEN, CYCLES 159 TO 170**

At the end of the ninth cycle of the raids, the Vigils flew over the foot of the cone and counted sixty-three foundries and casting pits destroyed or burned out.

The Arsenal's output of shells to the front fell by about a third for the following rotation. The Line Phalanxes felt it. The *22nd* and the *37th* and the *52nd* reported it within twenty cycles, a slackening of the Archflamen's barrages, a lengthening of the intervals between his counter-attacks, a falling-off in the number of repaired tanks coming up through the gates of the Cinder Wall. The Third's intelligence officers wrote a report on it that ran to forty leaves and was full of graphs. Kastellan read it, and signed it, and sent it up to the Lord Marshal.

The cost was severe. The bomber wing had lost forty-one heavy Wraths in the nine cycles, more than half its strength. The interceptor wing had lost fifty-eight craft in the fighting over the cone. Nearly half the crews lost had been logged null.

The *2nd* Bomber Squadron had lost seven of its twelve. The *Patience of Stone* came back from all nine raids. Tavish didn't know why. He said so in the mess hall, on the last night, to Pryor and Merriwether and Gryce, and none of them knew why either.

"Luck," Pryor said.

"Luck runs out," Tavish said.

"Not tonight it didn't," said Pryor, and poured him another drink.

The *4th* Interceptor Squadron had lost six of its twenty. Darrow was one of them. Two of the others were null too.

The Fleet replaced the losses within two rotations, as it had promised. New Wraths came down in the convoys, and new crews, from the Acheron and the Cocytus and the yards above, and the ground crews built new revetments for them at the fields beyond the Drum, and they flew. But the bomber wing didn't fly against the cone in such strength again. The cost had been too high, and the Fleet's air officers said so on Quarterdeck Nineteen, and Kastellan, who had read the figures, didn't argue.

Within a rotation, the Vigils found that the forges had been rebuilt.

They hadn't been rebuilt at the foot of the cone, in the narrow ground between the Wall and the lowest tier. They'd been rebuilt higher up. On the cone itself, on its upper tiers, beneath the walls of the Acropolis, where the Acropolis's wards protected them. The *Quiet Eye* flew over them on the two hundred and twentieth cycle of Rotation Eleven and photographed them, smoking under the faint gold shimmer of the wards, and the pictures went to Quarterdeck Nineteen, and Kastellan looked at them for a long time.

"Can the bombers reach them?" he asked the Fleet's liaison officer.

"No, Marshal. Not under the wards."

Kastellan put the pictures down.

"Then it'll have to be done from the ground," he said. "In the end."

**THE BALANCE IN THE AIR. ROTATION ELEVEN TO ROTATION TWELVE**

The Archflamen's strength in the air was hard to count. The swarms came and went with the river. The Fleet's air officers estimated it, at different times, at anywhere between two hundred and five hundred, of which most were Stymphalians and the rest drowned Lanterns, the heavier fighter-bombers that could still hold their own against a Wrath. The drowned and the swarms probably had the advantage in numbers at first. As losses mounted on both sides, and the Fleet's replacements came down on the convoys and the river's didn't, most of the crews on the fields came to believe, by the middle of Rotation Eleven, that the two sides were about level.

From then on the Wrath wings gained, if only by numbers.

Halloran flew a Wrath Second from the two hundredth cycle of Rotation Eleven. The *4th* Squadron was re-equipped with them that cycle, the first of the interceptor squadrons to be, and she flew hers for the first time over the Mora Cut and found that it turned as Darrow had said it would, inside the Stymphalians, so tight that she blacked out in the first fight and came to with the Wrath still turning, and she painted *Gangly Bastard* on its nose, under her own name, and the date of the fire, and the date of the eighth raid on the forges. Nobody in the *4th* asked her about it.

Twice in Rotation Twelve, the Fleet took a squadron of interceptors away from the Flats to the Acheron stairs for a month, where something had come up out of the deep and the Fleet's other charges needed them. The *4th* went the second time. Kastellan protested both withdrawals to Watchpoint Hades and was told that the Flats weren't the only front in Tartarus.

He was assured, too, that the Fleet's commitment would last as long as the siege, and that a steady flow of replacement craft and crews would keep both wings at strength. The Fleet kept that promise. The squadrons came back from the Acheron. The replacements kept coming down.

By the end of Rotation Twelve, the Wrath wings had flown more than nine thousand sorties over the Flats and lost three hundred and twelve craft.

They hadn't won the air over the Acropolis. Nobody on the fields pretended they had. The lances and the swarms over the cone were still too strong, and the Nest still stung everyone who flew over it, and the uploads still came in empty half the time. But they had won the air over the rest of the Flats. The swarms no longer struck the cable heads at the Stair. The convoys reached Kappa without loss. The Third's gun lines were no longer bombed at every watch. For the soldiers in the trenches, it was the most important change in their war since the Behemoths had come.

**CABLE HEAD TWO. ROTATION TWELVE, CYCLE 300**

Halloran went down to the foot of the Stair on the three hundredth cycle of Rotation Twelve, on a day's leave from the fields, and found the canteen at Cable Head Two, and went in.

She'd heard about the wall. The ground crews talked about it. She stood in front of it with a cup of the canteen's terrible tea and looked at it for a long time.

It was chalked over from end to end by now. At the top, in old faded chalk, two numbers, from Rotation Eight and Rotation Nine, with a line drawn underneath them, and under the line Iolanthe Brede's name, and the *4th* Squadron's, and the date of the seventy-ninth cycle of Rotation Ten. And under those, in a dozen different hands, filling the rest of the wall, names. Pilots. Gunners. Bomb-aimers. Navigators. Crews of the Wrath wings lost over the Flats and the cone, two rotations of them, chalked up by the cargo gangs of Head Two and the ground crews and the gunners of the flak towers and anyone else who came into the canteen and knew a name, with the dates beside them.

She found Darrow's, near the bottom. *K. Darrow, 4th. c157 R11.* Somebody had drawn a small Wrath beside it, badly.

A heavy grey-haired man came and stood beside her with a cup in his hand. He had bad knees and a loadmaster's flash on his shoulder.

"You knew him?" he said.

"My wingman."

"Sorry." He looked at the wall. "I'm Brodie. I run the yard here. I don't know any of them, really. Most of them. But they come in here, the crews, sometimes, on leave, like you, and they look at the wall, and sometimes they put a name up, and I thought..." He shrugged. "Somebody should know them. Down here. Seeing as it's our cables they kept up."

Halloran looked at the wall a while longer.

"He wanted a Wrath Second," she said. "He saw them come over on the fourth raid and he wanted one. He said so on the band, in the middle of the fight, with the whole Nest shooting at us. He never got one."

Brodie didn't say anything.

She took a stub of chalk out of the box on the shelf below the wall, and wrote under Darrow's name, small: *Flew a Second in the end. S. H.*

Then she finished her tea and went back up the Stair to the fields.

In the mess hall that night, the crews of the *4th* were talking, as the crews of the Wrath wings talked most nights by then, about what would happen if something larger came up out of the river to fly. Nothing larger had yet. The swarms and the Lanterns were all the drowned had put into the air over the Flats in four rotations. But the river was deep, and old, and had given up the drowned Behemoths and the drowned ships and the Frost Masons, and the crews on the fields had come to believe that it had more to give.

Halloran sat at the end of the table with her cup and listened, and didn't say anything, and thought about the Acheron stairs, where something had come up out of the deep in Rotation Twelve and the Fleet had needed the *4th* for a month, and nobody had ever told them what it was.
