## Chapter 16: The Low Geometry

*"The ceiling over the Flats remains secure. No hostile presence above the river has been observed this rotation."*

*Navarch Iole Saturnin, rotational report to the Tartarus Fleet, Rotations One through Eight*

---

**THE HEAVY CRUISER *PALLAS ASCENDANT*, NORTH OF THE LANCE ENVELOPE. ROTATION EIGHT, CYCLE 204**

Navarch Iole Saturnin had written the same sentence at the bottom of her report to the Fleet every rotation for eight rotations, and on the two hundred and fourth cycle of Rotation Eight, at the second bell of the dark watch, she stood on the bridge of the *Pallas Ascendant* and watched it stop being true.

The thing came up out of the river at the bend.

She saw it first on the main plot, as a heat source, a great bloom of white under the yellow water of the bend that the sensors couldn't resolve. Then she saw it through the forward ports with her own eyes, from forty kilometres away, as the river at the bend heaved and split and something came up out of it into the low geometry over the Flats on a column of steam and fire. It came up slowly. The river ran off it in torrents, yellow and orange and white with heat, falling back into the bend in long burning cataracts that lit the underside of the ash cloud for a hundred kilometres around. It climbed slowly on old engines that she could hear even at forty kilometres, a deep, ragged, labouring roar, and as it climbed it turned, slowly, end-on to the squadron and then broadside, and she saw its length.

Twelve kilometres. The plot said so, and she didn't believe it until she had made the sensor officer check the figure twice.

It was a hull of bronze-green divine steel, streaked and pitted and scaled with the black crust of the river bed, and at its prow, carved into the hull itself, a kilometre high, was the figure of a god. The god was Apollo. Saturnin knew the face. Every officer in the Fleet knew it from the recognition books. But half of it was gone. Half the god's face had been burned away, and the bronze of it had run and set again in long frozen drips down the prow, and through the ruin of it, along a third of the hull, ran a great split, and the split was packed with ice. White ice, glittering, that didn't melt in the heat of the river pouring off it.

She knew the ship. She had seen it in the recognition books too, and in the reports.

"Identify," she said.

"Répertoire has it, Navarch." The sensor officer's voice was quite steady. "AC-09-1642. The *Eye of Light*."

Nobody on the bridge said anything.

The *Eye of Light* was the ship the Order of the Hollow Crescent had captured, a long time ago, and the Empire had recovered at Luna. It was the ship that Minerva had crashed into the Tartarus front at Grid Delta in Rotation Four, as the waypoint for CODE 78E. Saturnin had read the report. Everyone in the theatre had read the report. After the Wedge was driven in, the wreck had lain half-buried at the river's edge at Grid Delta until the end of Rotation Five, when the river rose at Depth 38, and when the river fell again the wreck was gone. The Wedge's engineers had logged it as lost in the Phlegethon.

The river had not lost it. The river had carried it along its bed for two rotations, down to the bend at Depth 40. And somebody had found it there, and sealed its breaches with ice, and lit its reactors.

"The lances," her first officer said. "Navarch. It's inside the lance envelope. The ceiling-lances should..."

They didn't fire. Saturnin watched the plot, and the ceiling-lances of the Arsenal, which had fired at every ship of the Empire that had come within their reach for eight rotations, stood dark and silent on the spur while the *Eye of Light* climbed into the low geometry directly over them.

They had been told what to expect.

"Signal the Fleet," Saturnin said. "Priority. Hostile capital ship rising from the Phlegethon at the bend, Depth 40. Identified as AC-09-1642, *Eye of Light*. Operational. Inside the lance envelope. Not engaged by the lances." She paused. "Repeat, hostile capital ship. Request immediate reinforcement."

"Yes, Navarch."

She looked at the plot for a long time. At the great white bloom of the *Eye of Light*, climbing over the spur. At the little grey markers of her own squadron, strung out north of the lance envelope where they had held station for eight rotations, guarding a ceiling that nothing had ever threatened.

"And send the frigates out," she said. "North and east. All three. I want to know what else is coming up that river. They're to find it, avoid action and come back at full speed. Tell their captains that if any of them tries to be brave I'll have them beached."

---

She had been Navarch of the Tartarus Fleet's squadron over the Flats for nine rotations, since she had come down with the Third's first convoy in the *Pallas Ascendant*, and for nine rotations the Fleet had been taking her ships away.

It had been reasonable, every time. That was the worst of it. She had agreed with it every time. The squadron's task was to guard the convoys from the ceiling cracks down to Landing Shelf Kappa, and to hold station north of the lance envelope to keep the ceiling clear of the winged things that came up the river now and then, and the *Pallas Ascendant* alone could do both. She was one of four hundred of her class in the Fleet. She could outfight anything the deep had sent up through the ceiling at Depth 40 in six hundred years, and the Fleet knew it, and Saturnin knew it, and the soldiers of the Third in the trenches below knew it. They called her the Old Owl. Saturnin had heard it in the convoy traffic, the soldiers on the landing shelves talking about the Old Owl's lights going over at night, and she had been absurdly pleased.

So when the Fleet had asked, in Rotation Two, whether she could spare a cruiser for the Lethe holds, she had said yes. When it asked in Rotation Three for two of her frigates for the convoy routes on the Acheron, she had said yes. When it asked in Rotation Five for her second heavy cruiser, she had said yes. The river had produced nothing in five rotations that the *Pallas Ascendant* couldn't handle alone, and the Fleet needed the cruiser elsewhere, and her reports said that the ceiling was secure. Every rotation she had written it at the bottom of her report. *The ceiling over the Flats remains secure.* Every rotation it had been true.

By Rotation Eight she had the *Pallas Ascendant*. She had three Lantern-pattern frigates, all overdue for refit. She had three ceiling monitors, fit to fight but short of crew. She had two armed haulers and a troop lighter, the *Long Labour*, and three fire barges, old ore-haulers packed with keyed charges for use against things too large to shoot, which the Fleet had sent her in Rotation Six without being asked and which she had parked at the back of the squadron and forgotten.

She had reported the ceiling secure every rotation. She had believed it.

She stood on the bridge of the Old Owl and watched the *Eye of Light* climb over the spur, and thought about every one of those reports, and did not forgive herself for any of them.

---

**THE FRIGATE *IRIS FOUR*, OVER THE NORTHERN BEND. ROTATION EIGHT, CYCLE 205**

The *Iris Four* came back first, at full speed, as ordered, with her captain's report on the relay before she was back inside the squadron's screen.

She had found three ships. They had come up out of the river cloud north of the bend in the second watch, in a loose line, and the *Iris Four*'s captain had seen them on her plot at sixty kilometres and taken her frigate in close enough to read them before she turned away. They were raiders. The Répertoire's recognition files placed their pattern at two thousand years old, a pattern that had not been built since before the Third was raised, and they were crusted with the black of the river bed and patched with the same white ice, and their crews were at their stations. The *Iris Four*'s sensors had read them. Heat signatures at every gun. Figures on every bridge.

None of them were alive.

The three raiders had come close enough to read the squadron in their turn. The *Iris Four*'s captain had watched their sensors sweep over her frigate, slowly, one after another, and then sweep on past her, south, toward the *Pallas Ascendant* and the monitors and the haulers north of the lance envelope. Then they had turned away, all three together, and gone back into the river cloud without firing a shot.

Saturnin read the report twice.

"A probe," she said.

"Navarch?"

"They came to count us." She put the slate down. "They didn't want the *Iris Four*. They wanted to know what was behind her. Now they know."

She looked out of the forward port toward the river cloud in the north, where the raiders had gone. It hung low over the bend in great slow coils, red and black, the same cloud that had been turning over the Acropolis since the hundred and ninetieth cycle, and somewhere inside it, now, she could feel them, the drowned ships, waiting.

"They know my strength," she said quietly. "Or my lack of it. And when they come, they'll come in enough strength to overwhelm it."

Her first officer said nothing.

"Signal the Fleet again," Saturnin said. "Same priority. Tell them three drowned raiders of a pattern two thousand years old have probed the squadron and withdrawn. Tell them I assess a major attack within ten cycles. Tell them I need cruisers. Everything they can send." She paused. "And tell them..." She stopped.

"Navarch?"

"Nothing," she said. "Send that."

---

**THE FIRE BARGE *SLOW MERCY*, AT THE REAR OF THE SQUADRON. ROTATION EIGHT, CYCLE 208**

Bargemaster Orla Vantry had commanded the *Slow Mercy* for eleven years, the first nine of them hauling ore down the Acheron from the deep shelves to the holds, and she had never once in all that time expected to be asked to fight anything with her.

The *Slow Mercy* was not built to fight. She was an ore-hauler of the old Hephaestean pattern, four hundred metres of blunt square hull with a cockpit perched on the stern as an afterthought and a pair of engines underneath that had been old when Vantry's grandmother was a deckhand. The Fleet had bought her and her two sisters, the *Patient Hand* and the *Long Furrow*, from the Acheron Haulage Guild in Rotation Six, stripped out their ore bays, filled them with keyed charges from stem to stern, three thousand tonnes of them, and sent them down to the squadron over the Flats with their original crews still aboard. Nobody had thought to replace them.

"Fire barges," the Fleet's liaison had told Vantry, when she asked him what they were for. "For use against things too large to shoot."

"What things?"

"Things too large to shoot," the liaison had said, and gone away.

There were five of them aboard the *Slow Mercy*. Vantry. Caius Bell, her engineer, a vast silent bearded man who had been with her for all eleven years and had kept the old engines going on goodwill and wire. Wenna Doyle, the helmsman, nineteen, Vantry's sister's girl, who had come aboard on the Acheron as a deckhand three years ago and turned out to have the best hands on a helm Vantry had ever seen. And the two deckhands, Hesk and Ottery, who were brothers and did everything together and had never in their lives been further from the Acheron than the Flats.

They sat in the cockpit at the back of the squadron, behind the haulers and the *Long Labour*, and watched the plot, and waited.

"They're saying it's a fleet," Doyle said. She had the relay chatter on her headset, the squadron traffic, the ships talking to each other. "Under the bend. Drowned ships. Hundreds of them. They're saying the river's been keeping them. Every ship that ever went down over the bend. They're saying something's been fixing them."

"Who's saying?"

"Everyone. The monitors. The *Long Labour*'s gunners." She pulled the headset off one ear. "Aunt Orla. What do we do? If they come?"

Vantry looked at the plot. At the little grey marker that was the *Slow Mercy*, at the rear of the squadron, behind everything.

"We do what they tell us," she said. "Same as always."

"And what'll they tell us?"

Vantry thought about the three thousand tonnes of keyed charges in the ore bays under their feet.

"Something we won't like," she said.

---

**THE HEAVY CRUISER *PALLAS ASCENDANT*. ROTATION EIGHT, CYCLE 210**

The pickets counted fourteen hulls.

They came up out of the river at the bend in the first watch of the two hundred and tenth cycle, one after another, out of the yellow water on columns of steam and fire, and climbed the ceiling geometry toward the squadron in two long lines. Saturnin watched them come on the plot. She had known they were coming for six cycles, and she had spent the six cycles getting ready, and now that she saw them she found that she was quite calm.

Fourteen. The *Eye of Light* at the head of the northern line, with four hulls behind her: two raiders, a cruiser of a pattern the Répertoire couldn't place, and a great slow transport hulk, low in the water of the geometry, heavy with whatever it carried. At the head of the southern line, nine more, led by a drowned heavy cruiser that the Répertoire couldn't name either. It was big, nearly the size of the *Pallas Ascendant*, its hull black with the river and split along its spine, and as it climbed it caught the light of the bend and burned dull red along its whole length. The soldiers of the Third, watching it from the trenches, would call it the *Cinder Dawn*, and the name would stick.

"Fleet signal, Navarch," her signals officer said. "Cruiser group detached from the ceiling cracks at Depth 36. Navarch Cassia Thorne in the *Themis Unbowed*, with the *Hyperion*, the *Patience of Hera* and a squadron of escorts. Coming at best speed."

"How long?"

"Several hours, Navarch."

Saturnin looked at the plot. At the fourteen drowned hulls climbing toward her in two lines.

"Then we'll have to manage until she gets here," she said. "Squadron to action stations. All ships."

She met them with everything she had.

---

It was not much. She knew it wasn't much. The *Pallas Ascendant* at the centre of the line, with her full batteries and her shields at full and a crew who had trained every watch for eight rotations against an attack that had never come. Two of the monitors on her left, the third on her right. The two armed haulers behind them, and the *Long Labour* behind the haulers, with her troop decks empty and every gun she had manned. The three frigates, back from their picket runs, on the flanks. The fire barges at the rear.

The drowned fleet divided.

She had expected it to. She had hoped it wouldn't. The northern line, five hulls led by the *Eye of Light*, swung round the northern end of her line, wide, in a long slow curve. The southern line, nine hulls led by the *Cinder Dawn*, came down the southern side. They passed her line on either side, the two drowned lines going past each other and past her at close range, and for eleven minutes the low geometry over the Flats became a single gun battle that wrecked both sides.

The *Eye of Light* raked the length of her line as she passed.

Saturnin had read about the *Eye of Light*'s batteries. Everyone had. They had been built to fight the Princes, in the old wars, and the river had not harmed them. She watched them fire on the plot, a long rippling stream of white along the drowned ship's flank, and felt the *Pallas Ascendant* shudder under her as the first salvo hit the shields, and the second, and the third, and saw the shield readings on her display drop from full to half to a quarter to nothing in under a minute.

"Shields gone forward, Navarch!"

"Return fire. All batteries. On the *Eye of Light*."

The *Pallas Ascendant* fired. Saturnin felt it through the deck, the long rolling shock of the Old Owl's main batteries going off together, and watched the shells cross the gap on the plot and strike the drowned ship's flank, and watched them burst on the bronze-green hull and the white ice, and watched the *Eye of Light* sail on through them unhurt.

"The ice," her first officer said. "Navarch, it's the ice. The shells aren't getting through it."

"Then aim at the bronze."

The squadron came apart around her.

She watched it on the plot, and through the ports, and on the faces of her bridge crew. The frigate *Iris One* took a full salvo from the *Eye of Light* across her stern and went dark and began to drift, slowly, end over end, out of the line. One of the monitors on the left blew apart. Saturnin saw it through the forward port, a sudden white flower of fire, and then another, the second monitor, a few seconds later, and then a long rattling rain of debris across the *Pallas Ascendant*'s armour. On the southern side, the *Cinder Dawn* went past the *Long Labour* at a kilometre and launched her attack craft, a swarm of small black shapes that came off her flanks and went at the troop lighter's engines, and the *Long Labour* lost three reactors in two minutes and lost her thrusters and began to fall, slowly, out of the line, toward the Flats.

"*Long Labour* reports no thrust, Navarch. She can't hold station. She's asking for orders."

Saturnin looked at the plot. At the *Long Labour*, falling out of the line, with the *Cinder Dawn*'s attack craft swarming over her. At the rest of her squadron, breaking up around her.

"Tell her I can't reach her," she said. "Tell her to keep her guns firing."

The *Long Labour*'s crew kept her guns firing.

---

The *Pallas Ascendant* had been hit eleven times by the end of the first pass. She was listing. Saturnin could feel it in the deck under her feet, a slow lean to port that the stabilisers couldn't correct, and there was smoke in the bridge and somebody somewhere below was shouting on the internal channel about fires on the third gun deck. But she was still fighting. Her main batteries had found the bronze, at last, and hit it, and a drowned transport hulk in the northern line had taken a full salvo amidships and was streaming fire, and one of the raiders had gone down into the river cloud in pieces.

And two of the drowned cruisers in the southern line hadn't fired at all.

Saturnin saw it on the plot near the end of the first pass, and didn't believe it, and made the sensor officer check. Two cruisers in the *Cinder Dawn*'s line, old, crusted, patched with ice like all the rest. They had come down the southern side of her line at close range with their batteries trained on the *Pallas Ascendant* and every ship behind her. They could have finished the Old Owl. They could have finished every ship in her squadron.

They hadn't fired a shot.

And as she watched, they turned away. Both of them, together, out of the *Cinder Dawn*'s line, in a long slow curve, back down toward the river cloud.

"Navarch," her sensor officer said. "Why...?"

"I don't know," Saturnin said.

She never did know. She thought about it for the rest of her life, and she never knew. She thought sometimes that the dead crews of those two cruisers had remembered something, down there in the river, at their stations, that the others hadn't. She thought sometimes that it was something else entirely. She never found out.

She didn't know, either, that Thorne's cruisers were coming. Most of her relays had been shot away in the first pass, and the Fleet's last signal had said several hours, and that had been two hours ago.

---

**THE FIRE BARGE *SLOW MERCY*. ROTATION EIGHT, CYCLE 210**

The order came to the fire barges at the end of the first pass, on the last relay the *Pallas Ascendant* still had, in Saturnin's own voice.

"Fire barges," she said. "All three. Target the drowned transport in the northern line. The big one. She's carrying troops for the Flats. Steer onto her and abandon ship. Your lighters will pick you up."

Vantry listened to it twice. Then she took off her headset and looked round the cockpit at her crew.

"Well," she said. "There it is."

Nobody said anything. Bell sat at the engine board with his great hands resting on it, looking at the plot. Doyle had both her hands on the helm. Hesk and Ottery stood together at the back of the cockpit, as they always did, close enough that their shoulders touched.

"Wenna," Vantry said. "Bring us round. Take us up to the transport."

"Yes, Aunt Orla."

The *Slow Mercy* came round, slowly, the old engines labouring, and climbed out of the back of the squadron toward the northern line. Vantry watched the plot. She watched the *Patient Hand* come round on her left and the *Long Furrow* on her right, three square blunt hulls climbing together through the wreckage of the first pass toward the drowned transport, which was still streaming fire from the *Pallas Ascendant*'s salvo and still coming on, low and heavy, toward the Flats.

The *Long Furrow* died first. A raider in the northern line saw her coming and turned its batteries on her, and three thousand tonnes of keyed charges went up together, a kilometre to Vantry's right, in a single enormous white flash that blanked the plot and shook the *Slow Mercy* from stem to stern and threw Hesk and Ottery against the back wall of the cockpit. When the plot came back, the *Long Furrow* was gone, and so was the raider.

The *Patient Hand* made it to within three kilometres of the transport before the *Eye of Light* saw her. Then she was gone too.

"Bell," Vantry said. "Everything you've got."

Bell gave her everything he had. The old engines screamed. The *Slow Mercy* climbed, faster than she had ever climbed, faster than Vantry had known she could, through the smoke and the debris toward the drowned transport, and the transport grew in the forward ports, a long black wall of hull streaked with fire and patched with ice, and the drowned transport's guns began to turn toward them.

"Two kilometres," Doyle said. Her voice was quite steady. "One and a half."

"Lighter," Vantry said. "Hesk. Ottery. Get the lighter ready."

The brothers went. The lighter was in a bay under the cockpit, a little open boat with an engine on the back, enough to carry five, and Vantry heard them go down the ladder to it and heard the bay doors open with a groan.

"Wenna," she said. "Lock the helm. On the transport. Then go."

Doyle locked the helm. She did it properly, as Vantry had taught her, the course set and the lock checked and checked again, and the *Slow Mercy* went on toward the transport on her own, straight and true, at full speed. Then she took her hands off the helm and looked at Vantry.

"Go, Wenna."

Doyle went. Vantry heard her on the ladder.

Bell didn't move.

"Caius," Vantry said.

He looked at her from the engine board, a long steady look out of his great bearded face. He didn't say anything. He had hardly said anything in eleven years. He looked at her, and then he looked at the plot, at the transport ahead of them, and at the drowned transport's guns turning toward them, and Vantry saw what he saw.

The guns were turning. They would fire before the *Slow Mercy* reached her. A ship on a locked helm went straight. A ship on a locked helm was easy to hit. If the transport's gunners hit the *Slow Mercy* before she reached the transport, she'd go up a kilometre short, the same as the *Long Furrow*, and the transport would go on, down onto the Flats, with whatever was in her holds.

A ship with somebody at the helm could weave.

Vantry looked at Bell for a long moment. Then she went to the ladder and called down it.

"Hesk. Ottery. Wenna. Go. Now. Don't wait for us."

"Aunt Orla..."

"*Go, Wenna.*"

She heard the lighter's engine start, and the bay doors close, and the lighter go, falling away beneath the *Slow Mercy* into the smoke.

Then she went back to the helm and unlocked it, and took it in her own hands, and Bell gave her everything he had left.

The drowned transport's guns fired. Vantry saw the first salvo coming and pulled the *Slow Mercy* hard to port, and it went past her starboard side, close enough to feel. She pulled her back to starboard, and the second went past to port. She had been flying ore-haulers on the Acheron for thirty-one years, through rockfalls and flood-surges and the pale things that came up at night, and she knew how a heavy hull moved, and she flew the *Slow Mercy* toward the transport just as she had flown her through the Acheron narrows in flood, never straight, never still, and the third salvo missed, and the fourth.

The transport filled the forward port. Black hull and white ice and fire.

She looked across the cockpit at Bell. He was looking at her.

"Thank you, Caius," she said.

He nodded, once.

The *Slow Mercy* went into the stern of the drowned transport at full speed, and three thousand tonnes of keyed charges went off inside it.

---

**THE HEAVY CRUISER *PALLAS ASCENDANT*. ROTATION EIGHT, CYCLE 210**

Saturnin saw it from forty kilometres away, through the forward port of a bridge full of smoke, and knew at once what it was, and knew at once that it was not what she had ordered.

The fire barge had gone in under helm. She had seen it on the plot, in the last seconds, weaving, as no ship on a locked helm could weave. Somebody had stayed aboard.

The explosion broke the drowned transport's back. She watched it go, the long black hull folding in the middle and beginning to fall, slowly, out of the low geometry, trailing fire and ice and pieces of itself. Its drowned crew brought the descent under some kind of control. She never understood how. It went down not into the river but away from it, south-west, in a long burning slant across the sky, and came down at last on the Shale Plateau beyond the head of the Demus Gorge, a hundred and forty kilometres behind the Third's line, where it tore a furrow three kilometres long through the shale and lay still, with its holds shut.

The lighter from the *Slow Mercy* was picked up by the *Hyperion*'s escorts nine hours later, drifting over the Flats with three aboard, a girl and two brothers. The girl told the *Hyperion*'s captain what had happened, and the *Hyperion*'s captain wrote it down, and it was read into the record of the Fleet's board of inquiry at Callistus in Rotation Nine.

Bargemaster Orla Vantry and Engineer Caius Bell of the Acheron Haulage Guild had carried no implants. They had been hired as civilians, and nobody had thought to fit them.

---

While the transport fell, the *Eye of Light* went down.

Saturnin watched her go. She had come round the northern end of the squadron's line in her long slow curve, and now she turned, and dropped, down out of the low geometry, toward the Flats, into the lowest geometry of all, directly over the spur and the Mora Cut, deep inside the lance envelope where no ship of the Empire could follow her.

And she opened her holds.

Saturnin saw it on the plot first, a sudden great scatter of small heat sources pouring out of the drowned ship's flanks, hundreds of them, then thousands. Then she saw it through the forward port. Landing craft. Boarding boats. The *Eye of Light*'s own Lanterns, old and crusted and patched, coming off her in swarms and going down toward the Flats, toward the spur and the Mora Cut and the ash in front of the Third's lines, each of them carrying whatever the Forsworn had gathered in the river for two rotations.

Then the *Eye of Light* opened her lower holds, and lowered on her cargo cables four shapes that Saturnin, for a long moment, couldn't make sense of at all.

They were Behemoths. Walking engines. The great war-machines of the Forge of Hephaestus, which Saturnin had seen only in the recognition books and the Third's parades, each of them a fortress on legs, taller than the Old Owl was wide. These were old. They were older than any pattern in the books. They were crusted with the black of the river bed and patched with the white ice like everything else that had come up out of the river, and they hung on the *Eye of Light*'s cables, swaying, four of them in a row, as she lowered them slowly down toward the ash.

They touched down on the ash before the Mora Cut. The cables let them go.

They stood up.

Saturnin watched them stand up, one by one, through the smoke and the burning debris in the low geometry, four drowned Behemoths of the Forge of Hephaestus, lost in the river over the millennia, rising on their great legs on the ash in front of the Third's northern lines.

Then they began to walk.

---

**THE CRUISER *THEMIS UNBOWED*. ROTATION EIGHT, CYCLE 211**

Navarch Cassia Thorne arrived over the Flats with the cruiser group at the second bell of the two hundred and eleventh cycle, several hours behind the drowned fleet, and knew from the first glance at her plot that she was too late.

The drowned ships had passed into the low geometry over the Flats. The *Eye of Light* had already landed her cargo. The squadron that should have stopped her was a scatter of wrecks and burning hulks across a hundred kilometres of sky, and the Old Owl, the *Pallas Ascendant*, was listing, half her batteries silent, her shields gone.

Thorne came on anyway. She had one hope left. The *Eye of Light* had gone down into the lowest geometry to land her cargo, and now she would have to climb back out of it, slowly, on her old engines, past the cruiser group, and Thorne meant to be waiting for her when she did.

The *Eye of Light* climbed. The cruiser group met her.

It went wrong almost at once. Thorne's escorts went in first, in the old pattern, fast, to draw the drowned ship's fire, and the *Eye of Light*'s batteries cut them to pieces. The *Cinder Dawn* came up out of the geometry over the spur on the cruiser group's flank, with the remains of the southern line behind her. The *Pallas Ascendant*, trying to close and support, took a salvo from the *Cinder Dawn* across her stern, and her engines went, and she drifted out of the fight. The *Hyperion* was hit and hit again. Only the *Patience of Hera*, the old grand cruiser, a ship older than any captain in the Fleet, came through the first hour fit to fight.

By the end of the first watch, the battle had turned decisively against the Fleet. The drowned fleet still had four capital ships in action, the *Eye of Light* and the *Cinder Dawn* among them. The Fleet had lost the low geometry over the Flats.

Saturnin's last signal came in on the *Themis Unbowed*'s relay near the end of the watch, on the last relay the *Pallas Ascendant* could raise. It was addressed to the captain of the *Patience of Hera*, and Thorne read it anyway.

*To the captain of the Patience of Hera. I ask you to report my own view of today to the Fleet's board of inquiry, as I may not be in a position to report it myself. My view is that I have failed in my duty. I reported the ceiling secure for nine rotations, and it was not. Saturnin.*

Thorne read it twice. Then she put the slate down.

She looked at the plot. At the *Eye of Light*, climbing past the cruiser group, slowly, on her old engines, with her batteries firing in long rippling streams of white and the burned face of the god on her prow catching the light of the bend. In another hour she would be out of reach, up in the geometry over the spur, and nothing the Fleet had would be able to touch her there.

"Helm," Thorne said. "Ramming speed. Target the *Eye of Light*."

Her first officer turned and looked at her.

"Navarch..."

"I know," Thorne said. "Ramming speed."

---

The ram is still taught at the Fleet's school at Callistus. It was brave, and it failed.

The *Themis Unbowed* struck the *Eye of Light* a third of the way along her length, below the burned face of the god, at the full speed of a cruiser of the line. Thorne had aimed her at the split. She had seen it on the plot and in the recognition files, the great split along a third of the hull, packed with ice, and she had thought that if anything could break the *Eye of Light*, it was a cruiser driven into the split at ramming speed.

The ice held.

The *Themis Unbowed* broke herself on it. Her prow crumpled. Her hull folded back on itself from the prow to the bridge, and her reactors went, one after another, and she fell away from the *Eye of Light*'s flank in a long slow tumble, burning, toward the Flats. On the *Eye of Light*'s flank, where she had struck, the white ice in the split glittered, cracked across in a long jagged line, and held.

Thorne's crew of eleven thousand were transferred in the next four seconds. Ninety percent of them came back.

Thorne did not.

**THE LOW GEOMETRY OVER THE FLATS. ROTATION EIGHT, CYCLE 212**

The battle left the low geometry over the Flats a field of wrecks.

The Fleet had lost or abandoned eleven ships: the *Themis Unbowed*, four frigates and escorts, two monitors, the *Long Labour* and the three fire barges. Every other ship engaged was damaged. The *Pallas Ascendant* barely survived and would need two rotations in the yards. The *Hyperion* was badly hurt. Only the *Patience of Hera* came out fit to fight.

Of the drowned fleet, the Fleet could confirm the destruction of the transport on the Shale Plateau and four raiders. The rest had withdrawn into the river cloud, or into the geometry over the spur, where nothing could reach them.

The drowned now held the low geometry over the Arsenal, and could land whatever they wished.

The *Cinder Dawn* landed her cargo the next cycle: ten thousand Cinderborn of the Red Furnace, the Phlegethon's fighting congregation, under their war-priest Draugos the Kindled, who had burned and healed for four hundred years and whose followers held that he could not die by fire. From the river cloud, a drowned ship that the soldiers of the Third would come to call the *Rimebound* came down low over the spur and lowered onto it, on long cables, a warband of figures in ice-white armour: the Frost Masons of the Cocytus, siege engineers, who had sealed the drowned hulls under the bend for two rotations and would now build for the Archflamen defences the Third had never seen.

By the Répertoire's estimate, more than sixty thousand new enemies came down onto the Flats in the eleven cycles after the battle of the low geometry. None of them carried implants. All of them had come to kill the Empire's soldiers in a way that would last.

**LEAF 52-34, THE FORWARD TRENCH OF THE *44TH*. ROTATION EIGHT, CYCLE 212**

Centurion Marrow stood on the fire-step of the *6th* Cohort's trench on Leaf 52-34, against the edge of the Ganger's Cut, and watched the Behemoths walk.

They were a long way off. They had come down on the ash before the Mora Cut, thirty kilometres to the north, and they were walking south, slowly, along the line of the old northern spur, toward the Third's northern regiments. She could see them through her field-glass, four tall black shapes against the red glow of the ash cloud, swaying a little at every step, each step taking a long time. Even from thirty kilometres, through the haze, she could feel them walking. It came up through the fire-step into her boots, a slow, deep, regular shock, over and over.

The whole *6th* Cohort was on the fire-step watching them. Nobody spoke.

Above them, in the low geometry, the wrecks were still burning. She could see them too, scattered across the sky over the Flats, the remains of the squadron that had guarded the ceiling for nine rotations, glowing red and orange in the smoke. Somewhere up there, high above, she could see a single set of running lights, faint and slow and listing, moving north toward the ceiling cracks. Somebody beside her said it was the Old Owl, going home.

"She'll be back," somebody else said.

Nobody answered.

Optio Brandt came up onto the fire-step beside her. He looked at the Behemoths through his own glass for a long time.

"Ours?" he said. "Centurion. Are they ours? They're Forge pattern. The Forge built them."

"The Forge built them," Marrow said. "A long time ago. And then they went into the river."

She lowered her glass. She looked up, at the burning wrecks in the low geometry, and the empty sky above them where the Old Owl's lights had always been. She looked back at the pylon, eleven hundred metres behind the *6th*'s trench, with its little red lamp blinking through the haze. She looked south, toward the Demus Gorge, where the reformation peak was coming up onto a front with nothing behind it.

She thought about the Wall at Watchpoint Hades, and the nine thousand.

"There's nothing coming down the Stair behind us, Saul," she said quietly. "Is there."

Brandt didn't answer for a long time.

"No, Centurion," he said. "I don't think there is."

**CALLISTUS. ROTATION NINE**

The Fleet's board of inquiry sat at Callistus in Rotation Nine.

It found that Navarch Saturnin had been left with a squadron unfit for the station she held, and had reported it fit for nine rotations, and that the Fleet had withdrawn her ships in the belief that the river could produce nothing she could not meet. It found both errors to be the Fleet's and Saturnin's together. It declined to accept her own verdict on herself. She was relieved of the station, and given the *Pallas Ascendant* again when the yards had finished with her.

Navarch Thorne was commended posthumously for the ram.

The board's harshest findings were reserved for the Fleet's intelligence officers. They had held the Répertoire's reformation warning for Depth 40 for nine cycles without connecting it to the hulls on the river bed, which had been charted for two thousand years. A drowned fleet had been assembled under the bend for two rotations, within sight of a Fleet squadron, and nobody in the Fleet had thought to ask what the Frost Masons were doing there. The board found that they had failed in their duty, and that the Third had paid for it.

The record of the inquiry ran to eleven hundred pages. On page nine hundred and four, in the evidence of the captain of the *Hyperion*, there was an account of the last minutes of the fire barge *Slow Mercy*, as told by her helmsman, Wenna Doyle, nineteen, of the Acheron Haulage Guild.

The board did not comment on it. It noted that Bargemaster Vantry and Engineer Bell had been civilians, without implants, and that the Fleet had no provision for honouring civilians.

In Rotation Ten, after the Fleet had been asked about it in writing by a Navarch it could not ignore, it made one.
