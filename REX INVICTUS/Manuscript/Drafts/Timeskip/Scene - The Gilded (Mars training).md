## The Gilded

*Draft scene for the seven-year term on Mars, Year Four. Percy is twenty. Proposal, adapted beat for beat from the author's reference passage.*

---

**FORWARD BASE ANABASIS, JEZERO. YEAR FOUR OF THE TERM**

Over the next few days Percy found out that Cassel von Romhardt was not the only one who had come to Mars to get himself gilded.

The garrison had been swallowing replacement drafts and cargo lighters for a month, and along with the troopers and the crates came officers, a great many of them, and the ones who made Percy rub the back of his neck were the candidate officers fresh out of the Tharsis War College.

Von Hallerstein, von Kessrath, von Selmitz, a Pyrrhus cadet from the Ascraeus branch, two more von Romhardts who swore they were only distant cousins of Cassel's and of the late Varric's...

The roster had so many *vons* on it that Minerva had started counting them out loud.

*Nineteen,* she said, somewhere behind his left eye. *Twenty if you count the one who spells it with a capital V, which I do, because he would want me to.*

Most of them had graduated within the year or held fresh candidate commissions, and every one of them was turned out to the last stitch, dress greys pressed, boots polished until the dome lights swam in them, and they carried their arrogance in front of them so openly that Percy could smell it from the far side of the parade ground.

None of that was strange for the Empire. The Houses of the Pyrrhuiesian Command were a military aristocracy in everything but the name, and their sons and daughters had filled the officer corps for as long as there had been a corps to fill, so that an officer from a deck-born family, a Captain Joakim Brack or a Lieutenant Freya Sköld, was the exception that the Houses liked to point at when anyone said the system was closed.

The gilding did not surprise him either. Old families had been polishing their heirs on somebody else's battlefield since before Troy, and Percy had seen it done in two lifetimes.

"Looks like we've become popular," he said, and passed the roster across the desk to his chief of staff.

Tribune Lothar von Esche took it, ran an eye down the column and let a faint crease show between his brows.

"It was always going to happen, Lord Imperator. Leave your name out of it entirely and look only at how fast this cohort promotes. As a springboard, there is nothing better on Mars."

"A springboard."

Percy leaned back until the chair creaked under him, and the sound he made was not quite a laugh.

"So they read 'Sentinel' on the door, and they read 'Instruction', and they somehow walked straight past the most important word in the whole damned title."

"Assault," Esche said, and nodded once. "I suspect they believe that if they walk close enough behind you the battles will win themselves and the citations will follow. The dying is something they expect the troopers to handle."

"Then they're going to be disappointed."

Percy got up and went to the window. Down on the parade ground the new candidates were standing about in little knots, shaking hands, laughing at each other's jokes and comparing whose House had sent the better luggage, and they could have been at a garden party on one of the old estates under Olympus Mons for all the notice they took of the cohort drilling past them in full kit. His face settled while he watched them, and whatever warmth had been in it went out.

"How do you want them trained, sir?" Esche asked behind him. "Standard programme? Or..." He chose the next words with care. "Or should we go a little easier on them? If we happen to break the heir of a great House, the Office of Houses will send complaints to the Senior Commander, and the Senior Commander has enough on her desk."

It was a real problem and both of them knew it. Percy was Lord Imperator by verdict, CODE 18, signed and sealed, and Marien Pyrrhus had handed him the training of every soldier on Mars eight months ago with a face so grey that he had not argued, but the Houses did not care much for verdicts. They cared about whose sons came home with medals, and a Lord Imperator who made enemies of twenty of them at once would spend the next decade wading through their grievances.

Percy turned round and looked at the man who would one day plan campaigns that the War College taught for a thousand years, and there was nothing light left in his eyes at all.

"Lothar, you're still thinking like the House you came from."

He came back to the desk and put both hands flat on it, leaning over until Esche had nowhere to look but at him.

"Oh, they'll get special treatment. You can count on that."

Esche let out half a breath of relief, and Percy cut the other half off.

"Double drill. Train them into the ground."

The tribune's mouth came open, and stayed open, which was the first time in four years Percy had seen Lothar von Esche lose control of any part of his face.

"They're going to be line officers," Percy said. "Platoon and company. They walk in front of troopers. If the officer is a soft-legged shrimp who's sucking air after a hundred metres and goes green the first time somebody else's blood lands on him, what makes you think a single trooper will follow him over a parapet?"

He rapped the desk with two knuckles, and the heavy, flat knock of it went through the plating and up into Esche's elbows.

"In my cohort nobody obeys an officer for his collar tabs. Nobody obeys him for the *von* either. They follow him because he's the strongest thing on the line and they'd trust him with their necks, and they'd be right to. If he can't be that, he can pack his luggage and go home now, before he gets himself and twenty troopers killed in a trench on the Flats."

The words were heavy enough to ring in the tribune's ears, and Esche stood there with the roster in his hand and looked at a superior several years younger than himself, and felt something that he would have been embarrassed to call respect.

In an army that ran on lineage and seniority, there was perhaps one man on Mars who would say that out loud and then actually do it.

Esche had his doubts about the method, and they showed, but he was too good an officer to pretend the argument was wrong.

"Understood, sir. To the highest standard, then."

"No. Higher than that."

Percy showed his teeth, which was the closest thing to a smile anyone in the cohort had seen from him in a month.

"They drill with the troopers in the day. At night every officer and every sergeant gets a second session on top, and I'll run it myself."

Esche nodded and offered up a short, private prayer for the souls of nineteen proud young lords, or twenty if you counted the capital V.

Being taken under the wing of a commander whose body had stopped keeping to anyone else's limits was going to be a great deal more exciting for them than the front.

He had his hand on the door and the training schedule already half built in his head when Percy spoke again.

"One more thing, Lothar."

"Sir?"

Percy looked at him, and then past him at Adjutant-Tribune Wendel von Rauth, who had picked that moment to come in with the morning returns under his arm.

"If we're leading by example, the cohort headquarters can't be the exception."

He pointed at Esche, and then at Rauth, who had no idea what he had walked into.

"Every officer and sergeant who skipped the War College refresher and stayed back here, plus the two of you. Night session. With me."

Esche missed his footing on the door sill and caught himself on the frame.

The folder of returns slid halfway out from under Rauth's arm before he trapped it against his ribs.

"S-sir," Rauth managed. "We... us as well?"

"Obviously you as well." Percy sat back down, perfectly reasonable about it. "Staff officers are officers. If the headquarters gets overrun some night, what's your plan, stab them in the arse with a stylus?"

The two of them wore the faces of men who had just been told their families had died in a fire, and Percy found, to his own mild surprise, that his mood had improved a great deal.

"Don't look at me like I've sentenced you. I have, but it's for your own good. Look at the two of you, then look at me. This cohort is going to be the benchmark for the whole army, and the benchmark doesn't get winded on stairs."

Esche and Rauth looked at each other and found the same despair waiting there.

They both knew perfectly well what had been happening to their Lord Imperator since the war on Mars began in earnest. When the *1st* Sentinel Instruction Assault Cohort was first raised, Percy had already been far ahead of anyone else in it, and that was before Minerva started threading the Sentinel implants into him one by one. Now he had gone somewhere past ahead, into a category the medicae did not have a column for.

Only yesterday the engineers had finished the new four-hundred-metre course on the Jezero rim, and Percy had not even waited for the paint on the markers to dry before he ran it. To everyone watching, he had covered the ground so fast that he barely seemed to touch it, and he came back over the last wall with his face its ordinary colour and his breathing perfectly even, and asked the nearest platoon whether anyone wanted to go round with him again.

*Heart rate sixty-one,* Minerva had observed at the time. *You could at least pretend, Varlet. They are going to resent you.*

Night drill with that man would be a firing squad with a stopwatch, and both of them knew it.

Orders were orders, though, and an order from the Lord Imperator weighed about as much as Olympus Mons, so the two of them came to attention, saluted and said "Yes, Lord Imperator," which was the only answer the regulations allowed them.

The door closed behind them, and they had gone perhaps ten steps down the corridor before Rauth rounded on his superior with a look of deep personal betrayal.

"Lothar, I was right outside that door. I heard all of it. Why in Tartarus did you bring up special treatment? If you'd just kept your mouth..."

Esche let out a long sigh and tipped his head back to look up through the dome at the butterscotch sky, with the expression of a man from whom all joy had been permanently withdrawn.

"I didn't think the fire would come all the way round to us either."
