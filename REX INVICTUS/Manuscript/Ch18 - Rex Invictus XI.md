The hangar bay was abuzz with chatter and noise as the various mercs and technicians walked about in a section hollowed out in the belly of a battlecruiser. Exposed pipes and wires bled oxygen, coolant, and sparks. Rows of exo-suits stood in containment racks, their forms skeletal under the cold white lights. Beyond the glass bulkhead, Traitor moon S3-R12-P1-M2 hung suspended in the void, obsidian-black, a byproduct of a passing Hunter's of [[Artemis]] orbital bombardment that reduced most of its surface to glass and obsidian.

The platoon assembled slowly. Boots clanged on the plating, the sound sharp against the static drone of compressors. Hydraulic arms hissed as they loaded ammunition belts, power cores, and thruster modules into waiting frames.

Most were legacies, descendants of a few minor gods, conscripted into the desire of profit. For these mercenaries, their every breath was accounted for, every bullet logged, every movement an statistic in some investor's spreadsheet.

But today was different.

A few high-ranking members of Hadrian Corporation had come to spectate. As such, fuel and ammunition allocations for legacies of their status were raised from the standard twenty-five percent to seventy-five. A veritable fortune in expenditures.

"Gather up ladies. Contract is dead simple today, from up top. Some heretical forces decided setting up shop on Dionysian territory was a good idea. It's the standard '*kill everything that moves funny'*  action for the gents and gentleladies up in the high castle." Bellowed out Captain Kallix.

The gathered legacies nodded, and began hopping into their awaiting power armor.

Pneumatic clamps released with metallic sighs as the suits powered up. Armor plating folded around limbs in practiced motion, divine metals and alloy closing like jaws.

Lieutenant Serika stepped into her station, mag-boots clamping to the induction pad. She exhaled once, eyes fixed ahead as the frame closed around her.

"Interface sequence commencing," droned the technician. "[[Répertoire du Savoir Athénien]] initializing."

The interior hatch opened to a tunnel of flashing red lights and drifting debris. Alarms pulsed through the corridor. Automated turrets swiveled to engage.

Serika's HUD flickered as a shot grazed her pauldron. The armor's adaptive plating reformed instantly, sealing the breach. Her return fire cut through the drone that had fired it, its body spinning weightless, fluids crystallizing midair.

Each turn was the same: corridors, bulkheads, control nodes, all identical in sterile geometry. The only color came from the bloodless glow of their visors and the pulse of warning lights.

They reached the core chamber. Massive coils circled the room, feeding energy into the flak batteries above.

Kallix signaled. "Charges."

"Timer set, three-point-zero minutes."

A small teenage girl, ancient ceremonial robes draped over reinforced plating, charms and paper seals woven between reactive plates. Her mask was a flawless porcelain fox face, expression locked in permanent serenity. A *Miko*, it seemed.

Percy led the group down the wide marble steps from the praetorian courtyard, Aurum and Argentum padding silently at his heels. The plaza emptied slightly as senators and guards gave way, leaving only curious legionnaires and a scattering of citizens who had lingered, drawn by the rumors of the Greek stranger.

As they walked, the crowd grew, sensing the gravity of the challenge, and began murmuring among themselves. it only took a few minutes to make it down the paved roads to get to the coliseum.

Percy turned to face them, raising his hand, gesturing to the many weapons littered in the coliseum and the clearings surrounding it. "You'll get to pick my weapons for each fight," he said. "One per duel. Decide now."

Legionnaires and citizens alike leaned forward, pointing toward the racks at the edge of the arena: a slender dueling sword, the thickness of his thumb, with matching cloak, a massive greatsword, and a combat knife. Cheers and laughter erupted as the crowd voted, shouts echoing against the stone walls. Percy picked up the dueling sword first, testing its balance, letting the cloak rest over his arm. Next came the greatsword, hefted with a grin, and finally the combat knife, spinning it lightly before strapping it to his thigh.

The Colosseum's sand was coarse beneath Percy's boots as he circled Decimus, who shifted his weight behind a heavy bronze shield painted crimson. Percy's own weapon, a thin cavalry duelling sabre, rested calmly in his palm. The air smelled dry, metallic, tinged with sweat and dust. Around them, the stands filled with murmurs: legionnaires, senators, even a few old *lares* perched atop crumbling arches. Reyna and Jason watched impassively from the praetorian box, flanked by Bianca, Zoe, Eleni, and Grover, who looked pale. And with a roar, the duel began.

Percy pressed the attack, blade flashing in tight thrusts, cleverly hidden behind the swirling discs and fans of the rippling crimson-red cloak. Metal sang against metal: a flurry of blocks and misses. Decimus recovered, shield slamming into Percy's shoulder. It felt like concrete, solid and unmoving. Percy gritted his teeth, ramming back with equal force. The crowd roared—half cheering for Decimus, half gasping at Percy's speed. Sand clung to Percy's sweat-dampened neck.

Decimus charged again, shield leading like a battering ram. Percy braced, then twisted at the last second, sword hooking under the shield's edge. With a wrench, he yanked it sideways. The shield tore free, skidding across the sand. Decimus staggered, off-balance, sword arm exposed. Percy pivoted, but Decimus recovered faster than expected, gladius slicing upward. Percy jerked back, the blade close enough to feel the *whoosh* of the blade. He circled slowly, loosening his left arm. Decimus advanced, sword low and ready. Percy matched his pace. The crowd fell silent, sensing the endgame.

Decimus went for an overhead slice, but was blocked by the sabre, bending under the weight, hastily supported by Percy's left hand on the blade, pushing it back.

Decimus thrust his wrist downwards so the gladius's pommel drove forward, using the weighted pommel, to smash into Percy's chin and break the grip on the fabric. It was a clean idea: a short, powerful blow delivered where there was a seam in Percy's defense, enough to free the blade and reverse the leverage.

Percy saw the motion in the same instant Decimus committed to it. Instead of letting the pommel land, Percy rotated his cloak-wrapped arm inward, angling the fabric so the blow glanced along the padded surface rather than into a single point. He used the deflection to his advantage: as the pommel glanced off harmlessly, Percy stepped through Decimus's shortening reach, pulling the cloak tight and turning the trapped gladius into a lever. The pommel strike failed to unseat him, it only exposed Decimus' balance. Percy twisted, wrenching the blade sideways until the Roman staggered, weapon useless for a heartbeat.

Percy lunged. Decimus parried, sluggishly, but Percy followed through, blade scraping along the gladius until hilts locked. Close quarters. Decimus shoved, trying to break free. Percy held firm, left hand snatching the edge of his own cloak, as it fluttered loose. In one fluid motion, he whipped the fabric forward, wrapping it tightly around his forearm like a makeshift shield. Decimus's eyes widened, too slow. Percy surged forward, tangling his sword arm with the cloak-bound forearm, trapping the blade against his own padded limb. Decimus tried to pull back, but the cloak held his sword fast. Sand rasped under Percy's boots as he stepped in, driving the point of his sabre to hover a hair's breadth from Decimus's throat. The Roman froze, breath catching. Silence. Then Percy lowered his blade. "Yield." Decimus stared, chest heaving, before nodding stiffly. "Yield." The crowd exploded. Cheers, shouts, disbelief. Percy unwrapped the cloak, pale sand smearing the red wool as he offered a hand. Decimus took it, grudging respect in his eyes. Marcus stepped forward from the shadows, shield raised, face grim. Percy turned, flexing his wrapped arm. The fight wasn't over.

Percy stepped into the center of the Colosseum's sand, the roar of the crowd fading to a tense hush as Marcus approached. Percy took this time to hand off the duelling sword and cloak off to Eleni, waiting near the sidelines. The centurion's choice of weapon was deliberate: a Roman commander's halberd. A hybrid polearm with a blade curved like a gladius but tipped with a hook and spike, weighted for both reach and crushing impact. Percy, in contrast, hefted the massive greatsword he had selected, a claymore nearly as long as his armspan, the blade heavy and broad. A few of the experienced ones in the crowd knew that this was a bad matchup for the Son of Posiedon, but still eagerly watched on.

Marcus advanced first, halberd held high, tip glinting in the sun, gold and purple frills at the ends of the Halberd catching and reflecting light in a mesmerizing dazzle. The first strike was a sweeping arc, meant to intimidate and keep Percy at distance. Percy braced with the greatsword, planting both feet wide, and brought the blade down in a counter-arc, absorbing the impact with the flat of the steel. Sand sprayed as the halberd scraped along the greatsword, the sound ringing through the arena. Marcus grunted, feeling the vibration of the halberd's impact.

Marcus feinted a thrust, the hooked blade snapping forward, then spun to hook Percy's edge with the halberd's spike. Percy twisted, letting the halberd's spike glance off the broad steel and sliding his weight forward to meet Marcus in a forward lunge. The claymore's tip struck the sand just shy of the centurion's knees, sending grit into Marcus's eyes. Marcus staggered back, halberd raised in both defense and offense, trying to maintain reach.

Percy pivoted, using the greatsword's momentum to circle, forcing Marcus to spin with him, the centurion tried to use the halberd's hook against Percy's blade, attempting to catch it and twist, to disarm or throw off balance. Percy anticipated, letting the hook catch on the flat of the claymore and twisting the blade sharply, levering Marcus's arm upward. The halberd's momentum carried the centurion slightly off-balance, his feet sliding in the sand.

A sweeping horizontal strike from Marcus forced Percy to step back, which allowed the Son of [[Poseidon]] to bring the claymore overhead in a devastating downward arc. Marcus backpedaled, raising the halberd to block, but the sheer weight of Percy's blade slammed through the guard, shoving the polearm back several inches. Percy followed through, stepping forward, driving Marcus backward.

Marcus attempted a pommel strike this time, like Decimus had tried—a jab with the halberd's haft aimed at Percy's midsection. Percy twisted, shifting the claymore to absorb the impact on the broad flat near the hilt. The force rattled his shoulders but didn't dislodge him. Seizing the opportunity, Percy rotated the greatsword in a horizontal arc, the claymore's tip now sweeping toward Marcus's flank. Marcus barely spun away, scraping his boots through sand, the halberd scraping off the claymore with a shriek of metal on metal.

Breathing heavily, Percy readied the next strike. Marcus pressed forward, trying to regain leverage with wide, brutal arcs of the halberd, but with the massive momentum of the greatsword, breaking through with brute force was impossible.

Finally, with a well-timed feint to Marcus's right, Percy spun the claymore vertically, catching the halberd's shaft between his hands. Using leverage, he twisted sharply, wrenched the polearm sideways, and drove Marcus off-balance into the sand. The halberd clattered away, sand puffing around Marcus as he scrambled to rise. Percy raised an overhead strike at him, chest heaving, eyes locked.

Marcus's shoulders slumped, the centurion finally acknowledging the inevitable. "Yield," Percy said calmly, the sword's edge hovering just short of Marcus's hair. Marcus wiped sweat and sand from his brow, nodding stiffly. "Yield," he admitted, the roar of the crowd erupting in reaction to the spectacle.

The arena's stands thrummed with anticipation, sand shifting under the collective weight of thousands of eyes. Marcus and Decimus had already fallen to Percy's duels, leaving the final test: Praetor [[Jason Grace]]. Reyna, Bianca, Zoë, Eleni, and Grover watched from the praetorian box, tension taut. Even the legionnaires around the Colosseum's perimeter were trickling in, attracted by the crowds.

Reaching down, Jason drew a divine gladius from its scabbard, a blade humming faintly with its own energy, the steel etched with sigils that glowed as if alive. Lightning sparked along the hilt, arcing faintly around his arm. Jason held it out, eyes narrowing at Percy. "I will not hold back," he said evenly, voice carrying to the far corners of the stands. "No more games. This is how it ends."

"Riiiiiight."

He jumped from the ramp with a powerful bound, feet slamming onto the sand with a controlled impact that raised a small cloud, boots skidding into a crouched stance. His gladius came alive in his hands, charged with lightning that danced along the blade and licked up his forearms.

Jason took the first move, a wide horizontal cleave. His strikes coming in jagged, blinding arcs, but Percy was a storm of technique, using every part of his weapon, every angle of attack.

He lunged low with a standard forward stab, knife tip aimed for Jason's midsection, then pivoted on his heel, slashing diagonally across the gladius with the edge. As Jason parried, Percy brought his offhand up, jabbing with the flat of his palm to disrupt Jason's grip. He punched with the hand holding the knife, wrist snapping in a backhand strike, then shifted to an ice-pick grip, hammering the blade downward from above in a brutal overhead strike.

Jason countered with a lightning-augmented swipe; Percy rolled beneath it, letting the knife's spine collide with the Roman's forearm in a blunt strike, then twisted the grip to stab upward from under Jason's guard. A quick pivot, and Percy executed a cleaving horizontal swing, edge slicing past Jason's elbow as he leapt back to evade a retaliatory strike.

Dodging under yet another horizontal slash, the Son of Poseidon swept his legs low, but was dodged by a short hop by the son of Jupiter. Pommel strikes came next, Percy slammed the butt of the knife against Jason's forearm when the Roman attempted a downward cut, knocking the gladius sideways, immediately following with a snap stab to the shoulder. He alternated grips fluidly: standard for reach, ice-pick for power, reverse edge for hooking, half-hand off for offhand jabs, and back to a traditional hold to slash or rip across exposed areas, and a brutal kick to the Son of Jupiter's chest.

Every move was a transition; nothing wasted. Percy dodged left, avoiding another overhead gladius strike and used the knife's spine to shove at Jason's chest, then pivoted, elbowing with the knife hand while slicing downward with the blade's edge. He pressed the attack, feet weaving, sand spraying, strikes coming as a continuous stream: jabs, slashes, hammer-fists with the knife, pommel raps, underhand ice-pick plunges, flicks, and angled hooks.

Jason tried to meet him blow for blow, sparks flying as gladius and knife collided, but Percy's versatility, his hand, and even his legs, kept him one step ahead. Each feint, stab, strike, and bind flowed seamlessly into the next, turning the duel into a violent ballet where every surface of the knife was a potential attack, every motion a trap.

By the time dents could be seen in the Praetor's armor, Jason was visibly getting angry, and after a pommel catch, Percy had battered and tested every angle, and with a thrust, hurriedly blocked, left Jason staggered, unable to find an opening in the overwhelming assault. He coughed, and moved the dust out of his way. With the cover gone, he realized the Son of Posiedon had disappeared from view. And a split second later, he realized the feeling of warm steel kissing his Adam's apple.

"Yield?"

"Yes."

---

The Romans dragged him into celebration whether he wanted it or not.

Percy barely touched his plate.

Zoë watched him from the shadows. Eleni stood near his shoulder like a silent bodyguard. Reyna seated herself with crisp authority while Jason nursed bruises in their twin elevated seats overseeing the mess hall.

"Eat," Grover muttered. "You'll pass out before tomorrow."

But Percy was no longer listening.

**Perseus…**

His jaw tightened.

**Artemis.**

He realized what the invisible weight settled upon his shoulders.

She was in agony.

He rose abruptly from the table. Benches scraped. legionnaires stared. Reyna started to speak, but the look on his face silenced even her.

He left the feast without explanation.

The Aventine shrine was simple, half-forgotten, lit only by a small brazier. Percy stepped inside and felt the thread immediately.

A bond. He knelt before the brazier.

The pressure in his chest sharpened, pain, but distant, as if borrowed. The weight across his shoulders felt suddenly twice as heavy.

He inhaled once.

"Artemis," he murmured, voice rough. "Wherever you are… you shouldn't have gone alone."

He opened the small satchel the Romans provided, bread, jerky, fruit. None of it meant anything. He tossed the offerings into the flame.

It wasn't enough. He knew it as surely as he knew the tides upon a ship. So he created a dagger from the fountain using his powers.

He sliced his palm and let his blood fall into the fire.

The reaction was immediate.

The brazier flared silver-white, higher and hotter than should be possible. The flame licked up his arm without burning him. The air snapped taut like a bowstring drawn to breaking.

The pressure in his chest vanished for a heartbeat.

Then returned stronger, steadier, no longer fading.

Artemis.

She was alive.

And she felt him.

---

Across a continent, far from Roman walls, beneath the crushing dome of the sky's divine weight, the moon goddess smiled.

There is no elegance in this burden. No dignity. Only crushing force, ancient and deliberate.

By the third hour She was shaking. Not visibly, but inside, where gods are not supposed to tremble, Father emphasized it so.

She wondered, briefly, if [[Atlas]] feels this every time the sky rests on his shoulders.

If he endures this constant grinding, this slow erosion of self beneath the pressure of fate made physical.

She wondered…

–and then he opened his mouth.

A sneer. A taunt. Something about "little moon goddess," something about how she would break sooner than anyone.

And just like that, the curiosity dies.

Whatever he suffers, he deserves.

Whatever agony he feels, it is too little.

And whatever misery this curse inflicts on him, it pales before the irritation of hearing his voice.

Her arms nearly buckled from the urge to lunge at his throat.

She forces herself still.

Atlas laughed. A deep, grinding sound that echoed through the cavern.

"Struggling already?"

She ignored him.

The pain settled deeper, threading itself into the marrow of her spine.

She grit my teeth and forced her breath to stay even.

This is for him. This is because the blade, that infuriating, familiar sword, showed her what must come.

Her fingers clawed deeper into the stone floor. The pressure shifted, slamming through her shoulders.

She gasps despite herself.

The curse delights in punishing weakness.

Atlas chuckled again.

"Poor Artemis," he crooned. "The sky suits you poorly, perhaps you are used to your bow?"

If she had a free hand, she would break his jaw.

Instead, she kept her silence and endured.

The scenery remained unchanged for multiple days by now. Artemis could hardly keep track of time, Diana had long exhausted her strength and was recuperating in their shared domain.

Then she felt it.

A spark.

Warm. Mortal… ish.

Alive.

A thread coiled around her ribs, tightening, anchoring her to something beyond the crushing dome.

That idiot.

A pulse of strength flooded her chest, foreign, unfamiliar, nauseatingly warm.

Artemis's fingers curled into the swirling orange-blue sky.

"You fool," she whispered through clenched teeth. "You reckless, impossible—"

His offering flowed into her like a tide. She closed her eyes against the pain, and against the knowledge that this was exactly the future they both had wanted to force. A bond neither of them had intended. A bond she could not sever without killing them both.

Her pain bled through the bond. Quiet and suffocating. It pulled at him like a tide. He felt her ribs shudder under pressure not meant for any god, the weight grinding down her spine.

His jaw clenched. He forced himself still.

"Artemis," he said, "Take what you need."

He didn't wait for an answer.

He sliced open his palm again. [[Ichor]] that his Curse and armor usually kept locked flowed freely. His blood hit the brazier and the flame roared, silver-white, biting at the air.

He pressed his hand into the fire. Percy mentally reached inward and tore open the part of himself he never used, the veritable hot potato of divine energy mixed with demigod vitality, the raw, volatile core that would one day grow into something impossible. Like peeling away the foil around the steaming potato, he let the energy leak through into his body.

The shrine trembled.

Bronze light poured from his shoulders like steam under pressure. His spine felt hollowed out. His vision fractured at the edges.

He poured it all into the flame.

Divine energy.

Blood.

Fragments of his [[Achilles]] curse, the invulnerability that clung to his soul like a scar.

"Take it," Percy muttered, voice strained. "Take all of it. Just hold on."

He felt the bond ignite, a violent snap that ripped a breath from his lungs. His knees hit stone. His blood sizzled in the fire but kept flowing, drawn outward by the thread between them.

A pulse answered him.

Her pulse.

A force surged back along the bond like a shockwave. Artemis's divine presence, fractured moments ago, expanded with brutal speed. It was overwhelming. It was unstoppable. She felt good. It was still only a fraction.

Percy gritted his teeth, forcing himself not to collapse as her power flared across the room like a lunar detonation.

The force of it snapped the brazier in half. Marble cracked. Dust rained from the ceiling.

Percy stayed kneeling. He did not regret it.

The flame dimmed suddenly, collapsing inward as if exhausted. His blood stopped flowing. His hand shook uncontrollably.

The bond quieted to a hum.

He pulled his hand from the fire. The wound sealed slowly, far slower than it should have. He had taken from himself more than he intended, and given her too much.

Good.

---

Percy woke to the low thrum of [[New Rome]]'s war machine moving outside the barracks. The cot was stiff. The air held the usual mix of iron dust and lamp-smoke. Bianca was half-awake already, staring at the ceiling. Grover slept curled up with his jacket as a pillow. Zoë sat on the edge of her bed, cleaning her knives with calm, methodical attention.

Percy pushed himself upright. His body felt off.

Zoë looked over. "At last. Thou hast risen."

Bianca yawned. "Morning. You look… rough?"

Grover blinked awake at her tone, looking at his friend. "Yeah. Like you fought a cement mixer."

Percy rubbed the side of his head. "Just tired."

Zoë flicked a knife lightly between her hands. It was small, balanced, meant for throwing rather than combat. She lifted an eyebrow. "Let us see how fatigued thou truly art."

Before he could reply, she tossed it toward the empty space beside him. A harmless gesture. One he had sidestepped a hundred times without thought.

Percy moved to catch it.

The blade glanced off his palm.

A thin line opened. Blood welled up immediately.

The room went silent.

Grover frowned. "Huh… That's the first time I've actually seen you bleed."

Percy stared at the cut. It stung sharply. The skin did not seal. The ache didn't fade. He flexed his fingers. The cut tugged painfully. *Artemis had leeched most of the curse when he made the offering.* He realized.

Bianca looked toward the window at the glow rising over the Field of Mars. "Well. The rest of Rome's up. Legions are already moving, you might find the roman healers somewhere."

"Aedificatori."

"What?"

"Nothing. My bad."

The five Roman cohorts turned the base of Mount Othrys into a fortified grid. Palisades locked into place. Siege engines were calibrated with young demigod engineers murmuring prayers to Vulcan. Centurions barked orders against the rising wind that rolled off the mountain like a slow exhale.

The command tent stood at the center. Inside, the strategic table glowed with candlelight and enchanted ink. Reyna and Jason were officially directing the operation, but the room had long since ceded all practical control to Eleni.

She was still only a guest advisor, but her UOE training had overrun the tent within minutes.

Eleni stood over the map as if interfacing with an invisible system only she could see. Her voice clipped, precise, shaped by an entirely different military culture.

"Your left flank is not compliant with AtRep Formation Protocol Two Theta, for some reason." she said.

"...?"

Reyna cleared her throat. "Lady Theodosia, what does that mean?"

"It means your formation is unstable under projected metaphysical pressure," Eleni replied. "Correct it. Do not force me to repeat myself."

Jason tried again. "Could you define this… 'metaphysical pressure' thingy?"

"Yes." Eleni pointed to the projection. "Your defensive line is failing to meet Enforcement Vector Code standards. Your artillery axis is misaligned by eight degrees. And your cohort spacing exceeds the allowable variance for InCoOp's parameters"

Silence.

One of the older centurions whispered to another, "Who's this Incoop guy?"

Bianca muttered to Percy under her breath, "This is like watching someone speak English but also not English at all."

Percy nodded. "She forgets she's talking in… whatever future she is from. They cannot keep up."

Eleni continued unfazed. "Additionally, your siege engines require recalibration. Their fire cycles do not match. Your engineers must implement a sequential delay matrix, otherwise you risk a cross-system downtime."

Jason rubbed his face. "We have no idea what that means."

"Then learn quickly, time is limited."

Reyna attempted to translate. "I believe she means… fire the ballistae in order rather than all at once?"

"That interpretation is technically incorrect," Eleni added. "But it will suffice."

He moved to the far corner of the tent, pulled a polished drachma from his belt, and scraped a line across its surface. The air shimmered. He bribed Iris with four coins at once, and then ten more. Even she materialized faster than usual.

Her voice whispered from the light, exasperated but pleased. "You really do not understand subtlety. When you use that many coins, it feels like you are throwing gold bars at my head."

"Please," Percy murmured. "I need to talk to her."

"I know," Iris said. "I can feel it through your draft of emotions. Hold on. Honestly, you should work on your mental barriers. I can feel it through the rainbow."

The rainbow thinned into a narrow stream.

Artemis appeared.

Her outline shimmered under enormous strain. Power radiated from her in waves that made the lamps flicker and the map distort. She was stronger than [[Zeus]] now, stronger than anything [[Olympus]] had produced in millennia. And yet she was still kneeling under the sky, eyes closed, and her huntress garb hung in tatters around her body.

"Artemis!"

Her eyes widened the moment they met his.

"Perseus. You should be sleeping!"

"And you should not be holding the fatass sky who wants to hug his wife on your back," he answered quietly.

A tremor touched her breath. "I will manage."

"You say that like it does not feel like you are being crushed."

"I am being crushed," she replied. "Your offering keeps me conscious. That is all. Your strength flows into me even now."

"I know. I could feel you pulling on it."

She closed her eyes briefly. "I try not to. I do not want to take anything more from you."

"You can take everything if it keeps you alive."

Her molten silver eyes opened. This time they were bright with something raw. "Do not say things like that. I am not meant to hear them."

Percy stepped closer to the rainbow slit. "It is the truth. You matter to me."

Artemis inhaled sharply, almost unsteady. "You speak as though I am… more than just a goddess."

"Perhaps," Percy said.

She looked away for a moment. The sky above her shifted, pressing harder for daring to feel. Her body tensed under the invisible weight. The power surging around her spiked in pain.

Percy whispered, "I am coming to you. Rome is here. We are already under the mountain."

Artemis's voice softened to a whisper. "I feel the legion. They are like sparks at the edge of the dark. You should see Atlas, he's panicking even now."

"I will reach you."

Her gaze returned to him. "Promise me something."

Percy swallowed. "Anything."

"When you climb this mountain… do not let its malice make you doubt yourself. You gave me power that surpasses Olympus. I feel it thrumming through every boundary of my being. But you gave it because you care. Because you… feel for me. Do not let the mountain take you."

He stepped closer, his voice quiet enough that only she could hear. "I feel what I feel."

Artemis whispered, "So do I."

Iris cleared her throat awkwardly. "Ahem. I hate to interrupt for a moment, but my bandwidth is dying. Please wrap this up. Plus, I'm going to combust from the emotional pressure in here. When can I tell [[Hephaestus]] TV about you two?"

Artemis's eyes lingered. "*Percy*. Come quickly."

The rainbow cracked and dissolved.

Percy stayed silent for a long moment, the echo of her presence wrapped around his ribs like a warm, painful tether.

Percy drew in a slow breath, forcing his expression neutral again. He could not afford softness here, not with the Senate's chosen commanders and Eleni's future-war intensity less than ten paces away.

He turned to leave the corner.

A faint shimmer flickered.

Almost invisible.

Barely a thread of color.

The fading after-image of the Iris-call.

He froze.

The shimmer of the collapsing Iris-connection pulsed weakly, just enough for voices to slip through.

Iris spoke first. Her tone was light, but the clarity behind it was unmistakable.

"Young one… you should not doubt what you heard. That went both ways."

Percy's posture stiffened. "Milady Iris. You are misinterpreting the situation."

"No," she said, amused and certain. "I have handled her calls for centuries. I know how Artemis speaks when she is issuing orders, and how she speaks when she is trying not to show something."

Percy placed three drachmae into the fading beam. "This remains off-record."

Fleecy's voice entered quietly. "That buys some silence… temporarily!"

He offered five more. "Permanently."

Iris gave a low hum of approval. "Good. Saying there is nothing between you two is hard to believe."

Percy kept his voice controlled. "There is a mission. Nothing more."

"Then I must be hallucinating," Iris replied dryly. "Because that is the first time in all my millennia of message-handling that Artemis did not sound like a machine, or a general, or one of her hunters."

The light flickered.

"She sounded," Iris continued, "like a person. Talking to someone she cares about."

Percy cut the connection instantly. "This conversation is over."

The rainbow died in silence.

Iris chuckled. "Whatever you say, child."

The shimmer faded.

Silence returned.

Percy exhaled once, fully composed again.

Just in time.

Because Eleni's voice broke through the tent, sharp and unyielding.

"Lord Perseus. Cease your hiding. It is unbecoming of your status. Authorization delays are degrading operational clarity. Your presence at the table is now required."

Apologetically, Reyna looked over. "She means we need you. I think."
