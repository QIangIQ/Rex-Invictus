# Rex Invictus VII

← [[Ch13 - Rex Invictus VI|Rex Invictus VI]] · [[Manuscript Index|Index]] · [[Ch15 - Rex Invictus VIII|Rex Invictus VIII]] →

The ship had no name. That was the first thing Percy noticed. A small vessel, barely seaworthy.

The vessel groaned under its own weight, creaking and shifting with the rhythm of the waves. Annabeth didn't speak at first. She sat cross-legged near the stern, a notebook open on her lap. She was remarkably relaxed considering her legacy as a direct descendant of [[Athena]] in [[Poseidon]]'s waters. Her pencil scratched quietly over the page, sketching symbols Percy didn't recognize.

He sat across from her, arms resting on his knees, watching the horizon slink past.

"You sure this thing knows where it's going?" She asked finally, not looking up from her book

He looked up. "It's bound by old magic. I asked the right way. Plus, I can feel us making progress."

"Great," Annabeth muttered. "I just hope it doesn't decide to drown us out of spite."

Annabeth glanced at him then, her expression unreadable. "You okay?"

The question hung in the air.

He thought about saying yes. He thought about saying no.

Instead, he shrugged.

"Just tired."

That wasn't a lie.

Annabeth didn't press. She returned to her sketches, fingers tapping against the parchment.

They drifted through the [[Sea of Monsters]] like ghosts. The sky turned a glassy purple, clouds stretching like pulled cotton. Strange things moved beneath the surface—shadows without shapes, coils without ends. Once, a great eye blinked up at them from the water before sinking back down without a ripple. Percy was pretty sure it was his father's [[Kraken]].

Percy stayed awake long after Annabeth had curled up in her cloak near the prow. The ship had no helm, no anchor. It moved only when it wanted to.

He stood at the edge, staring at his reflection in the water.

It shimmered.

And for a heartbeat—just a heartbeat—he didn't see his own face.

He saw hers.

[[Artemis]].

Not her, exactly. Not the one he knew in the myths, or the goddess who glared at him across council chambers. But another version. A memory half-formed. Hair like crimson starlight and frost. A look in her eyes he couldn't quite place. Grief? Regret?

Love?

The image vanished.

Percy closed his eyes and exhaled.

"I'm going crazy," he muttered.

A soft breeze curled around him, salty and cold.

The ship lurched.

Annabeth woke instantly, dagger in hand. Percy caught her gaze and nodded toward the horizon.

Land.

A jagged stretch of black volcanic rock jutted from the sea like the spine of something ancient. A narrow beach lay ahead, scattered with bleached bones and the remains of broken ships.

"Polyphemus' island?" she asked, rising.

"No," Percy said slowly. "Not yet."

Annabeth frowned. "Then what is it?"

"The Isle of Sirens."

"We don't have to stop, you know, " Percy said. "We can go around."

"I have to see," she said. "I have to know."

Percy felt a knot in his gut.

"You sure?"

She didn't look at him. "I have to."

The boat drifted toward the shore, silent as a shadow. Percy didn't try to stop her.

The Sirens were everything the stories promised—and worse. They didn't sing with mouths. They sang with memories. With regrets. With truths you never dared say out loud. They peeled open your heart and whispered what could've been, what should've been, what never was. [[Lycaon]]'s heart throbbed, its divine origins helping him resist their temptation.

Annabeth fell first. He saw her eyes go wide, saw her mouth fall open in a silent cry.

He tried to hold her back.

She dove into the sand

"Annabeth!"

---

The rest of the journey passed in uneasy silence.

By the time they neared the island of Polyphemus, the map Percy had etched in the Athena cabin started to come to life. The swirling currents, the ruined ships, the ring of wreckage around the island like a moat.

The Fleece was there. Percy could feel it. A golden warmth, pulsing like a beacon through the air.

[[Clarisse]]'s ship was already anchored offshore. The *CSS Birmingham*, reanimated and bristling with Confederate undead, looked more out of place than usual—but it had done its job.

They approached by dinghy.

The crunch-crunch of the gravelly sand underneath their feet was the only sound that accompanied the duo as they walked up a beaten footpath.

Then came the buzzing.

The air thickened. Not with humidity, but with weight. Divine weight. It pressed against his shoulders, into his lungs, into his right pocket.

He reached into his pocket and pulled the pen free.

It didn't flick open.

Instead, it burned like molten steel.

Not like fire—like memory. The ink shimmered, and for the briefest second, he saw something impossible: the blade of [[Riptide]] in its full, uncapped form. A coiled mass of metal and scripture His old riptide, which he lost a few millennia ago.

No—Anaklusmos.

"Percy?" Annabeth stepped closer. "What is it?"

"I… I have to go back to a ship."

"What?"

He turned, face pale, eyes glassy. "I have to go. Just… stay. I'll be back."

Before she could stop him, he stumbled back toward the water. Each step felt heavier, as if the ocean were clinging to him. The pen kept melting.

---

Back aboard the *CSS Birmingham*, the deck creaked under his weight. The Confederate sailors—silent as ever, watched him with empty sockets, their ghostly forms turning just slightly as he passed.

He ducked into an old storage hold below deck.

He dropped to one knee and opened his hand.

He hissed in pain, the sword was almost white-hot.

The pen tumbled to the floor, and erupted.

Riptide uncoiled in mid-air. The ghost of Zoë bloomed over it like a golden corona. Like a halo around a biblical archangel.

And then, the blade began to... merge with itself.

Percy watched it, unable to move.

An echo whispered: *"Your hands are mine. Your weapon is not your own. Your story is written in the blood of futures."*

Another voice—calmer. Female. Regretful. *"Don't resist it. You will lose yourself."*

Minerva? No. Not quite. The voice was... quieter. Like *hers*, but not *her.*

Riptide pulsed. Glowed.

Percy staggered back as the blade collapsed inward, shrinking, folding in on itself. It solidified once more into a pen. Way heavier though. Sharper too. And when he picked it up, it no longer hummed like a demigod's relic.

It sang like a god's.

[[Percy Jackson]] felt an old part of him returning.

Percy Jackson felt the weapon attempt to wrestle itself from his grasp.

And Percy Jackson was suddenly no longer sure whose sword he was holding.

---

He gritted his teeth and forced *Anaklusmos*—no, *Riptide,* back into his pocket. The metal resisted, seething like a coiled snake. But it obeyed. For now.

The ship creaked as he emerged into the sunlight. Annabeth stood at the shore, arms crossed, gaze cutting through him like Athena's spear.

"You okay?" she asked again.

He didn't answer. Just nodded. Then turned toward the island.

Polyphemus' lair loomed like a shattered tooth against the sky. Shields, spears, crates, bones bleached white, flags torn to ribbons, the full nine yards. Looked more like a movie scene than a battlefield buried in ocean spray.

They moved quietly. Percy guided them through the shallows until the underbrush swallowed the sounds of waves behind them. The island smelled like rot and damp wool.

They found Grover by accident.

Or maybe the island wanted them to.

Tied between two rocks like some horrible parody of a scarecrow, Grover barely registered their arrival. His fake bridal veil was sagging. He blinked slowly, then gasped.

"Percy? Annabeth?" he whispered hoarsely. "You're real?"

Annabeth moved first, slicing through the bindings with a knife Percy had no idea she even had. "We have to move. Now."

Grover trembled, but pointed. "The Fleece. It's on the cyclops' back. He wears it like a cloak."

Percy blinked. He didn't remember that part. "That's disgusting."

"And effective," Grover rasped. "You can't sneak up on him. Most injuries get healed instantly."

The sky darkened as a massive shadow fell across the clearing. The voice that followed was jagged, rusted with age and madness.

"Smells like demigod flesh. Two pieces. Three? Oh, happy day."

Polyphemus charged.

The ambush broke instantly. Annabeth rolled left, Grover scrambled back, Percy, already having suspected something wrong, held his ground.

Anaklusmos flared.

His chest burned. Lycaon's heart working triple overtime to meet the adrenaline surging through his veins. The first strike was a blur of bronze, shaving a chunk off the Cyclops' stone club. The second strike cut deeper: into the fleece the golden pelt flickering as it was pierced with the weapon. The light seemed to try to flow into the weapon.

Percy felt something crack inside the sword.

Polyphemus swung again, but this time Annabeth was already moving. She shouted, "Now!" and Grover unleashed a screeching goat-call. Percy leapt, driving Riptide clean into the monster's shoulder. The fleece slipped.

Annabeth capped her hat and dove for the bloodstained hide. But the moment her hands touched the Fleece, it seemed to try to dive away from her fingertips.

The air exploded with magic.

Lush grass burst from the black sand. Flowers bloomed in reverse. The storm clouds peeled back like skin from the sky.

Polyphemus shrieked as the healing magic reversed, reopening old wounds and refusing to close the new ones.

The Cyclops collapsed, his body swallowed by the island's own enchantments, like a wound healing from within.

Annabeth stumbled back, her fingers blistered and raw. The Fleece pulsed on the ground like a living heart, golden strands twitching as if trying to burrow back into the island itself.

Percy caught her before she fell. "What happened? Are you—"

"It—" she hissed, shaking her hand in pain. "It rejected me."

Grover was still dazed, staring wide-eyed at the golden and powdery patch of earth where Polyphemus had fallen.

Percy glanced at the Fleece. The air shimmered around it. It didn't look like cloth anymore.

"Why did it react like that?" he asked. "You should've been able to touch it." She could last time.

Annabeth clenched her jaw. "Because I'm my mother's daughter."

Percy frowned. "Athena?"

"Yeah," she spat. "You remember the myths? The Fleece was sacred to [[Zeus]] and Poseidon, blessed by [[Apollo]] and Artemis. But it was stolen during the Age of Betrayals. Athena tried to claim it for her own temple. Said it was 'wasted on the wild.'"

Percy blinked. "And the gods cursed it?"

"Yeah" Her voice was bitter. "They wove a rejection, any child of Athena is treated like a threat. An intruder."

Percy looked at her hands again. The welts were shaped like symbols, tiny sunbursts, tridents, crescent moons.

*Brandings.*

"It attacked me."

The wind shifted. Percy could feel the Fleece still vibrating with power, resonating with something deep inside him. The sword at his side stirred again.

Grover perked up. "That might've been what saved us. The moment it turned on Annabeth, it stopped healing Polyphemus. It recognized her as an enemy... so it cut him off too. I think."

He knelt beside the Fleece. It didn't shy away from him. It glowed steadier. The grass bent toward him like a tide.

"Poseidon's son," Annabeth murmured.

"The Fleece was sacrificed to Poseidon in the legends," Grover added. "You're the intended recipient, if anything."

Percy touched it.

No backlash. No pain.

Just warmth.

Like saltwater sunlight.

The Fleece lifted slightly, threads coiling upward around his wrist—not binding, but welcoming. The symbols that burned Annabeth now shimmered harmlessly across his skin before fading entirely.

"I think it recognizes the sword," Percy said slowly. "Or what's inside it."

She looked at her hands. The welts had begun to fade, but her expression remained carved in stone.

"I wasn't prepared for my bloodline to be the enemy."

He grimaced, he hadn't expected the gods to be so unforgiving this time around. Something about this reality had changed slightly.

The Fleece pulsed once on his back, calm now. Polyphemus was gone. The island was quiet.

Annabeth turned away. "Let's go."

Commander Thaleia — Shoreline Encampment, [[Lethe]] Sector, Depth 74-B, Tartarus Frontline

Day 4,122 of Lethe Hold. No redlines tripped. No new engagements. The standoff endured.

The banks of the Lethe had not receded this cycle, nor had they surged. That was good. No news was always good news.

Stillness in Tartarus was an operational paradox—signaling either stability or absolute imminent collapse. [[Répertoire du Savoir Athénien]], commonly referred as AtRep, locus housed in Watchpoint [[Hades]], Depth 3, offered no definitive ruling. Its silence was, as ever, legally binding.

Commander Thaleia sat within the observation bunker of Hermesean Hold 17, one of several fortified brain-centers embedded deep into the strata overlooking the Lethe River. The name was ceremonial, like most Imperial assets in Tartarus. AtRep simply tagged it: Fortified Command Node [Sector-74B-17].

She reviewed the feed. Updates flickered along the bottom pane of her visor.

*CODE 5 – Signum Hostilis Visus – hostile or unknown presence observed.*

*CODE 15 – Sub Impetu Hostilis – under active assault by hostile forces.*

*CODE 18 – Speculum Falsum – target misidentified, false positive confirmed.*

*CODE 23 – Ad Scenam Pergo – attending incident location, scene deployment.*

*CODE 31 – Judicium Peto – requesting judgment/verdict from Enforcement.*

*CODE 31A – Judicium Novum (Minoris) – requesting update, sustaining minor operational damage.*

*CODE 31C – Judicium Novum (Maioris) – major damage sustained, command authority requesting urgent new judgment.*

*CODE 44 – Veritas Hostilis – requesting comprehensive target intel.*

*CODE 78 – Socius Voca – requesting support.*

*CODE 78E – Socius Voca: Periculum Maxima – backup requested, threat level E (severe/mass threat).*

The latest entry blinked amber:

*CODE 31C: Judicium Novum (Maioris)*

*Submitted from: Fulmen Peripheral Watch, Depth 75-C*

*Verdict: Observation. Deploy mental stabilizers. Hold position.*

"Fulmen Watch is failing," said Ilon, still in cognition gear. "They've issued three 31Cs in two shifts. AtRep keeps denying full-scale intervention."

"Because it's not a collapse yet," Thaleia replied, flatly.

"Because we don't have the reserves, more like." Ilon muttered.

Thaleia said nothing. She keyed into the field overlay. Fulmen's outer trenches glowed in a soft red hue, status: Declining Stability Index (DSI). If they fell, Tartarus might push higher, but more likely, they'd simply consume the local geography into their silent campaign of entropy.

She issued a revised deployment for her own line.

*Troop Cycle Rota: Sector 74B*

*Half-strength rotation. Issue amber stimulants (sub-psychoactive). No full recalls. Enforce mental cycles every 3 hours. Observation stance. Ave Imperator.*

Outside, the Lethe lapped gently against its banks. Ten thousand years ago, it had claimed an entire battalion by whispering their names in their mother's voices. Now the Firstborn demigods, those still functional, no longer reacted. Their minds were sealed, filtered, and cleaned of anything not sanctioned by Athena.

Ilon returned, slate in hand. "Four more requests for 31A verdicts. Troopers in Sections E-9 and D-2 report cognitive dissonance after seeing 'faces' emerge from the river fog."

"Standard hallucinations," Thaleia said. "Deny all 31A requests. Override with 23—attend the zone. Ensure physical confirmation before escalating. Log a potential Code 18."

"They'll resent it."

"They're still thinking, which means they're still usable. [[Olympus]] knows why we need to farm this godforsaken river. The amount of hostiles in these few centuries have been fucking ludicrous."

Ilon left. The overlay dimmed slightly. The trench held. The war paused, as it often did. It was a trick of the Enemy, to wear down the rhythm of war until peace felt like a thought.

Lethe's fog thickened again, crawling closer to the trench perimeter.

Thaleia leaned back. Athena would not render another true judgment today. She never did, not until loss was already certain.

---

*Quarterdeck 19* was permanent infrastructure. Modular? Yes. Replaceable? Catastrophically no. Too much institutional memory locked into its subsystems. The interior was color-coded by failure probability—red for unstable storage, orange for toxic containment, blue for personnel restationing corridors. There was no green. Dionysian aerospace was rarely given too much maintenance, but "*Nineteen"*, as most of the supervisors called it, sat at a very unfortunate Relay Gate between Olympian Airspace and a distribution nexus in the upper areas of Tartarus. And as such, were swamped in much more logistical demand than their five thousand, nine hundred and ninety-nine other counterparts.

The *Répertoire* spat out a triage dump the moment Cael's neuro-signature registered:

*Sector 72-A, Depth 4 – Ammunition allocation conflict. Requesting distribution clarity. Verdict: Redirect all 7.5mm slugs to Depths 88B–99C. Lethe sectors to default to plasma incineration packages. Prioritize density over kinetic accuracy.*

*Sector 71-G – Encounter with biomorph-class, unknown, exhibiting reverse-temporal phenomena. Hellhound Alpha suspected.*

*Verdict: Inconclusive. Passive surveillance protocol. No engagement authorization.*

*Sectors 17A, 23F, 31E – Immunosuppressant depletion, failure of lymph-cascade integrity.*

*Verdict: Dispatch Cradle-Class ration frame (Type C). Single-issue. Mark sectors as Partially Critical Supply Status.*

Cael approached his terminal. A Chair of Command, model *[[Aureus XII]]*, molded to spine and pulse pressure. Data flooded the floor-to-ceiling holoarray. Manifest summaries updated mid-scroll:

*Plasma-Kernels: 912,411 units (1.3% over expected stock)*

*Full-Psych Sealant: 13,889 doses remaining (reclassification to Restricted Critical Asset pending)*

*Anti-Entropy Protein Isolates: 0.27 standard tonnes (shortfall projected in 4.2 segments)*

*Cognitive Shunts (Class III): formally expired. Substituted with Wetware Salvage, Type B, battlefield-extracted*

*Eligible Firstborn: 42,000 in wait queue*

*Firstborn disqualified (mutation, memory fracture, loyalty lag): 33,780*

*Approved for Mind-Shard Ascension: 2,118*

He waved them all aside.

Whatever tasks worth doing were usually delegated to his more eager underlings.

He sent the summary package to Olympus, Sub-Node Delphi. Estimated response didn't look too good. 19.3 cycles. Marked Non-Urgent. No override available.

Athena's shard embedded within *Quarterdeck 19* was not conversational. It had been heuristically stripped to support mass-scale triage and output validation. No ethics suite remained, only ledger balance and operational survivability calculus.

A ping. Another supply escalation:

*Origin: Sector 91C – Requesting neuromancer inhibitors. Mild psycho-discordance, early-stage thought-fracturing.*

*Stock Check: 1,900 doses.*

*Request Total: 12,000 units.*

Cael issued:

*Verdict: Denied. Dispatch 300 units.*

*Supplemental Action: Isolate cohort in staging area 4C. If dissonance exceeds 17%, authorize cognitive excision.*

A staff servitor confirmed the decision and fed it into the Deckmaster Chorus' ledger. The order's status lit amber: Monitored Degradation.

Above, the *Hammer of [[Mnemosyne]]* disengaged from Dock Tract 2, auto-piloted under *Répertoire* directive. It carried 21,000 trauma-seal kits, 400,000 hemo-patches, and 2.5 million grams of nutrient material—none of which could be tasted by the target recipients. The Firstborn demigods on the Hell-lands were long since decoupled from gustatory functions.

Another runner approached, hermesean-blood, conscripted logistical caste, unarmed, ident-tagged as *Logos Petronius-11.*

"Magister," he said, presenting a scroll. "Time-discrepant reports from *Fulmen Caelestis* Drop-Lines. Athena's archive logs resupply as 8.3 cycles past. Local unit manifest cites 6.2."

Cael didn't look up. "Verdict?"

"Pending. Sub-Node Delphi not engaged."

"Do nothing. If their frontline suffers catastrophic delay, they'll escalate to 78E."

The runner nodded. No motion wasted. No argument. Left.

Cael turned to a new request sheet. *The Asphodel Colony, orbital vector 9.7,* had approved a new batch of artificial wombs. *900 births authorized.*

*Intended lifespan: ~13.5 years. Projected tactical utility span: 3.4 years post-deployment.*

He verified neural-template alignment: *all units coded for verdict recognition. No creative module. No language upload beyond command Latinic.*

He would have felt bad, if only this was not the first time he had to authorize this sort of act.

He signed the issuance order.

*Authorization Seal: Archmagister Cael Varros*

*Supplemental Enforcement Tag: Continuity Maintained*

The screen pulsed once. Finalized. Logged to the Judgment Core.

Quarterdeck 19 continued its churn. No alarms. No overt system failures. But everything rode the edge of insufficiency. Water reclamation down 3%. Atmospheric seal integrity showing microfractures. Crew sleep quotas suspended again. Morale index: statistically negligible.

And still the requests flowed.

And still the verdicts answered.

And still the numbers climbed, not toward end, not toward outcome, only toward extension.

Logistics didn't win wars. It prevented collapse. Cael Varros was on his 3,341st shift in the orbital quarterdeck above Nysa IX. At 812 years old, he'd long stopped counting his total service years. The war hadn't changed. Only the ledgers had.

Across the chamber, junior magisters barked at runners, inputting new figures from the latest warfront reports. One posted a live figure: Projected mortality due to supply delays across Tartarus sectors over next five cycles: 128,442.

"Flag it," Cael said, knowing his digital adjutant would always listen. "Send the projection to Regional Apollonian Command."

A data strip printed from his right forearm. He tore it off and scanned. Request from the High Temple: Would the Archmagister care to approve the next Bloodline Draft requisition? *Selection parameters: firstborn, psychically stable, minimum myth-quotient index 0.9.*

He rubbed his eyes, checked the orbital holding logs. Too many were aging out of service before dying. Tartarus' recent awakening was costing them attrition through themselves, not battle.

He signed the requisition.

Behind him, a broken monitor displayed a static-laced line from centuries ago: *Final Victory Imminent.* No one had the clearance to delete it.

He turned back to the spreadsheet. A new request had just come in — fresh supplies of copper-grade ink-resistant fibers for the 400th reinforcement wave, still held in the High Mars orbitals. That meant another three days of backlogged void-freight authorizations.

Cael cracked his neck and resumed. The war wouldn't end. But his line of the ledger had to balance before the next shift.
