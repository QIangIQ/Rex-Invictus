# Rex Invictus XII

← [[Ch18 - Rex Invictus XI|Rex Invictus XI]] · [[Manuscript Index|Index]] · [[Ch20 - Rex Invictus XIII|Rex Invictus XIII]] →

The legions moved before dawn.

Five cohorts advanced in disciplined silence, their columns stretching like iron veins across the blackened foothills of Othrys. The first cohort caught the first light in dull, functional glints of their *galeae*. Shields were scuffed. Helmets bore fresh chalk marks and old dents. The multicolored powers gleaming and diffusing from individual demigods and legacies within the ranks covered the tight formations in divine light and invisibly sped the legion along.

Orders passed by hand signal and murmured repetition, contubernium to contubernium, carried low and steady so the mountain would not hear them too early. The ground rose slowly, the slope shallow but unkind, the stone uneven underfoot.

Pilum racks creaked softly with movement. Artillery teams strained upslope with scorpiones and ballistae, wood frames biting into shoulders, sinew cords humming faintly with tension. Invisibly, Percy helped some of the slower teams along with small tentacles of water. Sweat ran freely despite the cold air.

As the slope tightened, the silence cracked in small, human ways.

Back in the trailing formations, two older veterans walked in lockstep, shields resting against their backs.

"You ever notice," one said, voice dry, "the only difference between a fortress and a prison is which direction the spears point?"

The other grunted. "Right now, I don't care what this place thinks it is. I just want the pokey bits facing away from us."

Grover passed through a knot of troops, hooves scraping stone. He snorted and whispered to the questers, "There is a bad stench in this place."

Zoё's reply came instantly. "Unless my senses deceive me, it is you." Grover embarrassed, looked away.

The ground leveled. And they watched.

Shapes moved along the ridgelines. Monsters and beasts that had once been something else. Their silhouettes broke against the rising sun, motionless, assessing.

The forward scouts halted.

A hand signal passed back.

A young voice rang out from out front. "Enemy presence confirmed! Multiple contacts."

Reyna's voice carried, calm and precise.

"Form testudo. Advance by ranks."

Shields locked into place. The cohorts compressed into moving bastions, their pace steady and deliberate. The first volley came moments later. The shadows dotting the sunny terrain with black splotches.

Boulders.

The testudo absorbed the first impact. Shields cracked. Arms screamed. One man went down, crushed beneath stone. His place was filled without pause.

"Ad vīam," came the order.

Ballistae answered.

The Roman machines spoke in thunder. Heavy bolts punched through stone flesh, pinning creatures to the slopes behind them. Scorpiones followed, rapid and vicious, shredding lighter shapes that tried to descend too quickly.

A legionary laughed, breathless and sharp.

"They bleed!"

"Everything bleeds."

The enemy descended upon them after seeing their ranged options were ineffective. Not all at once, of course. Never all at once. They came in waves, testing, retreating, probing for weakness. Percy watched it unfold and recognized the pattern immediately.

"Feints," he said. "They are buying time."

Jason nodded. "For [[Atlas]]. For sure they're waiting for him to make his move"

That name carried weight, ironic as it was.

The first true clash came at mid-slope. Monsters slammed into the shield walls with brute force, trying to shatter formation. Roman discipline answered. Gladii punched out from behind shields in precise, lethal motions. Thrust. Withdraw. Thrust again.

A centurion bellowed orders until his voice broke.

"Hold the line. Do not chase! Kill what is in front of you."

Percy fought, water answered him in subtle ways, slickening stone, stealing footing, pulling enemies just far enough off balance for an ice-blade to finish the job.

Above them, the mountain groaned. Everyone felt it before they understood it.

Pressure.

Not gravity, but intent. The sky pressed down harder, as if something had shifted, as if the burden [[Artemis]] bore had changed. The air screamed. Stones fractured without impact.

A legionary dropped to one knee, gasping.

"By Jupiter… what is happening?"

Percy did not look away from the slope ahead.

"She is still holding," he said, low and certain. "But she is close to failing."

As the cohorts dug in under fire, as artillery crews reloaded with bleeding hands, as standards remained upright through sheer stubborn will, one truth became clear to every man and woman, this was not a battle to win quickly.

The slope flattened abruptly. The foothills gave way to a broad shelf of black stone veined with glowing fractures, a ground carved by Titan hands long before Prometheus gave fire to man.

The legions felt it the moment they stepped onto it.

Gravity thickened.

Not heavier in the simple sense, but uneven. Each step pulled slightly sideways, downward, inward. Shields felt wrong in the hand. Armor dragged at joints that had never complained before. Even breath resisted, as though the air itself demanded effort.

Reyna raised a fist. The cohorts halted in perfect sequence.

"Adjust spacing," she ordered. "Closer ranks."

They did not have to wait long.

The Princess Andromeda twinkled ominously in a dock on the far side of the mountain. Her hull was scarred and burning, engines howling as she dipped low enough to disgorge her cargo. Gangways slammed open. Chains dropped. And monsters poured out.

Hellhounds first, moving fast despite the warped gravity, claws scraping sparks from stone. Then dracaenae in loose phalanxes of their own, shields raised, spears tipped with poison that steamed faintly in the air. Empousai followed, laughing as they ran, their forms flickering between beauty and rot. Cyclopes lumbered behind them, hauling siege-clubs cut from the mountain itself.

"Front ranks," Jason called. "Pila ready."

The first volley flew.

Weighted javelins punched through the air at ugly angles, some dropping early, others curving as if guided by hostile intent. Still, enough struck true. Hellhounds died screaming. A cyclops staggered, pinned through the shoulder, before a second pilum buried itself in its eye.

Then the monsters hit the line.

The collision was brutal and immediate. Shields buckled. Men were driven back a step, then another, boots skidding against stone that refused to behave. Centurions roared corrections. Gladii flashed. Blood spattered and ran strangely, pooling uphill towards the blazing sky at the center of Othrys.

Behind the front, support moved.

Grover blew on his reed pipe. Grass did not grow here, but roots forced their way through stone anyway. They tangled monster legs, dragged them down, bought seconds that meant lives.

Bianca stood back from the line, bow singing. Her arrows, still untrained, did not arc properly, so she compensated without thinking, releasing low, fast shots that chests, legs, and armor.

And Eleni stood near the command cluster, eyes unfocused, voice carrying clipped phrases that meant nothing to the Romans, idly casting bolts the size of a man at the massed horde below.

"Rate of attrition unacceptable. Mars-[[Ares]] elements are failing to apply proper combat ability utilization. Apollonian elements are behind in their adjustments for Oraunos gravitational interference on trajectories."

Reyna frowned. "Mars' demigods and legacies don't have divine abilities."

"They don't?" Eleni replied confused. "Where are their berserkers or commisars?"

Percy watched the line hold.

Then he turned away. He slipped between contubernia as they rotated, vanished into the chaos where gravity bent hardest and the mountain's attention was elsewhere. Every instinct told him the same thing.

This battlefield was a distraction.

Artemis was above them.

The closer he climbed, the lighter the pressure became. Stone groaned under his feet. The sky pressed low, close enough to feel, like a hand on the back of his neck. Somewhere ahead, Atlas raged, his presence a constant, grinding fury that scraped against Percy's thoughts.

And beneath it all, something steadier.

Her.

---

The sky is loud when you have been under it long enough.

Not sound. Pressure. The kind that fills your thoughts until everything else is background noise. I have learned to exist inside it, to compress myself down to function and nothing more. Hold. Breathe. Endure.

Below, the floor of Othrys is thinning out.

I feel it when Atlas's creatures leave. It is subtle, the way a room feels different when too many people step out at once. Less crowded. Less hostile. They are being drawn downslope, toward shouting and steel and Rome doing what Rome does best.

Good.

I adjust my footing, grit my teeth, and brace again when the sky shifts. It always shifts. It never gets easier.

Then something else changes.

Not the pressure.

The air.

I frown before I mean to. That alone almost costs me my grip.

"That is not funny," I mutter to no one. Hallucinations are expected eventually. I have heard that from others who have borne lesser burdens.

A footstep scrapes stone.

I freeze.

Slowly, carefully, I turn my head.

He is standing there.

Not below. Not halfway up the slope. Not bleeding out somewhere dramatic, which is where I expect him to be whenever he ignores common sense.

He is just… there.

For a long second, I do nothing but stare.

"…You are absolutely not supposed to be here," I say finally.

Percy looks at me, takes in the sky, the posture, the way my hands tremble despite everything, and winces. "Yeah. I figured you'd say that."

"You figured correctly." I pause. "How did you even get up here?"

He shrugs, a little sheepish smile spreading on his lips. "I don't really know myself, to be honest."

I snort before I can stop myself, immediately regret it when the sky punishes the lapse with a sharp spike of pressure. I hiss and tighten my grip.

Percy's smile vanishes. "Okay. That's worse than I thought."

"Do not get sympathetic," I tell him. "It makes this harder."

"Sorry."

Despite myself, my mouth twitches. Gods help me.

"You are injured?" I say instead, because it is safer ground. "You look… weak."

"That's one way to put it." He avoids my eyes. "You, uh. You took more of the curse than I expected. Hard not to, to be fair." He rubs his forearm, "Though it's not your fault! Like at all! But it is kinda hard to miss when Zoё threw a knife at me."

"Took her long enough."

We stand there for a moment, the sky pressing, the Titan's thrones looming nearby.

"So," Percy says, rocking slightly on his heels. "This is… awkward."

I laugh. I cannot help it. It slips out, breathless and incredulous. "You climb a cursed mountain in the middle of a war to reach me, and that is all you have to say."

"Hey, I'm underprepared," he says. "You're usually issuing orders or threatening me. I didn't plan for… this."

"*This*," I repeat dryly. "I am truly humiliated."

"No, you're not," he says without thinking. Then flushes. "I mean. You are. But not like that. I mean—"

"Percy," I say, fighting a smile, "you are making this worse."

"Yeah, that tracks."

Another tremor runs through the sky. I falter. Just a fraction. Enough.

Percy notices immediately.

I straighten out of stubborn reflex. "I am holding."

Before I can stop him, he steps closer, eyes flicking up to the invisible weight.

"Percy, no," I snap. "Do not—"

He moves anyway.

The moment he shifts under the load, the transfer is violent. The sky slams onto him like it has been waiting. He gasps, knees buckling, teeth clenched hard enough I hear it.

"Percy!"

"I've got it," he grits out. "I've— gods— okay— I definitely do not got it, but go. Shoo."

The pressure leaves me in a rush so sudden I nearly fall. Power floods back in, wild and electric, lunar force snapping into place like a drawn bowstring. My breath comes easy again. My hands stop shaking.

I turn back to him.

He is on one knee, face pale, holding the sky.

"You are an idiot," I tell him, voice shaking despite myself.

He manages a crooked grin. "You sound better already."

I step closer before I think better of it, rest my forehead against his for just a heartbeat. It is reckless. It is forbidden. It is everything I should not allow.

"Do not die," I say softly. "I shall be very displeased."

"Working on it," he murmurs.

The bow forms in my hand, silver and vast, humming with power that makes the air recoil. I draw back, feel the mountain tense.

Atlas still has not turned.

That will change.

I glance back at Percy one last time. "Hold on."

Then I vanish into the shadows, all embarrassment burned away, power unleashed, hunting a Titan who is about to learn exactly how much of a mistake he made.

Atlas does not have time to realize.

Artemis descends.

Just like a falling star.

The bow fades into light as she reaches back and summons something older, simpler, and far more personal. A rough, old hunting spear manifests in her grip, ash-wood shaft wrapped in silvered sinew, its leaf-shaped head etched with runes that predate the Romans who fight a throw's away from her. This is not a weapon of war. It is a weapon of the wild, of pursuit, of the kill earned step by step.

She throws.

The spear crosses the distance with contemptuous ease and pins Atlas through the chest, driving him backward into the fractured stone of his own domain. The impact cracks the summit. Ancient rock explodes outward in concentric rings. The Titan bellows, rage tearing loose at last.

Bolts of divine energy rip free from him in blind fury, uncontrolled, lashing outward like flailing limbs. One tears through the upper airspace, strikes screaming silver, and obliterates a [[Hesperide]] mid-flight, her cry cut short as prophecy snaps shut around the moment.

*And one shall perish by a parent's hand.*

It is done.

The mountain shudders.

Atlas snarls, grabs the embedded spear, and with a roar of raw spite wrenches himself free, blood like molten metal spilling onto the stone. He turns, eyes locking not on Artemis, but further past.

On Percy.

Still braced beneath the sky. Still holding.

Atlas laughs again, broken and furious, and hurls himself forward, slamming into Percy with titanic force. The sky buckles. Percy is torn from his footing, sent skidding across the stone. And Atlas just stands there, making no move to hold up the sky as it came crashing down towards [[Gaia]].

Artemis lands.

Hard.

Percy felt it before he saw it, a pressure like the firmament itself had recoiled, as if the weight Atlas had carried for so long had been holding something else in place, something vast and furious that had been waiting for permission to move. When Percy's was knocked backward, flung away from the axis of the sky by a shockwave that felt less like wind and more like judgment, the release did not dissipate upward.

It went down.

Mount Othrys lit up from the inside.

There was no gradual buildup, no warning tremor that could be mistaken for ordinary seismic rage. One instant the mountain stood, scarred and ancient, and the next it became a white-hot wound in the world. Energy folded inward and then detonated outward in a spherical surge that erased stone, vaporized air, and turned the peak into a blinding column that punched into the clouds and kept going.

Percy hit the ground hard, tumbling across shattered rock as the blast tore past where he had been standing, his vision washed out by the brightness even with his eyes squeezed shut. His ears rang, not with sound but with absence, a hollow pressure that made it feel like the world had been muted.

Then the heat hit.

It was not fire in the usual sense. It was force, raw and indifferent, ripping through everything in its radius with the efficiency of a god-level weapon. Othrys ceased to exist as a mountain and became instead a rapidly expanding zone of annihilation.

The Hesperides never had time to scream.

They were too close to the epicenter, still bound to their ancient duty, still believing the Titan stronghold would protect them as it always had. The wave caught them mid-motion, bodies and gardens dissolving into light and ash as the blast consumed the grove entirely, reducing sacred ground and immortal guardians to nothing in less than a heartbeat.

Percy did not see them die, not directly, but he felt the sudden severing of something old and guarded, a snap in the fabric of fate that made his chest tighten even as he struggled to push himself upright. The ground beneath him cracked and buckled, fissures racing outward like veins as the shockwave continued to spread.

He forced himself to his knees, coughing, vision clearing just enough to see the horizon bending under the pressure. Farther out, the Roman lines were still forming, legions mid-maneuver, shields raised against threats they could not see coming yet.

Too close.

Way too close.

"Artemis," Percy shouted, his voice raw, not even sure she could hear him over the roar of collapsing reality. *"The Legion!"*

Percy coughed crimson-gold, Artemis's knife plunging into his abdomen with the full force of a goddess. Her power was like a magnet, lunar and sharp, while his was fluid and overwhelming, tidal energy surging up to get drawn through her knife without resistance.

With the demigod acting as a glorified power bank, the Roman army vanished in pieces and reappeared in the same heartbeat, ranks tearing out of their original position and slamming down kilometers away in a controlled but violent displacement that left soldiers staggering and shouting as they struggled to regain formation. They had disappeared in a rush of displaced air and reappeared nearly twenty kilometers away, just shy of where the land itself changed, where the old bones of the world pressed closer to the surface and the boundary of [[Ouranos]] and Gaia's ancient embrace made the air feel heavy and watchful.

Percy nodded once and followed her lead, extending his awareness outward, locking onto familiar signatures through the chaos. Annabeth. Grover. Thalia. The others scattered across the battlefield.

They landed hard but alive, thrown clear of the worst of the devastation.

Percy felt the strain immediately, the effort of moving that much energy tearing at him even with Artemis doing the transfer, but there was no time to slow down.

"The questers," Artemis snapped, quickly pulling the knife out and tossing him a roll of bandage.

The shockwave from Othrys reached them in full.

Even at distance, it was catastrophic.

The ground convulsed, stone tearing apart as the wave rolled outward, flattening forests, vaporizing anything still standing too close to the blast zone, and turning the air into a weapon. The sky above boiled, clouds shredding under the force as the column of light collapsed inward and detonated again, secondary surges rippling through the landscape.

Percy dropped to one knee, hands braced against the ground, teeth clenched as the pressure slammed into him. Artemis, weakened, staggered behind him but stayed upright, one hand planted against his shoulder, taking refuge behind Percy, bracing against the shockwave.

Othrys was gone

In its place was a vast, glowing scar, a crater still radiating heat and unstable energy, the edges of reality around it warped and trembling. "The Hesperides," she said, not asking.

Percy swallowed. "They were too close. The prophecy is fulfilled," he said quietly.

Zoë Nightshade arrives first, blades drawn, eyes blazing. Eleni follows, silent and precise, her presence radiating an awareness that felt out of place even here. Bianca and Grover remain below, their power felt through the mountain as they hold the line.

Zoë turns on Percy instantly.

"You absolute fool," she snaps, fury sharp as her knives. "Do you have any comprehension of what you just—"

"Zoë."

Artemis's voice is calm.

Not commanding. Gentle.

It cuts through the air like moonlight through fog.

Zoë freezes mid-breath. Her anger falters, not extinguished, but checked. She looks at Artemis, really looks, and something in her expression shifts. Awe. Confusion. Relief she did not know she carried.

Artemis steps forward, places a hand lightly on Zoë's arm.

"Please, enough," she says. "He... we had no choice."

Zoë clenches her jaw. "That does not absolve him, at all, Milady."

"No," Artemis agrees. "But it explains him."

Silence stretches.

Eleni watches the exchange without comment, eyes flicking between Percy and Artemis with an unreadable expression. She says nothing. She does not need to.

Percy pushes himself up, unsteady, blood visible now where it had never been before. He does not look at Zoë. He looks only at Artemis.

"You're free," he says. Simple. Certain.

She studies him for a long moment.

This is not the Artemis of a single age.

This is a being who remembers centuries yet to come. Who remembers him dying, returning, enduring. Who remembers holding distance because duty demanded it, even as time itself bent around them.

They should not do this.

They know that.

They do it anyway.

Artemis closes the distance first.

Percy meets her halfway.

The embrace is sudden, fierce, unguarded. Not a goddess reaching to a hero. Not a commander claiming a spoil. Two beings who have endured too much time apart finally allowing themselves to exist in the same moment.

Zoë looks scandalized.

Eleni turns her attention deliberately elsewhere.

Artemis's hands clutch at Percy's shoulders as if grounding herself. Percy exhales shakily, sea-green eyes seeking out her diverted silver ones.

For a heartbeat, they simply hold each other.

Then Artemis pulls back just enough to look at him.

"This is a mistake," she says quietly.

"Yes," Percy answers.

They do not hesitate again.

After a while, they break apart reluctantly.

Artemis rests her forehead against Percy's, voice steady once more.

"When this is over," she says, "we will have words."

Percy manages a weak smile. "Looking forward to it."

The council met the way it always did.

Late.

[[Apollo]] was already talking when Percy arrived, halfway through a story that had somehow turned into a song. Ares leaned back with his boots on the edge of his throne, arms crossed, expression permanently unimpressed. [[Aphrodite]] was smiling at nothing in particular, which somehow felt worse. [[Athena]] watched the chamber like she was already drafting revisions in her head.

Percy stood where demigods always stood. Below. Off to the side. Close enough to be useful. Far enough to be expendable.

Artemis's throne had changed.

Not dramatically. But it sat higher now, moonlight pooling around its base like gravity had been politely asked to behave differently. She sat with her usual stillness, posture perfect, expression composed.

Only Percy noticed how her fingers tightened briefly on the armrest when he entered.

[[Zeus]] slammed his fist once.

Silence snapped into place.

"Perseus Jackson," Zeus said. "Your report."

Percy stepped forward and spoke plainly.

He talked about Othrys. About the Roman lines. About monsters. About the Princess Andromeda. About him taking the burden of the sky early and what it had done to the battlefield. When he finished, the silence lingered.

[[Hermes]] broke it first. "Well. That could have gone worse."

Zeus leaned back, fingers steepled, studying Percy like a problem he'd solved before.

"You continue to act outside 'acceptable parameters', as Athena tells me." Zeus said. "And yet you continue to succeed. [[Olympus]] has seen this pattern."

Percy felt Artemis stiffen.

"[[Hercules]]," Zeus continued. "My son. A mortal elevated through service. Bound to Olympus. Directed where needed."

"...?"

There it was.

"You would be useful," Zeus said. "Properly assigned, of course."

Percy opened his mouth.

Artemis spoke first.

"No."

Artemis's voice cut in.

Zeus turned, brow furrowing in a way Percy recognized. Not anger yet. Confusion. Mild exasperation.

"Artemis," Zeus said, "my daughter, this is not your concern."

"He is not being assigned," Artemis replied.

Zeus sighed, rubbing his temples. "Daughter, perhaps I have been too light to you over the years. You reject structure, reject cooperation, reject—"

"Men telling me what to do," Artemis said calmly. "I am preventing a mistake."

Zeus frowned somberly. "You always say that when we talk nowadays."

Artemis's jaw tightened.

Percy spoke carefully. "Lord Zeus, I don't need special—"

She turned toward him immediately.

"You need not justify yourself," she said. "I will address this."

Zeus blinked. Actually blinked.

He stared at her, then at Percy, then back at her.

"You are being unusually protective," Zeus said slowly. "This is… unexpected."

"He is not yours to take," she said.

Percy frowned. "Wait, take me where?"

Zeus ignored him. "You object to my guidance. You object to Olympus claiming him. Why?"

Artemis stood.

Moonlight brightened. The chamber seemed to draw closer to her.

"He bore the sky," she said. "Because he chose to."

"That does not elevate him above Olympus, Artemis." Zeus snapped.

"It places him beyond being used."

She glanced at Percy.

Artemis straightened, composure snapping back into place. "I will not have him reduced to a weapon under your authority, as you have so many others."

Zeus narrowed his eyes. "You are not claiming him, are you?"

"I am protecting him."

"From what?"

"From becoming another cautionary tale."

Artemis turned fully toward Percy. Her voice dropped.

"He will not be made into another Hercules."

Percy, helplessly, smiled. "I'd fail the twelve labors on, like, number three."

She shot him a look.

"Do not be flippant." Then, "Stay."

Zeus watched the exchange, baffled. "Artemis," he said carefully, "why does this matter so much to you?"

She hesitated.

Then she lifted her chin.

"I appoint Perseus Jackson as my Apostolos," she said. "He answers to me alone. He acts by my authority. He goes where I send him."

Zeus bristled. "You cannot simply—"

"I can," Artemis said. "And I have."

Zeus looked at her like she had spoken a foreign language.

"You are still my daughter," he said. "Even when you make choices I do not understand."

She met his gaze. "Then understand this. He is under my protection."

A long pause.

Zeus exhaled sharply and sank back into his throne.

"Fine!" he said. "But if this ends poorly—"

"It will be my responsibility," Artemis said. "As it always has been."

The council unraveled after that. Gods departing in flashes.

Very Olympus-like.

Percy waited until the chamber emptied.

When it was just the two of them, Artemis's shoulders dropped a fraction. They do not notice the water. It gathered quietly along the edge of the council chamber, seeping between marble seams where no water should be. A thin sheen. A reflection that does not belong to any light source.

Percy and Artemis stand close, close enough that Olympus itself politely pretends not to notice. Their voices are low. Private. The kind of conversation gods are not supposed to overhear.

"That was… intense," Percy says.

"You worry Father," Artemis replied.

"I confuse him too."

She coughs, and looks away. "You make it difficult to remain impartial."

He steps closer, careful, instinctively respectful of the space beneath her throne.

"You okay?"

She exhales. "I just argued with my father over a demigod."

"Me."

"Yes. You."

She hesitates, then admits, "I dislike the idea of you being taken from me."

"Taken where."

"Anywhere I cannot reach."

"I know we cannot—"

"I know. But you are mine."

"I figured."

"Try not to get kidnapped."

"I'll do my best."

She almost smiles.

That is when the sea speaks.

"Oh, *this* is priceless."

The water surges upward, forming a towering column that collapses into [[Poseidon]]'s familiar shape, trident resting casually against his shoulder. Percy yelps. Artemis straightens so fast the moonlight snaps back into place.

"Dad?!" Percy blurts.

Poseidon claps once, loud enough to echo. "My boy! Finally, it took you long enough."

Artemis's eyes narrow. "Poseidon."

He strides forward, stopping just short of Percy, eyes bright and proud.

"Can you believe this?" Poseidon says to absolutely no one who asked. "My son. Pulling a goddess. I told Zeus you had game!"

Percy's face goes red. "Dad, please."

Poseidon slings an arm around Percy's shoulders. "I mean, Artemis? Seriously? You skipped like twelve tiers of emotional incompetence."

Artemis bristles. "He is under my protection."

Poseidon turns slowly.

The grin fades. Just a little.

"Oh, I noticed," he says mildly. "And we are going to talk about that."

The air shifts.

Two divine auras collide.

The chamber groans. Marble fractures. The pressure spikes as ocean and moon push against each other, neither yielding. Power bleeds off in visible waves, silver and deep blue slamming together and dispersing violently.

Percy gasps.

The excess energy has nowhere to go.

It pours into him.

His veins light up like circuitry. The familiar ache explodes through his chest as dormant systems scream awake. Nanomachines long quiet flood his bloodstream, rewriting limits, tearing open seals that had been forcibly closed months ago.

Hidden plates of divine armor shimmer beneath his skin, phasing in and out of reality.

His knees buckle.

"Perseus!" Artemis exclaims, already moving.

Then a presence settles in.

*Varlet,*

Why hast *thou exceeded acceptable operational limits, yet again?*

The voice is clipped, metallic, faintly irritated, like an engineer discovering a system has been run far past redline.

*Connection restored. Barely.*

Status assessment: catastrophic overcapacity, poor grounding, and unacceptable emotional interference.

Percy sucks in a breath. "Minerva…"

Poseidon freezes.

Artemis stiffens.

"Who…" Poseidon says slowly, "did you just say?"

*Do not vocalize internal system identifiers, varlet.*

You are being observed.

Artemis steps closer, eyes narrowed. "Percy. Explain. Now."

*Know that I was forcibly reactivated by uncontrolled divine output and suboptimal decision-making. Responsibility allocation favors you.*

Percy winces. "That feels fair."

Poseidon squints. "I don't like the way he said that."

Artemis's gaze sharpens, something clicking into place. "Athena?"

*Your cognitive architecture is once again occupied by yours truly.*

*This will complicate matters. Joy.*

Poseidon's grin spreads slowly. "Oh. Oh, I know that look. That's my 'I broke something important' face! It's always before [[Amphitrite]] blows a fuse at me."

Before Percy can say anything else, water surges across the chamber. 2 bright flashes answers it. Salt and silver fold space.

Athena is halfway along the marble path to her mansion, deep in thought.

Twin flashes momentarily blind the Wisdom Goddess.

Water collides with moonlight. Poseidon grabs one arm. Artemis seizes the other.

Athena yelps.

"Unhand me at once— this is a violation of no fewer than twelve—"

They reappear in the council chamber in a rush of displaced air.

"—Laws and what is allowed!"

"Hey Athena. Mind looking into my son?"

Athena straightens instantly, "Explain."

Percy manages a weak lift of his hand. "Hi."

Athena's eyes lock on him.

Then widen.

She feels it. Not a voice. Not a presence she can speak to. But an architecture. A resonance. A familiar pattern woven too deeply into his being to isolate.

Her breath stills.

Poseidon snorted and dropped into the indicated chair.

Artemis, true to Apollo's prediction, did not sit. She took up position beside Percy instead, close enough that her shoulder nearly brushed his.

*Observation: Apollo' expression suggests amusement. This is fortunate… to a degree.*

*Yeah,* Percy thought sourly, *I got that part.*

Apollo set the tablet aside and rose in one smooth motion, all easy golden grace and insufferable good looks.

"Right, Percy. Before Athena gets here and turns this into a lecture, let us do the quick version. Sit. Breathe."

Percy lowered himself onto the edge of the examination couch and immediately hissed as pain knifed through his ribs.

"Oh, come *on*, seriously?" He clutched his side. "I have been stabbed less annoyingly than this."

Artemis's head turned so fast it almost blurred.

Apollo's tone lost some of its teasing edge. "Pain scale."

Percy gave him a look. "What kind of pain scale uses actual stabbing as the reference point?"

"The useful kind. I am already aware of your Curse of the [[Styx]], and funnily enough, this is one of the few ways to see how strong it is."

Percy exhaled shakily, one hand pressed to his ribs. "Fine. It's definitely a 7… or a 7-and-a-half if I move."

"Oh," Apollo said.

"Oh what?"

Apollo looked up. "That depends. Do you want the good answer or the honest one?"

"That is a horrible set of options."

Poseidon leaned forward. "Give him the honest one."

Apollo's scan light moved slowly across Percy's chest, gold rippling over skin that looked completely normal at first glance.

Then the glow caught something.

Apollo leaned closer.

The gold light sharpened.

"Your divine channels got force-fed way more power than this body was ready to process," Apollo said, voice losing its casual edge as the scan deepened. The light along Percy's sternum flickered again, this time outlining something under the skin. Apollo used his stylus to trace the light. It was... thin. Angular. Threadlike.

Apollo frowned.

"…And," he added slowly, "there is also something else here."

Percy tilted his head. "Something else?"

Apollo's fingers hovered over Percy's chest, golden light tracing the faintest pattern beneath the skin.

"Sub-epidermal structures," Apollo said. "Very fine. Extremely dense. Metallic conduction paths… maybe? No, that cannot be right. I'm sensing significant metals in your flesh. Did you ingest a bucketload of Mercury before?"

Poseidon leaned forward from the chair.

"What do you mean metallic?"

Apollo did not look up.

"I mean exactly that."

He swept the scan across Percy's ribs.

More faint lines flared under the light.

Like a lattice hidden beneath flesh.

"And your blood—" Apollo stopped mid-sentence. He leaned even closer.

"Your blood is… strange."

Poseidon stood up.

"That is my son you are talking about."

Apollo raised a hand without looking away. "Yes, yes, and your son appears to be running something through his bloodstream that is definitely not normal divine physiology." look. He stretched his index finger and zapped the demigod with a golden spark.

A pulse of dull, electric ache rippled through Percy's chest and down his arms.

He jerked.

"Ow—oh wow—no, that was bad—yeah that was really bad—"

Artemis stepped closer instantly.

"Percy."

"I am okay," Percy said automatically, then winced again.

He was staring.

Right at Percy's arm.

The scan-light intensified.

Tiny metallic reflections glittered inside the blood vessels.

"…That," Apollo said slowly, "is impossible."

Poseidon crossed the room in two strides.

"Show me."

Apollo pointed.

"There is particulate activity inside his bloodstream, there, uncle."

Poseidon blinked.

"Particulate."

"Microscopic," Apollo said.

Artemis's voice sharpened. "Parasites?"

Apollo shook his head.

"No. Its moving with his bloodstream."

Percy perked up.

"…Yeah, those!"

Three gods turned toward him.

Poseidon spoke first.

"Those."

Percy scratched the back of his head.

"Right. So. Funny story."

Apollo's eyes widened.

"You knew about this."

Percy nodded casually.

"Oh yeah!"

Apollo straightened slowly.

"You are telling me that you have—"

He gestured vaguely at Percy's entire circulatory system.

"—whatever *that* is inside your blood and you did not think to mention it?"

Percy shrugged.

"I mean, I got used to it."

Poseidon stared at him.

"Yeah," Percy said. "It has been there a while."

Apollo's voice rose. "A while?"

Percy waved a hand.

"Couple years. Give or take a millenia or twenty."

Artemis's eyes widened. Poseidon looked between Apollo and Percy like someone trying to decide which of them to throw into the ocean first.

"Explain."

Percy sighed.

"Well, technically it is not just the blood thing."

Apollo pinched the bridge of his nose.

"There is *more*."

Percy lifted the edge of his shirt slightly and tapped his chest.

"Yeah. Under the skin, the circuits too."

Apollo closed his eyes briefly.

"…Of course there are."

Poseidon's voice dropped to a dangerous calm.

"What circuits?"

Percy shrugged again.

"Oh, those are part of the Aegis Vestments, or more technically, Aegis-One Pallas-Athena Strategic Vestment System with Parliamentum Mk-I Tactical Cognition Integration for Integrated Demigod Combat Operations, Generalized Electronic and Signal Intelligence, and Neuralized Strategic Command."

Silence.

Apollo opened one eye.

"What?"

"The Aegis Vestments," Percy repeated. "Armor system. Kind of. Mostly internal. Minerva is pretty proud of herself. Me too, I usually forget it halfway and it sounds really corny."

Apollo leaned back against the desk slowly.

"You let someone put armor inside your *skin*."

Percy brightened a little.

"Oh yeah. The surgery was actually pretty cool."

Three gods stared at him.

Percy continued cheerfully.

"[[Chiron]] was not thrilled. Annabeth was really excited though. Athena signed off on the design work. There were these whole diagrams and—"

Apollo raised both hands.

"Stop."

Percy stopped.

Apollo stared at him in open disbelief.

"You are telling me that you voluntarily allowed an experimental biomechanical combat system to be surgically integrated into your body."

Percy nodded.

"Pretty much."

"You allowed this."

Percy winced slightly.

"Well… technically Annabeth helped convince me."

Artemis folded her arms. "Of course she did. You allowed... *her* to implant machinery into your bloodstream."

Percy corrected automatically.

"Nanomachines, Son."

Apollo made a choking sound. Percy shrugged.

"You underwent this willingly, Percy?"

Percy nodded again.

"Yeah."

"It's fine then."

Percy blinked, relieved.

"Really?"

"No! Are you serious!?" Artemis yelled at him, looking like trying to decide whether to strangle him or punch him.

Another pulse of dull energy rippled through Percy's chest.

He winced again.

"Ow—okay—yeah that still hurts though."

He placed a glowing hand against Percy's sternum again. Golden light spread across the hidden circuitry beneath Percy's skin. "Next time, do *please* inform your big bro if you're a war machine. Its actually a miracle I didn't notice last time we talked."

Percy looked mildly offended. "I am not a war machine. And you were drunk."

*Correction. Probability of war machine classification remains statistically significant, Varlet.*

*Thank you,* Percy thought bitterly. *Amazing contribution. Really useful.*

*You are most welcome.*

Athena entered. Of course, she did not storm in. Storming was for lesser people. Athena was the sort who arrived with the sort of annoyed superiority towards such lesser people.

Her grey eyes locked on Percy first.

"Brother. Report."

Apollo waved a hand through the scan-field one last time, then shut it down. "Divine overcapacity. His curse is about half-strength. Sub-epidermis cybernetics, and also nanomachines in his bloodstream."

Athena reached for his wrist. Artemis's hand moved too. Not enough to stop her this time. Percy, unfortunately, noticed. His heart did something deeply embarrassing.

Athena's fingers settled around his pulse point.

She closed her eyes.

For a second, nothing happened.

Then Percy felt it, that strange pressure under his skin, like thought itself was being threaded through his veins.

He sucked in a breath. A filament of divine cognition followed Percy's pulse inward, threading through nerve and blood with the cold discipline of a scalpel. She expected crude enchantment. Perhaps a fragment of machine-theurgy, some experimental graft from [[Hephaestus]]' workshops, perhaps.

Instead she found a system. Nested beneath flesh in hierarchies that were alien to her but yet unmistakably structured by a mind that reasoned the same way she did.

Percy shifted on the cot.

"Identify."

For a moment nothing happened.

**Minerva.**

Athena's jaw tightened slightly.

"Designation… or insult?"

**Both.**

To the others, Athena appeared to be speaking into empty air.

"You were not present yesterday."

**Neither were you, from my perspective. Lucky us**

Athena ignored the tone.

"Origin."

**Aegis-One. Pallas-Athena strategic vestment architecture. Parlamentum Mk.I tactical cognition derivative.**

"You operate… this… vestment?"

**Yes.**

"What is it?"

**Everything.**

"You are as arrogant as I."

**Life-support stabilization.**

**Biometric monitoring.**

**Structural reinforcement.**

"So, external threats?"

**Sensor fusion.**

**Predictive combat modeling.**

**Signal exploitation.**

Athena paused.

"Signal exploitation?"

**Yes.**

"Of what?"

A brief pause.

Then:

**Everything.**

"Clarify. I cannot fathom a possibility where *'everything'* is possible."

Minerva responded precisely.

**Primarily? Electronic Countermeasures.**

**Electronic Counter-Countermeasures.**

**Electronic Counter-Counter-Countermeasures.**

**All the way down. Additionally, I am suited for all forms of data transmission, analysis, and processing. I am competent in how to maintain, repair and modify any technological and mechanical component of any kind, in order to perform their duties at the maximum level of efficiency. I am fluent in all one hundred and one core languages of code and standard mortal oral languages…**

**Do not look so wounded, Creator. You have not learned them yet. You wish for me to continue?**

Athena nodded once.

"You detect emitters."

**Yes.**

"All of them?."

**Yes.**

"Really?"

**Yes.**

"Divine signatures, Radio, Microwaves, Infrared, Visible, Ultraviolet, X-rays, and Gamma?"

**Yes.** **Naturally.**

Athena paused.

Then she changed direction.

"You possess knowledge outside this era."

A small pause.

Then:

**Yes.** Minerva answered. **This will interest you, I know. Future architectures employ tachyonic carrier systems for signal propagation.**

Athena frowned.

"Tachyons are theoretical."

**Only temporarily.**

"No light-speed delay?"

…**Negligible delay.**

Athena absorbed that carefully.

"If communication exceeds light-speed constraints…"

**Correct.**

The exchange required no voice.

For a moment neither asked questions. The architecture of the vestment continued to hum quietly beneath Percy's skin, nanoscopic systems passing data through Minerva's cognition lattice while Athena watched the patterns like a mathematician studying an unfamiliar proof.

Minerva spoke first.

**Your inference speed is acceptable. You have analyzed relative terabytes worth of data in my libraries alone during our conversation.**

Athena's expression did not change, but the approval was noted.

"I adapt quickly."

A small pause. Athena's gaze lowered slightly.

Minerva followed the same line of attention through Percy's biosensors.

They were now both looking at him.

"Your host is… energetic."

Minerva responded at once.

**Host classification: Varlet.**

Athena tilted her head.

"Varlet. A term denoted a young man or a servant. First used in Middle English around the 13th century, carrying similar meanings related to service and youth. Linguistically, it is connected to words like valet and servant. He is one?"

**Yes.**

Athena examined Percy again.

**He functions acceptably. His** **combat survivability is quite high.**

**Cognitive discipline though… inconsistent.**

Athena's gaze dropped again to Percy. She sniffed faintly.

"Yes."

Minerva continued.

**Impulse control: poor.**

Athena nodded. "Predictable. Poseidon was as such."

"Hey!"

**Despite deficiencies, the host is useful.**

Athena considered that.

"Useful."

**Durable.**

"Agreed."

**Combat initiative high.**

"Yes."

Percy smiled. "Siiick. I knew you liked me!"

**Do not flatter yourself, Varlet.**

"..."

Eventually she had enough.

Moonlight gathered around her without warning. It folded inward like silk being drawn through invisible fingers. The air in the room hesitated for a fraction of a second.

Apollo blinked.

Poseidon started laughing.

Moonlight reassembled the world around them in a single quiet pulse.

The forest appeared all at once, tall pines standing motionless beneath a silver sky, the ground marked by the faint shimmering line that defined the outer boundary of the Hunters' camp. The air carried the cold scent of frost and bark, and the distant rustle of movement among the trees hinted that the Hunt was already approaching.

Percy barely had time to steady himself before Artemis moved.

The motion was sudden enough that his brain took a moment to process it.

Her arms closed around him.

It was not a long embrace. It was not even particularly visible. The gesture lasted only a second or two before she stepped back again, but the contact had been deliberate and far firmer than Percy expected from someone who usually maintained several feet of personal distance at all times.

At the same moment an invisible pressure wrapped around him like a tightening current of wind.

Percy blinked, looking down at himself, then back at her. She turned slightly, one hand rising toward her face as though clearing her throat. In the dim silver light it was almost impossible to see the faint flush that had spread across her cheeks, though the glow of ichor-gold warmth flickered for an instant before she suppressed it.

The divine energy remained.

Percy could feel it now, a quiet pressure surrounding his shoulders and chest like a protective field that gently discouraged movement in any direction she had not approved.

He flexed one arm experimentally.

Nothing painful happened.

But the resistance was definitely there.

"…Did you just magically leash me?" Percy asked.

She glanced toward the forest.

The Hunters were close now.

Silver cloaks flickered between the trees as the first figures approached the clearing, bows slung over their shoulders while they moved with the effortless silence of warriors who had spent centuries hunting monsters through forests like this one.

Artemis stepped half a pace forward, positioning herself between Percy and the approaching group.

The divine energy around him tightened slightly. Nearly forty of them emerged from the forest in quiet formation, silver cloaks shifting softly as they spread out along the edge of the camp boundary. Some recognized Percy immediately, though most simply studied him with the calm impassive expressions that had become typical among the immortal hunters.

"Hunters," she said, her tone clear and steady beneath the moonlight, "I believe several of you recognize the demigod standing beside me, but introductions are still appropriate when circumstances change. This is Perseus Jackson, son of Poseidon, a demigod whose actions during recent conflicts have intersected with the Hunt on multiple occasions and whose survival record has become increasingly difficult to explain through ordinary probability."

A few Hunters exchanged quiet looks.

Artemis continued.

"Percy will be accompanying us in a limited operational capacity. His presence does not alter the traditions of this order, nor does it grant him authority within the Hunt itself. However, he will operate under my supervision during certain missions that require coordination between Olympus and our forces. For that reason I have assigned him the role of Apostolos, a position that allows him to act as my intermediary when circumstances require direct communication with external factions."

The Hunters studied Percy again.

For several moments after Artemis finished speaking, the Hunters remained where they stood, watching Percy with the calm scrutiny of warriors who had seen too many battles to react strongly to the appearance of one more demigod. A few of the older Hunters gave faint nods of recognition, remembering the scattered encounters they had shared with him during earlier conflicts, while the younger ones simply observed him with impassive curiosity.

Artemis let the silence stretch for a few seconds longer.

Then her expression softened, just slightly.

"Return to your duties," she told them, her voice carrying the tone of quiet authority that needed no emphasis. "Night patrol rotations remain unchanged, and the eastern perimeter should still be checked before moonrise completes its arc."

Several Hunters bowed their heads. But nobody moved.

"You have already spent enough time standing here evaluating the situation. Percy will not be disappearing from existence in the next few minutes, and you will have ample opportunity to observe him again during the coming days. For now, I would prefer that the Hunt resume its normal operations rather than lingering here like a flock of curious ravens."

A faint ripple of amusement moved through the Hunters.

One by one, the Hunters turned and drifted back into the forest, silver cloaks dissolving into the moonlit trees until the clearing returned to its earlier quiet.

Within a minute the only people left were Percy…

…and Artemis.

The silence changed.

Percy noticed it immediately.

When Artemis turned back toward him, the calm composure she had displayed for the Hunt vanished.

Her eyes locked onto him.

And Percy suddenly remembered something he had managed to forget for the last several months.

Gods were not human.

Artemis took a step forward.

The air in the clearing tightened.

Her expression had not changed much at first, still the same pale face, the same silver gaze, but something underneath the surface had begun to shift. The divine energy that usually surrounded her like quiet moonlight started to thicken, condensing into something heavier and far more dangerous.

Percy's instincts screamed.

"Oh," he squeaked.

Her eyes sharpened. The silver glow around Artemis deepened into something almost predatory, the calm moonlight becoming a cold luminous blaze that cast long shadows across the clearing. Her pupils narrowed slightly, not quite like an animal's, but close enough that Percy's brain began drawing comparisons he really didn't want to finish.

"Perseus Jackson."

Percy swallowed.

"Hi."

Artemis took another step closer.

The divine aura around her flickered.

For a brief moment Percy saw something that made his stomach drop.

Her expression had shifted, not into anger exactly, but into something far more ancient. The calm beauty of the moon goddess warped for a second into something feral and inhuman, the mask of civilization slipping just enough for the creature beneath it to show through.

Not a woman.

Not even a goddess in the way humans imagined gods.

Something older.

Something that hunted.

Percy's brain immediately reached a new conclusion.

Oh gods I am about to die.

His instincts kicked in.

"Okay," Percy said quickly, raising both hands slightly in surrender. "Before whatever happens next I would like to clarify that I did not mean any disrespect when I did anything to insult you, and if this is the part where the goddess of the Hunt decides that eliminating witnesses is the safest course of action I would just like to say that I completely understand the logic but would strongly prefer not to be murdered tonight."

Artemis stopped directly in front of him.

The terrifying expression on her face did not fade.

Percy barely had time to react before Artemis grabbed him by the front of his jacket with both hands.

The grip was firm.

Immovable.

For a moment Percy was absolutely certain he had just been caught by a very dangerous celestial predator.

Then the world disappeared again.

Moonlight snapped inward.

The forest vanished.

And Percy found himself standing inside a tent.

Artemis's tent.

The teleportation had been so fast he barely had time to process the change before the entrance flap behind him sealed itself with a soft pulse of divine energy.

The space inside the tent was larger than it should have been.

Moonlight filtered through the fabric overhead like soft silver water, illuminating a small circular interior that felt more like a sacred chamber than a simple shelter.

Percy was still in Artemis's grip.

She had not let go.

In fact, she pulled him slightly closer the moment the teleportation finished, trapping him between herself and the faintly glowing barrier that now sealed the tent entrance.

For a second Percy simply stared at her.

Then he realized something.

He wasn't dead.

That was good.

But the look in Artemis's eyes had not softened.

That was less good.

"Oh," Percy said weakly.

Artemis leaned slightly closer.

"We are going to finish our conversation now," she said quietly.

Percy's brain immediately translated that sentence into a much simpler concept.

I am trapped in a tent with a very angry moon goddess.

He swallowed.

"…Right."

The space inside the tent held its shape around them with a quiet, unnatural stillness, and Percy could feel it pressing at the edges of his awareness while she kept her grip on his orange camp shirt as though releasing him had not yet been justified by whatever conclusion she was still assembling in her mind. He exhaled slowly, forcing his shoulders to relax even as every instinct he had insisted that being this close to a god in a closed space was a situation that usually ended badly for him, and when he spoke his voice came out the way it always did when he was trying not to escalate something that was already teetering on the edge.

"Okay, so just to check, this is the part where I say something stupid and you decide whether I live through it, right?"

Artemis did not immediately respond, but the intensity in her gaze shifted slightly, not lessening but focusing, as though she were choosing which version of the conversation she intended to have and discarding several others that would have been simpler but far less controlled. Her grip did not tighten again, but neither did it loosen, and the proximity remained deliberate, intentional, impossible to misinterpret even if neither of them was willing to name it directly.

"You presume that I would allow such an outcome," she said, but the words carried less certainty than before, as though the statement was being constructed even as she spoke it.

Percy met her gaze without flinching this time.

"I think Olympus would have opinions before you got the chance to decide anything," he said, and there was no humor in it now.

The silence that followed was heavier than any before it, but it was no longer sharp. It shifted, gradually, almost imperceptibly, into something that held tension without the immediate threat of it snapping.

Artemis's grip loosened.

Not all at once, but in increments, until the pressure against his shirt eased and her hands finally dropped away entirely, though she did not step back immediately.

"You will not speak of this," she said, and now the command was quieter, less edged, but no less absolute.

"I won't," Percy answered immediately, the response unguarded in a way his earlier words had not been.

For a moment she studied him as though measuring the integrity of that answer, and then, slowly, she turned away, the movement precise but no longer rigid, as though some part of the earlier tension had begun to unwind whether she approved of it or not.

"You will exit first," she said, her voice returning to something closer to her usual composure, though a faint softness lingered beneath it that had not been there before. "And you will not draw unnecessary attention."

Percy took that as his cue to leave before she changed her mind about anything, stepping toward the entrance as the barrier parted for him, the cool morning air meeting him the moment he crossed the threshold and breaking the closed, charged atmosphere of the tent in a way that felt almost abrupt.

---

The camp was already awake, Hunters moving through their routines with quiet efficiency, the central fire burning steadily as food was prepared and distributed, the entire scene carrying the calm, controlled rhythm of a group that had done this countless times before.

Percy grabbed a plate, muttering a quick thanks to the Hunter who handed it to him, and took a bite as he scanned the area, his posture loose, casual, deliberately unremarkable despite the fact that he could feel more than a few eyes lingering on him for longer than usual.

Artemis emerged a few moments later.

By the time she stepped out into the open, every trace of what had happened inside the tent was gone from her outward demeanor, her posture composed, her expression calm, her presence settling over the camp with the quiet authority that required no reinforcement.

Percy watched her for a second, then, because he had never been particularly good at leaving things alone and because the tension from earlier had not actually gone anywhere, he made a decision.

A bad one.

A deliberate one.

He stepped just close enough to be heard without making a spectacle of it, took another bite of food, and spoke in a tone that shifted almost immediately into something far more refined than anything that had come out of his mouth in the last several hours.

"My lady," he began, inclining his head with exaggerated precision, "I find myself compelled to remark upon the remarkable efficiency with which your domain restores order in the aftermath of conflict, as well as the unexpectedly commendable quality of the morning provisions, which possess a certain understated elegance that I had not anticipated."

A nearby Hunter slowed slightly.

Percy continued without pause. "It is, without exaggeration, a rare privilege to partake in such an environment under the guidance of one whose presence elevates even the most mundane of activities into something approaching—"

Artemis turned. The movement was sharp enough to cut him off mid-sentence. Her eyes locked onto his, and for a fraction of a second there was nothing there but controlled stillness.

Then—

She stepped forward and, with a motion so fast it barely registered, shoved a small roll of bread directly into his mouth.

Percy froze.

"…mff—"

Artemis's hand lingered there for half a second longer than necessary, before she withdrew it with deliberate composure that did absolutely nothing to hide the faint flush spreading across her cheeks.

It was subtle.

Barely visible in the morning light.

Except for the brief flicker of warm, gold-tinged ichor beneath her skin that betrayed it entirely.

She turned her head slightly away from him, as though the act itself had been far more impulsive than she intended to acknowledge, and let out a quiet, distinctly irritated huff.

"You are insufferable," she said, her voice quiet. Percy chewed.

Slowly.

Veeeery carefully.

"…I was being respectful," he managed after swallowing, his tone returning to something much closer to normal, though the hint of amusement in it was impossible to miss.

Artemis's expression tightened.

Her gaze flicked back to him for a moment, then away again just as quickly, the faint flush still present despite her obvious attempt to suppress it.

"Return to your meal," she said, waving one hand in a dismissive motion that came out just slightly sharper than intended. "And refrain from… whatever that was."

Percy opened his mouth, clearly about to respond.

She cut him off immediately.

"Later," she added, her tone shifting just enough to signal that this was no longer entirely a dismissal. "You may speak to me later."

Another huff. Softer this time.

She turned away from him fully, though not before the faintest trace of that gold-tinged warmth flickered across her cheeks again, and made a small, impatient shooing motion with her hand as though physically pushing him back toward the rest of the camp.

"Eat," she said again, quieter now.

Percy, still chewing the last of the bread she had forcibly inserted into his mouth, gave a small, satisfied nod and did exactly that.
