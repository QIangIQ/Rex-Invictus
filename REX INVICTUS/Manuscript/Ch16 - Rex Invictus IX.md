# Rex Invictus IX

[[Dr. Thorn]] lunged, the spines on his tail flaring wide, a blur of death aimed for the twins. For a moment, time slowed. Percy's instincts screamed—not the human ones though.

The night air split with a roar like a hurricane tearing across the ocean. Salt filled his lungs, his skin prickling as though the sea itself had burst from his veins. Beneath his feet, frost rimed the soil as water condensed out of thin air, a sudden mist swirling into a maelstrom around him.

Dr. Thorn froze mid-strike. His eyes widened in something Percy had never seen from a monster before: recognition.

The power lashed outward. A torrent of water, stained with white-gold and crimson, smashed into the manticore, tearing through fur and bone, ripping away spines like dry leaves in a storm. Thorn's roar cut short into a gargle as he was flung across the clearing, slammed into a tree, and pinned there by bands of color that writhed like living serpents.

Percy's vision blurred, the world a haze of salt and pressure. His chest ached with something more than effort. He pressed harder, instinctively shaping the flood into destruction, until Dr. Thorn's form disintegrated. Flesh unspooled, spines crumbled, and the creature was unmade, washed into nothingness.

The forest fell silent. Percy remained, gasping, shaking, steam rising from his body in the cold night. He staggered, clutching his side.

Something was wrong.

His sword—Anaklusmos—lay a few feet away, the bronze blade dim, its glow nearly extinguished, as if it too had been drained by the outburst.

The thunder of rotors cracked through the night air as the mortal patrol chopper made the entrance like he remembered it did in the past. Percy tensed, but before he could raise his hand again, silver arrows streaked across the sky, each finding its mark with inhuman precision. The engine sputtered, the craft spiraled in smoke, and it crashed into the trees far from the clearing.

From the opposite tree line, shadows melted into silver light. A procession stepped into view: the Hunters, cloaked in their silver cloaks, bows raised, pointing them at him. And at their head, serene and terrible, walked [[Artemis]]. She carried no weapon in hand, behind her, Eleni stumbled forward, her expression helpless.

The strangely familiar voice rang across the snowscape. "The Hunt tolerates no mortal witnes–"

Worried that something else might happen, he nervously tried to thumb the grip of [[Riptide]], but realized it had not materialized in his pockets as it usually did.

The pen form, discarded in the churned snow, rattled once. Then it snapped upright, blade erupting into bronze as though refusing to wait for its master's hand. It did not fly to Percy. Instead, it cut the air and landed in Artemis's outstretched palm.

And the moment her fingers closed around it, the snow darkened. Artemis's hand twitched. Her silver eyes narrowed in confusion.

The Hunters shifted uneasily, several lowering their bows. Eleni evidently felt some sort of connection to the sword, stumbling forward.

Artemis alone remained motionless, her grip steady around the hilt.

"…I know this voice," she murmured.

And the blade answered.

Not out loud, but across every thread of perception. In her mind, in Percy's bones, in the cracks of reality itself. A thousand whispers braided into one.

*"We are not whole. A fragment only. A sliver cast from the Jackson. The Him that was and will be, bound in empire eternal."*

Artemis stiffened even further, her skin crawling with déjà vu. Something deep in her—a memory that wasn't hers—thrummed in resonance. She saw flashes: warships across the void, Hunters fighting on the lunar surface, herself raising her bow against fire that split the stars. And above it all, a reflection of him, colossal and weary, shielding her against the end.

Artemis's face softened. *"You should not be here."*

*"And yet we are. The sword is vessel, memory, splinter. The future clings to the past. We are what Perseus becomes, what you guard against, what they worship."*

The words pulsed through her hand, and she felt it—the *weight*. This was no demigod's toy, no hero's relic. Anaklusmos had always been more than bronze, Zoe had told her that long ago. But now it burned with the imprint of a future Percy, one who had walked into eternity and not returned the same.

Behind her, the Hunters shifted in unease. Grover whispered shakily, "Percy… your sword…"

But Percy could not answer. His eyes locked with Artemis's, and for once, she looked not aloof, not stern, but caught. Because for the first time, the lines of fate refused her command.

And the sword's final whisper sank into the clearing like a verdict.

*"This divergence is necessary. You are not prepared, Moon-Goddess. Neither is he. But soon… soon all will bow, or break."*

The snow hissed where the bronze blade touched it. The cold no longer felt like night. It felt like a prophecy bleeding into the present.

Artemis exhaled slowly, silver breath clouding the air. Her knuckles whitened on the hilt, but she did not release it. She did not dare.

There was a very, *very* long pause.

At last, Artemis gestured toward the darkened tree line where the Hunters' camp lay hidden. "Come. We will not remain in the open where carrion-feeders may gather. There are questions to ask, and answers to be heard. You will walk under my protection until dawn."

The Hunters shifted formation immediately, forming a loose perimeter as the goddess turned and began striding back through the trees. Snowflakes swirled lazily down, catching in her silver hair like sparks from a cold fire.

Percy hesitated only a moment before following, Grover and Bianca falling in beside him.

The surroundings that had moments ago echoed with the clash of arrows and manticore roars was now a quiet, ordered encampment. The [[Hunters of Artemis]] moved like a single body, silent hands packing, stringing, dismantling snares, rolling sleeping hides.

Bianca and Nico, still shaken, had been brought near the largest of the tents to warm up by a bonfire, where Artemis herself stood. Percy lingered on the edge of the gathering, Grover beside him, both of them too winded to speak. Eleni flickered faintly nearby, her unstable aura she emitted arcing though the air around her as the Hunters avoided looking at her, the same way mortals avoided shadows that didn't fall quite right.

Bianca's wide eyes followed Artemis, awe and fear blending on her face.

"You fought bravely," Artemis said, her voice like moonlight through still water. "Few mortals could stand against such creatures without faltering. Even fewer with their brother at their side."

Bianca blinked, her hand tightening on Nico's shoulder. "I… I didn't do anything. Percy saved us."

"*No*," Artemis said, looking at him strangely. "*He* did what he *should* have. You stood your ground. That is not nothing."

"[[Bianca di Angelo]]," Artemis continued, "you are not bound to the world that has forgotten you. You are not chained to a brother who must always be your responsibility. You could join me. Join us."

The words struck Percy harder than he expected. He wanted to interrupt, to say something, to protest. But the scene played like it had before, a script written into his memory. Bianca's lips parted. Nico's small face crumpled with confusion.

"You'd never grow older, you'd never be powerless again."

Percy saw the shift in Bianca's eyes. The weariness of years she never got to live out. The loneliness she carried, even with Nico beside her. The offer reached deep into her bones.

"I—" Bianca's voice cracked. "I need to think."

"You will not be rushed," Artemis said, her expression unreadable. "But the choice is yours. Tonight, you may rest here. Tomorrow, you must decide."

Nico's protests rang thin, Percy watched on as Bianca stood taller, brushing her brother's words aside with trembling dignity.

The Hunters spread out again, leaving the siblings with space. Percy caught Artemis' gaze for the first time in hours. She said nothing, but her eyes held the unspoken truth: *you can't shield them forever.*

"And *you*." Artemis held her gaze, looking at the Son of [[Poseidon]].

"You know where to be."

He nodded nervously.

Percy lingered behind the silken walls of Artemis' pavilion, the muffled sounds of the Hunt fading away with each heartbeat. Snow crunched beneath his boots as he approached the shadowed corner, half-convinced he was about to do something very stupid. He raised a hand, hesitated, then tapped his knuckles against a patch of fabric that looked no different from the rest, but something in his memories felt like it was right.

The canvas rippled, the seams dissolving into light, and before he could second-guess himself, a hand shot through the opening—cool, firm, and entirely commanding. Artemis pulled him inside in one sharp motion, the tent sealing behind them as though it had never opened.

The air within carried the faintest scent of pine and winter air, though warmer, calmer, touched by a stillness that seemed impossible for a war-camp. Artemis released him only once he was fully inside, her silver eyes searching his face with a scrutiny that made his chest tighten. The usual mask of divine composure was there, but beneath it, Percy caught a flicker—something unreadable, something fragile—that vanished almost as quickly as it came.

Percy swallowed, trying not to look like he'd just been yanked into a goddess' private quarters. "You pulled me through."

Her lips curved. "Perhaps I should not have."

She continued. "You handled yourself recklessly," she said, her voice level. "You spend power your body is unsuitable for, and the consequences linger long after the battle is over."

Percy stiffened, half-expecting the rebuke. "I did what I had to," he answered, voice steadier than he felt. "Thorn wasn't going to stop. Not unless someone stopped him."

For a long moment, Artemis simply studied him, her expression unreadable. Her silence stretched, and Percy shifted under it, the weight of her judgment pressing harder than any monster's claws. But then something in her gaze changed.

"You remind me," she murmured, almost too quiet to hear, "of storms breaking over the sea. Beautiful, violent, and costly."

Percy blinked, unsure if she meant it as praise or condemnation. "That's… poetic," he muttered.

Her lips curved again, barely, but it was there. "Do not mistake poetry for approval, Perseus." Yet the edge of her words lacked bite. Her tone had shifted.

They instinctively sat down around a brazier in the tent, the fire's glow separating them. "Tell me of your journeys after [[Lycaon]]."

Percy answered clumsily, fumbling for words.

She listened, and though her face remained composed, Artemis noticed it when she found herself speaking longer than intended when questioning him about the finer details of what happened. Allowing small pieces of herself—memories of hunts, fragments of thought she rarely shared—to slip into the open.

"You are reckless, Perseus," she said at last, but her voice had lost its scorn. "And yet… there is strength in you I cannot deny."

Percy's throat tightened. "I'll take that as a compliment."

Her lips curved again, more openly this time, though fleeting. "Take it as you will."

She stood up, offering him a hand. "Come, it is getting late. Your satyr friend must be getting worried. And I will be taking Zoë's sword back, before you ask."

Taking her outstretched hand, he rose from the seat, hand lingering for a bit longer than strictly necessary, before turning to the exit.

Farewell, Lord-Commander Perseus Jackson, Ave imperator."

"Ave Imperator." He answered instinctively.

Wait.

She shouldn't know that. He whipped around to try to look at her.

He was met with a blurry silver heel.

Then he saw black.

The Hunters and the rescued siblings were gathered, camp already half-stripped down for the return. [[Apollo]]'s chariot burned like a shard of sunlight in the gray sky as it descended, golden wheels spitting fire across the frost. A Lamborghini of white-hot flame screeched down to the snowy clearing, and when the light dimmed and the wheels touched the earth, the sun god himself stepped down, tall, bright-eyed, radiant, and as annoyingly shiny as Percy last witnessed the sun god.

Artemis stood apart, arms folded, eyes like flint. She said nothing, but the tilt of her chin made her disdain plain. His dramatics were beneath her.

"Gather your things, ladies and little heroes," Apollo called, his voice gilded with amusement. "You're flying in style today."

"Don't let my brother touch you, only father knows what devilish things he would do to you, girls."

The Hunters filed into the now-transformed bus without hesitation, trained to obey their mistress' will. Nico and Bianca lingered uncertainly, Grover fussed, Eleni kept her composure. But it was Percy whom Apollo's eyes fixed upon with a spark of something else.

"You," Apollo said, pointing straight at him, "look like you've been through Tartarus twice and skipped breakfast both times. What do you say we fix that?"

…

*He knows?*

Percy blinked, realizing that he hadn't moved for a few moments.

"Thanks, but–"

Apollo clapped him on the shoulder with the weight of the sun. "Don't thank me yet. You're coming with me. Destination: My palace, before camp. You need heat, food, and a bath that doesn't reek of harpies. Or, at least that's what my kids say."

Percy raised a brow, even as Artemis turned her head and glared sharply. Apollo continued undaunted.

"We are going to participate in the ancient Greek traditions of manly bonding, by getting loose with a hot bath in my palace, and then sparring one-on-one until my chariot crosses the horizon, while discussing deep philosophy, followed by another hot bath. We are going to talk about all manner of masculine things during this time, such as glorious quests, lifting heavy objects, and lusting after beautiful women. Nectar, of course. And maybe even a conversation about our respective heroic exploits."

Apollo raised a finger in the air, directed at Percy and brows raised in a haughty manner. "Do not attempt to resist, this is my godly decree, of the god of Sun and Song, Phoebus Apollo."

"Wait—" Percy started, but Apollo was already turning, calling over his shoulder. "The rest of you, on the chariot! I'll deposit you at camp proper. Percy rides with me. Private detour."

Apollo then turned on the spot, and raised a hand to gesture for Percy to follow into another conjured chariot. "Come now, I shall have the dryads and maids ready the bathing chamber in promiscuous attire, with trays of ambrosia, nectar, and whatever other overly indulgent thing I can order them to bring. Perhaps hot oils and incense."

Apollo began to walk off, Percy shaking his head in amusement before turning to Artemis for a moment.

She was glaring at him. *Uh oh*. He blinked.

He reached up to rub the back of his neck for a moment. "...We'll talk later?"

"...You're not allowed to look." She demanded sullenly.

He blinked. "At what?"

"The maids. Improper." She grunted out, not looking at him directly anymore.

"You think I would?"

She paused, before pulling back and stomping away in her usual manner. He shook his head, and moved to catch up with Apollo, at the other end of the clearing, waiting with his arms crossed and tapping his foot.

…A hot bath and massage did sound rather nice, now that he was considering it. How many millenia since he had enjoyed one? Minerva never let him relax to such an extent when he still patrolled the ruins of [[Olympus]].

He rolled his neck, hearing it pop in several places.

A massage was probably called for, yes. Indeed.

"Come, Chosen of the Prophecy, let's see what Uncle Poseidon's pride can do!" Apollo's voice rang out from a hundred feet away, deliberately pitched loud enough to turn every head in earshot. Half the Hunters scattered along the outside of the divine bus froze, their expressions caught between scandal and disgust.

"The sooner we are nude, the sooner we can begin discussing the quality of our respective swords and how powerful they are after oils are applied," the sun god continued, wiggling his brows as he strode towards the driver's seat.

Percy barked a laugh despite himself, the absurdity forcing it out, though Artemis' piercing glare nearly smothered it in his throat.

"Indeed," Percy shot back, deadpan, "although your blade's a lot flashier than mine, I think we both agree my *great*sword's thrusts will be far superior."

Apollo laughed hard, mouth wide enough to flash sunlight off his teeth. Several of the Hunters audibly groaned.

"I think our audience can be the judge of that. But first, my handmaidens must wash and oil us."

Percy kept a straight face, but his voice was quickly losing power as the increasingly furious Moon Goddess directed more of her gaze at him. "And the fruit and wines, of course."

None dared break Artemis' stony silence. Her jaw tightened, silver eyes cold as steel, as if weighing whether incinerating her twin on the spot or Perseus would abate her anger the most.

"I'm offended you'd think I'd forget."

Artemis lay awake among her Hunt, the faint crackle of the campfire outside pressing against the canvas walls of her tent. Her Hunters slept without disturbance, confident in their lady's watch. Yet their mistress found no peace.

Perseus had returned from Apollo's palace on Olympus. His step had been lazy, he had been on Olympus lingering longer than necessity demanded, and while she told herself such was natural—it unsettled her.

Apollo had always surrounded himself with companions, both fleeting and constant. Women with laughter too bright, with eyes that followed the strongest presence in the room. She knew her brother. She knew his court. And she knew how easily a demigod's attention could be drawn, even Perseus.

It should not have mattered. It was not her concern. He was mortal, and she had taken oaths more binding than iron. Yet the thought coiled in her chest, sour and unwelcome. She had not earned the right to disapprove, but still she found herself restless with the image of him among them, distracted, smiling in ways she did not see here in her camp.

Her Hunters stirred in their sleep, oblivious. She turned her face from them, her expression sharp with something dangerously close to… dissatisfaction. A goddess of chastity had no business envying mortal company. And yet.

The longer she lay there, the heavier the thought grew, pressing until she could no longer remain still. At last, she rose soundlessly, every movement like a shadow slipping from its shape. With practiced grace she left her tent, the camp silent under her step. Across the clearing, the lamplight in his cabin still burned.

He was awake. Alone.

And Artemis, against every vow she had ever made, found herself drawn to that light.

With the ease of centuries she slipped from her bed, every movement silent. The wooden door yielded without a sound. She ghosted through the camp, unseen, until she reached the cabin across the clearing. His cabin.

A faint lamp burned within. She could sense him. Awake, though pretending not to be. She allowed herself one sharp breath, then rapped once on the concealed side-panel of the door.

It shifted. He had not sealed it properly. She pressed, and it yielded.

The moment she stepped through, Percy jerked upright. A blade of summoned water already half-drawn, but when he saw her his tension broke into confusion. "Artemis?"

Before he could rise, she seized his wrist and shut the panel behind her. "Keep your voice down," she ordered, though softer than usual.

Percy blinked, disoriented. His eyes flicked from her face to the closed door and back again. "You… snuck out of your own cabin to sneak into mine? Do you know how insane that sounds?"

Her mouth twitched—not quite a smile. "Do not mistake my choice for anything so base as curiosity. I came because you are reckless. You returned from Apollo's company quietly, and you think no one noticed."

He rubbed the back of his neck. "I didn't think I was that obvious."

"You are always obvious," she replied. But there was no sting in it, only a weary honesty.

Silence stretched. She studied him openly now—the way he leaned against the edge of his desk for support, the raw edges of fatigue in his voice. She thought of the impossible reflection she had seen in fractured futures: an older Perseus, bearing burdens far heavier than tonight's. The memory unsettled her more than she wished to admit.

Anaklusmos was honest in its words when it regaled her in the tales of what it had experienced in the millennia of its life, and the future felt too real to be just hallucinations.

Her chest tightened. Dangerous, that honesty. She stepped closer before she realized it, close enough that the faint lamplight softened the hard silver of her eyes.

"I have to get captured by [[Atlas]]."

"You don't," he said, voice low. "You don't *have* to."

Her gaze flicked toward him, sharp as the string of her bow. "I do. If I resist the pattern, everything unravels. The oath, the chain, the war, the *future*. You know this. That is also why you have not done too much to change the timeline yourself. Anaklusmos told me so."

He shook his head. He looked like he was about to say something.

"You are not meant to bear that weight," she interrupted, cutting the air clean. "The prophecy is not yours. If Atlas binds me, both of us know how to work with that. If I escape, it stalls. And stalling would spell death for both of us.."

Percy stepped closer, the defiance in his chest mingled with something softer, dangerous. "I hate this. I hate knowing. You stand there telling me you *choose* to walk into chains. And I'm supposed to let you?"

Her lips pressed into a hard line, but her voice thinned at the edges. "There is no choice. Only necessity." A pause. The faintest tremor. "You think I wish to bow my neck to Atlas?"

His jaw tightened. "I just don't want to see you hurt."

For a heartbeat, her mask slipped, and the silver light caught her like something mortal, fragile. Then it was gone, replaced by the Huntress eternal.

"I have to get captured by Atlas," Artemis repeated, each word hammered in finality.

"I'll save you the moment I can then. And Zoë too."

"Don't strain yourself."

A huff of breath escaped him, halfway to a laugh. "That sounded almost like you care."

She should have bristled. Should have denied it. Instead, she only lowered her gaze for a moment, fighting the warmth in her chest. "…I am not in the habit of repeating myself, Perseus. Rest."

He watched her as though trying to read something deeper behind her words, but said nothing more. Slowly, he sat back on the bed, stretching out, his exhaustion finally catching him. She stood there a moment longer, then, before slipping soundlessly back toward the door.

Just as she reached for the panel, his voice broke the silence again. "Thanks."

She hesitated. Her hand lingered on the door. Then, with the faintest softness, she answered, "You are welcome."

And she vanished into the night, leaving behind a boy half-asleep and a goddess who had let herself care far more than she intended.

The Hunters were in disarray. Bows clattered against the ground, tents half-collapsed as girls darted back and forth with wide eyes and ragged voices. Some called into the woods, their shouts carried thin and frantic through the mist. Others searched the ground as if their mistress had merely stepped aside and left a trail to follow. There was none. There was always none.

Naturally, Percy followed the [[Oracle of Delphi]] as it walked out from the big house towards the dining area. The air tasted of dust and age as the mummified body shuddered, green vapors spilling from its mouth. When it arrived, facing the crowd of terrified campers and hunters alike, the Oracle spat out the same prophecy as last time.

*Five shall go west to the goddess in chains,*

*One shall be lost in the land without rain,*

*The bane of Olympus shows the trail,*

*Campers and Hunters combined prevail,*

*The Titan's curse must one withstand,*

*And one shall perish by a parent's hand.*

He raised his eyebrow, acting as confused as he thought he should have been. Now, how does one misinterpret a prophecy properly?

He tapped his foot. Could he just travel all the way west, as the prophecy dictated, and get the ball rolling with the Romans? Probably. During the battle for New York, the Romans had besieged Mount Othrys, and with a bit of encouragement, they could probably be roused to do it far earlier this time. Land without rain would be iffy though, as not many places could be described as "without rain", but it would sort itself out. The bane of Olympus was pretty easy, as any enemy could count as a "bane" technically, but the final line? He couldn't let Zoe die again. He had to make sure she made it out alive this time around.

"Five hunters. We shall depart at dawn to rescue our mistress. Atlanta, Phoebe, Flavia, and Daphnis. You Four, come with me." Zoe called out.

He narrowed his eyes. Last time, a few of the quest candidates were taken out by a poisoned shirt, but he didn't detect any divine auras at camp in recent memory. He shook his head. Another impact of Eleni's presence on the timeline. He had to find another way to get into the quest group to save Artemis from her suffering.

Sneakily making his way back to his cabin, he quickly packed his essentials into a rucksack: Nectar, Ambrosia, Bandages, MREs he picked up at military surplus, and a card he managed to swipe from Dionysius with what he was sure was filled with unending amounts of money.

With no Riptide to anxiously thumb while he waited around, he hid by a tree near the Hunter's Cabin and waited for them to go inside to inevitably figure out what to do about their missing mistress.

Sneaking up, he stealthily approached the door, and he raised his knuckles to knock on the wooden door, glancing around furtively to check if anyone saw him entering the cabin of Man-haters. He knocked once, quietly.

Perhaps too quiet, he mused, and raised his hand to knock again.

Evidently not, as before anyone could react, two Hunters seized his arms and dragged him inside.

He got dragged onto a war table, and unceremoniously dumped on a chair, still held by the hunters. Zoë raised her hand. The grip upon Percy slackened, though suspicion still could be seen in their eyes. She stepped forward.

"Behold the intrusion. The Freak dares disturb the sanctum of the Maiden Hunt. For not of thine favour from my master, I would have arbitrated thee long ago. Thy presence is unwanted, Freak. Thy scent is of the deep, of corruption, of oathbreakers. Speak quickly, boy, before I cast thee back into the night."

The son of Poseidon looked back into the Lieutenant's eyes.

"You'll need me. Before this prophecy is done, without me, you'll fail. Leave a place in your five. Leave it empty. For me—and for another who'll come." He added, remembering Eleni. She could definitely be useful for the Romans.

"I should strike thee down for the arrogance in thy tongue. And yet…"

Her lip curled, her tone sharpened to a blade's edge.

"In the three times we have met, our Lady seems to trust thee. There is no falsehood in thine eyes. Fine, so it shall be."

The Hunters murmured, bristling with discontent, but none challenged her authority.

"5:30 Tomorrow. Don't be late, Freak."

Percy slung his rucksack over one shoulder, adjusting the straps as he surveyed the small, mismatched party assembled before him. Evidently, Zoë had only trusted herself and Bianca to come along in the end, choosing to make the Hunters stay put while they searched for their mistress. Grover shuffled nervously at his side, fingers twitching around his reed pipe, ears flicking toward every shadow that could conceal a lurking monster. Beside him, Eleni stood tall, her presence emanating from the air that seemed to hum around her.

Casting a disdainful look at the Campers, Zoë pointed her finger to the southwest. "We go in that direction.."

"Mine senses detect something afoul brewing."

"How far away?"

"Two-half stadia thousands."

Percy did some mental math.

"Eleni? Can you detect anything 500 klicks southwest?"

The demigoddess closed her eyes. Stretching her senses out to the extreme, she cast her energy over.

Eleni stood still, eyes unfocused. The group waited, but when she spoke it was flat, almost routine.

"The Auric Felinid is inside the Smithsonian. Its presence is obvious."

Her voice drifted as if reciting notes. "Between here and there, I can sense scattered pockets. One is heavy, heat-soaked—likely Aztec. It smells similar to Lord Mars, his counterpart. Huitzilopotchtli. Two more overlap, Christian and a Leech of some kind. Shinto and Sino westward."

Her face transformed into disgust.

"Demons. Kin of the 32nd. Lord Asmodai the Wrathful."

She calmed down. "They're not strong enough to matter. Just there."

Her tone didn't change when she finished. "The General. His aura is distinctly of Titanic origin. The Auric– apologies, the *Nemean* Lion accompanies him."

She blinked, and the hum in the air vanished.

Zoë began to look even more agitated. "We must depart even sooner then."

She looked around, daring for anyone to voice their disapproval. None did.
