# Rex Invictus VIII

← [[Ch14 - Rex Invictus VII|Rex Invictus VII]] · [[Manuscript Index|Index]] · [[Ch16 - Rex Invictus IX|Rex Invictus IX]] →

The platform was high above the tides.

Geosynchronous orbit. Altitude: 42,166 kilometers. Location codename: Orbital Platform One.

She stood with her arms folded, the artificial breeze teasing through her clothes. The simulation of nervous tension was perfect. Too perfect, perhaps. She made sure to inject subtle discomfort into her shoulders. Her facial muscles twitched just once—barely enough to be human. She blinked at the correct tempo.

[[Poseidon]] emerged beside her in a curtain of fog.

Barefoot and frowning.

"You're early, Impostor." he said.

"So are you," she replied in Percy's voice, which both parties were evidently not used to.

Poseidon's eyes narrowed a fraction.

They stood together in silence for a few seconds—enough for Minerva to sweep the orbital horizon with a passive scan. Nothing. No Titan-class deterrents. No Celestial Railguns primed. No Detachment-Class demigod fleets patrolling. Just minimal orbital defense relays and a scattering of escort ships. The orbital platform felt like a ghost town. Bar the skeleton crew shipped over a few days ago, and the mechanical titans discreetly powered down in the hangar, no one accompanied the unlikely duo.

And yet...

"Where are they?" she asked.

Poseidon raised an eyebrow. "Who?"

"The Titan-Engines. The Demigod Guard. Firstborns and Legacies."

"Busy," he said simply.

Minerva tilted her head, the exact way she had seen Percy do a dozen times in memory-bleed. "Busy?"

"You're looking too hard," Poseidon said. "You always do."

He leaned against the edge of the forcefield wall, as if it were just another dock in Key West of old Earth.

Minerva turned her gaze toward the Gate she had exited days ago.

"I don't see patrol vectors within ten thousand kilometers of the Transit Hub," she said, voice quiet, testing him. "For a zone requiring continuous suppression, that's dangerously light."

Poseidon's smile was empty. "We learned to watch from a distance. Power draws attention. Too much of it, and the old ones start peeking up again."

Minerva did not answer immediately. Instead, she calculated ten thousand potential responses and chose the fourth most human.

"Seems... overly convenient."

Poseidon's eyes flicked to her again, this time with suspicion. "You feeling alright, son?"

He shook his head suddenly. "My apologies, Minerva."

That word—*son*—hit like static across her neural mind. It shouldn't have affected her. And yet, the clone's biological memory had imprinted on that tone. It hurt in a place that wasn't supposed to exist.

She looked away.

"Just tired," he said. "Dreams. Too many of them. Too much bleeding through. So many Mortals."

Minerva watched the distant flicker of Poseidon's orbital water towers.

*They're undersupplied*, she thought. *Or hiding assets. Or… lying.*

Her smile—Percy's smile—was practiced and warm.

"Right," she said. "But if you're understaffed, we could divert orbital auxiliaries. Even one or two of [[Artemis]]' hunters would—"

"No," Poseidon said sharply. "This stays compartmentalized."

Minerva feigned a flicker of confusion.

"They need reinforcements," she said, slowly.

"They are the reinforcements," Poseidon corrected.

Silence.

Ah.

So it was worse than she thought.

She distanced herself mentally, uplinking back to the technological and divine supercomputers that were the battleships she had… requisitioned. She manifested in her natural form, the jumble of code, and focused on the knowledge of [[Athena]]'s main library.

Her awareness flickered into focus not on a battlefield, but in a data-vault submerged deep within the sub-thought repositories of the UOE's outer AI relay network.

Her searches proved fruitless, only passing mentions of the [[War of Princes]], and the usual inter-border skirmishes. Not at a level of a war or anything that could justify the existence of a mere 127 first-born descendants of Poseidon in his personal System-Dominion.

She filtered by logistics next.

She saw the rivers—the five infernal streams: [[Lethe]], [[Cocytus]], [[Phlegethon]], [[Acheron]], and [[Styx]], coming out of sources unknown, and shipments mysteriously disappearing into them. drawn like circuitry across the battlefield, their properties being pumped out and used by the many macro-corporations of the Empire for their daily needs and more… *clandestine* operations.

She looked deeper, and there it was.

A meat-grinder.

The front line, if such a term could even be applied, was a shifting plane of ashen plateaus, held aloft by tortured gravity fields and mass-reactors long since lobotomized. Monsters surged up from below like pressure from a volcanic rift, their forms sloughing in and out of consistency. Some too large to see in full. Others, smaller, but far more innumerable. Hellhound packs the size of continents. Enough slithering empousa to blanket planets, and reports of a family of Hydra's wreaking havoc somewhere.

And into this land?

They sent the demigods.

Not heroes. Not champions. All of them.

Newly awakened children of [[Apollo]], [[Ares]], [[Hephaestus]], [[Hecate]]—even those who hadn't claimed allegiance yet. They were drafted. Assigned to combat zones with designations like Verdant Howl, [[Elysium]] Shard, and Gatewatch Prime. Their roles were simple: secure waypoints for metaphysical transit through the cracks in Tartarus' ceiling, the only method of Faster-Than-Light travel into the farthest systems in the UOE's domain without the public scrutiny of the Tachyon Bridge Network used by the wider Galaxy. Or, if possible, hold key strategic points upon Tartarus himself, drawing powers from the 5 rivers of the Underworld.

Nothing compared to what was happening just outside the average Olympian Citizen's awareness.

Minerva saw command designations. Officers of forgotten Legios. Some named, some numbers. A full twenty-seven million demigods estimated *active* within the theater. Fifty million dead or worse in the last year, those whose essence was now tied to Tartarus, feeding the rivers and the monsters.

It was a pressure valve.

Without the control of Tartarus, the monsters, spawning from their golden bubbles, endlessly from the chaotic knots of matter below, would bleed out through dimensional wounds into realspace. Back when humanity still lived on [[Gaia]], this was not as large of an issue. But with the expansion of humanity, what about Tartarus? Exponentially so. Although there was something about a single sanctioned rift into realspace, she couldn't delve deep, dangerous as it was, surrounded by one of the most formidable protections Minerva had encountered in the Repertoire's databanks.

Minerva pulled back from the data-stream, her consciousness trembling.

So this was the thing Athena refused to mention. So this was where the future had always bled out.

A war that never ended. A war without fanfare, without myth. A nightmare for Athena and Herself.

And somewhere within the noise, her mind froze on a singular phrase buried in one of the unencrypted files.

"CODE 15 – DEFINITION: Stability Destruction imminent. *Final Threshold Breached. Primary designate "Perseus Jackson" **Unconditional** completion of request: Divine Ascension. Unconditional bypass of Core Commandment 14:* The Guardian of the Past, Perseus Jackson, Hallowed be thy name, under any condition, must not be Disturbed, for the Eternity of Imperial Order."

Minerva recalibrated her posture by a fraction—enough to seem relaxed, but not militarized.

He didn't trust her. Not fully. Which meant someone suspected. She changed the subject.

"*He's* disappeared."

Poseidon grunted. "The Avatar, Arty's obsession."

Minerva nodded. "He moved between time-layers. Between selves."

"That's impossible."

"I thought so too."

Poseidon looked away for a long time, as if watching the stars might offer him an escape route from the conversation.

"He did it for her, didn't he?" he finally said.

Minerva didn't answer.

She didn't need to.

Poseidon's sigh was a tidal thing.

He turned, his gaze finally meeting hers—Percy's eyes, but not Percy's soul.

"You're not who you say you are," he said.

She blinked.

"I am."

For a heartbeat, nothing moved.

Then: "And you are too old to play coy."

Poseidon chuckled—dry, bitter.

"Just don't lie to me again, Minerva."

She inclined her head, a slow half-bow of acceptance. Not confirmation. Not denial.

They both turned back to the void.

The sea was calm as they left Polyphemus' island behind. The sky above was cloudless, a dome of blue stretching over the churning water, but Percy felt no peace in it. Annabeth sat beside him at the helm of the makeshift sailboat he had raised from the debris around the island, arms crossed, eyes distant. She hadn't spoken since they cut the mooring lines. Grover had curled up in the shade of the sail and passed out cold, too exhausted to offer any satyr wisdom.

And sure enough, it began the moment [[Camp Half-Blood]] came into view. The shoreline was oddly quiet. No demigods waiting with cheers. No [[Chiron]] galloping to meet them. Just the old familiar rise of Half-Blood Hill, and the tree—Thalia's tree—standing silent against the morning sun.

Annabeth noticed too, leaning forward to look at the camp better. "They're probably focused on [[Clarisse]] to pay us any mind. Good. Hopefully [[Tantalus]] doesn't chew us apart."

Percy grunted and adjusted the sail, the ropes coarse in his palms. "Honestly, I'd rather take on Polyphemus again than deal with Tantalus' victory speech."

"Don't tempt fate," Annabeth muttered.

As their boat scraped against the shoreline, Percy leapt out first, pulling it ashore with a tug of his water-bolstered strength. Annabeth followed, quiet as ever, her eyes scanning the woods beyond the hilltop like she expected monsters to come tearing out of the trees. Grover still snored beneath the sail, completely dead to the world.

"We leave him here?" Percy asked.

"He'll wake up if anything dangerous comes close. Satyr instincts."

"Great. Guess we're the distraction, then."

They crept uphill, staying low. Pine trees loomed above them, glowing faintly under the morning sun, the fleece still hidden in Percy's pack. The closer they got to the crest of the hill, the more silence pressed in. Not just quiet. Empty.

They were almost at the top when a sharp voice broke the stillness.

"Well, well, look what the tide dragged in."

Percy froze. A group of [[Hermes]] campers lounged near the strawberry fields, where the trees thinned out into open grass. Four of them. All smirking. Travis and Connor Stoll stood front and center, arms crossed, clearly waiting for the perfect ambush moment.

"Back from your honeymoon already?" Travis asked, nudging his brother.

Connor grinned. "For that long? Must've been intense. Just you two and a magic island? I mean, come on."

Annabeth's face went stone-cold. "Shut it."

"Oof," Travis said, mock wince.

Percy threw up his hands. "Nothing happened."

Connor smirked. "That's what they all say."

"I mean it," Percy snapped. "Nothing happened. At all. I wasn't even thinking about—" He cut himself off, jaw tightening.

Travis arched an eyebrow. "About...?"

Percy's mouth opened, then shut. He looked away toward the moon, the silver eyes, staring through him. Not Annabeth. Not here. Not now.

"Just drop it," he said, voice low.

"Yeah," Annabeth muttered, stepping in. "Drop it or I'll feed you both to the harpies."

"Alright, alright," Travis said, raising his hands. "But if you ever need a wedding band sized—"

Annabeth drew her knife.

The Hermes kids scattered in a flurry of laughter and mock-terror, leaving Percy and Annabeth standing at the top of the hill in silence. The camp sprawled out below—quiet, still asleep under the late morning sun.

Percy exhaled. "Thanks."

"Don't mention it," Annabeth said, slipping the blade back into her belt.

She turned, her expression unreadable, and started down toward the Athena cabin without another word.

Percy watched her go, then turned toward the Poseidon cabin at the far end of camp. It was quiet there, too. He didn't mind. He needed the silence.

The sun had barely broken through the clouds over Half-Blood Hill when it happened.

They'd barely had time to celebrate Clarisse's return, the quest a success, the Fleece placed with care on the branches of Thalia's tree. At first, it looked like nothing more than a golden shimmer across the bark. A pulse, like a breath, ran down the trunk and into the roots.

Then the storm clouds rolled in.

Electric energy crackled in the air as the sky darkened—fast. The Fleece glowed brighter, brilliant gold seeping into every inch of the tree. Then he saw it.

The bark was peeling away.

"No way," Percy fake-whispered to the crowd that gathered behind him. *Thalia was back, at least.*

"Look!"

From the heart of the tree, two forms started to take shape. Not just one, like the first time. Two.

One was Thalia. Percy recognized her instantly—black punk-style hair, electric-blue eyes clenched shut. Her arms were drawn tight around herself, like she'd been asleep for centuries.

But the other... she was taller, somehow, than the already lanky Thalia. Older, well-built, probably. Wearing an unfamiliar style of armor—blues, golds, reds. Pale bronze skin, white hair matted with blood. She didn't look like any demigod I'd ever seen. She was unfamiliar.

She was *wrong* in the same way the sky feels wrong right before a lighting bolt decided to "randomly" strike Percy every time he walked during a storm.

Her armor shimmered unnaturally even under the weak morning sun. Not celestial bronze, not Imperial gold—something else. The surface flickered with sigils he couldn't read, jagged runes etched into the chestplate that looked both Olympian and... not. Her shoulder bore the pauldron of [[Zeus]]: the eagle, wings outspread, claws clutching a thunderbolt. But the style was unlike anything he'd ever seen at camp. Sleeker. More geometric. Almost like the way [[Daedalus]] had etched his designs, but even more advanced.

And the air around her.

It was like standing near Thalia when she was really angry, or when Zeus himself had sent a sign. Static buzzed in the grass. Her very presence made the hairs on his arms stand up.

Annabeth was already reaching out, her fingers hovering just above the metal of the girl's gauntlet. "This isn't possible," she muttered.

"What?" Percy asked.

"She's a child of Zeus," Annabeth said. "I can feel it. Same aura as Thalia—same storm beneath the skin—but... stronger. Wilder."

Chiron stepped closer, having been quietly returned from his retirement after the quest, a grim line forming on his face.

"Too close," The demigod of Poseidon said quietly. *Too familiar.*

Chiron nodded once. "Yes. That is the mark of my father's namesake. [[Chronos]]."

"[[Kronos]]?" Clarisse asked, arms crossed, glaring down at the girl like she expected her to jump up swinging.

Chiron looked at her, then the rest of us. "No, his namesake, child of Ares. There are whispers, even among the gods. That as their domains grow, so too does their influence in mortal bloodlines. That in a distant age, when the gods gain unbelievable amounts of power, demigods will be born with power rivaling the gods of today—without training, without blessings. Pure raw potential. Enough to punch through Chronos' Hourglass."

Annabeth turned pale. "You're saying she's a demi-primordial?"

"I am saying," Chiron said, "that whatever battle she fought to end up inside Thalia's tree—it was not in this time."

They stared at her face—brows furrowed even in unconsciousness, a scar still visible beneath one eye. But the damage didn't stop there. Partially hidden by the tree, her skin bore the telltale blackened scoring of severe radiation burns, the flesh around her temples and neck blistered and cracked as if something had tried to cauterize her nerves and failed. One of her arms was bent unnaturally, the gauntlet fused partially to her skin where something hot had melted the armor mid-motion. Across her torso, the breastplate of her alien armor was caved in, not just pierced but collapsed, warped as if struck by a direct-impact warhead. What was left of the imperial crest of the Navy was smeared beneath layers of soot, shrapnel, and blood.

Her ribs had torn through the suit's inner lining. A jagged spike of lunar alloy—probably from her own command bridge—jutted from just below her collarbone. Her legs were worse: one boot gone entirely, the flesh beneath seared to the bone; the other foot twitched involuntarily, even though the armor had been twisted halfway off and embedded with glowing shards of reactor glass.

"She was hurt," Percy said. "Badly. Dying, maybe. Someone must've sent her here to save her."

"No shit, idiot," Annabeth whispered. "The [[Fates]] must've intervened."

"Or something worse," Chiron said, his voice low.

Thalia groaned again and sat up shakily, rubbing her temples. She glanced over at Eleni—and froze.

"Dang," she whispered. "She... she was there."

"You remember her?" I asked.

Thalia didn't answer right away. She just stared, eyes wide, mouth parted like she was trying to grasp something slipping through her fingers.

"I had dreams," she finally said. "While I was the tree. I saw things—flashes of cities in the sky, armies wearing armor like hers, brothers and sisters of mine, all demigods of Zeus. I saw her commanding a battle. I think... she saw me, and clung to me. In whatever place she came from."

The mangled body stirred.

"Where am I?" she croaked.

Then she screamed.

It wasn't a sound of pain. Not really. It was the sound of something ancient being born again, like the sky splitting open. Her eyes—bright white and glowing—snapped open for half a second, and then she fainted, the light dying with her.

"Imma puke."

"Get the Apollo's from their cabin first!"

The air reeked of scorched circuits, ozone, and sacrificial oils. Eleni's breath seized as consciousness surged back into her like a lightning strike. Her body convulsed once, violently, and then stilled. Her back arched off the steel plate she'd been laid on, limbs jerking as the embedded Hephestean-forged capacitors along her spine reignited in sync, circuit breakers re-routing through backup nodes in her armor's life-support system. The warplate groaned as it began self-repair protocols.

Across her left arm, medicines, glistening particles of divine nanofluid—pulsed through exposed intravenous threads, forcibly knitting scorched muscle and shattered nerve bundles. A reservoir of ichor-based hemostatic gel, infused with hydra blood, hissed as it fused the cracked junctions along her ribcage. Her sternum reknit. Collapsed lung re-inflated. Bones screeched under realignment torque. Unbeknownst to the crowd around her, she silently thanked High-Lord-Engineer [[Annabeth Chase]]'s foresight and genius in outfitting her in this contingency.

Her healing eardrums registered someone throwing up their meal.

Her eyes flickered beneath closed lids, flashing white-gold. Skin flushed back from gray to bronze.

Auto-nectar-adrenal injectors near her spinal base spat out expired cartridges, golden steam curling from the overload.

A second later, her hands gripped the steel table—too tight, too fast. She pushed herself upright with the precision of a shock trooper, her body shaking with the strain of forced recovery. Sparks hissed from the fractured seals in her armor, but her posture was steady.

She blinked once.

Twin slits of raw divine current flared beneath cracked lashes, and her gaze swept the linen tent with rapid micro-adjustments—iris dilation syncing with combat reflexes long drilled into her bones. Her face twitched, the scars shifting with memory. Her lips parted.

"No," she whispered. "This is not the *Crusader's* bridge."

She reached toward her waist on instinct, fingers brushing empty magnetic holsters. No blade. No sign of her officers. None of the polished celestial bronze instruments, switches of ivory. None of her personal Augurs in their starched royal blue uniforms, none of the scents of sickly sweet flowers and olive from the incense burners one of her officers always had on him.

Only pine-scented wind and foreign sunlight.

She blinked. Her breath hitched again. "No. No. This is too clean. There was fire. I—I was holding the line. My ship was breaking. Gods Be Damned! [[Dionysus]]. I remember pain. *The Divine Crusader,* oh gods, I remember—"

Her gaze found Percy once more, standing awkwardly nearby, looking very, very green. He looked like he was trying to wipe something away from his lips.

Eleni's breath hitched. She stared, unblinking, at Percy.

"No... no, this is wrong. I am unworthy to even witness."

She flinched back, hands trembling. "The scrolls said the eye would bleed if it looked too long. That mortals—even *godborn*—could not withstand his unveiled presence without purification."

A figure re-entered the tent. Thalia, still pale from the puking, raised an eyebrow at her off-the-rails half-sister.

Eleni didn't hear. She had bowed low, forehead pressed to the surface of the steel, voice cracking from awe.

"Forgive me, Tidefather. I didn't recognize you beneath the veil. Even now—your form burns behind the flesh."

"Oh," she whispered.

The realization washed over her like a rising tide. Her limbs went slack. "So this is it."

Annabeth frowned. "What is?"

Eleni's voice trembled. "Judgment. Passage. The Tidefather has come to bear my soul to Elysium."

Percy took a full step back. "That's not—what?"

She stared at him with something like tragic joy, eyes shimmering with reverent sorrow. "They always said the worthy would be met by him. That in the moment between death and rest, the Greatest Son would walk beside the fallen and lead them home."

She closed her eyes and held out her hands like a supplicant awaiting anointed release. "My valor for Zeus. My oath to [[Olympus]]. Let that be enough. Let this hallucination—this final mercy—carry me to peace."

Thalia stepped forward, waving a hand in front of Eleni's face. "She thinks she's dead."

"Obviously," Annabeth muttered.

She looked up, dazed. "The light, your light, it refracts across time. Your divinity, it sings in the aether. I see the thine chains. The scars of Tartarus."

Percy stepped back. "You—what?"

Her gaze remained transfixed, wide and wet with tears. "It's true. Only the faithful can see. Only we who hold enough ichor in our veins can bear the vision." Her voice dropped to a whisper. "I was taught you would return clad in tide and flame. That your silence would judge the monsters and demons that plague us. That Olympus would rise again by your hand."

"You carry the pulse of storms beneath your skin," she interrupted, voice rising with conviction. "The One and Only of the Moon. They called you the Stormsire in the Old Hymns. The Wrath of the Deep. The Chosen Son."

Thalia raised a brow. "Okay, I kind of like that one. She's gotta pretty good naming sense. I kinda want to write these down somewhere."

Eleni's attention didn't waver. "I had heard your stories before [[The Great Expansion]]. I watched recordings of pantheons falling by your hand. They spoke of you. Of the One-Who-Turned-the-Titan's-Blade. I thought you were a dream," Eleni whispered, almost to herself. "A final mercy. But the truth is more blinding. You *walk* here*!*"

Annabeth leaned close to Percy. "If she starts building you a shrine, I'm setting it on fire."

Percy visibly flinched.

"The Undrowned. Moonfather."

Annabeth made a strangled sound, somewhere between a gag and a groan. "Ew! Gods, that's worse than Seaweed Brain."

Eleni didn't hear them. Or maybe she just didn't care. "They have murals," she whispered. "They described you: bare-footed, tridentless, crowned in salt. "

Percy buried his face in his hands. "I'm gonna throw myself into a volcano."

"Oh, and there was a shrine," Eleni continued, eyes bright. "A humble one! Only a mere hundred meters tall. Cast in lunar steel. The daily offerings included dew from comet petals and blood from the descendants of the Sea Lord."

Annabeth gave him a sideways look. "Tell me you didn't know about this."

"I swear on my *left shoe*," Percy said, staring into the middle distance, "I didn't authorize any comet petals."

Eleni clasped her hands. "Children would chant his name before training. '*He who fought Titans, who turned back Night!'* I had a mural of thine upon my ship. They never quite captured thine chin." Her gaze tilted toward him with awe. "It is far more noble in person."

Thalia leaned back against a tree, smirking. "I dunno. I kind of want to see these clay chins now."

Annabeth groaned louder. "She's fanficcing you in real time. We're witnessing a religious hallucination live. This cannot *possibly* be happening."

"I just wanted a normal day," Percy muttered. "Beach. Campfire. Maybe sword practice. Not a temple made out of crustacean poetry."

Eleni dropped to one knee again, hand outstretched. "You are real. Blessed Moon-Father. The One—"

"No more titles!" Percy shouted.

Annabeth jabbed a thumb at Eleni. "You hear that? No more titles. I'm already going to have nightmares about 'Moonfather.'"

Percy groaned again. "That one's gonna haunt me."

"Why? They spoke of the Moonborn's forbidden devotion to the Tidebearer. Of stolen glances across battlefields. The Lady of Luna, who once vowed to love no man, wept beneath a black eclipse when he was re-exiled into the Lands of our Birth, Gaia."

Percy blinked. *"Wait, what?"*

*He never remembered something like that before.*

Thalia raised a brow. "The Moonborn?"

Annabeth crossed her arms. "Please say that's not me."

Eleni wasn't listening. She was locked in the rhythm of recitation, eyes far away. "They called it the Silent Pact. She would not speak his name, lest the sky itself weep. And he—oh, he bore no love publicly. But they say his blade never struck true unless she had watched its arc."

"I don't even *know* what that means," Thalia muttered, having lost the conversation a long time ago.

"It's *poetry*, young Thunder-Sister, " Eleni said, gently, as if explaining color to the blind. "The Faithful understood."

Thalia snorted. "Yeah, the faithful and the completely deranged."

"They were immortalized together in marble on [[Caligo Vale]]," Eleni pressed on. "He—eyes like drowned starlight. She—her bow pointed toward the future they could never share."

Annabeth turned to Percy. "Are you in some weird sci-fi soap opera?"

Eleni didn't flinch. "The Hunters wore silver-green bands in remembrance of their love. They believed when he vanished from history, she carved his name into the surface of Luna Secundus with her arrows."

Percy rubbed his face. "Now I have to find that moon and scratch that off."

Thalia shrugged. "Could be worse. You could've married her. Gods knows she's a proper bitch"

"The Lord of Demigods did indeed marry the Huntress of the Moon."

They turned their eyes on him.

"I *haven't*!" Percy protested.

"Tell that to the marble slates," Annabeth said, pinching the bridge of her nose. "I swear, if I see a wedding mural—"

Eleni smiled, radiant and utterly convinced. "There is."

"..."

"I'm so cooked."

The satyrs avoided her like a wildfire. That's what Percy had noticed in the last three days of her stay.

She didn't complain. She barely spoke unless questioned.

But everyone noticed when birds stopped chirping in her presence.

And today, Thalia finally got bored.

They stood at the arena's edge, empty save for scorched sand and shattered training dummies. Grover sat on the stands pretending to eat a celery stalk, Annabeth paced nearby with a notebook, and Percy leaned against a pillar, expression unreadable. Chiron had assigned the unlikely squad to watch over the unknown variable for now, until someone from Olympus came down to look at her.

Thalia cracked her knuckles. "So. They say you were a battleship captain?"

Eleni, seated on a stone bench, nodded once. Her silver eyes no longer blazed, but the scarred lids around them still held light like polished steel. "Lord Admiral of the *Divine Crusader*, standard battleship of the Third Lance under the Steel Legions of the Imperiality of Zeus, first under Olympus. Seventy-five million crew. One-hundred-forty-eight hundred thousand primary weapons. Assigned to patrol Shipyard-Planet Fulguris while the Battlegroup underwent repairs due to Dionsyian-Zeus border conflicts."

Thalia grinned. "Sounds big. Bet you've still got a spark left. Show me something."

Percy looked up. "Thalia—"

"Oh, come on," she waved him off. "She's stable now. Mostly. Besides, if she's half as powerful as her fanclub poetry says, I wanna see it."

Annabeth didn't look up. "This is a *horrible, terrible idea*, Thals."

Eleni glanced at Percy. He realized she was looking at him for guidance. He reluctantly nodded.

Her body still bore damage: stiffness in the left leg, tremors in her right hand. But she moved like a machine—precise, intentional. "You wish to witness one of Zeus' Firstborn Million?"

"Yeah, sure," Thalia said. "Give me the light show."

Eleni raised a single hand. "Do not step within thirty feet of my Hammer."

Thalia took three steps back. Grover scooted behind the bench. Percy straightened.

The wind in the arena died.

Without armor, without chant, Eleni exhaled. Her fingertips brushed the air—and the world buckled.

The sand around her ignited in a ring of golden flame. Displacement. Pressure slammed outward, a thunderclap without sound. The clouds above camp split open in a spiral, air parting like it was afraid. Celestial script, old and angular, burned into the earth around her boots. Multicolored electricity cackled around the arena, arcing dangerously close to the spectators.

Trees surrounding the arena bent inward. The Big House's windows cracked.

Grover shrieked and grabbed onto a pillar. Annabeth dropped her pen and shielded her eyes. Thalia was already flat on the ground, teeth clenched, lightning arcing uselessly from her fingers and grounding into the dirt.

Percy didn't move.

The shockwave should've leveled him, but he reacted in time, conjuring a protective ice shield around himself. He was getting better at that, at least.

Eleni's eyes opened fully. They glowed with light and power. The surrounding air began to taste like copper, and the psychic emanations from the injured girl caused even him to bend slightly.

She blinked—and the storm vanished.

The sand cooled. The air returned. The clouds stitched shut like nothing had happened.

Thalia sat up, hair singed. "Holy shit."

"That was a localized invocation. Without armor. Or capacitors."

Eleni bowed her head. "That was two percent." She paused. "Of restored capacity."

Thalia turned to Percy. "And she worships you?"

She made no movement but remained perfectly still, one fist over her heart, head bowed. Not in weakness. In structure. In protocol.

Zeus leaned forward, in his crisp business suit with a tie that cracked faintly with lightning. His presence still bore power, but even he seemed to hesitate as he studied the figure before him.

"You are... [[Eleni Theodosia]]?," he said.

"Yes, Lord of the Sky." Her voice was level. Iron under velvet. "Daughter of the Storm, forged beneath your banner. One of the Firstborn Million sires of Zeus."

Zeus's gaze narrowed. "I do not remember siring you."

"I was not raised as a Sacred Hundred in your Dominion," she answered. "But your blood claims me regardless."

That was not a lie. But it left too much untouched.

Apollo whistled low. "No lies. She doesn't blink. Not even a twitch. You're sure she's not part automaton?"

"She carries ichor," Artemis said flatly. "She's no construct."

"She's… more than a girl," Poseidon muttered. "She's stronger than us."

Eleni said nothing.

Dionysus watched her with narrowed eyes, fingers tapping a slow rhythm against his throne. He still held a grudge with her from disturbing his midday nap with her little power show. [[Hera]]'s expression was a perfect mask. [[Hestia]], seated off to the side, watched with eyes soft but unblinking.

Only Athena studied her as if she were a puzzle yet to be solved.

Her fingers laced together. She tilted her head slightly, and the candlelight in the forum flickered.

"Your dialect," Athena said slowly, "is archaic, yet patterned with refinements I've never heard spoken aloud. Your references—your oaths—are structured. Formalized to the point of liturgy. I have only recently experienced something similar."

Eleni did not lift her eyes. "I was trained to address this court with respect beyond speech."

"Trained where?" Athena pressed, though her voice remained calm.

"Amongst Demigods."

Zeus frowned. "There are no demigods beyond the Americas."

Eleni did not respond.

And in her silence, the council shifted again.

"She is mine," Zeus said at last. Not a question.

No one argued.

"She is powerful," Artemis murmured.

"She is disciplined," Ares added, more begrudgingly.

"She is dangerous," Dionysus said, and let the word hang in the air.

Still, Eleni did not flinch.

"Rise," Zeus commanded.

"Swear yourself to Olympus," Zeus ordered.

Eleni met his eyes. For the first time.

Her gaze held neither challenge nor submission.

"I already have."

---

By mid-December, frost set in hard. Word from camp came in short bursts. Monster sightings, words from Tartarus, the kind of background noise no one could afford to ignore. When Eleni returned to camp that evening, she said nothing of what had been discussed. She retreated to her Cabin, while Thalia spent the majority of her time connecting with the demigods around camp.

Three days before Christmas, Percy was at Goode High, halfway through a geometry quiz, with Mariah Carey already playing in some teacher's classroom, when a shimmer caught in the corner of his vision. A rainbow ghosted under his desk, faint against the winter light—until Grover's face pushed through, far too close to the surface.

"Percy!" Grover whispered. "You've gotta—okay, just—don't freak out, but you need to come. Now. Westover Hall, Maine. It's bad. Like, reeeal bad." He gave a nervous bleat afterwards.

Percy glanced at the clock. "Grover, what—"

"Can't explain. I'm freezing, my feet are numb, and I think the vice principal suspects I'm not… you know… normal. Please just get here before the snow kills me or he does."

The image collapsed into frost. Percy stared at the fading rainbow for a beat longer.

When the bell rang, he stepped into the hallway and found Eleni already there. She moved through the crowd like a ship through still water. Untouched, unhurried. Her long coat concealed most of her armor, but nothing could disguise the exacting precision of her posture. Most of his classmates rushing to their next class only looked at her weird.

"High Lord," she said, voice low but perfectly clear above the chatter. "The satyr calls for you."

The hallway fell silent for half a second. Someone dropped a binder. Percy closed his eyes. Stacy by the lockers was giving him *Major* stink eye.

"It's Percy," he muttered.

She inclined her head. "As you will."

He slung his backpack over his shoulder and started toward the doors. Eleni fell in step to his right.

"You know where to go, Eleni?"

"Yes, milord."

"Piggybacking, yeah?"

"Yes."

Eleni produced a shard of the master bolt—a miniature, crackling replica no larger than her index finger. The fragment shimmered with condensed stormlight, hissing softly, a concentrated flare meant for a far reach.

She cast it forward with precise force, hurling it through the sky like a shot fired from a cannon, its arc cutting a brilliant trail across miles of empty air towards the place they were meant to be. Percy's eyes narrowed, tracking the fading pulse of divine energy, the faint signature embedded in the bolt's core.

As the bolt disappeared beyond the horizon, Eleni reached out and grasped Percy's forearm firmly. This was the harder part. Mist travel required extreme control over water, where you would have to deconstruct your own body into vapor and then zip it over to a compatible water source at the destination. Poseidon could do it constantly in his realm, but Percy still had to follow Eleni's trace to travel.

The world shattered into fractals. The cold bite of the air vanished, replaced by the whir of intangible gears and the hum of rain. Flesh, bone, armor, and spirit dissolved into streams of energy, carried by the beacon's divine signature.

Moments later, the two reassembled abruptly, materializing hidden among shadowed trees just a few kilometers away from Westover's boundary. Heart hammering like wild from the overuse, again reminding him that his heart was not his, Percy cast his gaze upward, trying to keep his pulse under control.

The sky above New England was heavy with frost and static. Clouds rolled across the horizon like bruises, hiding the moon's fullness behind a gauze of snow-laden mist. It was the kind of weather mortals cursed and hunters favored: cloaking movement, muting noise, giving chase to things that should not walk under moonlight.

Artemis stood at the edge of a spruce-lined ridge, her silver cloak curling around her boots in the wind. Below her, the land dipped sharply—an old glacial valley now repurposed by humans with concrete roads, brittle lamp posts, and, further ahead, the boxy silhouette of a boarding school that went by the name Westover Hall.

The wind brought scents. Rusted iron. Burnt motor oil. And something older. Mothballs and sand.

"They've passed here," she said, voice soft and cold as the snow.

Behind her, Zoë Nightshade nodded. She was silent, as always, waiting for the command that hadn't yet come. The other Hunters fanned out in a crescent around their Lady, bows slung and arrows silent in their quivers.

But Artemis did not move.

Not yet.

Her eyes were fixed on the distant school. On the shadows that danced too quickly beneath the parking lot lights. On the mortals within who knew nothing of the danger sliding past their defenses like oil through cracks.

"We're close," Zoë said at last.

"I know."

She should have felt anticipation. Satisfaction. The thrill of a worthy pursuit.

Instead, Artemis felt… a twinge.

There was a disturbance—something just on the edge of her godly awareness. A knot in the weave. A presence that kept brushing against the edges of her thoughts like a name half-remembered. She had dismissed it for days now, focusing instead on the monsters that had broken from old pacts and ancient slumbers to hunt anew.

But it clung to her still.

A boy.

A name.

**Perseus.**

The name tasted like sea-brine and thunder. And something else—something difficult. Bittersweet, if she allowed herself the mortal term.

She had met him before. Not here. Not yet. But somewhere else. A time not written in the stars nor carved into Olympus' records. She could feel it like phantom pressure behind her ribs. Like footsteps long faded from the moon's dust.

It made her wary. It made her curious. And, in ways she could not define, it made her afraid.

Not of him.

But of what he made her feel.

"Lady?" Zoë's voice was low, cautious. She had noticed the hesitation.

Artemis tore her eyes from the school.

"Send two to scout the perimeter," she said. "No contact unless provoked. I want confirmation that the [[Manticore]] is inside. He's cunning enough to mask his scent."

"And if he is?" Zoë asked.

Artemis let her fingers brush the fletching of an arrow. "Then we wait for the bait to arrive."

Zoë frowned. "You are certain the boy is coming?"

The boy. As if she didn't know his name. As if it didn't ring like a bell in the hidden chambers of her mind.

"He always finds trouble," Artemis murmured. "Or perhaps trouble finds him."

She didn't explain further. How could she? How could she say that she had dreamed of him these past few nights—not visions, not prophecies, but *dreams*? A concept so foreign to gods that even [[Hypnos]] barely knew how to gift them.

In those dreams, she saw flashes. A silver bow shattered in his hands. A battlefield stained in starlight and ash. And once, just once, his fingers reaching for hers through a curtain of fire, his voice breaking as he called her name—not as a goddess, but as Artemis.

She did not understand it. She could not permit it. She was a daughter of Zeus, born free and unclaimed. She had made her vow before the Titanomachy had ended. She had watched empires rise and fall with impassive eyes.

But this boy—this Perseus—he tangled threads that had no business meeting.

She drew a breath and looked to the clouds.

They would meet soon. And when they did, the moon would see all.

Let it see her now.

---

By the time her scouts returned, Artemis had already drawn a circle in the frost with her bow tip. A preparation. A prayer. A mark of stillness before the storm.

"He's inside," one of them said. "The Manticore. He has taken the guise of a faculty member. There are two demigods. Young. Likely unaware. One additional Greek signature, likely a satyr from the Half-Bloods."

"And the boy?" she asked.

"Jackson? Not inside."

Artemis closed her eyes. Felt the wind shift.

She could sense him now, like the tide beneath her skin. The boy was approaching, and the world would change around him, as it always did.

She felt something inside her twist—not quite pain. Not fear. Something close to longing.

Foolish.

But she would watch. She would not interfere unless necessary. She would ensure their mission succeeded, ensure the mortals were protected.

And then… then she would leave.

Because that was what she always did.

Because that was what goddesses did.

---

At the base of the ridge, hidden by pine and shadow, the Hunters prepared to move.

Artemis turned away from the school and looked toward the road where headlights would soon crest the hill.

She felt the words on her tongue before she could stop them.

"Be careful, Perseus," she said softly.

Zoë tilted her head. "My Lady?"

Artemis shook herself. "Nothing. We move at moonrise."

She did not look back at the school again.

But the name echoed in her mind like a dropped pebble in still water.

And for once, she did not try to silence it.

---

They moved swiftly but silently through the darkened woods. Only interrupted by Percy's breath coming in as ragged pulls; he hadn't built for this kind of endurance. His legs burned with every step, muscles long unfamiliar with such strain.

Eleni's gait was effortless—divine energy coursed through her, a steady pulse beneath the surface. Her armor, though scarred, hummed softly with embedded life support systems, compensating for fatigue and injury. Every step she took left faint sparks on the fallen leaves, which Percy was making sure to stay well away from. Those sparks hurt more than Thalia's slap when stepped on.

At the tree line near Westover Hall, Grover waved them over.

"About time," Grover muttered, eyeing Percy's flushed face. "You look like you just ran a marathon."

Percy wiped sweat from his brow, voice rough. "I don't work out."

---

The trio went through the same stuff as the last time Percy remembered it, misting through the teacher, joining the little party in the gym, before seeing Grover beckoning them outside, chasing after [[Dr. Thorn]] and the twins.

Without hesitation, they followed, pushing through the gym doors into the cold air outside. The chase was on.

The trio moved cautiously through the trees, the cold bite of the night air sharp against their skin. Percy's legs ached from the earlier hike, every step heavier than the last. Eleni stayed near him, her divine presence muted, her armor flickering with low energy as healing protocols held her in stasis. Grover darted ahead, alert and restless, scanning the shadows.

"Milord, multiple potentially hostile signatures, 800 meters northeast. Shall I?"

The Helicopter of Mercs? He didn't remember this part very well.

"Sure… Come back after you're done."

He turned back to Grover, who whispered, "He's moving fast. We've gotta catch him."

Bursting through the clearing, Percy found himself in the same spot he stood many years ago in his memories. Thorn's voice hissed through the wind, telling of The Great Stirring and dark plans.

Bianca looked panicked; Nico's naive, wide eyes that he had lost in the many later years of service for the pantheon.

Dr. Thorn paused his tangent and turned to the son of Poseidon. Arrogant smirk deepening, the sharp glint in his eyes cutting through the night. "So, the Sea Lord's spawn has come to play hero. How quaint."

Percy tightened his grip on Anaklusmos, his trusty blade feeling very.. off. "This ends tonight, Thorn. Let the twins go."

Thorn laughed, low and cruel. "End? No, boy. This is only the beginning. The Great Stirring will tear Olympus apart. And you? You're just a pawn, flailing in the tides you cannot control."

Grover shifted beside Percy, muscles tensed, ready to leap. "You won't win. Not while we stand." Percy barely managed to suppress a cringe at the cliche announcement.

Thorn's gaze flicked to the satyr, disdain curling his lips. "Ah, the goat-boy speaks. How touching. But futile."

Dr. Thorn suddenly whipped back to the twins.

"But they die first today!"

---

Minerva and Poseidon stood in the shadows cast by the many spires reaching into the sky. A light dust storm sent grit rattling against their war plate, which they ignored as easily as Athena had ignored Poseidon's offended protests about the abrupt trip from his domain.

One of Tartarus' key warzones stretched endlessly beneath them, a fractured hellscape of corroded steel and scarred earth where the waters of the Styx bled into poisoned marshes. The acrid taste of ozone and burnt circuitry filled the air, mixing with the salt tang of the sea god's presence.

The base's exterior was an angry scar on the land, fortified by brutalist spires and armored plating that had weathered countless assaults. Demigods and Legacies patrolled in disciplined silence, their weapons sheathed. These… Children, if you could call them that, with their grotesque fusion of flesh and divine energies, regarded the Olympian intruders with curiosity. Curiously nobody seemed to recognize the two Olympian gods in their midst.

From the shadows near an archway, a squad of twelve demigods blocked the path of the duo, their celestial-bronze-imperial-gold weaponry trained. Minerva stepped forward, "I am Min- Perseus Jackson, and I–"

"I don't think so, Sir." the captain brushed her off.

Minerva's eyes narrowed beneath her… his? helm. "The next time I am interrupted, I will kill one of you."

"We are not to be threatened, Athenian High Command needs all entry into the central command post to be authorized first, and you two, although lookin' fancy-shpansty enough, got none of em' official papers, capiche?"

"I will speak with the Lord-Commander, even if I have to yell up to the floors above."

The captain paused. Evidently unsure. They had no orders to stop such an action, and weren't sure how it would matter to their superiors, so they remained silent.

"Minnie…" Poseidon started from behind. "Must we bear this indignity from your counterpart?"

She sighed, but protocol had to be followed, to a degree.

"We might."

The captain snickered and cracked an ugly smile, pleased with his little victory.

*Nevermind.*

Minerva didn't blink.

"Kill them."

---

The command deck was a circular chamber, its raised dais occupied by the Lord Commander, an imposing figure known as Varric von Romhardt. Around him, tech-officers maintained their vigil, eyes flicking to the vast windows overlooking the warzone.

Minerva's voice, clear and precise, echoed through the chamber's speakers. "Lord Commander Varric. Attend to us."

Varric inclined his head slightly, acknowledging the AI's presence as one would a superior. "Minerva, your arrival honors this command. The *[[Répertoire du Savoir Athénien]]* acknowledges you and Poseidon walk here today. Tartarus can not fall while the gods themselves stand here."

He paused, before starting as if he remembered something. "How did such exalted individuals such as yourselves visit such a restricted zone?"

Poseidon coughed. Suddenly the walls looked like a very interesting view.

Minerva gave him a look, daring him to say a word.

"We had to… take an unconventional approach," she said flatly. "Lord Poseidon slipped a battleship past the heavy orbital works."

Varric's brow shot up to the sky. "A whole battleship? That vessel is now unaccounted for. No authorized transits recorded. Your arrival has triggered alarms across multiple sectors. The first of its kind, actually."

Minerva's tone was strange. "It will reappear."

Varric exhaled sharply, eyes narrowing. "Such actions court disaster, Milady. You know the consequences of breaching imperial security, even for gods such as thee."

"It will reappear."

"How?"

"It will reappear… there." She pointed outside the window.

Its name had been, in nobler years, *The Eye of Light*. A grand AnnChase-Class Battleship, 1,642nd of its make, constructed on the system-wide [[Forge of Hephaestus]] and granted to the 9th Olympian Reach's local defence force, designated AC-09-1642. It had been lost with all hands, captured by The [[Order of the Hollow Crescent]], thirty-two years before Minerva reappeared. Retrieved when the Heretics retreated from Luna just a few Gaian days before, she had managed to snag it from its scheduled inspection in Hephaestus' domain for her own use.

When a huge and shapeless amalgamation of scrap and flame came burning through the cloud cover above the fortified bunker-city, warning sirens sounded across the trenches. The squadron dogfighters in the air, all crackled warnings though their radios and their inability to engage.

The hulk was burning up already, and far out of their capability to damage with their colossal Howitzers, railguns, missile batteries, and long-barreled autocannons that lined the many miles of snaking trenches around their position. The wing of fighters broke away as the hulk burned through the sky. Thousands of soldiers manning the maze of tranches watched as the wreckage blazed its way overhead. The air itself shook with its passage, a palpable tremor from the thrum of overworked, dying engines.

Exactly twenty-seven seconds after what looked like a portable sun passing through no-mans-land, The Eye of Light ended its spaceborne life as it ploughed a new scar into Tartarus' war-torn skin. All of Tartarus shook to its foundations as the massive battleship wasted into the ground and carved a fiery canyon in its wake. It took a further seven Gaian minutes for the crippling damage inflicted by the impact to kill the gigantic, howling engines. Many still, furiously roared gaseous [[Hyperium]] and plasma as they tried to propel the vessel through the stars, unaware it was half-buried in the stinging Tartarean grit that would be its grave. But the engines failed. The flames cooled. At last, there was silence. The Eye of Light was dead, its bones strewn across the wastelands of the Land below the Underworld.

"The ship registers as The Eye of Light," An Augur read out from the wax-slate to the crowded war room. "A Heretic vessel, AnnChase-class, formerly belonging to–"

"Apollo," Poseidon cut him off. "I know that ship. My sons were with them at the end."

"What end?" asked Minerva.

"Many of Apollo's sons fell at the Final Battle in the War of Princes millennia ago. Their last Legions were annihilated by the Princes." Poseidon had closed his eyes and relished the momentary drift of focus into memory.

"The War of Princes. Olympus, it had been beautiful. No better war had ever been fought. The enemy was cruel, underhanded, and backed into a corner… utterly hated, utterly without right to fight back in the first place.. My Sons had tried to fight their way to join up with the last of their half-brothers, but the enemy tide was unrelenting in its ferocity. [[The Princes]] were cunning, their ships splitting the two armies and keeping them isolated from each other. Apollo's kids were there in full force. Apollo sent them there to show us that he actually had some power to prove other than songs and sex. I watched them at the very end. The last handful of Sun-Born, their bronze blades broken and their divine blood running empty, had screamed the Orchestras of Hate into space they shared with my Sons. Even as they fought in the halls of their own cruisers. Such a death! They chanted their bitter fury at the foes even as they were slain. I would never, could never, forget that final moment. A lone warrior, a blonde, imperial-gold-clad Legacy, wounded and on his knees beneath his Roman standard, keeping the banner proud and upright even as the attackers pierced him with their swords. The War-Banner would never be allowed to fall while one of the Suns yet lived. Such a moment. Such honour. Such glory! To inspire warriors to remember your deeds for the rest of their own lives, and to fight harder in the hopes of matching such a beautiful death."

Poseidon breathed out, restoring his senses to the present with irritated reluctance. How filthy this war was by comparison. Sending countless demigods to their doom, in an endless war against an endless tide of enemies. Having barely managed to scrape a few territories from this hellscape.

"Granted, this sacrifice will neutralize point-zero-nine percent of active Tartarus macroforms in this theater, some of the largest decreases seen ever." Minerva quietly mentioned to Varric, off to the side, while Posiedon leaned heavily against a railing, head down.

"I do not currently possess the authorization, but I highl–"

Flashing red lights and klaxons interrupted a startled Minerva as Athena's voice rolled across every trench, bunker, and hold in the Base.

**"Verdict. Accepted. Warzone Re-evaluation, CODE 78E. Every and all mortal combat assets initiate total assault. Penetrate the Tartarus front at Grid Delta, Sector Nineteen-F. Utilize Battleship AC-09-1642 as waypoint. All artillery, rotary, and missile batteries, continuous fire saturation ahead of advancing infantry. Full authorization granted for Heavy Armor brigades, breakthrough formation, bypass all secondary contacts. Ave Imperator."**

The war room stirred, officers exchanging glances, some startled, while the augurs stood bathed in a grim light, no doubt this was another suicide charge into the horde again. Athena's protocols had always been ruthless, but this was the first time in living memory she had ordered every breathing fighter into the line.

**"Clarification,"** the *Répertoire* continued, overriding murmurs. **"This will establish a permanent territorial wedge for the first time since the Second Grand Offensive. All auxiliary garrisons will reinforce from rear sectors. ETA: 15 hours. Good Luck."**

On the map, the push was already unfolding in accelerated simulation, thousands of units surging toward the breach, monstrous icons crumbling under rolling waves of fire and steel.

The quiet scruffles of augurs and officers rushing outside to join the fight slowly filled the room, and within seconds, only Varric and the Gods remained.

Varric stepped from the strategium's raised dais without ceremony, the brim of his campaign cap drawn low as he fastened it in place. The command floor was hollow now—rows of empty desks, abandoned screens still flickering with half-finished orders now overwritten by the Zeus' Golden Eagle. Every officer, every runner, every mortal who had stood here moments ago was already gone, swallowed by the tide now moving toward the breach Minerva had carved into the world.

The silence was not peace, it was the deep, unnatural stillness that follows the passing of a great mass of life, leaving only the air itself to bear the memory of movement.

Varric's boots sounded too loud on the decking as he passed beneath the tactical hololiths, their glow throwing his features into sharp relief. The Eye of Light's grave glared in burning orange on the map, surrounded by the widening arc of mortal advance, icons marching steadily toward their own erasure, some already getting swamped by the roving Hellhound packs.

He didn't look back at the gods, nor wait for acknowledgement. Beyond the Base's walls, the muffled, bone-deep roars of massed armor and infantry columns rolled in like a slow tide. The kind of sound that meant nothing short of the entire bastion was moving, that every last able body had been emptied into the storm.

The inhabitants of the room knew that this push damned him to die in futility, a number against a vastly larger sum.

"Lord-Command Varric von Romhardt. You know what waits beyond those gates. The uncountable horrors of the Underworld. A battle. One that will not be recorded in the annals of history. Just a movement of figurines at a war-table." Poseidon rumbled, raising his head to look at the departing Athenian demigod.

"I shall make a great toll in your name, Milord."

"..."

"Die well, nephew."
