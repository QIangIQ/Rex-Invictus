# Rex Invictus XIV

Percy noticed it immediately, though he could not have explained how. The air lacked that strange, guiding pressure he remembered from before, when corridors had seemed to lean subtly in his favor, when impossible turns had aligned just enough to suggest that [[Daedalus]]' creation did indeed like his offering from last time. Now the stone pressed in with a kind of passive resistance, which filled the normally composed demigod of [[Zeus]], with a small sense of claustrophobia.

"This place feels worse than you described, Milord. Or any of the texts I have read before from the Empire's archives" she remarked, stepping over a fracture in the floor that revealed a drop into indistinct darkness, pausing for a bit to watch a pebble descend quietly into the darkness.

Percy adjusted his grip on a bottle of water, though it remained capped. "That's because this time I might have forgotten to appease it just now."

*Good job, Varlet. And to think I was beginning to think you had forgotten the events of the weeks prior.*

"Sometimes I wonder if you actually like me."

*I tolerate you.*

"That's the closest thing to affection you've said all week."

*Do not become sentimental.*

"It's Minerva," he said, looking at the confused Demigod commander behind him. "She's being helpful."

Eleni gave him a flat look that managed to carry both skepticism and exasperation. "If by helpful you mean making commentary that only you can hear while we are navigating a structure that usually requires communication between all members, then yes, I'm sure she's invaluable, Milord."

*I like her,* Minerva said immediately*. She demonstrates pattern recognition. You should consider emulating that.*

Percy stepped over a section of floor that dipped slightly under his weight, then snapped back into place once he cleared it. "She says she likes you."

"That's concerning," Eleni replied, turning left at an intersection that had not been there a moment before. "[[Athena]], and by proxy, Minerva, tends to insult us when we are in their presence back in the Empire."

*Not immediately,* Minerva said*. I prefer to establish rapport before introducing criticism. It ensures the target is invested in the interaction.*

Percy rubbed his temple. "She says not yet."

"An honor for me, it seems."

They continued in silence for several minutes, though silence in the [[Labyrinth]] never meant absence of sound. Eleni slowed slightly. "You're sure this exit leads to Alaska, Milord?"

Percy nodded, though the certainty came from somewhere that was not entirely his own. "Yeah. Land beyond the gods, Minerva wanted to test my abilities without the coverage of [[Olympus]]."

*Assuming we reach the exit without the Labyrinth deciding to fold you into a decorative wall feature,* Minerva added*. Which remains a statistically viable outcome. It would be most inconvenient. Do try not to be one, Varlet.*

"Encouraging," Percy muttered.

Eleni glanced at him again. "She said something reassuring, I assume."

The corridor ahead narrowed abruptly, forcing them into single file. Percy moved slightly ahead, his senses sharpening as the air shifted again. There was a faint pull now, subtle but distinct, like a current beneath still water.

He paused.

"Wait."

*We are approaching an exit vector, M*inerva said, with a tone that suggested she was pleased with the outcome. *Probability has increased to eighty-nine percent, which I find acceptable given your tendency to complicate straightforward scenarios.*

Eleni looked ahead, eyes narrowing slightly as pale light began to bleed into the edges of the passage. "That looks real."

"Feels real too," Percy said, increasing his pace.

*Do try not to sprint blindly toward the unknown aperture, Varlet.* Minerva added. *It lacks originality and tends to produce suboptimal results. I have no objection to heroism in principle. I dislike your wasteful execution of it.*

"I've done worse and survived."

*I have already calculated seventeen superior alternatives to your present decision. You have selected none of them.*

Eleni caught up to him easily. "You're smiling."

"I'm not."

They reached the final stretch together.

Cold air rushed in.

Real air. Biting, freezing, stinging air. Enough to numb his clutched fist around a hastily conjured ice blade

Percy slowed only slightly before stepping through.

Eleni followed without hesitation, perhaps relieved to be free of that trapped, heavy space.

The transition was immediate.

Stone gave way to open sky, pale and vast, stretching over a landscape of sparse vegetation and distant elevation. The ground was solid, unmoving, entirely uninterested in rearranging itself beneath their feet.

Alaska.

"We're clear here. We can test everything without interference."

Eleni nodded, already shifting into a more focused stance. "Good. Then we start immediately. I want to see exactly how far you are above the rank-and-file like us, Milord."

*I am very interested in that as well,* Minerva added, her tone sharpening with anticipation. *There are several variables I have been unable to properly evaluate due to external pantheonic constraints, and I would very much like to rectify that limitation.*

Percy flexed his hand slightly, feeling the faint, ever-present current of something deeper than water, something that had been growing steadily more defined.

"Yeah," he said quietly. "So would I."

They continued, the labyrinth's hallways stretching, twisting, and folding around them. Faint echoes of shifting metal and humming energy followed them, a constant reminder of the maze's reactive nature.

After what felt like hours of careful navigation, they approached a final stretch. The corridors grew straighter, less cluttered with unpredictable folds. The air shifted again, colder, dryer, carrying faint hints of wind and the distant dry bite of snow.

Ione looked forward. "…I think we're about to exit."

Iane's constructs extended one last time, solidifying the path and distributing weight evenly across the floor. The storm-eyed demigod sensed the opening before they reached it. "External environment detected," he said. "Prepare for transition."

Merek squared his shoulders, armor compensating for the change in gravity as the corridor opened into an unbounded space. The walls of the labyrinth receded, folding inward until the backroom-like hallways vanished behind them, replaced by cold, bright air.

Neumond floated slightly ahead, panels adjusting rapidly to stabilize sensors. "Atmospheric readings consistent with subarctic biomes. Surface detected. Route complete in 12 hours on the nose."

The five of them stepped forward together, boots touching snow-laden ground for the first time in this leg of the journey. The labyrinth had given way to the untamed, white expanse beyond, the echoes of corridors and violet energy fading behind them. The Alaskan tundra stretched outward, endless and indifferent, awaiting their next move.

Fluorinated silica too, creating a shear-thickening suspension that would remain fluid under controlled conditions and solidify under sudden stress. It was the same thing in the heavily armored Ekdromoi's lungs.

Percy did not understand the chemistry, but followed Minerva's careful guide, pumping the slimy fluid down their throats and into their lungs where they replaced the need to breathe. After that, Percy injected them with carefully selected injector pens, a series of neurological dampeners, body-temperature and metabolic stabilizers, painkillers, and divine energy stabilizers.

**Tithe Day — [[Callistus]] Undercity, Sector Seven**

The notice had come on a Tuesday, stamped onto the tenement boards in Imperial red ink before the sun-lamps in the upper dome had finished their morning cycle:

**BY ORDER OF THE OFFICE OF THE TITHE — HOUSEHOLD LEVY, LEGIO XX VALERIA VITRIX — REPORT FOR ENROLLMENT, GATE NINE, 0600.**

The lights bathed the lower square with a brown yellow light, the steampunk architecture of the surrounding buildings only diffracted and scattered the beams into a nasty muted brown that pervaded the undercity. My brother's name was third on the list. Ezra. Nineteen years old, bad knee from the foundry line, no more soldier in him than there was in the pipes he welded twelve hours a day. By noon there were maybe two thousand of us outside the Tithe Office. By the time the light-cycle dimmed for evening shift-change, the crowd had swallowed three city blocks and every stairwell feeding into Founder's Plaza, and somebody — I never found out who — had a bullhorn and a voice like a man who'd been waiting his whole life to use one.

"They call it a levy!" he screamed, and the plaza screamed back. "You know what a levy is when they don't ask and you can't say no? It's got a different name where I'm from!"

"THEFT!" ten thousand throats answered him, and I was one of them.

I found my neighbor Petra near the fountain, her little boy on her hip even now, even in that crowd, because there was nowhere left in Sector Seven she trusted to leave him. "They took my cousin's boy off Meridian last cycle," she said, "Sent him to the front against… I don't even know what. Somewhere… I guess..."

"They'll stop when they see how many of us there are," I told her. I believed it, too, Gods help me. Ten thousand bodies in a plaza built for parades looks like power when you're standing inside it.

"They've got Tins for a reason, love," she said, and hitched her boy higher. "This isn't the first Tithe Day Callistus has thrown a party for."

Tins. That's what we called them, when we called them anything printable. Not to their faceplates, obviously. The old-timers called the street patrols "Buckets," on account of the Heavy Security helmets, and Medium Security were just "Meds". But Enforcers were Tins, you didn't get an Enforcer, you got a whole squall of them, chrome and black Quelled-Stygian wading out of the security stations like the buildings themselves had grown teeth.

The bullhorn man was still going. "Legio XX doesn't need volunteers because Legio XX doesn't have volunteers! You want to know how many of Precintator Arqbal's block came home from the last levy? You want to-"

I vaguely remember in the recordings, in the old family stories about the '09 grain riots, somebody always said the lights flickered first, like the planet itself was gathering breath. They didn't. The Grid doesn't need to gather breath. One moment the Tithe Office gates were just gates, and the next they were open, with them, a sound of what felt like the arc-forges of the lower factories.

Six of them came through first rank, and every one of them was built wrong for a human body, too broad through the shoulder, too smooth where a joint should show, matte-black plate scored with the small brutal dents of a hundred other riots on a hundred other Tithe Days, and the faceplates, Petra's boy started crying before I did.

"UNLAWFUL BLOCK-FODDER," the lead Tin said, and the Speaker made his voice sound like it was coming up through gravel and old pipe, "YOU ARE UNLICENSED. YOU ARE UNLAWFUL. YOU ARE, AS OF THIS MOMENT, CONSIGNED TO PUNISHMENT IN VIOLATION OF THE UNDYING LAWS OF THE EMPIRE, SECTION 12."

Somebody near me, I want to say it was the bullhorn man? But I'll never know, screamed back something about rights, about the Charter, about the promises stamped on the Founder's Arch a thousand years before any of us were born.

The Tin didn't even turn its head toward the voice. "BLOCK-FODDER. YOUR ATTENDED EDICT: GLOSSECTOMY."

Uh-oh. Nobody in that plaza knew what that was, but it sounded pretty scary. A crate went over near the Office steps. Somebody's cousin, somebody's husband, threw a length of pipe that had been holding up a market stall an hour before, and it hit a chestplate and bounced off ringing like a struck bell, and that ringing sound was the last ordinary noise I heard for a long while.

The six of them reached for their belts at the same instant, and the mauls came up in a single motion, like a machine finishing a stroke it had already started. Just a click, a shared click from six throats of steel at once, and then a low electric snarl as the heads of the batons woke up and started spitting blue along their length.

"FERALS," the line sergeant said, not shouting it so much as grinding it out, the way you'd grind a name into a lock you meant to break. "REPROBATES. WILFUL DEFACEMENT OF THE PSF. ATTENDED EDICT: MAULING."

Then they were in among us, and the plaza stopped being a plaza and became something with no shape at all, just bodies moving away from a center that kept getting smaller.

I lost sight of the bullhorn man in the first few seconds. I lost sight of Petra a few seconds after that, her boy's scream folding into every other scream until none of them belonged to anyone anymore. A maul came down somewhere to my left with a crack that had a wet sound underneath it, the arc from the electrified maul paralyzing the left side of my face alongside my upper body.

"LAWBREAKERS," another Tin roared, close enough that I felt the word more than heard it, felt it in my teeth. "THIS. IS THE WILL. OF THE EMPIRE."

Somewhere near the fountain a maul connected with a stretch of scaffold pipe and blew sparks in a fan wide enough to light three faces at once, and none of the three faces were smiling, and none of them were Tins.

I ran. I am not ashamed of that now, though I was for a long time. I ran with my hands over my head and my knees buckling every ten steps, and behind me the voices kept coming, flat and gravel-scraped and endless, cataloguing us as we fell.

"CHAFF."

"FOOT-SCUM."

I found the mouth of a service stairwell and threw myself down it three steps at a time until the plaza's light and sound thinned out above me into something almost survivable. I don't know how long I sat on the cold grating listening to the plaza empty itself out overhead. Long enough that my legs stopped shaking before my hands did.

Ezra's name is still third on a list somewhere. Petra's boy, I found out two days later, is safe, and Petra is not, and I have not yet worked out how to tell her boy that in a way he will understand before he is old enough to understand it anyway.

Percy knelt in the wreckage of the hillside for a long moment after the last injector pen hissed empty, doing nothing but breathing.

It hurt to breathe. That, at least, was familiar.

*Vitals stabilizing across all six subjects*, Minerva reported, her voice stripped of its usual dryness for once. *Your patients will survive the next hour. Whether they survive the next several depends on facilities neither of us currently possesses. I recommend a hospital. I am aware you do not have one. I recommend [[Camp Half-Blood]] instead, as the nearest facility with divine-grade medical staff.*

"Yeah." Percy's voice came out cracked, salt-rough. "Figured that part out myself, actually."

*I am delighted your cognition remains functional. Truly, a banner day.*

*Now. The Labyrinth.*

Percy exhaled and pressed two fingers against the snow-slick ground, feeling for that old, patient, half-sentient hostility he'd felt the moment he'd stepped in. Daedalus' creation did not forgive easily, and it had already made its opinion of him known once today.

He carved a shallow line across his palm — the same palm the QVARTUS round had punched clean through an hour ago, still tender, still faintly pink with new skin — and let it fall into the snow in a slow, deliberate line, murmuring something in a dialect of Greek that predated Athens by a few thousand years.

---

The Labyrinth received them differently this time. There was no maze to solve, no wrong turns waiting to swallow them whole, only a single corridor. Percy carried Eleni across his shoulders. Merek's dead weight in his cracked power armor took two trips and a length of rope improvised from a sliced parachute cord one of the Ekdromoi had, mercifully, been carrying.

It was on the second trip, dragging the twins between two makeshift slings, that the wall to his left flickered.

Brown-yellow brick, and the backs of three men in plate carriers and balaclavas sprinting flat-out across a brown brick fallout bunker, and behind them, gaining, unhurried, each stride eating the distance a person had no right to eating, a broad, heavy shape in a rust-streaked bull's-skull helmet, swinging a sledgehammer the size of a mailbox in one hand like it weighed nothing, bellowing something in Russian.

**"УБЕЙ УБЕЙ УБЕЙ!"**

*Fascinating,* Minerva said, entirely too calm.

"Should we—"

*No.*

"Poor guys."

*Correct. This is not our jurisdiction, our conflict, our century of relevance, or, notably, our problem. I shall request someone to deal with it in due time. Also, Varlet, you are currently carrying two unconscious enemies, hauling a score more, and bleeding from a hole in your palm that has reopened twice already. Prioritize. Though I will note that the man bearing the axe is wearing some variant of the Crye Prescision AVS plate carrier, which does seem out of place for a location such as this.*

The wall solidified back into ordinary, disinterested stone before he'd even finished the thought, and whatever was about to happen to those three men happened somewhere Percy would never know the ending of. He decided, on reflection, that he was fine with that.

"Add it to the list," he said instead, hauling the twins a little higher.

*The list of things you have decided are not your problem is extensive and growing, Varlet. I commend the restraint. I did not expect it from you, historically.*

"I contain multitudes."

*You contain a hole in your hand.*

---

The corridor spat them out into familiar ground — the low, pine-shadowed slope above the strawberry fields, a dirt covered daughter of [[Demeter]] quickly spotted them appearing from thin air.

"MEDIC! MEDICS, NOW, INCOMING WOUNDED—"

*Admissible.*

"High praise."

*Do not let it go to your head. There is very little room left in there for it.*

He tipped his head back, closed his eyes against the pale Long Island sun, and did the one thing that felt, after everything, entirely appropriate.

**"Phoibos Apollon. 'Blessed Symphony', 'Apostle of Fate', 'Undying Light', whatever you go by nowadays. I'm calling in family."**

**BANG.**

"Perce." Apollo dropped to a crouch, hands already glowing faintly gold, scanning him head to toe with the unhurried, laxity. "Cuz. You look like you got hit by a truck. Like the ones from the isekais I love watching."

"Missed you too."

"That's—" He looked up, sharp and sudden, sunglasses sliding down his nose. "Boy. Did you *fight*?"

"Fuuuuuck. Art is gonna murder me sideways. Could you please not do this to me?"

Percy peered over his blinding aura. "Those six gonna live?"

"Are you serious?"

Apollo suddenly stood up, and leaned slightly forward and thrust his chest out with his hands in front of him.

"My kids are absolutely tremendous, really. I raised the most confident, best children you'll ever meet. All my friends tell me my children are the best, the brightest! My kids are great. My cabin is enormous-est. It's clearly the best cabin to live in at camp. Maybe even the most beautifulest cabin in the world! Better than what [[Hermes]] does. Much better. Everyone's talking about it. You think you should doubt my sons? The Healers! The kids you brought in, they shouldn't die. It would be bad, very bad. Total disaster. Very sad. But they're gonna fix it. My great beautiful children can do what no one else can do! It's gonna look incredible. So incredible!"

"You, on the other hand, you're staying right here until I say otherwise. Those are the cousin's orders, the divine brother-in-law's orders."

"Seriously?!"

"Buddy. If unc P or Art even *smells* a rumor about your current state, half the camp would be crucified by sundown! And I'm not in the mood to regenerate my balls again."
