# Rex Invictus XIII

← [[Ch19 - Rex Invictus XII|Rex Invictus XII]] · [[Manuscript Index|Index]] · [[Ch21 - Rex Invictus XIV|Rex Invictus XIV]] →

[[Camp Half-Blood]] woke in layers.

First came the smell of smoke from the dying cookfire pits and the sharper, homelier scent of eggs, toasted bread, and burnt bacon from the pavilion. Then came the noise, demigods shouting across the green, armor buckles knocking against benches, the occasional pegasus screaming from somewhere near the stables like it had ran into the wall again. Then came the divine problem.

Namely, [[Artemis]].

She crossed the camp just after sunrise with the Hunt at her back, silver-clad and perfectly composed. Hunters moved around her in practiced silence, each one armed, alert, and faintly distrustful of the many boys and men making fools of themselves by noticing they were there.

And Percy, because the [[Fates]] had decided his life still lacked sufficient humiliation, was walking half a pace to Artemis's right in his newly established position as her Apostolos.

Which meant everyone was staring.

Not openly, because most people at camp possessed a stronger survival instinct than that, but enough that Percy could feel it. A fork paused in midair over a plate of pancakes. Somewhere near the [[Athena]] table, Annabeth sat very still with the expression of a girl trying to decide whether she was witnessing Percy's usual shenanigans, insanity, or the early symptoms of both.

Artemis, without glancing at him, said, "If you smirk one more time, Apostle, I shall assume you are enjoying this."

Percy folded one hand over his chest with a solemnity so fake it ought to have counted as a crime. "Dear lady, I am overwhelmed not by pleasure but by the incomparable weight of office. To be so publicly decorative this early in the morning is an affliction I would not wish upon lesser men."

*Lie still for two seconds, Varlet. I am rebalancing your vestment actuators around the damage you so generously accumulated during my departure. I will say, your recent eloquence is quite a foreign surprise.*

Several Hunters looked annoyed.

Artemis cut him a cool glance. "If decorative you so wish, decorative you so shall be. Be a good decoration and stay quiet."

"That is a most wounding thing to say, fair lady."

"You have survived Titans, monsters, and prophecy. I trust you shall survive honesty, hmm?"

"Of course, Milady. Forever and always."

That got a small, disgusted sound out of Thalia, who had been loitering near the pavilion entrance and now leaned against Percy with her arms crossed. "Did you two rehearse this?" she asked, "or did Annabeth's grammar rub off onto poor old Seaweed Brain?"

Percy brightened at once. "Lady Thalia. What joy. I was beginning to fear the camp might face this grim morning without a suitable quantity of criticism."

Thalia stared at him.

"What the fuck? See, this is exactly what I mean!"

Artemis moved past her into the pavilion. Percy followed, because that was technically his job and definitely not because it let him keep annoying her at close range. The pavilion quieted in ripples as they entered. [[Chiron]], standing near the central aisle with a mug of coffee, gave Percy a long look that suggested he was reserving his judgment. Artemis surveyed the room.

"My Hunters will breakfast here this morning," she said. "They leave again by midday. They will not be impeded."

A few campers nodded quickly.

Percy stepped smoothly into the silence with a little half-bow toward the nearest serving table, one hand extended as though he were presenting a ballroom rather than a camp mess hall.

"My dear and warlike ladies," he said to the Hunters, "might I recommend the strawberries before the [[Ares]] cabin notices them? Heroism has its place, but there is no virtue in contesting red fruit with those people before caffeine."

[[Clarisse]], from three tables over, looked up from her plate. "You got something to say, Jackson?"

He turned his head just enough to include her in the performance. "Only that the martial spirit of Ares has many admirable qualities, Clarisse. Restraint at breakfast has never been among them."

Some of the Ares kids laughed. Clarisse rolled her eyes and went back to eating.

Artemis, who had paused beside one of the long tables, said very quietly, "What are you doing here?"

"Public stewardship, dear lady."

Her eyes narrowed a fraction. "Sit down."

"With the greatest of pleasure, my lady."

He moved to sit a good distance from the increasingly irate silver goddess. Making sure not to be beside her. A Hunter barely older than twelve sat down two benches away and whispered to another, "Does he always talk like that?"

Phoebe shook her head.

"Pass the honey, Percy."

"At once. You see how briskly I obey, Chiron? Truly, I am the image of disciplined submission."

Chiron took a long sip of coffee. "I suspect that depends entirely on one's definition of discipline."

Percy passed the honey pot to Artemis. Their fingers did not touch. They came close enough that he felt, more than saw, the faint cool pressure of her divine aura shift and tighten for the smallest instant. A tiny thing. A thing no one else would have noticed. To Percy it might as well have been a hand at his wrist.

He forced himself to keep the smile light and frivolous.

Artemis, for her part, poured honey over her bread with the composure of an empress presiding over a military tribunal. "Eat," she told him.

"There is no need to sound so grim about it, you know."

Across the pavilion, [[Apollo]] chose that exact moment to appear.

It was not enough for him to enter like a normal god. No, naturally, he arrived in a blaze of light at the edge of the pavilion, wearing enough gold to bankrupt several city-states and smiling like he had personally invented cheerfulness.

"Well," Apollo said, taking in the room, Percy, Artemis, and the entire increasingly fragile arrangement. "This is already interesting."

Artemis's shoulders became even straighter.

"Good morrow, lord of the golden nuisance. How heartening that the morning has not deprived you of vanity."

Apollo clutched his chest. "Artemis, your little sea-prince is getting witty."

"He is getting insolent," Artemis said.

"Ah." Apollo grinned. "So he is feeling better."

Percy spread a hand. "Your concern restores me more thoroughly than nectar."

"I have that effect on people."

"Yes," Percy said. "Particularly on those trying very hard not to strangle you. It appears Milady is on my side this time."

A very audible snort came from one of the Hunters.

Apollo laughed, dropped onto the bench opposite them without invitation, and immediately started stealing fruit from a nearby plate. "So," he said, eyes dancing between them, "how goes this new and exquisitely awkward arrangement?"

Artemis answered first. "It goes."

Percy placed a hand over his heart. "I strive hourly to uphold the dignity of the office, though my lady's esteem remains, I confess, difficult to win."

Apollo looked delighted. "You gave him a title and now he sounds like me!"

"He chose that voice himself," Artemis said.

"An artistic triumph," Percy said. "I am a fascinated moth, fluttering near authority."

Artemis turned toward him at last, full attention, cool and sharp enough to make several people at neighboring tables suddenly become very interested in their food. "Perseus."

There was warning in it.

Also, under that warning, the smallest trace of strain.

He knew what it meant. Enough. So Percy bowed his head slightly. "As my lady commands."

Artemis's expression eased by a degree so small only someone looking for it would have seen. Percy saw it. Apollo saw it too, judging by the way his smile turned briefly thoughtful.

Breakfast went on. Conversation resumed in careful pieces. Campers relaxed. Hunters ate. Chiron resumed drinking coffee like a soldier behind siege walls.

And Percy, condemned to good manners, discovered that Artemis had nudged the plate of strawberries three inches closer to his elbow when she thought no one was looking.

He stared at them.

Then at her.

Artemis did not look back. "Eat," she said, in the exact same tone she might have used to order a squad to advance under arrow fire.

Percy took one, unable to stop the corner of his mouth from lifting.

A constant, low-pressure vibration went through the hull, making the assembled stormtroopers' teeth chatter uncomfortably when they stuck their face to the window to see the retreating view.

Mars was already gone from the rear viewport, reduced to a dim, rust-colored scar swallowed by distance. Ahead: nothing. Inside, five figures occupied the troop bay in loose formation, mag-locked boots occasionally clicking as the inertial dampers adjusted to something that wasn't quite conventional thrust.

"Alright," Ione said, tapping the side of his helmet. "Now, tell me why we have this gear again."

A pause.

"No," Iane said immediately.

"Sis, you have to realize most of this stuff is reserved for people who know what they're doing, right?"

"And that appears to be you and I, no?"

Across from them, a younger demigod, lean, dark hair tied back, light flickering faintly across his irises, rested one hand against the inner hull.

The metal around his palm warped slightly.

Air currents, impossible in the vacuum of space, spiraled around the dropship in tight, controlled vortices, feeding into something larger.

He exhaled slowly.

The ship surged forward.

"Feels like cheating," Ione said.

"Because it is, " the storm-eyed demigod replied. "There *is* a reason AtRep chose us."

Next to him, seated cross-legged and completely still, the [[Hecate]] legacy did not open her eyes.

Thin lines of dim violet light traced across the deck beneath her boots. Her voice came flat.

"You are not 'cheating.' You are forcing a localized spatial collapse—"

"I'm going faster," he cut in.

"Yes."

"So shut it. That's the part that matters."

A fourth member, broad-shouldered, Roman features, armor plating heavier than the rest—leaned back against the bulkhead, watching the interaction with mild disinterest.

"What's this stuff called?" he asked, lifting one arm slightly. The gauntlet segmented open along the knuckles, revealing layered bronze circuitry and something faintly glowing beneath.

"Power armor," Iane said.

He stared at it.

"…that's boring."

"Then don't ask."

The fifth—quiet until now—shifted slightly, adjusting the collar of a cloak that didn't quite behave like fabric. It lagged half a second behind movement, like it had its own opinion on positioning.

"It's all overdesigned," she said.

Ione perked up.

"Finally, someone with taste."

A thin line of heat traced itself along the floor.

The Hecate twins reacted, quickly raising their smoking boots from the surface of the rumbling ship. When the five looked down at the floor, they saw a few smoking inscriptions. Tiny fragments, runic structures seemed to have etched themselves into the deck plating, in small arrays of funny looking symbols. Ione opened his mouth.

"…It's doing the thing again."

"Because you were moving," the Hecate legacy said.

"I'm sitting."

"You shifted your weight."

"That counts?"

"Of course it does. Did you not read your equipment list, or the briefing?"

Then—

"So," Ione said, leaning forward slightly, "what's the actual name for these boots, sis?"

A pause.

Longer this time.

The Hecate legacy opened one eye.

"Hexenlaufgerät Erweiterte Thaumaturgische Bewegungsplattform für Runische Vorfeldkonstruktion und Kontinuierliche Glyphenvernetzungseinheit. Special issue from the upper command."

Ione stared at his sister.

"…these?"

She closed her eye again.

"Correct."

The storm-eyed demigod smirked faintly.

"While you were talking, we've basically arrived."

"How do you know?" the roman legacy asked.

He tilted his head, feeling something no one else could. "Neumond can feel the debris and machinery around [[Gaia]] right now. He's interfacing with navigation right now to get us into the atmosphere."

After a few tense minutes of jolting around as the armored orbital dropship maneuvered itself invisibly, dodging satellites and sensors, it eventually settled into a spot where AtRep believed it to be safe to drop.

The dropship held its impossible stillness for several seconds longer than necessary, suspended between frames of reference that only one of the five occupants could properly interpret. The rear ramp remained open, and the violent rush of high-altitude wind forced its way through the troop bay in a constant, grinding pressure that made even controlled breathing feel thinner than it should have been. Below them, the cloud layer stretched outward in an uninterrupted sheet of white, completely opaque and utterly indifferent, offering no hint of the terrain that waited beneath. Gaia existed somewhere below that surface, but from this altitude they might as well have been a concept rather than a place.

Ione stepped forward first, not out of urgency but because there was no practical reason to delay. Iane moved with him, their spacing aligning naturally as though the decision had already been made long before either of them physically acted. The subtle synchronization between them was not rehearsed in the moment; it was the result of repetition, of shared systems and shared instincts that had long since replaced the need for explicit coordination.

Merek watched the two of them approach the edge, visor angled downward toward the endless white expanse beneath. "You have the drop point sorted out?"

"Yes," Iane replied, her voice level and without hesitation.

"That tracks," Merek said, shifting his stance slightly as he prepared to follow.

Without further exchange, the twins stepped off the ramp together.

For a fraction of a second, their bodies were subject to nothing but gravity, falling cleanly into open air with no visible resistance or structure beneath them. Then the Hexenlaufgerät systems engaged, not as fragmented stepping constructs but as a unified projection. Lines of searing violet-white script erupted outward beneath their boots in expanding arcs, not forming discrete footholds but an interconnected surface that grew in real time as they descended.

The rune did not follow their motion; it preceded it. Each step did not merely create support but contributed to a larger, continuous construct that extended outward in concentric geometry. Lines intersected at precise angles, forming a massive circular array that suspended itself in the air as though it had always been there and had only now chosen to reveal itself. It suddenly flashed red-gold, and with a hollow ring reminiscent of a massive church bell, the magic circle came to be.

"Maintain alignment, brother." Iane said, her voice threading through the comms with controlled precision. "This is a shared construct. Any deviation will introduce instability across the entire lattice."

"I am not deviating," Ione replied, his cadence perfectly matched to hers.

"You are approaching threshold output on your third chain. My augur reports 5 Parzivals per second. You go above 6 and we'll become visible to gaian-age ELINT."

"It is within tolerance."

"It will not remain within tolerance if you continue feeding at that rate, idiot!"

The lines beneath them stabilized as Ione subtly reduced output, the rune responding immediately by redistributing load across the structure.

As the outer boundary of the construct completed, the air itself distorted in response. The effect was not visual in any conventional sense, but there was a palpable shift, a subtle resistance that had not been present moments before. Their descent changed accordingly. What had been acceleration became controlled drift, the massive circular array beneath them acting as an anti-gravitational field that resisted further increase in velocity without halting motion entirely.

Above and slightly offset from their position, the Poseidon-[[Zeus]] legacy, Straban, did not participate in the construct. His method of descent did not rely on inscription or external systems but on direct manipulation of the medium through which they moved. He stepped into open air as though stepping onto an unseen surface, and the space beneath his feet responded by compressing into localized pressure fields that held just long enough to support his weight.

He moved downward at an angle that did not correspond to any natural fall trajectory, hands resting casually at his sides as though the entire process required no particular effort.

"There are more efficient ways to achieve this," he said, his tone carrying a faint edge of amusement.

"If you can spare time to gerrymander, you can spend it clearing our drop spot," Iane replied without looking at him.

Merek stepped off the ramp without further delay, committing fully to a descent that relied on neither controlled constructs nor spatial manipulation of the three currently freefalling through the high altitude clouds. The power armor engaged immediately, internal systems compensating for the sudden shift in acceleration as inertial dampers absorbed the initial forces. He did not attempt to slow himself through any refined method, instead allowing the suit to manage the stress as he dropped through the cloud layer.

"If airboy doesn't wanna do it, I shall!" Merek smirked.

"And how would you do that?"

"I reach the ground before the ground reaches me in a way I do not like."

The four could only watch mutely as the clouds swallowed a hulking, giant humanoid hunk of divine steel with surprising silence.

Moments later, a sharp series of mechanical deployments cut through the air as the Neumond activated his descent system. The wings unfolded in layered segments from his back, each plate locking into position with audible precision. Micro-thrusters along the edges ignited in controlled bursts, stabilizing his fall into a managed glide.

His movement was neither as elegant as the Straban's nor as geometrically precise as the twins, but it was exact in its own way. Adjustments to pitch and roll occurred continuously, guided by onboard calculations that accounted for wind, altitude, and velocity in real time.

"Control surfaces responding within expected parameters," he reported, his voice steady and clinical.

"Nobody asked, buddy." Merek's voice came through faintly, the signal weak and tinny, no doubt from the environment.

"It remains relevant."

"To you."

"That is sufficient."

As they descended through the cloud layer, the transition from abstraction to environment occurred almost instantly. Visibility dropped to near zero, the world reduced to motion, pressure, and the faint glow of systems operating at the edge of their tolerances. Moisture condensed across armor and exposed surfaces, freezing and dissipating in rapid cycles as they passed through the densest portions of the atmosphere.

Then they broke through.

The mountains emerged with abrupt clarity, jagged ridgelines cutting through the air in sharp relief. Snow clung to exposed stone, while dense forests filled the valleys below in dark, unbroken stretches. The absence of human presence was immediately apparent, not as an observation but as a condition of the environment itself.

Merek struck the ground first, and the impact carried enough force to vaporize the stone beneath and around him. The power armor absorbed the majority of the stress, but the excess translated into the terrain, sending cracks radiating outward from the point of contact. He remained crouched for a moment as the suit recalibrated, then rose to full height.

The Hephaestian legacy descended next, reducing speed through a combination of thrust and aerodynamic adjustment before making contact with the ground in a controlled, sliding motion. The wings retracted in segments, folding back into their housing with mechanical precision.

"Landing confirmed," he said.

Above them, the twins completed their descent. The massive rune beneath them contracted in functional scope as they approached the ground, its outer edges dimming as the system reduced output. The structure held until the final moment, then collapsed cleanly as their boots made contact with solid stone, hissing into the now-sandy patch of the mountain.

The air distortion dissipated immediately, leaving only faint residual heat etched into the surface where the construct had existed.

The Straban arrived last, transitioning seamlessly from downward motion to a brief horizontal glide before allowing gravity to carry him the final distance. His landing produced no visible disturbance.

For several seconds, none of them spoke. The wind moved through the mountains in long, uninterrupted currents, carrying with it the cold, empty stillness of a place untouched by human presence.

Then the Hephaestian legacy extended his gauntlet, and thin scanning filaments deployed outward, barely visible as they pulsed against the surrounding terrain.

"Environmental scan complete," he said after a brief pause. "No human signatures detected within operational radius. Lucky, eh?"

"That *is* hella nice," Merek said.

The steppe stretched out in every direction like it had no intention of ending anytime soon, the wind rolling through the grass in slow, steady waves that didn't care about the five armored idiots standing in the middle of it. The dropship, cloaked, landed silently a few moments later behind them, metal contracting in quiet little pops, the only sign anything mechanical had been here at all.

Neumond detached first, of course.

The small drone slipped free from its mount and hovered up between them, panels shifting as it stabilized itself. A faint projection flickered into existence, pointing somewhere that didn't look any more important than anywhere else.

"Atmosphere consistent with pre-FTL Gaia data provided by AtRep."

Merek squinted out at the horizon, hands on his hips. "…so we're early."

"Correct."

"How early?"

"Indeterminate."

"…fantastic."

Ione shifted his stance, boot grinding lightly against the dirt. "So we just landed in the middle of buttfuck nowhere, I take it?"

"Pretty much," Neumond said.

"Good. I was worried we'd actually hit something important."

The storm-eyed demigod stood a little ahead of the rest, head tilted slightly like he was listening to something buried under the wind itself. After a second, he let out a quiet breath.

"…yeah, no. This isn't it."

Merek glanced over. "No shit, Genius. You tellin' me this ain't secretly Alaska or the 2 other spots we're supposed to recon?"

"No."

"Shame..."

The Hecate legacy stepped forward before that could spiral into something even dumber. The ground reacted immediately, thin magma-red lines hissing themselves into the charred sand beneath her boots.

"We're not walking," she said.

Merek looked down at the glowing script, then back up at her. "…already?"

"Yes."

"Could've at least pretended to walk for a bit."

"Hell no. Are you retarded?"

"Hey—" Merek threw his hands up slightly. "I've barely stretched my legs for hours sitting in that tin can. Let me enjoy this for a few seconds at least. This armor fucks with my stiff joints. Not all of us get the liberty of being not 8 feet tall, you know."

"Mind the way you talk to my ship, prick," Neumond cut in without even looking at him. "You've just been stealing all the rations from the mess hall again."

"That is unproven slander."

"You ate three days' worth of protein packs."

"I was hungry."

"You're always hungry."

"Yeah, and whose fault is that? You people keep putting me on missions instead of letting me sit somewhere and eat like a civilized human being."

"Didn't you poison yourself by eating the bait cheese on a mousetrap a few weeks back? You also managed to get a black eye from that too, if I remember correctly."

"Fuck you."

Iane only answered with the finger as she stepped into position beside her brother without missing a beat, the lines beneath them shifting as the second input locked in. The circular runic structure immediately stabilized, the symbols linking more tightly, the glow sharpening as the purple magic circle distributed the load properly.

"Maintain alignment," she said. "And try not to screw this up."

"I corrected."

"Because I told you to."

"I was already correcting."

"Sure you were."

Merek looked between them. "…do you two ever get tired of hearing yourselves?"

"No," they said at the same time.

"…yeah, that checks out. Freaks."

The array expanded outward in layered rings, not just a circle but something denser, overlapping geometry building on itself until the ground beneath them looked less like dirt and more like a blueprint someone forgot to turn off.

Heat started to rise off it.

The air shifted.

The Hecate legacies didn't wait.

"*This land is not of mine, and I shall feed the thirsty earth with my flesh."*

The lines flared brighter, light running along every symbol like current through a circuit.

"*And everything that walks upon this land shall be my ally."*

At the center, a shimmering blue Delta symbol materialized, hovering a few inches above the ground. The 5 stepped in without hesitation.

The moment the last of them crossed, the entire construct collapsed behind them, the steppe snapping back to normal like nothing had happened.

Neumond hovered ahead, the 4 legacies behind him were craning their necks to look at the rapidly shifting projection constantly adjusting as it tried to make sense of something that didn't really want to be mapped.

"Route established," it said. "Proceeding toward Arctic region Obscurus One."

Merek rolled his shoulders slightly as he walked. "Based on what?"

"Predictive… reconstruction…"

"You have no idea, do you?"

They moved forward anyway, boots hitting the ground in a rhythm that didn't quite match distance. The storm-eyed demigod kept the lead, not slowing, not hesitating.

After a bit, Ione spoke up again.

"So. Alaska Hill."

"Huh?" Merek said. "Is that where we goin'?."

"In the Mid-2030s," Iane continued continued for her twin. "Our Lords and Ladies began pressuring the government to expand aggressively North, which is what caused the Arctic Resource War. "

"It was…" Neumond said. "Gold deposits, wasn't it?"

"Partially correct. They needed the old Gaian region named "Alaska" to construct the first real version of AtRep apparently. The natural cold, and free land, became a natural choice. The gold was there simply as a bonus."

"Huh."

Neumond's HUD pulsed faintly, but he turned around to talk to the small group, "Environmental conditions in the Arctic region reduce system strain. Ambient temperatures below negative forty degrees significantly decrease cooling requirements for large-scale computational arrays, which is what our Lords and Ladies needed at that time. They masked it as security from the Slavic peoples of northern Eurasia."

"Translation," Ione said, "cold place good for big brain machine."

"Correct."

"And also," Merek added, "that whole 'land beyond the gods' thing."

"We might be suppressed slightly," Neumond confirmed.

"…so we're going somewhere our powers don't work properly."

"No. We have packed enough Divine Stimulants and drugs to keep us running hot for the next week or so. Granted, you won't need to sweat, shit, eat, drink, or blink for the next few months but hey, no power tank right?"

"Fuuuuck"

"That's the spirit."

The shuttle drifted high in the poisoned skies of ancient Earth, its battered hull half-hidden inside a field of distortion noise while it threaded the outer blind spots of Sol's orbital defense lattice. Even thirty thousand years after the rise of the [[Undying Olympian Empire]], the cradle of humanity remained one of the most heavily fortified star systems in the galaxy. Thousands of automated defense platforms hung in distant orbit like patient predators, their sensor cones sweeping endlessly through the void, waiting for the smallest unauthorized signal to betray an intruder.

Octavia's ship survived there only by being too insignificant to notice.

That, and by being quiet.

Inside the cockpit, the atmosphere was considerably less quiet.

"You son of a bitch! We don't even have enough drachmae to live on, and you want us to come all the way to Gaia for a treasure hunt? Did someone shove a grenade up your ass, you bastard?"

Octavia's voice thundered through the cramped interior of the spacecraft as a datapad flew past Asimov's head and shattered against the bulkhead. The former Hunter of Artemis leaned forward over the pilot's console, her silver hair tied back in a rough knot and her expression twisted into the sort of fury that only someone living permanently on the edge of bankruptcy could produce.

Asimov ducked instinctively.

"Sis! Please believe me! This is a really reliable source! One of the [[Evergreen Family]] branch managers personally hacked the AtRep system! I told you he and I are childhood friends, didn't I?"

"You're spewing bullshit mixed with horseshit, you dimwitted moron! Oh, you poor, stupid fool. Can't you see that bastard's just playing you? You're the biggest sucker in the universe, I swear! Use your fucking common sense! It's been over thirty millennia since we ventured into space. Do you think no one's been sniffing around Gaia for anything valuable all this time? Anything worth a damn was stripped or nuked clean ages ago, you idiot!"

Earth.

Gaia.

The birthplace of gods and monsters alike.

Now it was nothing more than a graveyard.

For thousands of years the planet had been drenched in radiation storms, biological horrors, and the lingering scars of ancient wars. Entire continents had been erased. Cities had been reduced to layers of vitrified glass and toxic dust. The Empire kept the system under heavy quarantine, partly for security, partly because no one particularly wanted to deal with whatever still lived down there.

Treasure hunts on in the Sol System were a joke among bounty hunters.

Which was exactly why Octavia was so furious.

For once she had believed Asimov might have found information about a real target, some high-profile traitor or cult leader worth serious money.

Instead she had been dragged halfway across the galaxy to dig around in radioactive ruins because of a rumor about the Empire's "greatest weapon."

Octavia leaned forward and began repeatedly knocking her forehead against the control panel.

Beside her, the girl who looked barely fifteen quietly reached out and stopped her.

"Big sis," Lauren said calmly, her voice as smooth and polite as ever, "unless you want to pay for the spaceship repairs out of your own pocket, please calm down."

Octavia turned her head slowly.

"Lauren, aren't you fucking pissed off too? We're barely scraping by, and now we're in this backwater for what? All because this son of a bitch said there was a treasure somewhere! Fuck, the gate usage bribes alone!"

Lauren tilted her head slightly.

"Honestly, we're tight on money because of the property damage you caused. And we can just take the expenses out of Asimov's savings, so it doesn't matter. You don't mind, right, Asimov?"

Asimov froze.

"…Why are you touching my wedding savings… Ah, no. Do as you please."

Lauren smiled faintly.

Asimov immediately changed his tone.

Getting punched by Octavia was survivable.

Getting on Lauren's bad side was not.

The android might look like a polite young girl, but every bounty hunter in three sectors knew that messing with her would leave you homeless in a matter of minutes.

Asimov had seen her dismantle entire corporate shell companies just because someone refused to pay her commission.

"Fuck," Octavia muttered, leaning back in her chair. "If I'd known it'd be like this, I should've ignored this idiot and gone to catch that perverted bitch with a dick. How much was the bounty on her head again?"

Asimov tapped his fingers on the console thoughtfully.

"…But still, if it came from the AtRep central database, wouldn't there be a possibility?"

"This fucking idiot is still at it!"

Smack.

Octavia's palm slammed into the back of Asimov's skull.

He pitched forward across the control board.

"You're still talking about possibilities after hearing that bullshit about the universe's greatest weapon? Fuck, I'm speechless. I really pity the woman who promised to marry a blockhead like you."

Asimov groaned and slowly pushed himself upright.

"Ugh… Let's at least take a look with the scanning equipment we brought. We went through the trouble of renting top-grade equipment from the Hadrian Harmonic bigheads."

Octavia stared at him.

She sometimes wondered why she kept him around.

Then again, idiots had a strange tendency to stumble onto useful information.

"Fine," she muttered. "Do whatever you want. I guess we'll just consider this bad luck. So where did your amazing source say this universe's weapon was?"

Asimov brightened immediately.

"It should be around 27.99, 86.92… I'll start the scan."

Octavia ignored him and leaned back in her seat.

Outside the viewing canopy, the surface of Earth stretched below them like a scarred corpse.

The terrain was jagged and broken, mountains shattered into twisted ridges and craters carved deep into the planet's crust. In the distance one enormous impact basin dominated the landscape like the mark of a god's fist.

She rested her chin on her hand.

In a place like this, there wouldn't be any trace left of human life.

So what the hell was this universe's weapon nonsense?

They were already short on drachmae, and now she was illegally digging around in a system bristling with very angry orbital defenses.

She must be the universe's biggest fool.

Fucking Asimov.

"Asimov," Octavia said suddenly without turning around, "why are you running a human signature scan?"

"Just trying to be thorough since we're already here."

"Do you really want to die? Did you lose your mind because I let you off once? We came to find a weapon. What the hell are you doing a human life scan for? Do you think the weapon would grow legs and run around?"

Octavia stood up slowly.

Give him an inch, and he takes a mile.

What the hell would a lifeform scan detect in a wasteland where everything had been killed by radiation thousands of years ago?

Cockroaches the size of your palm.

And things worse than cockroaches.

Her hand was already reaching for her sword when Lauren's voice spoke quietly from behind.

"…Oh."

Octavia turned with murder in her eyes.

Lauren's expression had changed.

"We've got something."

"What?"

"A response."

"…What?"

Octavia blinked.

Asimov jumped to his feet.

"See! I told you so, didn't I?!"

"Shut the fuck up," Octavia snapped instantly. "We came to find a weapon, so why is a life form scan picking something up instead of an electromagnetic scan? Lauren, are you sure you didn't set the wrong option? It's not a cockroach, is it?"

Lauren shook her head.

"No. It's a human-sized response."

"It's not a mutant cockroach that grew to human size after drinking radiation, is it?"

"How would I know?" Lauren replied calmly. "It could even be a wanted criminal deliberately hiding on Gaia."

Octavia slowly turned toward the screen again.

---

That would be profitable.

She leaned forward.

"Where's the location? Let's run a thermal scan first."

Lauren studied the readings.

"…It's a bit strange. It's underground."

"Underground?"

Octavia grabbed the panel and pulled it closer.

The scan clearly showed the signal originating beneath the surface.

"Damn," she muttered. "Is it really a mutant cockroach?"

Lauren placed her finger on the console.

"I'll run a spatial scan first."

A few seconds passed.

"Spatial scan complete," Lauren said. "Big sis, this doesn't look like a cockroach. It seems more like a person."

"Huh? Really?"

"Yes. The scan shows a space that looks like a man-made structure underground. There's even an entrance to the surface. This area seems to have lower radiation levels, so something might have managed to survive somehow."

Lauren's voice softened slightly.

"Although the lifeforce readings are extremely weak."

Octavia's eyes lit up.

"…Lauren," she said slowly, "we might have hit the jackpot."

Someone hiding underground on Earth.

Alive.

In a radiation wasteland.

That wasn't normal.

That was a bounty.

And not just any bounty.

A big one.

Her heart began beating faster.

"What should I do now?"

There were two options.

Report it to the Empire and collect the reporting reward.

Or capture the target herself and collect the full bounty.

Low risk.

Low return.

High risk.

High return.

Octavia grinned.

"There's no need to even think about it."

"Lauren," she said, grabbing her descent pack, "let's try to catch this one ourselves."

"Directly?" Lauren asked, "Wouldn't it be better to report it to the Empire?"

"We won't know if it's really a wanted criminal until we check it out. What if it's an unofficial criminal that the Empire doesn't recognize? We'd be doing all the work for nothing."

Money was what mattered.

Whether it was a cultist, a traitor, or some insane extra-pantheonic fanatic, if someone paid for them, that was enough.

Octavia strapped the radiation shield to her waist.

"Hey," she said, pointing at Asimov, "we still have some radiation shields left, right? Get two out. No, wait. Get three, including one for that guy."

"Huh? Am I coming too, sis?"

"Of course you are," Octavia said. "What, should Lauren go instead? She's a teenage girl who doesn't even have divine blood. At least you can use some power, even if you're second-rate."

"I'm like… a third generation legacy."

"Who the fuck asked you to fight? You're assisting. Shut up and follow me."

A moment later the rear hatch of the ship opened.

Radiation clouds churned far below them like a boiling sea.

Octavia grinned.

"What a fantastic view."

Asimov peered down nervously.

"Can't we just go down in the spaceship? If you get struck by lightning while descending we won't even find your corpse."

"Are you going to pay for the spaceship's rad-shields?"

"…No."

"Then jump."

Asimov activated his shield and leapt into the storm.

Octavia sighed and stepped toward the hatch.

Then paused.

"By the way, Lauren."

"Yes, big sis."

"What's the name of this area? Just curious."

Lauren searched the ancient Earth map database.

"Hold on… The coordinates are… Ah. Found it."

Octavia jumped.

Lauren's voice crackled in her ear.

"It's called Mount Everest."

Hours later—

Octavia landed among the ruins.

And immediately realized something was wrong.

Very wrong.

"Holy shit."

She stared ahead in disbelief.

Because the battlefield in front of her made absolutely no sense.

Dozens of soldiers lay scattered across the broken ground.

Their armor bore the insignia of the Undying Olympian Empire.

They had been defeated.

By one person.

"This is truly lamentable," the figure said calmly, standing among the fallen. "To think that third-rate fools with no understanding of the divine dare to discuss power while relying on bizarre contraptions."

Octavia blinked.

Because the figure speaking…

…looked like a woman.

"How dare you lowlifes," the woman continued, her voice filled with cold disdain, "who haven't even mastered the basics of your own power, attempt to lay hands on my esteemed self?"

One of the wounded Roman legacies tried to stand.

"Urgh… A mortal dares to lay hands on a soldier of the Undying Olympian Empire? Whatever trick you used, you're making a grave mistake today, young hero."

The woman paused.

"…Olympian Empire, you say."

Then the world changed.

A shockwave of power exploded outward.

Octavia's instincts screamed.

Her entire body went cold.

The woman's hair lifted as if caught in an invisible storm. Pale blue fire ignited in her eyes. The air itself trembled with the pressure of something that felt less like a person and more like a natural disaster given human form.

Even Octavia—once a Hunter of Artemis—felt her knees weaken.

The Roman legacy swallowed.

"W-Who might you be, ma'am?"

The woman considered the question.

Then smiled slightly.

"What use is there in telling you?" she said lightly.

Her voice carried a faint trace of amusement.

"I am Bianca Di'Angelo."
