# Rex Invictus VI

The camp meeting was held in the pavilion. Long oval tables filled the marble space, but the usual chatter was absent. Percy, seated alone, was at the [[Poseidon]] table, Tyson noticeably not present, having not yet met him.

"Campers," [[Chiron]] began, "We are in danger."

"It's obvious someone is targeting us," Chiron continued. "We suspect it is the forces of [[Kronos]], the Titan."

"So what do we do?" [[Clarisse]] barked from the [[Ares]] table. Her armor still smoked charcoal black from the recent fight from the Colchis bull outside. "We go after them, right? Smash whoever's behind it."

"There's nothing to thump," [[Dionysus]] said, annoyed. "And I would remind you, niece, this isn't a sanctioned quest. For now, at least."

Heads turned. Annabeth spoke, her voice sharp. "We can't just sit here while the tree dies. Without the barrier, we're finished. Monsters will flood in."

Percy knew this part. He raised his hand. "There is a way to fix it. Isn't there?"

Chiron nodded slowly. "The [[Golden Fleece]]. It's said to restore life to anything it touches. If we could retrieve it. It's on Polyphemus' island. The Cyclops guards it like treasure. And… it's in the [[Sea of Monsters]]."

Now voices rose in alarm. The Sea of Monsters was not a place anyone volunteered to enter.

"So we get the Fleece, save the tree," Clarisse said. "Why are we even talking? Let's go!"

"I'm afraid it's not that simple," Chiron said. "The Oracle must approve the quest. And…"

He paused.

"I'm being dismissed as camp director."

A stunned silence fell.

Huh, he didn't think [[Tantalus]] would reappear this time around.

"Due to the recent incidents," Dionysus said, smug, "the Council of [[Olympus]] has decided to replace Chiron with a new director. Someone… stricter."

"Thank you, Milord," Tantalus said, voice oily. "I do so hate these sentimental farewells. Now—let's talk about quests."

He snapped his fingers, and a scroll appeared. "I am the temporary voice of The Council in this camp, and I have approved one quest. One camper. And after reviewing the candidates, I have chosen…"

He paused for effect.

"Clarisse."

It was strange, being in command.

Minerva let her ships drift slowly towards the main palace at the center of the system, and busied herself with some less important matters.

The food supplies for the entire crew. How much they had. Where the food is stored. The vulnerability of the holds. What weapons they can withstand. How they appear from any direction of space. Food ration planning. Unsustainable food ration planning,

casualties. Which decks are the riots likely to break out once starvation is a reality.

Water centers. How many are required to be fully operational in order to supply the entire sailor population.

Lists of medical facilities. Medical supply stocks are constantly rechecked.

Marines, for planetside landings. Total available weapon supplies. Ammunition supplies. Projections for how long those arms will last. The individual gun crews for each battleship. Who leads the individual loading teams. Their security, their fitness. Their proximity and response times.

Air (or more usually, space) assets. Landing strips, backups. The access to and from these sites. Air superiority. The numbers of light and heavy skirmishes and dogfighters, individual pilot alterations to their vessel. Bombers flight readiness, accuracy, repair speed, and speed estimates. The records of every pilot and officer among the ship.

Automated defense emplacements. What anti-air turrets she can control, and which ones are best left to the crew. Where they are placed, blind spots, optimal firing arcs. Calibers, firerates, projective velocities, cooling cycles, belt size, special ammunition needed? Resupply speed, their ammunition amounts, and from where that ammunition comes. Automated transportation?

The engines themselves. Their only way of transport in the vastness of space. [[Hyperium]] Fuel consumptions, max speed, ignition speeds. Power drain balanced with shields, turn rate, durability.

Shields themselves. Do they redirect with anti-kinetic shields at a loss of power? Can she afford to calculate and teleport away every ballistic projectile coming towards her with void shields?

On and on and on.

She relishes in the challenge for nine days.

Nine. Days.

Of course, it had to come to an end. She descended to the surface of Orbital Platform One, Where the Sea god was waiting.

The cabin door shut with a soft *thunk* behind Annabeth as she entered. The lights inside were already dimmed, the air buzzing with low murmurs and the scratch of quills on parchment. Dozens of her siblings glanced up, some still hunched over project layouts or random work.

"Meeting," she said. "Everyone, now."

They gathered quickly. [[Athena]] campers didn't waste time.

The Athena cabin was quiet, too quiet for a place known for constant planning. Annabeth stood at the center of the room, arms crossed, brow furrowed.

"She picked *Clarisse,*" she said at last. "Tantalus picked *Clarisse*."

One of the older Athena boys, Malcolm, leaned forward on a scroll-stuffed desk. "The Oracle didn't even speak."

"No quest announcement, no prophecy," muttered Julia from a top bunk. "Tantalus just rolled in, threw around authority like he owned the place, and now Clarisse gets the Fleece?"

Junior, sitting near the war map, snorted. "He picked her because he knew no one would object. No one can object. Not when the Council backs him."

"And Chiron's been dismissed," Malcolm added grimly. "Somehow *Mr. D* looked smug about it. Thought they tolerated each other."

Annabeth didn't speak. Her gaze was on the window, watching torchlight flicker in the woods.

Finally, she said, "Percy should've been chosen. Especially in the *Sea* of Monsters."

"He's untested," Junior replied. "Technically. He hasn't finished a full year at camp."

"Luke then?"

"Traitor, apparently, if Percy is right. He hasn't been wrong really."

She put her head into her hands. "Look, whether you like it or not, we can't do shit with what we have currently. Luke, Clarisse, and more recently, Percy, represent our strongest fighters in the whole camp. The rest of us are more like normal people with useful abilities."

No one spoke for a moment. Then Julia, thoughtful, said, "He's also... been different since returning. You've noticed it too. Lady [[Artemis]] might have done a number on him"

"Did you hear the screams? Something changed in him, that's for sure." Annabeth said.

"He's distracted," Callista replied. "Sometimes talks like he knows things we haven't told him yet. Last week, I caught him finishing my sentence before I started it."

"He's possessed," Malcolm said quickly. "Mr. D said so."

Annabeth hesitated, then nodded. "He mentioned someone named 'Minerva'. That's *Roman*. Not Greek. With how she spoke to us, probably some sort of malignant spirit of some kind. Either that, or he's managed to give us all collective schizophrenia at the ripe old age of 16."

Annabeth continued, "Or maybe it's something else. Either way, I want to know what he remembers. If he remembers anything at all."

"And you think Clarisse might fumble the quest," Thersites said.

"I *know* she will. And when she fails, Thalia's tree dies. We die."

"So what are you suggesting?" Malcolm asked, voice cautious.

Annabeth didn't answer right away. She picked up a pencil and drew a small X on the camp map. The Poseidon cabin. Then she drew a line to their own.

"We don't have the quest. We don't have Chiron. We don't even have accurate maps of the Bermuda triangle. But we have Percy. I want to know what he knows. I want to know why he acts like he's seen the future. And I want to know who this... *Minerva* is. Because something tells me the answers to all of those might save our lives."

The silver corridors of Luna's orbital platforms hummed with the chilly cold of its atmospheric systems. Artificial gravity, unable to fully replicate that of [[Gaia]]'s, made each bootfall feel like stepping in snow.

Sylvi Morn adjusted the clasp on her combat harness, chewing her cheek. "She's really here?"

Kaia Telan nodded, eyes still wide. "Confirmed. Direct transit signature. No decoys. No proxies. It's actually... her."

Sylvi whistled low. "Why? We're just doing border sweeps. There's been no breach, no distress calls."

The two young Hunters were slowly walking near the eastern wing of the bastion, idly doing their rounds around the base. They both stumbled simultaneously into a stop when they saw their Mistress.

Lady Artemis was almost exactly like how she had been described in the old epics. The silver gossamer tunic, covered by numerous pouches and gear. Her silver eyes tracked her surroundings. Her bow, which entire generations of Hunters have not even seen, was slung on her back.

Behind her strode titans of legend, though here, they walked as if weighed by centuries.

Thalia Grace still bore her signature defiance. Her armor bristled with capacitors and wires, trailing across her whole body. Periodically, some would pop and fizz, arcing bolts of lighting to her surroundings.

The other Hunters who accompanied their goddess kept their distance, but they all looked to be far more senior than anyone else on Luna.

Sylvi leaned toward Kaia and whispered, "Do you think something happened? Forming a real gods-damned warband?"

Kaia didn't answer. Her breath hitched.

Because Artemis stopped.

Right in front of them.

No words passed. She looked at them both, just long enough for the room to feel... *too small*. And then she spoke.

"Watch carefully," Artemis said, eyes sweeping through them like they were transparent. "Even the smallest stone may bruise the skin of a god."

And then she was gone. Down the corridor. Into an elevator.

The doors sealed shut behind the Goddess and Thalia, and the rest of the hunters fanned out in the hallway taking up positions around the hall.

Sylvi exhaled a breath she didn't realize she'd been holding.

Kaia blinked, throat dry. "What the hell is happening?"

Sylvi shook her head slowly. "I don't know. But if the Lady herself is here, *if all of them are,* then it's not just another day."

She looked toward the sealed sanctum.

Thalia Grace stood in silence beside her Lady.

They stood side by side, in the small elevator, rapidly shooting down into the center of the moon.

When the door opened at the core, her breath hitched.

She'd seen many things in the millennia since she'd become immortal, but nothing had quite prepared her for *this.*

The chamber was a perfect sphere, an area hollowed out in the center of the moon, barely a few ships wide in diameter.

And in the center,

There *he* was.

It was *him*. The presence. The shard of impossible being nested in the deeper reflection of him. The quiet, patient watcher behind the still form.

It didn't speak. Didn't move. Just… existed, outlined faintly in refracted silver, like a shape reflected in the far corners of her eyes. It looked up, and Thalia saw her own thoughts falter, her rage falter.

"You see him," Artemis said softly.

Thalia didn't nod. Didn't speak. Her voice wouldn't come.

Artemis flashed a soft smile.

"Nobody else could see him except for me. He seemed to have formed from my energy alone with *Anaklusmos*. Good to know my trusty lieutenant can see *it* too."

Artemis stepped forward, arms folded behind her back. Her silhouette was outlined in pale light, and she seemed even younger than usual, like a memory of herself, untouched by the galactic empire she represented, reverting back to her form she took when Thalia first met her.

"He is not [[Percy Jackson]]," Artemis said. "Not anymore. Not ever."

"I know," Thalia rasped. "But he's still in there, isn't he?"

The goddess didn't answer. Not right away.

Instead, she lowered herself onto the lip of the floating observation ring, eyes scanning the still form in the center of the chamber. He had no clear features.

"I still don't get it," Thalia said, voice half-whisper. "All of us here. You here. The Moon, not even under threat, and we've got every top Huntress from Eos to Hecatia lined up like it's Judgment Day."

Artemis didn't answer at first.

She stood, still as a statue, floating at the gravitational epicenter of the rocky satellite orbiting Gaia.

Thalia tried again, this time more direct.

"Is it because of him?"

The goddess didn't turn.

"Everything is because of him," she said simply.

Thalia swallowed. "You never told us what actually happened way back then in [[Caligo Vale]]. Not really."

There was a silence then, long and deep.

Then Artemis spoke.

"Caligo Vale did not matter. Its location was chosen by [[Hera]] on a whim, but the bond was a *necessity.* The Olympian Court was crumbling. Poseidon would not act. [[Zeus]] would not bend. The Empire needed a stabilizer, someone to represent both the human demigod legacy and divine continuity. We were bound. With his role as the Guardian of Gaia, he would not intrude as much as any other entity eligible under Poseidon's dominion."

"So they made you *marry him*?" Thalia asked, aghast. "That sounds more like a contract than a ceremony."

Artemis let out the faintest sound—a laugh, maybe. Or a sob.

"Do you think I would have let that happen if I could choose?"

Thalia frowned. "I… I don't know."

The goddess turned now, fully. Her face held the weight of centuries, and something Thalia rarely saw in her: tiredness.

"I had to do it," Artemis said. "For peace. For stability. For the Empire."

Her voice dropped. "And because, deep down, I had already given him a part of myself long before then."

Thalia blinked. "You *loved* him?"

"I loved what he was," Artemis replied. "Not because he chased glory. Not because he bore prophecy. But because he was *real* with me. Even when I hated him for it."

Another pause. Then Artemis added:

"And because he never wanted me to change."

Thalia exhaled slowly. "And now?"

Artemis gestured to the surface beyond. "Now I watch over moons I do not sleep on. I command girls who no longer run beneath the stars but on fortress-moons and orbital platforms. And I enforce a surveillance network that ensures the safety of thousands of trillions."

"I have not indulged in the simple pleasure of chasing prey in millennia. You do not know how much I yearn to be among the forestry and trees again. The satisfaction of a well placed shot. Sometimes, I wonder if any part of me still remembers *how to hunt."*

She closed her eyes.

"He is… my wound. A divine wound. If you could see him when we were bound, you would have felt it." Her voice cracked– just once.

A long silence fell.

In the center of the room, the presence of *Him Within the Moon* remained unmoved. Unbothered. As though he was only waiting.

Thalia finally spoke.

"So you *do* love him."

"No," Artemis said sharply. Then, softer: "Not in a way that would satisfy either of us. It is a quiet love. The kind that hides beneath duty. That goes unspoken. That will never, ever be returned."

"Because he's not him anymore."

"No," Artemis whispered, and turned her gaze back to the figure. "Because I am *me*."

A long silence followed.

Then Thalia sighed and sat down beside her. "This sucks."

Artemis gave a ghost of a smile. "Yes. It does."

The ground *shivered*. Just once, but deep enough for every sentinel system buried beneath Luna's crust to jolt awake. Shields flared. In orbit, hundreds of crimson signatures lit up the tactical canopy. The Hunters scrambling to analyze the situation.

Artemis stopped.

Thalia's eyes snapped upward. "Incoming?"

Then the first blast hit.

It didn't strike the sanctum itself—it wasn't meant to. Instead, it clawed across the moon's surface in a wide arc, like a scythe swung by Kronos. Shields broken, dust and star-glass ignited in a fan of light that could be seen from [[Apollo]]'s Solar Auxilia.

A heartbeat later, the voice came.

It laced itself across every frequency—audible, psychic, divine.

***"This is the beginning and the end. Prepare to greet the Him you have longed for. Welcome the moon as it closes in around you…"***

Grabbing her lieutenant's shoulder, she flashed back to the top of the elevator, in the middle of the Hunters idly waiting for her to come back. Artemis' silver eyes sharpened. "Seal the sanctum," she ordered, flicking her fingers through the comm-thread.

Outside, the sky *wept fire.*

***"For He shall save you! Be vital, for He embraces the living. Feel him stir now, upon this holy place…"***

"I hate cultists," Thalia spat, drawing her signature spear, now elongated into its original form, crackling with electromagnetic light.

"They're not just cultists," Artemis murmured. "They are *followers.* His *heretics.* Perseus has not yet risen, and already they corrupt his name."

A second barrage screamed down, wide-beam plasma shafts, cutting vertical streaks into the surface. Artemis raised a hand. A silver wall flared to life above them, intercepting it.

From above, warships blotted the stars. Not the sleek forms of the [[Undying Olympian Empire]]'s cruisers, these were angular, bristling things, chained together by strange materials.

And they brought the Heretics along.

***"Slough off your mortal suffering and be reborn…"***

Thalia's hand trembled. "He's… listening. I can feel him."

"So can I," Artemis said, softly.

***"We are all of us but echoes of his shadow… The giver of blessings innumerable and the Lord of Demigods…"***

A sudden distortion bloomed across the field. Teleportation breach. Dozens of the cult's warriors, a fusion of mortals, twisted demigods, and screaming zealots, ripped into reality. A few howled as they charged the outer wall, while a few others began entrenching themselves in the moon's dusty crust.

"Target designated: [[Order of the Hollow Crescent]]," Artemis said calmly.

Then her bow unfolded, a thing made of raw moonlight, twice as tall as a mortal. With one pull, she drew back a shaft made of starlight and loosed it into the sky.

It struck one of the invading warships, and the vessel compacted. For the onlookers, it was as if the ship disappeared, but for the more observant, they would have seen the unlucky ship instead turned into a neat sphere of divine steel, barely an arm's length across.

Looking through a window, Thalia spotted an ethereal figure, sitting cross-legged on the barrel of an orbital cannon, Almost as big as the defective Talos she had fought in the junkyard back then with Percy, there he sat, invisible to everyone else except her and her Goddess…

Him.

The blurred reflection. A figure watching the chaos.

"Percy," she whispered.

He looked older. Tired. But *aware.* Listening.

***"He shall grant us freedom from the uncaring gods…"***

***"Rise, Perseus Jackson. Let our blood be thine and thine actions be truth. RISE!"***

For a moment, the moon shuddered—not from fire, but from the divine waking.

Artemis turned toward the tower as well.

"I don't know why they're trying to awaken Him," she said. "But they've misunderstood everything."

"Then tell them," Thalia said.

"I will."

And Artemis took a step forward—toward the field, toward the fire, toward the unholy summoning.

***"Speak it!"***

***"Speak it, my kin, my kin of the Hollow Crescent. Speak the names, The One Son. The Tidebreaker. The Gentle Storm. The Not-God-Who-Walked."***

As if they could hear her, Thalia could faintly sense every hostile ship suddenly switch their targeting systems to the lone Goddess. Pinprick pale lights could be seen from the ships, no doubt a barrage of missiles and beams, silent in the vacuum of space.

The bombardment should've ended Artemis, who stood alone in an empty patch of untouched land in the middle of the sea of bunkers on the moon.

***"The Moonbound waits with arms wide. He will walk with you. He will shelter you from ruin in his benediction. He will plant strength in your breath and authority in your voice!"***

A shape blinked into being. Vast.

Across the Hunters' facilities, there came a *quiet.*

His face was familiar, but flickered like a glitched memory file. His form seemed to be holding on, if just barely. Divine energy she could almost taste drifted from his shoulders like steam.

The cult fell silent. Ships held orbit. Even the zealots stopped mid-charge.

***"He hears us,"*** one breathed, trembling. ***"He sees us. We are chosen—"***

And then the giant moved.

Not towards them.

But toward Artemis.

He stepped between her and the orbital fleet. Slowly. Protectively.

His hand—larger than any of the surrounding Hunters—reached downward, palm facing Artemis.

She looked up.

"Perseus," she said. Not startled. Just… quiet.

From above, a cult dreadnought broke from its formation.

Too fast. Too direct.

A concentrated lance of dark energy spiraled down from it, aimed directly at Artemis.

Thalia turned, shouting.

Too slow.

The beam hit. Not her, at least.

A hand—massive, translucent, and radiating broken blue-gold light—closed around the lance like a child's toy.

The impact sent a shockwave across the crater, ripping up stone and divine wards alike. But not a scratch touched Artemis.

She looked up, startled.

***"He—he denies us! WHY!?"***

One of the zealots screamed as their ship detonated from within.

***"He was meant to RISE!"***

***"He was meant to FREE US!"***

Percy's form did not answer.

He only stood—shielding Artemis.

A final beam lanced down from one of the heavier cult dreadnoughts.

Percy's form raised a hand.

The beam *shattered.* Light scattering into salt.

Below, Artemis whispered, almost to herself, "You shouldn't have answered me. Not now. Not like this."

***"This is not the Him we called."***

***"This is not the Rex Deus."***

***"He has been tainted. Warped. Poisoned by the Moon."***

Twin voices snarled across the system-wide communications.

***"He is not yours, Moon-Goddess. He is the Becoming. The Deliverance. The Everfather of Demigods."***

***"You think you claim him by proximity? By sentiment?" came the second. "How dare thee! He was Our Lord before you even cared."***

Down below, Percy turned his head slightly, his expression unreadable.

He raised his left hand.

A wall of celestial water, massive, shimmering with constellations, rose behind the moon, forming a barrier between Luna and the cult fleet. When their next volley struck, it simply... stopped. Like arrows against the tide.

The Twins hesitated.

One of their cruisers began to break apart under divine gravity, crumpling as if held in a child's hand.

Silence reclaimed the crater.

Dust settled. Hunters exhaled. A few dropped to one knee in reverence.

The figure turned to Artemis, still beside him.

She hesitated. "Then you still—"

*"Always."*

Her breath caught.

But before anything more could be said, the giant looked up. As if hearing something through the void.

And Artemis, just for a moment, sensed the presence watching them.

---

The waters churned.

Minerva hovered, a few inches off the surface of Orbital Platform One. Next to her stood Poseidon, arms folded, trident glowing faintly as the currents thrashed around them.

"You feel it too?" he rumbled.

"Yes," Minerva said. "It moved."

"He shouldn't be able to. Not yet. Not while fragmented. It isn't even the real *him*."

Minerva turned toward him, golden eyes narrowing.

"And yet he did."

They both stared at the surface projection, watching as ripples of divine force radiated outward from Luna like a beacon.

"Artemis was the anchor," Minerva said. "She always was. Even if she doesn't see it."

Poseidon gave a low, unreadable chuckle.

"She sees it. She just doesn't want to admit she *needs* it."

Minerva tilted her head.

"Don't do that. You remind me of Athena too much when you do that."

---

He stood beside her.

A ghost, a shard, a silhouette of divinity.

Not Percy Jackson as the world knew him, but the reflection. The unknowable.

*Him Within the Moon.*

His form was still immense, but shrinking—almost imperceptibly. Down from the towering colossus that had shielded her. Down to something that cast only a slightly longer shadow than her own.

"I didn't think you would come," she said, not looking at him.

*"I didn't either,"* he answered, voice lighter now. Worn, but more closely familiar. *"They were calling me. Demanding for me. You were so… alone."*

Artemis drew in a breath.

He didn't speak.

She turned to look at him then, properly. And for a moment, he looked just like the boy she remembered. The one, lost outside of Westover Hall, facing the long-gone [[Dr. Thorn]]. The one who had changed her, even if she never said it.

"I hated you," she admitted. "For what you did. For being forced into it. For being who you are."

He flinched, only slightly.

*"And?"* he asked.

"I don't anymore," she whispered.

They stood in silence, moonlight folding over them. The ruined heavens above crackled with residual energy, but neither moved.

Artemis reached up—not hesitantly, but not with certainty either—and placed two fingers to the center of his chest, where his heart should be. They glowed faintly against the shimmer.

"I can feel you everywhere," she said. "Every moon, every step. But only here do you seem... real."

He looked down at her hand.

*"I don't know what I am,"* he said. *"But I know that I would shield you again. Even if I disappear for it."*

She met his eyes. For once, not the goddess. Just Artemis.

"You already have."

And for a moment... just one, the space between them felt less vast. Less divine.

A gust of dust brushed past them. Like the turning of a page.

Percy's form began to flicker, just slightly. Like static running through a memory.

Artemis's eyes narrowed. "What is it?"

He staggered. The blue-glow of his limbs began to fray at the edges. Not burning, not breaking, but… pulling.

Something, somewhere, was calling him.

*"I think…"* he said slowly, *"My Original is calling me. Back there. In the past."*

"The past?"

He nodded once.

Artemis stepped forward. Her hand hovered by his shoulder, unsure if she could touch him without breaking the illusion.

"I don't want this part of you to go," she admitted.

"I'm not sure I do either." He smiled, soft and sad. "But I think he needs me."

A thread of moonlight stretched from her hands to the center of his form. Another, somewhere, far away—into a world of cabins, of campfires, of the damned prophecies.

And with a sound like a ripple in still water—he was gone.

Artemis exhaled. The crater felt heavier now.

"Then go to him, *Anaklusmos*" she said to the empty air. "And let the [[Riptide]] remind him of what we forgot."

---

When Percy decided it was a high time to leave the pavilion, strolling toward his cabin, he didn't make it more than halfway. He usually skirted around the volcanic climbing wall, which shielded him from the chilly winds that flew into camp during this time of year.

"Hey, Seaweed Brain," said a voice behind him, partially muffled by a flat rock formation.

He turned just in time to see a pillowcase drop over his head.

"What the—?!"

Something smacked his knee. A sack? No—someone's satchel. Then arms grabbed him—two, maybe three people—and he was yanked sideways off the path. A blur of pine needles, confused yelling, and then silence as the world dimmed.

He was being kidnapped.

[[Hermes]] cabin?

No. Too clever. Not enough dumb giggling.

Athena cabin.

---

Athena kids surrounded him in a semicircle, arms crossed, looking eerily in sync.

Annabeth stood at the center.

"Sorry about the bag," she said. "We needed to make sure you weren't going to bolt."

Percy groaned. "Annabeth, you know I'm not the enemy, right?"

"Then *prove it*," she said, eyes narrowed. "Tell us who Minerva is."

Percy looked around, pretending to be confused. "I can't. I barely know anything about her either."

Annabeth crossed her arms tighter, somehow. "Not good enough. We know you two talk regularly. You've zoned out on us too many times for it to be your ADHD. We all know that you shouldn't be this smart.

Percy stared at them. "Ouch. I didn't ask for her, you know. Plus, I feel like you took me here to do something other than asking about the funny voice in my head."

The rest of the cabin nodded in assent. Someone at the back said, "We need to make sure the daughter of Ares does not fail the quest. What she seeks is what we need."

Someone handed him a naval map. "Okay seaweed brain, lets see if 'Minerva' can help us out, or else we might resort to more *unsavory* methods." Almost rolling his eyes at Annabeth's childish posturing, and neglecting to mention how easily he could break out of the bindings consisting of duct tape and towels, he freed one of his arms and grabbed a marker.

Percy tugged the map closer.

"Okay," he said, "This is gonna be rough, but, just follow me."

The triangle he drew was lopsided, but recognizable. The rough shape of the Bermuda Triangle emerged, surrounded by curled, sharp lines indicating unnatural currents.

"This is the Sea of Monsters," Percy said, pointing at the inner triangle. "Where Clarisse is headed."

He shaded in the southwestern corner. "She will head to Florida, where the wreck of the *CSS Birmingham* is. The God of War will raise it for her use. Sunken Civil War ship full of undead Confederate sailors. Don't know how it falls under his domain, or how it got there, but that doesn't matter."

Annabeth blinked. "That's... oddly specific."

Percy continued, quickly. "South of that, you've got [[Circe]]'s Island. Close enough to Costa Rica to resupply, but far away enough to stop local officials from poking their noses to where they don't belong. It moves sometimes, but it tends to stick around near here. Probably the largest source of magical and divine materials outside of Olympus that we can probably get our hands on easily."

A few of the campers snorted.

"North side?" Percy drew a crude cyclone icon. "That's where [[Scylla]] and Charybdis hang out. Giant whirlpool. Sea monster teeth. All that fun. Sits right at the center of the whole thing. Gives them the best results when they hunt."

He tapped near the center. "This. Right here. Polyphemus' Island. That's where the Fleece is."

Annabeth's brows furrowed. "How do you know exactly where he keeps the Fleece?"

"I told you, I don't know," he muttered, trying to hide his knowledge from the future. "I don't know what she is. I don't even know if she's real."

"But you trust her," Annabeth said flatly.

Percy hesitated.

He looked at the map. Looked at the triangle, the swirl of impossible sea paths and ancient monsters. He thought of the dreams, the flashbacks, the whispered blueprints in his brain that came to life when he needed them. He thought of a voice that always sounded calm, but never kind.

"Sometimes," Percy said. "She's the one of the only ones who cared."

Malcolm leaned forward. "Cared? Is she... a god?"

Percy shook his head. "No. Not like that. Annabeth would know more." He stopped. "Not you Annabeth, the future you."

The cabin froze.

*Whoops.*

"What?"

Percy turned to look at the blonde demigod of Athena, and then nodded, slow and uneasy.

Silence followed. The map sat between them now, marked with the haphazard lines of his work.

Annabeth finally spoke, voice low. "Does this mean… No it can't be. It lines up though. The implications…"

She turned around, and before slamming the door to her office, Percy could faintly see the beginnings of an iris message. His heart sank. He was caught.

Malcolm, seeing the technical cabin leader gone, took over. "We'll use it. Your map. It might save Clarisse's life."

Before they pushed him out of the door, he managed to scribble the rough area of where the *Princess Andromeda* was floating, waiting for Luke's arrival.

---

The small office was lined with scroll racks, blueprints, and ancient busts. The air was thick with dust and tension.

The shimmering mist of the Iris Message flickered to life in front of Annabeth, refracting the candlelight into sharp, geometric beams. On the other side stood the goddess Athena, her mother, looking like she was reading a book on her bed.

She spoke first.

"You do not call lightly, daughter."

Annabeth's voice was clipped. "I think you knew I would."

Athena tilted her head, barely. "This is about Perseus Jackson."

Annabeth didn't reply.

The goddess sighed. "Tell me more."

Annabeth swallowed. "He knows things. Too much. Maps of the Sea of Monsters. Strategic placements. Future events. And he referenced me—not me now, but… me from some alternate future." Her fingers clenched. "He mentioned someone named Minerva."

At that, Athena's gaze sharpened. She didn't speak, but the shift in posture was clear.

"Who is she?" Annabeth asked. "Is she you?"

"No," Athena said flatly. "Not a singular chance."

That chilled Annabeth more than any yes could have.

Athena stepped closer to the Iris shimmer, her features cold with calculation.

"Minerva is a remnant. A divergence, I believe. I hypothesized that it was me, once, in a world that no longer exists—or perhaps never fully did. She is arrogant, insanely so. If she has embedded herself in the aberration that is Perseus Jackson, then this could spell disaster for us."

Annabeth stared. "She's in his head"

"She is near him. That is not the same."

"But she's giving him knowledge. Knowledge that shouldn't exist."

Athena nodded once. "Yes. And that is the most dangerous kind. Not because it is false—but because it is true, and yet out of time. Like fire in a library."

Annabeth hesitated. "Is he dangerous?"

Athena gave no answer for a moment.

Then: "Without a shadow of a doubt. But he is the gateway. The medium through which the danger walks. But also an opportunity. If we manage to pull information for the future without consequences like he has, disregarding the paradoxes we make, it could advance our supremacy within months. As they say, a drop of ink used is a sea of blood saved..."

"Thank you." She spoke, taking up the desk beside her bed.

She paused after looking the first time, Iris conveniently panning the view at a few papers.

Annabeth saw her mother mimic what she did when she was focused on something. She mentally ran the numbers again. Then again. Then again.

"This is accurate? You swear that this is accurate?" She shot at her daughter. Annabeth, unsure, raised a brow at her. Annabeth leaned forward into the mist to read the haphazard notes she drew again. After a moment, she nodded and leaned back.

"As far as I'm aware, yes."

Athena exhaled slowly.

"I must redraft my plans." She simply declared. Annabeth blinked, then tilted her head to look at the stack of papers on the side of the table. The eight neatly organized stacks of A4 paper each exactly one-and-a-half meters tall.

"All of them?"

"Yes."

Annabeth shivered. "Should we report this to Olympus?" she asked, already dreading the answer.

"No," Athena said. "The Council would overreact. They would destroy the host to silence the whisper in their fear. That would be... inefficient."

"You mean Percy."

Athena inclined her head. "I mean the vessel. Your personal attachments are your weakness. You must evaluate this like a strategist."

Annabeth's jaw clenched. "He's not a vessel. He's Percy. And he didn't ask for any of this."

Athena's gaze hardened—minutely. "You don't know that… yet."

There was a long silence.

Finally, Athena's voice returned to its usual measured tone. "Continue monitoring him. Do not antagonize them, her intent remains... opaque."

Annabeth looked down. "And what if he is trying to help?"

"Then *she* is playing a very long game."

The Iris shimmer began to fade.

Athena's final words came like an afterthought—yet were anything but:

"Be cautious. Even when you think you're ahead on the board, daughter—remember: Minerva has played the long game better than I possibly ever did."

The message cut.

---

The boy was a faultline. Athena was nearly at a loss. Nearly. Too intelligent. Too chaotic. Too aligned to forces she did not control.

Problem was, events were proceeding without him. Clarisse La Rue was ready to depart with the blessing of Tantalus. Jackson remained static. Contained. *Lazy.*

Athena tapped her forefinger against a floating icosahedron: a projection of Jackson's current psychological resilience. Stable. But not unshakable.

He needed pressure. Something to motivate him.

Jackson would not remain in camp long if he believed someone else was in danger, she mused. Especially Annabeth. Especially Grover.

"[[Grover Underwood]]," Athena muttered, eyes narrowing. The idiotic lesser creature was currently detained in Polyphemus' territory.

A gentle nudge through dream-space. A vision sent, if Minerva was present, she wouldn't suspect a thing. Grover's voice, pleading. The island. The Fleece. The scent of sea and monster.

Annabeth would not sit idle. Her instincts, mirroring her mother's, would drive her toward intervention. But Athena needed it clean. Jackson, now "accidentally" freed by a malfunctioning camp barrier. A "coincidental" encounter at an old sea god's shrine. Annabeth would follow. She always did.

Deploy them near the daughter of War.

Let them think it's luck.

Let them believe it is fate.

It is neither. It is design.

Jackson reaches the Island. The Fleece is reclaimed. But not before the Princess Andromeda intercepts them on the return.

Not before [[Luke Castellan]] faces him.

Not before Athena gathers all the data she needs on Minerva—through Percy. Because even if Minerva was shielding her presence, even if she was fragmented…

"She cannot suppress the consequences of her predictions," Athena said, folding her hands. "If I cast a die, she must see the result. A chess master must always move second. Let the first move be mine."

---

The beach was quiet the morning Percy left.

[[Camp Half-Blood]] had a rare kind of stillness, the kind that felt like the breath before a storm. The canoes bobbed lightly in the dock's tide. The mist curled low over the sand, smelling of salt and old driftwood. The Apollo kids were busy pretending not to notice the broken boundary line stretching in a pale crack down the far hill, where the magic had buckled two hours before from an new unnamed demigod escorted by an exhausted satyr. The [[Hephaestus]] cabin had already dispatched a small team to diagnose the malfunction.

Mr. D hadn't said goodbye. He'd only looked at Percy, eyes unseeing. He knew he would leave. No explanation. No fanfare. Just a nod, and a glance toward the cove.

Annabeth had met him halfway down the slope, a small pack on her back and eyes that hadn't slept.

"Grover's in trouble," Annabeth said, wasting no time.

"I know," Percy replied.

There was no argument. There didn't need to be.

Clarisse had already gone. Sent ahead with her undead crew and a war god's smile. Tantalus had waved her off with a goblet of pomegranate wine, too pleased with himself to notice how wrong it all was.

The camp watched from a distance as the two of them stepped past the final pillar of the barrier. The moment they crossed, there was a sound like a snapping rubber band.

By the time they reached the old sea shrine Percy remembered was hidden along the coast somewhere, the sun had started to rise, bleeding gold through the cracks in the trees. The shrine itself was little more than a crumbling half-circle of marble sunk into the earth, choked by moss and ivy. Barnacles clung to the base of its old columns. At its center, a stone bowl filled with brackish water reflected the clouds. Percy stepped forward.

"This is it?" Annabeth asked, nose twitching.

"It's the last known marker to [[Pontus]]," Percy said, "One of the old sea gods. Pre-Olympian."

"Sounds charming."

"Try not to say anything dumb."

"That's rich coming from *you*."

He stepped up to the bowl and stared into the water. Something stirred beneath it—something deep. Then, his reflection blinked.

A voice rose, old and thin and rasping with centuries of salt.

"You carry the scent of the King"

Percy didn't speak.

"You walk with a dead future, mortal. A goddess with no pantheon. A war with no victor."

The water shivered.

"[[Atlantis]] watches through thee. But the currents still remember my name. What do you wish for?"

"Transport to the *mare monstrum maledictum*, the Sea of Monsters, paid in due time with an offering of similar exchange, upon completion of my quest."

A gust of sea-wind blasted out from the bowl, nearly knocking them back. When Percy opened his eyes, a coin rested in the bowl. Obsidian black. Engraved with a spiral trident.

The ship emerged like a wound in the sea.

No sails. No crew. Just a long, narrow vessel carved from something too dark to be wood. It groaned as it rose from the depths.

They boarded without speaking.

By dusk, the shore was gone.
