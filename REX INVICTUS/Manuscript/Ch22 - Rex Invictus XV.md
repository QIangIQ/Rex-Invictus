# Rex Invictus XV

← [[Ch21 - Rex Invictus XIV|Rex Invictus XIV]] · [[Manuscript Index|Index]] · [[Ch23 - Interlude I|Interlude I]] →

After a few hours, a positively miniscule child of [[Athena]] nervously approached him and informed him that the 5 people he brought in were locked in one of the 2nd floor guest rooms in the big house. Dionysius, perhaps sensing the gravity of the situation, had, for once, expanded his coverage to blanked the surrounding area, covering the porch with a thick purple haze and letting it spill down the lawn.

The five captured Ekdromoi occupied a hastily moved long dining table at the center of the room, each seated within a separate reinforced chair that had been secured directly into the floor. Their armor had been removed for medical treatment, though pieces of it remained stacked nearby under careful observation. The soldiers themselves looked considerably less imposing without their equipment.

Ione had his arms secured behind the chair, his torso wrapped in layers of soaked medical gauze where [[Eleni Theodosia|Eleni]]'s pressure had broken several ribs and [[Percy Jackson|Percy]] had later repaired what could be repaired. Iane sat opposite him, her throat bearing a faint, perfectly healed line where Percy had reconstructed the damage. Her expression remained completely unreadable.

Straban looked worse. The injuries to his lungs had required extensive intervention. [[Apollo]] had managed to repair most of the internal damage, although the divine physician had complained repeatedly about the foreign compounds circulating through his bloodstream, citing it as an 'completely new form of medicae'. Merek's body had required the most work. Even without the armor, he was enormous, with enough reinforced musculature to make the chair look undersized beneath him.

Neumond was the strangest.

The Hephaestean demigod had been almost completely reconstructed from the waist down after the damage inflicted upon his wings and lower body. The bronze wings had been removed from their damaged mounting points and placed on a separate table. Apollo had repaired the organic reinforced spine of his back while [[Minerva (AI)|Minerva]] had stabilized the mechanical wings and bodily augments to revert some control over his wings, much to his confusion.

Apollo stood behind them with his arms folded, occasionally looking toward the equipment that had been confiscated from the soldiers. Several weapons had been disassembled and placed beneath transparent containment fields. The Morrow-IX rifles were there, along with magazines of QVARTUS ammunition, several injector pens, fragments of their armor, the small black defensive sphere, and the remains of Neumond's equipment.

Percy kept his eyes on the five soldiers.

"Ya'll aren't really volunteering information."

Eleni glanced toward Percy, she had long realized the Intelligence inhabiting the Lord of Demigods was a very unique version of the one pervading the Undying Empire from the future.

"Can you confer with AtRep?"

The 5 soldiers gave a start.

"Yeah."

"Ask her whether she has figured out who these people are."

Percy paused.

*I have identified their equipment, physiological architecture, weapons, tactical doctrine, and several technological principles. This is one of the black divisions of the UOE. Pause for just a moment, Varlet. I will request my… other to provide more information on the other side.*

"She doesn't know for now."

Eleni frowned.

"*Minerva* doesn't know? Then perhaps we should start with the obvious."

She turned to look at them.

"Who are you?"

Silence.

The five soldiers looked at one another.

It lasted only a few seconds.

Merek finally looked directly at Percy.

"You're going first."

Percy raised an eyebrow.

"Me?"

"You know who we are."

"No, actually, I really don't."

Merek's eyes narrowed.

"You knew our ammunition."

"I had help."

The five exchanged another brief glance.

Neumond looked toward the containment field holding the MORROW-IX rifle.

"Those babies are only allowed past the blackout zone for the civvies, and that info shouldn't even have been invented for a few millenia, at least."

"Let's just say that the Repetroire told me"

That produced the first genuine reaction. Neumond's expression became strange.

"The Athenian Repertoire is only accessible through a data-terminal of some form, and then directed to a local watchpoint… you possess nothing of the kind. Were you a counter-unit, you would need some form of external augments bulkier than mine to confer with Watchpoint, and my wings naturally emit enough ECM to annul most attempts."

"She lives in my bloodstream, brain, spine, and soul. She's been there for quite a while."

*You make me sound like an infestation.*

"Anyway."

Iane spoke.

"AtRep identified *our* ammunition?"

"Yes."

"How? That shouldn't be possible. That info is highly classified."

"Guess I have high clearance then."

Iane stared at him.

"If you have that much clearance and are clearly a member of the Empire, why would you attack us and show hostile on IFF?"

"You shot me through the hand without introducing yourselves."

"Let's establish something before this becomes unpleasant. You five are alive because Percy spent considerable effort keeping you alive. You are currently sitting inside one of the safest locations available to demigods. Nobody here particularly wants to hurt you."

"Demigods? Those godspawn outside barely qualify as third-generation legacies with their divine output. No way near the saturation of even one of the Citadel's least powerful demigods. How does this place even count as a training ground?"

"That said, we would very much like to understand what exactly you brought into Alaska."

Neumond looked at him.

"Alaska?"

"Yes."

The five exchanged another glance.

Straban finally spoke.

"We were deployed to the Arctic region."

Percy watched him carefully.

"By whom?"

Silence.

"Who sent you?"

Straban remained still.

"Pyrrhuiesian Command."

Eleni's expression changed.

"Do you know them?"

"Yes. They caused my defeat and the events to send me next to you, my lord. The Pyrrhus dynasty is one of the strongest in the rankings and are your half-brothers. Us Theodosians are similar, and we frequently butt heads for border conflicts."

Merek's eyes moved between Percy and Eleni, watching for the smallest change in expression. Ione did the same, although his attention kept drifting toward the containment field around the confiscated equipment. Iane remained almost perfectly motionless, while Neumond looked increasingly interested in the exchange.

Eleni seemed to realize that she had said more than she intended.

Straban's brow furrowed. "You… You are Theodosian?"

"Perhaps."

"You addressed this… person as your lord."

"Also correct."

"A Theodosian speaking as though you believe him to be not an ordinary officer. What entity would give him such an authority?"

There was a pause.

"We know what he is."

Percy raised an eyebrow.

"Do you?"

Merek's eyes narrowed. "We know what he is supposed to be."

"So we're doing that thing where everyone knows something but nobody wants to say it out loud."

Iane looked at him.

"That would be the prudent course, *déspota*."

Percy tilted his head.

"Don't call me a despot."

"Then cease behaving like one."

Neumond snorted.

Minerva's voice quietly entered his thoughts.

*That is one way of describing your decision-making history, Varlet.*

Percy's mouth twitched.

Eleni noticed.

"You are speaking with her again."

"Yeah."

"The other intelligence."

"Minerva."

"Yes."

Percy glanced toward the five soldiers.

"She's listening."

Neumond's head turned toward him.

"Can she hear us?"

"Yes."

"Can she see us?"

"Yes."

Neumond looked profoundly uncomfortable.

"That is unsettling."

Minerva spoke through Percy before he could answer.

***I find your discomfort refreshing.***

Neumond's eyes widened.

"That was her?"

Percy nodded.

"Yep."

Neumond stared at him.

"She sounds like AtRep."

The rest of the Ekdromoi nodded

Iane looked toward Percy.

"You said she resides within you."

"Yeah."

"Entirely?"

"Pretty much."

"Then you have enough augments to host a Watchpoint."

*Say yes, Varlet. Sister has given me enough information to work with for a while*

"You know that term, I am sure."

Percy gave him a flat look.

"I know a lot of terms."

"That was not what I asked."

"No, it wasn't."

The five exchanged another glance.

"Yes. I am able to host the Repertoire"

Straban finally broke the silence.

"You are not PCA."

Percy shook his head.

"No."

"You are not one of Citadel's children."

"No."

"You are not one of the Cohors Praetoria."

"No."

"You are not Extra-pantheonic."

"No."

"You are not Theodosian."

Eleni answered that one.

"No."

Straban's eyes narrowed.

"Then what faction do you serve?"

Percy and Eleni looked at each other. Merek's expression changed.

"You cannot say."

Percy shrugged.

"The gods."

Ione leaned forward slightly.

"You are someone."

"Everyone is someone."

"An important figure."

Percy looked at him.

"That's a pretty broad statement."

"You actually said it."

"I didn't say anything."

"You said UOE."

"So did you."

"Yes, but I was testing you."

"And now we're both aware that we're both aware."

Iane frowned.

"That sentence was atrocious."

Percy shrugged.

"Welcome to modern English."

Merek looked toward Eleni.

"You are of the Undying Empire."

Eleni did not answer.

Merek looked back toward Percy.

"And you."

Percy shrugged again.

"Maybe."

Ione frowned.

"That is not an acceptable answer."

"It's the one you're getting."

Merek leaned forward, and he began counting the facts with his fingers.

"You know the Repertoire. You possess an internal AtRep. You recognized our QVARTUS ammunition. You knew the designation of the MORROW platform. You have knowledge of Imperial military architecture that should not exist in this era."

He paused.

"You are from the UOE."

Eleni finally spoke.

"So are you. Now that we have cleared this up, with direct clearance from my lord, I will request a temporary promotion to become a Selenide Bulwark for Lord Phoebus Apollo and invoke a CODE 4 for the release of information."

She turned back to Apollo and Percy.

"Milords, if you would."

Mystified, they signed across the dotted pad on a hologram in front of their heads.

The hologram flared once. A thin ring of pale light closed around each signature, then broke apart into a line of glyphs Percy didn't recognize. Minerva recognized them immediately.

*CODE 4 accepted. Provisional Selenide Bulwark designation logged under Phoebus Apollo. Temporary, revocable, and highly irregular. Your Theodosian is creative, Varlet.*

The five soldiers changed right away.

Something small and metallic clicked at the base of each of their skulls. Straban winced.

"Suppression lock," Eleni said, answering the question on Apollo's face before he could ask it. "Every black-division operator carries one. It physically prevents disclosure of operational detail without a valid CODE 4 from a high-ranking individual. Without it, hostiles are able to easily pry info with their psychic members."

Apollo's eyebrows climbed. "Charming. And my future self signs off on that?"

"Your future self designed its medical tolerances, milord."

"...Also charming."

Neumond rolled his neck. It was the first sign of ease any of them had shown. "Well. That's a relief. I was starting to think I'd die tied to a chair, by a guy in a T-shirt."

"Hey. It's a good shirt."

Merek ignored both of them. His eyes were on Percy.

"Where do you want us to start?"

"The beginning's usually good."

Merek was quiet for a moment, as if deciding how much of it he actually believed himself.

"The beginning is [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]]."

"In our time," Merek said, "Tartarus is not a pit beneath the world. It is a front."

"The Tartarean Front. It has been active for longer than any of us have been alive. Longer than our parents. Billions of them every day across the whole Empire's territory. Lord [[Hades]] and the Director are almost permanently helping us hold the containment lines. Everyone else holds the ground in between."

"Everyone else," Percy repeated.

"The Army," Straban said. "Nearly all of it. Every Pantheon-aligned legion, divisions that can hold a line. The fleets that aren't on picket duty are in orbit over the breach worlds. The Empire is the largest power humanity has ever built, and almost all of its strength is pinned down in the hole. The fleets and the forces around the galaxy amount to less than 1 percent of the forces currently active. On average down there, a Titan or Giant reforms every 24 hours, and other monsters even faster."

Apollo had gone very still. Percy thought about the pit [[Kronos]] had crawled out of, and how many years it had taken to put him back.

He didn't like the picture.

"Pyrrhuiesian Command came down on a sector near the [[Phlegethon]]," Straban went on. "Our reserve echelon had been pulled back to rotate out. Two understrength cohorts, the support elements, a carrier, and part of its escort wing. We were staging at a transit anchor on the Lethe side when it happened."

"When what happened?"

"It took everything within eleven kilometers of the anchor," Iane said. "Men. Ships. Supply dumps. Part of the anchor itself. And some of what was on the other side of the containment line."

Apollo's jaw tightened. "Monsters came through with you."

"A few. We handled them." Merek's tone made it clear that handled had cost something.

"And then?"

Neumond let out a slow breath.

"And then we opened our eyes on… Mars? The planet named after our lord of war."

He laughed, short and without much humor.

"Red dust. That was it. None of the orbital shipworks, no Grid over [[Olympus]] Mons, No megacities. No Hydra-class pickets, No [[Olympus Secundus]]. Just rock and cold and a sky that was far too empty. Half the cohort thought we'd been thrown into Tartarus proper. The other half thought the Empire was dead."

"Our chronometers couldn't find a Watchpoint handshake," Ione said. "Nothing. AtRep was silent. For the first moments we believed we were the last Imperial contingent alive."

"AtRep fixed the date eventually," Iane said. "Stellar drift. We are thousands of years before our departure point."

The room was quiet.

Somewhere in the back of Percy's head, Minerva spoke. For once her voice was soft.

*Sister's records corroborate. On her end, the shear registered as a loss event. Every soul within that radius was listed as killed in action. Their names were documented by Lord Varro, Overseer of the Legio Adminstratoris in the 12th Olympian reach.*

"How many of you are on Mars?"

Merek hesitated. The CODE 4 was clearly doing its work, because he answered anyway.

"A little over a legion's worth of bodies. Approximately 4 million fighting men and auxiliaries."

Apollo muttered something in Ancient Greek that Percy was fairly sure wasn't a prayer.

Percy leaned back against the wall.

"And your job?"

"Operation Anabasis-105," Straban said.

Apollo gave a short, bitter laugh. "Of course it is."

Percy looked between them. "I'm missing the joke."

"Xenophon," Eleni said. "Ten thousand Greek soldiers stranded deep in enemy territory, with no support and no way home but to march. The Empire's naming committee has a sense of irony, milord."

"The Empire's naming committee is my sister," Apollo said. "She has no sense of irony. She just thinks it's accurate."

"First, establish the era and confirm divine presence. Second, catalogue the Pantheon's current strength: which gods are active, which demigod enclaves exist, and their divine output. Third, locate any fixed point that could anchor a return, highest possibility, Tartarus.

"And the fourth," Straban said, "determine whether Earth can support the force."

Percy's eyes narrowed.

"Support the force."

"Mars cannot feed a legion forever." Straban did not look away. "The carrier's fabricators can only run for so long. The water reclamation is overburdened. If there is no way home, then Command needs to know whether there is somewhere else to go."

Eleni cleared her throat. "Why Alaska?"

The five exchanged a look. This time Iane answered, and she spoke carefully, like someone reciting a text she had been made to memorize.

"Because of Alaska Hill."

"Never heard of it," he said.

"You wouldn't have," Iane said. "It hasn't happened yet."

She shifted in her chair as far as the restraints allowed.

"Every Imperial cadet studies the Battle of Alaska Hill in their first year. It was the first extra-pantheonic war in nearly two thousand years, and the first time demigods fought in a modern mechanized war. Lady Athena authorized a computational fortress in the Arctic. The cold carried the thermal load of the processor arrays, so they could run at a scale no temperate site could cool. It was built to house Parlamentum ."

"The architecture that became the Repertoire," Straban said. "AtRep's first cradle."

"So you went looking for the birthplace of AtRep," Percy said.

"We went to the one place on this planet where our history says she will eventually be built," Iane said. "If any part of her exists in this era, even the seed of her, it would be there. An early node, a survey marker, a buried relay. Anything our instruments could talk to."

"And if there was nothing?"

"Then we would know exactly how early we are," Straban said. "The Arctic also dampens divine presence. The oldest records call it the land beyond the gods. The Pantheon's senses are weakest there, and our equipment was designed not to rely on them."

"Also, it's cold," Neumond added. "Our armor dumps a lot of heat. It's much easier to hide my heat signature at minus forty."

"Did you find anything?"

"What do you think?"

Merek hadn't taken his eyes off Percy the whole time.

"The defense of Alaska Hill was commanded by a son of [[Poseidon]]."

The room went silent.

Percy shrugged. "There's a lot of us around."

"There are not, milord," Eleni said quietly.

*Parlamentum Mk.I supported that defense, Varlet, Minerva murmured. I was there. You were there. Or we will be. Your language has no proper tense for this. You have probably forgotten your age.*

Percy decided not to think about that too hard. Apollo had one hand pressed to the bridge of his nose. "So. To summarize. A lost legion from the far future is sitting on my planet, running out of water, and sending armed scouts to case Earth for real estate. Wonderful. Fantastic. [[Zeus]] is going to love this."

Percy pushed off the wall.

"Last question. Then we'll figure out what to do with you."

The five of them waited.

"If you'd found somebody like me, and actually managed to report it, who were you reporting to?"

Neumond answered first. He was the most relaxed of the five and had gotten comfortable, and he answered the way a man answers a question he has answered a hundred times in training.

"Standing doctrine. Any contact above god-tier threshold is flagged straight to the Director of Anti-Espionage and Celestial Surveillance. Lady [[Artemis]]'s people handle anything that—"

"Oh, Styx," Apollo said.

"—and if the contact turned out to be the important, well." Neumond was still talking, still on autopilot, not reading the room at all. "Lady Artemis has standing orders about them. Personal ones. Nobody gets to lay a hand on it before she does, and she was very clear about what happens to anyone who—"

Iane kicked him hard under the table.

Neumond stopped.

He looked at Iane. He looked at Merek, then he looked at Apollo, who had gone the color of old ash.

"...What?"

"You said her name," Apollo hissed. "With her titles. Two times. Brotha. You just prayed."

"Oh shiet."

The temperature in the room dropped.

It didn't drop gradually. One moment the air was warm from the afternoon sun coming through the windows, and the next Percy's breath fogged in front of his face. Frost ran across the glass in silver threads. The light outside turned wrong, pale and cool. On the porch, [[Dionysus]]'s thick purple haze was shoved, rolled back down the lawn.

Percy felt it pass over him. Divine sense, sharp and lunar and utterly familiar, dragged across every inch of the room like the beam of a searchlight. It touched the confiscated rifles, the QVARTUS rounds, the Hephaestean wings on their table, and five strangers in reinforced chairs.

Then it found his hand.

"Everyone," Apollo said very quietly, "stay very still. Especially you five."

**BANG.**

The light simply changed. Silver poured into the room from no particular direction, and she was standing between Percy and the table as if she had always been there: auburn hair braided tight down her back, silver eyes blazing, a bow already in her left hand and a hunting knife reversed along her right forearm.

She looked at Percy.

In two strides she had his hand in her grip. She turned his hand over, palm up, and stared at the fresh pink skin where a QVARTUS round had punched straight through less than a day ago. Apollo's work was good. It was nearly invisible.

"*Who."*

The single word had weight. The reinforced chairs groaned where they were bolted to the floor. One of the bolts under Merek's chair sheared with a crack like a gunshot. The containment fields over the confiscated weapons flickered, spat sparks and collapsed. The window behind Apollo crazed from frame to frame with frost.

Arrows formed in the air. They appeared, a dozen, then two dozen, lengths of solid moonlight hanging motionless in a loose ring around the five Ekdromoi, every point aimed at a throat or an eye or the gap between two ribs.

None of the soldiers tried to move.

Merek, still restrained, bent forward as far as the bonds allowed, as close to a bow as he could manage. Percy heard a word.

Director.

Eleni was already on one knee.

"Sis," Apollo tried, both hands up. "Sis. Artemis. It's Dionysus's house. If you level it, he'll whine about it for a century—"

The arrows drew back a fraction of an inch.

Percy stepped into her line of sight.

He didn't pull his wrist free. He covered her hand with his other one, so she was holding him and he was holding her back, and he ducked his head until she had to look at him instead of past him.

"Hey," he said quietly. "Hey. Look at me."

Her eyes stayed fixed over his shoulder.

"Artemis."

That did it. Her gaze snapped to his, silver and furious and, underneath all of it, something rawer that she would never have let anyone else in the room see.

"They shot you."

"Yeah."

"They shot you. Through the hand. And you brought them here, into a house, and gave them chairs."

"They're bolted down. Mostly." The sheared bolt under Merek's chair rolled across the floor and bumped his shoe. "...Mostly."

"Perseus."

"I'm fine. Apollo patched it up. Look, it's fine." He flexed his fingers inside her grip. "And I dropped a glacier on them, so honestly, we're kind of even."

"You are not even." Her voice dropped, low and dangerous. The arrows hummed. "Nobody is ever even with you. Nobody touches you."

"I know."

"I don't think you do."

"Arty." He said it softly, only for her, and felt her hand tighten around his wrist until the bones creaked. "They're lost. They're a long way from home, and they were doing their job. And right now they're the best intel we have on something big. I need them talking, not skewered."

She looked at him for a long moment.

Then she looked past him at the five soldiers for the first time. It was a long, level, measuring look, the look of a huntress deciding whether something was prey or merely in the way.

One by one, the arrows went out like candles.

The frost on the windows didn't melt, and she didn't let go of his wrist.

"You five," Artemis said.

"Director," five voices answered at once.

Her eyes narrowed a fraction.

"...Director."

"Yes, Director."

Something flickered across her face. Her gaze slid to Percy and then to her brother. Apollo very pointedly found something fascinating on the ceiling.

"Understand me," she said to the soldiers. "I don't know you, and I don't know your Empire. But if any one of you so much as points at him again, I'll find you. In this century or any other one you run to."

Neumond swallowed audibly.

"Standing orders confirmed, Director."

Iane kicked him again.

Artemis finally loosened her grip, but only enough to slide her hand down so her fingers laced through his. Percy was fairly sure it was on purpose. She moved to stand at his shoulder, half a step in behind him, and slumped lazily on him with her arms around his chest.

"Continue," she said. "I'm listening."

Apollo paced the length of the room and back. Eleni stood at the window with her arms folded, watching frost that still refused to melt. Artemis stayed exactly where she was, draped over Percy's back with her chin hooked on his shoulder. "Four million," Apollo said at last. "Four million. Do you know how many demigods are at this camp?"

"From my augurs? 140-ish" Neumond said.

"I was being rhetorical."

Percy rubbed his face. "Okay. Help me with scale here. When you say carrier, I'm picturing an aircraft carrier."

Neumond laughed, then winced as it pulled at his stitches. "No."

"A UOE carrier is a little over four kilometers from bow to stern," Iane said. "It carries its own foundries. Once it lands and anchors, it can print a forward base within a week."

"A base the size of what?"

The five of them considered it.

"Your Manhattan," Straban said eventually. "Give or take."

Eleni didn't turn away from the window. "They will have started the Grid by now, milord. It's doctrine. The power lattice goes down first, then the foundries, then the stacks. Our architecture was designed for a war where everything has to be bigger than whatever is trying to kill it. Within a few months, a mortal with a backyard telescope will be able to see structures on Mars."

"Weeks," Merek said. "Command was already pouring the first pylons when we launched."

Apollo stopped pacing. "My sun shines on Mars. I should be able to see this myself."

"Can you?"

Apollo closed his eyes. The room warmed by a few degrees, and the frost on the windows beaded and ran. Artemis made a small displeased sound against Percy's neck. After a moment Apollo opened his eyes again, looking sour.

"I forgot. [[Ares]] keeps it shut. He always has. It's his planet, Phobos and Deimons, his sons, keep my vision obscured."

"The moon doesn't look at Mars," Artemis said, before anyone could ask her. "It never has."

"So the gods are blind," Percy said. "Who isn't?"

It was Eleni who answered. "The mortals."

"Their rover," Straban said. "On the second day, a mortal surface unit acquired our drone. It tracked the drone with its camera mast. The Major's orders were to sever its uplink, and we did."

Percy stared at him. "You cut NASA's feed."

"Is that what they're called?" Ione asked.

"We need to know what they know," Percy said. "And who they've told."

"I could walk into Houston," Apollo offered.

"And light up every Mist-sensitive mortal and every monster in Texas on the way in," Artemis said.

*I can do it faster,* Minerva said. *And nobody will ever learn we were there.*

Percy relayed it. Every eye in the room moved to him.

"How?"

*Their networks are primitive. That makes this harder, not easier. They are isolated in all the wrong places. The flight control floor in Houston will not accept a connection from Long Island. This camp also sits under a magical border and a wine god's haze, and both of them swallow signals.*

*So we need a satellite.*

*We need one that speaks to NASA every day. The Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter is ideal. It has circled the moon since 2009, and its ground link feeds into their mission network at Goddard.*

Percy was quiet for a second.

"The moon."

Everyone looked at Artemis.

She didn't lift her chin from his shoulder. "No."

"You don't know what I'm going to ask."

"You're going to ask me to let go."

*Hold out your hand, Varlet, Minerva said. The injured one. The tissue is still regenerating, so it will take less persuading.*

He held it out anyway, palm up.

For a moment nothing happened. Then a bead of blood welled from the center of the fresh pink scar. It came out cyan-yellow and too bright, gold-shot ichor marbled with thin filaments of yellow circuity. It gathered into a sphere, trembled, and folded in on itself over and over until it had become a tiny segmented thing the size of a grain of rice. Hair-fine cilia fanned out from one end and tasted the air.

Apollo was on his feet at once, leaning so close his nose nearly touched Percy's palm.

"That's a virus."

*A hacking biovirus,* Minerva corrected*. An organic carrier with my code folded into its proteins. It will eat its way into their hardware, rebuild whatever it needs from the satellite's own materials, and open a door for me. Six hours after it wakes up, it will die. I would prefer it did not live any longer than that.*

Percy repeated this, with less enthusiasm.

"That's restricted technology," Neumond said faintly. "Anywhere in the Empire, you'd need a Tribune's seal just to be in the same room as one of those."

"Welcome to the same room," Percy said.

Artemis sighed, long and deliberate, against the side of his neck. Then she unwound her arms from his chest one at a time, fingers trailing his shirt and hair. She picked the biovirus off his palm between two fingers and held it up to the light. The cilia turned toward her.

"If this bites me, Perseus, I'm feeding you to the Hounds."

*It might*

"She says it won't," Percy lied.

She pointed at the five Ekdromoi without looking at them.

"Stay. Don't move. Don't let any of them look at you."

Then she was gone in a hard silver flash that left the scent of snow in the air.

In lunar orbit there was no sound at all.

The moon turned below her, grey and pocked and endlessly familiar, its long shadows sharp enough to cut on. The Earth hung off to one side, blue and white and small. Artemis found the orbiter by feel before she saw it. It was a squat box of gold foil and struts with a single solar wing, humming faintly with the mortal electricity.

She settled onto its frame as lightly as a hawk on a branch.

"Yeah," Percy said. He stood too. "I guess we're about to find out if the Accord works."
