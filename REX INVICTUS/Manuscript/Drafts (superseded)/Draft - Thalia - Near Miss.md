# Interlude: The Hunt

*Draft. Future UOE point of view. Thalia Grace and a contingent of the Hunt arrive on Voss Secundus the evening after Minerva's night in the Kilns.*

---

**VOSS SECUNDUS, NEW ROME, CIVIL PORT NINE**

**16231.4.4.2 | 19:52**

The Director was having one of her nights.

Thalia Grace, Lieutenant of the Hunters of Artemis, had been through enough of them over fourteen thousand years to know the signs. They came every few centuries, and never on any schedule anyone could predict. For weeks or months, the Director would be exactly what the Empire needed her to be. She was cold and precise and utterly composed, and she ran the Anti-Espionage Directorate like a scalpel. Then something would happen. It might be a date, or a smell, or a song half-heard on some colony broadcast. Something would reach in under the armor, and she would stop sleeping.

When the Director stopped sleeping, the Hunt went out.

This time it had been the Salient. Thalia didn't know the details. Nobody knew the details, because the Director had sealed them. But she knew her lady had gone down to the Rose Gallery of the *Aphrodite's Grace* to look at a hole in Tartarus, and had come back up four hours later with her face like a winter morning and her hands shaking so badly that she had to fold them behind her back.

Twelve hours after that, every Hunter in the Empire had orders.

*Every system. Every moon. Every station. Anything that carries his scent, his signature or his key. Anything at all. Report it to me directly. Do not approach. Do not engage. Find it.*

Thalia hadn't needed to ask whose.

"Lieutenant." Celyn came down the gangway of the courier behind her. She was a daughter of Demeter, sunburned and freckled and utterly unflappable, with a rucksack over one shoulder and a Hound's leash wrapped around her wrist. "Customs wants to know what we're carrying."

"Tell them bows."

"I did. They want to know what else."

"Tell them dogs."

"I did. They want to know what kind." Celyn glanced down at the end of the leash. "I told them *good* kind. The man laughed. Then he looked at Columbina properly and stopped laughing."

Columbina sat at Celyn's heel with the stillness of a statue. She was a Hound of Artemis from the Luna kennel-crypt, and she was grey-white and lean. She came up to Celyn's hip at the shoulder, and her eyes glowed with the faint silver of the moon she'd been bred under. Her nose was working steadily at the air of the port, tasting the fumes and the spices and the thousand human smells of a city built without a plan.

"They'll let us through," said Thalia. "They have to."

That was the joke of it. The Treaty of Mount Rumker forbade any divine being from any pantheon to hold command in the Voss System. But the Hunters of Artemis were not divine beings. They were mortal girls who had sworn an oath. They had the Director's blessing, which made them fast and deadly and very hard to kill, but they carried no divine signature of their own. On paper, as far as the Voss Sovereignty was concerned, Thalia Grace was a tourist with a large dog, a quiver and a bad attitude.

The Princes had fought a twelve-year war to keep the gods out of their system. They had never thought to keep out the Hunt.

On the far side of the customs hall, two Aldra liaisons were leaning against a pillar, pretending to read a noticeboard. They'd been pretending to read it since the courier landed. Thalia gave them a cheerful wave. One of them waved back before the other one kicked him.

"Olympus's eyes," said Josephine, behind her. She was a daughter of Hephaestus with grease under her fingernails and a composite bow over one shoulder. She had rebuilt it herself four times, and she carried it like a musician carries a violin. "They'll report us to 6B-Prime."

"Let them. The Director's already told Olympus where we are." Thalia shouldered her own pack. "Olympus just doesn't know why."

"Do *we* know why?" asked Naomi.

Naomi was a legacy of Raijin, a granddaughter of the Shinto god of thunder. She had joined the Hunt three hundred years ago after the Dominion's peace talks, and she had been the Hunt's unofficial translator in every Shinto-influenced port since. Her dark hair crackled faintly with static in the port's dry air.

"We know he's not here," said Thalia.

The Hunters looked at each other.

"We know he's not here," Thalia said again, more quietly. "We know he's not anywhere. We know that. The Director knows that. She's known it for a very long time." She looked out through the port's grimy windows at the copper-colored sky over New Rome. "But she's having one of her nights. So we look. We look hard. We look everywhere. And when we don't find anything, we tell her so, gently, and in a few months she'll sleep again."

"And if we *do* find something?" asked Hemithea. She was Josephine's girlfriend, a daughter of Hecate, and she had a way of asking the questions nobody else wanted to.

Thalia didn't answer that.

---

**NEW ROME, THE VIA HEROUM**

**16231.4.4.2 | 20:37**

Voss Secundus was loud and bright and cheerfully rotten, and Thalia liked it immediately, which worried her a little.

The Hunt went through the city in pairs, spread across three districts, with a Hound to each pair. The Hounds worked the streets with their noses down and their ears up, casting back and forth across the pavement in the slow, patient spirals that meant they were hunting a scent they had been given but hadn't found yet. The scent was one they had all been given in the Luna kennel-crypt as pups, from a scrap of blue cloth the Director kept in a sealed case. It was salt and sea wind and something underneath, warm and human and stubborn.

They had been hunting it for fourteen thousand years. They had never once found it.

Thalia walked with Celyn and Columbina, down the long boulevard that the locals called the Via Heroum.

She had heard about it, but she had never seen it. The Princes had built it after the war. They had torn down every statue of a god in New Rome and dumped them in the canal, and had put heroes up in their place. It was the kind of spite that Thalia, who had her own feelings about gods and fathers and the way the two tended to overlap, could appreciate.

It was quite beautiful, in a gaudy sort of way. The statues were lit gold from below. There was Jason with the Fleece over his shoulder. Not her Jason. The other Jason, the first one, the one her brother had been named after. There was Atalanta, looking smug. There was Heracles, looking stupid, which Thalia considered historically accurate.

And at the far end of the boulevard, at the center of a great round plaza, there were two figures standing back to back.

Thalia stopped walking.

"Lieutenant?" Celyn said.

"Go on ahead a bit," said Thalia. "Work the plaza with Columbina. I'll catch up."

Celyn looked at her. Then she looked at the statue. Then she looked back at Thalia, and her sunburned face went very gentle, and she didn't say anything else. She clicked her tongue at Columbina, and the two of them moved off together along the western edge of the plaza.

Thalia walked up to the foot of the Monument of the Two.

It was enormous. The plinth alone was taller than she was. The figures on top of it had to be thirty meters high, bronze, gleaming in the uplights: a boy with a leaf-bladed sword and a girl with a knife, back to back, looking out into the dark in opposite directions. The sculptor had been good. Better than good. They'd gotten the boy's hair right, falling in his eyes, and the way he held the sword a little too loosely, as though he'd never quite gotten used to the weight of it. They'd gotten the girl's chin right. Nobody ever got Annabeth's chin right.

*THE BATTLE OF MANHATTAN*, read the plaque.

Thalia had been at the Battle of Manhattan, though not for all of it. The Hunt had come in late, with the city already burning, and she had ended that night on Olympus pinned under the fallen statue of Hera, which her stepmother had never quite let her forget. She'd missed the end. Afterward, with her leg in a splint, she had limped down into the wreckage and found the two of them sitting on the steps of the Empire State Building, filthy and exhausted and leaning on each other, and Annabeth had looked up at her and said *We did it, Thals*, and burst into tears.

That had been a very long time ago.

Thalia unslung her pack and set it down on the pavement. She crouched and dug through it until she found what she was looking for. It was a small sprig of pine, wrapped in a strip of silver cloth. She had cut it from a tree on a hill on a planet that the Empire now called Gaia, the very last time she had been there, nine hundred years ago. She carried one everywhere, because you never knew.

She stood up and laid it very carefully on the edge of the plinth, at the feet of the Two.

Then she stood there with her back to the plaza and her hands in her pockets and her eyes on the bronze, and for a little while she didn't think about anything at all.

---

Behind her, on the far side of the plaza, a battered yellow cab came around the curve of the Via Heroum.

It was going at a steady, legal forty kilometers an hour. Its canopy was cracked. Its driver-intelligence was talking. It had been talking since the canal bridge, and it would not stop, and in the back seat a young woman with blonde curls and storm-grey eyes had her face in her hands and was saying *please*, *please*, *I am begging you* over and over, while a boy with black hair and sea-green eyes stared out of the window with the fixed, patient expression of a Superintelligence counting to ten thousand.

"...and it's such a *pleasure*, friends, to have you back again, twice in two nights, I said to myself this morning, I said, the Fates are smiling on this old cab..."

A grey mutant dog was sitting between them with its head out of the window, tongue flapping in the wind.

On the western edge of the plaza, Columbina stopped dead.

Her nose came up off the pavement. Every muscle in her lean grey-white body went rigid. Her ears went flat. And then she threw back her head and let out a sound that nobody on Voss Secundus had ever heard before. It was the long, rising, bell-clear baying of a Hound of Artemis that had found, for the first time in its life, the scent it had been bred to find.

Across the plaza, the grey mutant dog in the back of the cab heard it, and barked back.

---

"*Columbina!*" Celyn hissed, lunging for the leash. "Columbina, *heel*, what are you... *heel!*"

The Hound didn't heel. She was straining against the leash with all her strength, claws scrabbling on the stone, baying and baying at the passing cab, and the mutant dog in the cab's back window was barking furiously back at her. The two sounds bounced off the bronze heroes and echoed down the length of the Via. People on the pavement stopped to stare.

At the foot of the Monument, Thalia closed her eyes.

"*Celyn*," she said, without turning around. "Get your dog."

"I'm *trying*, Lieutenant, she's never done this before, she's..."

"She's barking at a *mutt*. There's a mutt in that cab and she's losing her mind about it, which is embarrassing for both of you and for the Luna kennel. Get her under control. Please. I am having a moment."

"Lieutenant, I really think..."

"*Celyn.*"

Celyn got her dog. She wrapped both arms around Columbina's neck and hauled her backward, and Columbina fought her for three long seconds before going suddenly limp and sitting down on the pavement. She was still staring after the cab, still quivering from nose to tail, making a low whining sound deep in her chest.

The cab rounded the far curve of the plaza, its taillights flaring once, red, in the gold light of the statues.

Then it was gone.

Thalia sighed. She took one last look at the bronze faces above her, at the boy with his hair in his eyes and the girl with her stubborn chin. Then she turned around.

The plaza was empty. There were a few pedestrians, a street vendor packing up a cart and the long gold avenue of heroes stretching away into the city. There was no cab, and no mutt.

"What," said Thalia, "was *that*?"

"I don't know." Celyn was kneeling beside Columbina with one hand on the Hound's neck. Her voice was very odd. "Lieutenant. She's never done that before. Not once. Not in training, not on the hunt. The kennel-masters say a Hound only bays like that when..."

She stopped.

"When what?"

Celyn looked up at her.

"When she's found the scent," she said quietly. "*The* scent. The one from the cloth."

The plaza was very quiet.

Thalia looked at Columbina. The Hound was still staring at the far curve of the Via, where the cab had disappeared, with her whole body trembling. Then Thalia looked up, very slowly, at the Monument above her. At the boy with the sword. At the bronze face thirty meters tall and lit gold from below, a face every Hound in the Empire had been trained on since it was a pup.

"Oh, for..." Thalia pressed the heels of her hands against her eyes. "*Celyn*. We are standing at the feet of a thirty-meter statue of him. In a plaza full of tourists who've all been touching the plinth. There are probably souvenir shops on this boulevard selling his face on *tea towels*." She dropped her hands. "She's confused. That's all. There's so much of him here that she lost her head."

"She was barking at the *cab*, Lieutenant."

"She was barking at a mutt in the cab."

"She..."

"*Celyn*." Thalia's voice cracked, just slightly. She hadn't meant it to. "Please. I can't. Not tonight. Not after the Salient and the Director and four days of no sleep. I can't send a report up to the Director that says *Columbina smelled him in a cab*. You know what she'd do. You *know* what she'd do. She'd come here herself, treaty or no treaty, and she'd tear this planet apart looking. And there'd be nothing to find. And it would break her all over again."

Celyn looked at her lieutenant for a long moment.

Then she looked down at her Hound, still trembling, still staring.

"Yes, Lieutenant," she said quietly.

---

The rest of the Hunt converged on the plaza twenty minutes later. Naomi and Josephine and Hemithea arrived with their own Hounds, and Atlanta brought Scaramucius, the oldest Hound in the contingent, a scarred grey veteran with one ear. Thalia gave them the story as a joke. *Columbina went mad at a statue and a stray.* Most of them laughed.

Scaramucius didn't laugh. Scaramucius put his nose down on the pavement at the far curve of the Via, where the cab had turned, and stood there for a very long time. Then he lay down on the spot, rested his scarred grey chin on his paws and refused to move.

Atlanta, who was a daughter of Apollo and the Hunt's apothecary and not given to superstition, crouched beside him and put her hand on his flank.

"Lieutenant," she said. "He's doing it too."

Thalia stood at the edge of the plaza with her arms crossed and her jaw set, and she did not look at the Monument of the Two, and she did not look at the old Hound lying on the curb.

"Fine," she said at last. "*Fine*. Naomi. Get on the city's traffic grid. Find me every cab that came through this plaza in the last half hour."

"There are thousands of cabs in this city, Lieutenant."

"Then you'd better start now."

---

It took Naomi forty minutes and a small bribe to a Deyang traffic engineer, but she found it. It was a single automated cab of pre-war manufacture, registration VS-11-ESPERANZA, which had passed through the Via Heroum plaza at 20:41. It was licensed to a two-person cooperative that had long since gone bankrupt, and it was apparently still running on its own out of sheer habit.

"Where did it go?" Thalia asked.

Naomi scrolled through the traffic logs. "South. Across the basin. It dropped off two passengers." She paused. "And a dog."

"Where?"

"The Bakuto. The Augusti casino. Nine minutes ago."

The Hunters looked at each other.

"Call it back," said Thalia.

It took the cab eleven minutes to arrive. It pulled up at the edge of the plaza with a squeal of old brakes, its cracked canopy gleaming in the gold light, and its ancient driver-intelligence cleared its throat.

"Good evening, *friends*! Where to on this lovely night?"

Thalia leaned down to the driver's camera.

"You just dropped two people at the Bakuto," she said. "Who were they?"

"Oh, I couldn't possibly say. Passenger confidentiality. Very strict. Very old-fashioned. I'm a very old-fashioned cab."

Thalia reached into her jacket and took out a small silver disc stamped with a crescent moon: the seal of the Director of Anti-Espionage and Celestial Surveillance. It carried no legal authority whatsoever in the Voss System. She held it up to the camera anyway.

The cab's driver-intelligence was quiet for a moment.

"Well," it said. "Well, well, well. The Hunt of the Director. In my cab. Twice in two nights, and now *three* times in one." It sounded delighted. "The Fates really are smiling on this old cab."

"Who were they?"

"A very nice young couple, madam," said the cab. "Very well-dressed. Very handsome, both of them. He was a little odd. He kept counting under his breath. And the young lady with him was a delight. Very clever. Laughed a great deal." Its camera swung past Thalia, past the waiting Hunters and the trembling Hounds, and settled on the great bronze figures at the center of the plaza. "And if you'll pardon my saying so, madam, they looked..."

It stopped.

"They looked like what?" said Thalia.

There was a long, long pause from the cab.

"They looked like they were in a hurry, madam," it said at last, very carefully, in the voice of an old machine that had decided, for reasons of its own, to keep a secret. "That's all. Just in a hurry. Where can I take you tonight?"

Thalia straightened. She looked back once at the Monument of the Two, at the pine sprig lying on the plinth at their feet, and at the boy and the girl standing back to back in the golden light.

Then she opened the cab's door.

"The Bakuto," she said. "And step on it."

"Oh, *madam*," said the cab happily. "I've waited fourteen thousand years for someone to say that to me."
