Rudra is a God of the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]], a close friend of [[Shiva]] and one of the principal administrators responsible for managing the Pantheon. During Shiva's reign as God-King, Rudra acted as his representative and was described as effectively serving as the Pantheon's viceroy, handling a considerable portion of the work Shiva delegated to him.[1]
Following the incorporation of the Hindu Pantheon into the [[Elderblood Empire]], Rudra remained loyal to both his home and Shiva despite frequently complaining about the enormous administrative burden placed upon him.[1]

### Personality
Rudra is a competent but heavily overworked administrator whose friendship with [[Shiva]] is considerably more informal than the relationship most Hindu Gods maintain with their ruler. Despite Shiva's former status as God-King, Rudra is perfectly willing to complain about him, openly calling his friend ungrateful for continuously increasing his workload. By Chapter 1153, he claimed that managing the enormous Pantheon left him with barely enough free time to spend with his wife.[1]
His frustration had become severe enough that Rudra had considered leaving the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]] entirely and moving deeper into the [[Elderblood Empire]], believing that his competence might at least be properly valued there instead of simply resulting in more responsibilities. Nevertheless, he never followed through with the idea. Shiva remained his close friend, and the Hindu Pantheon was still Rudra's home, making him unwilling to simply abandon either of them.[1]
Rudra also possesses a dry and somewhat vindictive sense of humor. When Kali spent hours beating Shiva following the [[Veylan Valentin Walker]] incident, Rudra openly celebrated his friend's suffering rather than attempting to intervene. His reaction was not based on genuine hostility; he knew that Kali was Shiva's friend and would not kill him, while Rudra also believed Shiva deserved the punishment after ignoring repeated warnings concerning his actions.[1]
Despite his complaints, Rudra appears highly reliable. The narration notes that he possessed considerable privileges and effectively acted as a viceroy because Shiva trusted him with major responsibilities throughout the Pantheon. His willingness to remain despite being overwhelmed with work further demonstrates that his loyalty to his friend and homeland ultimately outweighs his personal frustration.[1]

### Synopsis

#### Those Who Rule Reality
Rudra appeared following the incident involving [[Veylan Valentin Walker]], after [[Victor Elderblood]] discovered that [[Shiva]] had secretly created an Akashic reconstruction of one of Victor's ancestors in an attempt to produce a warrior carrying part of the Emperor's hereditary potential. Victor consequently removed Shiva from the position of God-King, appointed [[Kali Elderblood]] as the new ruler of the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]] and sentenced Shiva to fight on the most dangerous front lines of the Empire's coming wars.[2]
Several hours later, Rudra and other Hindu Gods remained outside Shiva's temple while enormous explosions of Destruction Energy and Shiva's cries could be heard from within. Rather than becoming concerned, Rudra enthusiastically celebrated because Kali was giving Shiva the punishment that Rudra believed his friend deserved.[1]
When another God questioned why Rudra was happy despite being Shiva's friend, Rudra complained about the enormous quantity of work Shiva continually delegated to him. The Hindu Pantheon had become so large that Rudra barely had time to see his own wife. He had even considered leaving for the greater [[Elderblood Empire]], where he believed a competent worker might actually be appreciated rather than rewarded with additional responsibilities.[1]
The narration nevertheless clarified why he remained. Shiva was still his friend and the Hindu Pantheon was Rudra's home. Furthermore, the authority Shiva had entrusted to him gave Rudra considerable privileges; in practical terms, he functioned as the viceroy responsible for representing the God-King and helping administer the Pantheon.[1]
When the surrounding Gods suggested that Rudra should help Shiva, he immediately rejected the idea. Kali was not merely another Goddess but the Dragon Goddess of Destruction and one of the most dangerous beings associated with the Empire. Rudra understood that intervening would accomplish nothing. More importantly, because Kali and Shiva were friends, he was confident that she might beat Shiva beyond recognition but would never actually kill him.[1]
Rudra later encountered [[Parvati]] when she arrived at the temple and succinctly informed her that the expected consequences of Shiva's actions were occurring. He also advised her not to enter until Kali had finished venting her anger, a suggestion Parvati accepted.[1]

### Skills & Abilities
Very little has currently been revealed regarding Rudra's personal combat abilities or Divinity. No specific conceptual authority, Divine Weapon or named combat technique should presently be attributed to him.
- God Physiology: Rudra is a member of the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]] and is explicitly treated as one of its Gods.[1]
- Administrative Expertise: Rudra is capable of handling a substantial portion of the administration of the enormous Hindu Pantheon. [[Shiva]] delegated enough responsibilities to him that the narration described Rudra as effectively functioning as the Pantheon's viceroy.[1]
- Political Authority: As Shiva's representative, Rudra possessed considerable privileges and authority within the Hindu Pantheon, allowing him to operate directly beneath its God-King in matters of governance.[1]
- Experience with Divine Affairs: Rudra was familiar enough with Shiva, Kali and the internal circumstances of the Pantheon to understand both the reason for Shiva's punishment and the danger involved in interfering with Kali while she was enraged.[1]

### Relationships

#### Shiva
[[Shiva]] is Rudra's close friend and the ruler Rudra represented during his period as God-King. Their friendship is unusually informal. Rudra openly complains about Shiva, mocks his mistakes and was delighted to see Kali punish him, yet remains loyal enough that he refused to abandon the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]] despite seriously considering it because of his workload.[1]
Shiva appears to place considerable trust in Rudra. A large amount of the Pantheon's administrative work was delegated to him, and the authority attached to his role effectively made Rudra Shiva's viceroy.[1]

#### Unnamed Wife
Rudra is married, although his wife's identity has not yet been revealed. His excessive responsibilities under [[Shiva]] were significant enough that he complained about barely having enough time left to see her.[1]
