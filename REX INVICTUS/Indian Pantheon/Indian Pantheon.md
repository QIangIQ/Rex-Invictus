**[[Indian Pantheon]]** is the family of gods of Hindu and Vedic tradition.

---

## Mythology (IRL)

The Vedic hymns (about 1500 to 1000 BC) center on Indra the storm-king, Agni the fire, Soma, Varuna and Mitra. Classical Hinduism organizes the divine around the Trimurti of Brahma the creator, Vishnu the preserver, whose avatars include Rama and Krishna, and Shiva the destroyer, together with the Goddess (Devi) in forms such as [[Parvati]], Durga and Kali. The great epics, the *Mahabharata* and the *Ramayana*, and the Puranas are the main mythic sources. The gods (devas) war with the asuras, much as the [[Olympians]] fought the [[Titans]].

The Hindu Pantheon is one of the principal Divine Pantheons of [[Yggdrasil]] and the traditional supernatural authority associated with India. Its Heavenly territory is centered around Svarga, while its wider Divine system historically included the separate Hindu [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] ruled by Yama. The Pantheon contains several exceptionally powerful Gods, most notably [[Shiva]] and Kali, whose authority over Destruction made the faction one of the strongest military Pantheons remaining on Earth before the establishment of the [[Elderblood Empire]].[1]
For much of the modern era the official God-King was [[Indra]], Lord of Svarga and God of the Skies, Lightning, Thunder, Storms, Rain and War. In practice, however, Indra's political authority was repeatedly overshadowed by Shiva, whose personal strength, age, reputation and influence within the Pantheon greatly exceeded his own. By the period following the destruction of the [[Norse Pantheon]], the internal leadership of the Hindu Gods had effectively become divided between the nominal God-King and the far more respected God of Destruction.[2]
This arrangement formally ended when Shiva decided to take direct control. Indra was quietly removed from office and Shiva became the Pantheon's actual God-King shortly before [[Victor Elderblood]] began the final unification of Earth.[3]
The Hindu Pantheon initially attempted to approach Victor through political cooperation rather than submission. Shiva correctly concluded that Earth's existing Divine order could not realistically prevent Victor's rise and attempted to secure an alliance before the situation became irreversible.[3] The effort ultimately failed because the difference in power had become too great for Victor to consider them equals. During the Imperial conquest, Victor entered the Pantheon personally and offered Shiva and Kali only two choices: submit to the Empire or be destroyed and have their Divinities repurposed.[4]
Shiva surrendered. Victor subsequently annexed all Gods, resources, territory and history belonging to the Hindu Pantheon into the Elderblood Empire. Unlike hostile Pantheons that were dismantled, however, the Hindu Pantheon was granted the same political arrangement as the Shinto Pantheon: it retained internal self-government while becoming obligated to obey Imperial summons and recognize Victor's sovereignty.[5]

### Divine Structure
The Hindu Pantheon historically consisted of more than a single Heavenly government. Its Divine civilization was divided between the Gods associated with Svarga and the independent infernal administration of the Hindu [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]].
Unlike smaller Divine factions lacking complete afterlife systems, the Hindu Pantheon possessed both a Heavenly God-King and a separate Ruler responsible for its Hell.
A simplified historical structure can be represented as:

The political connection between the two sides was not always stable. During the Demonic War, the current Yama separated his Hell from the Pantheon's Heavenly administration, preventing Indra from properly reconnecting the Hindu afterlife with Svarga.[6][7]

### Svarga
Svarga is the principal Heavenly territory of the Hindu Pantheon and historically served as the seat of its God-King.
Under [[Indra]], Svarga represented the formal political center of Hindu Divine authority. Indra held the traditional position of God-King and Lord of Svarga, but the title did not automatically make him the strongest God within the Pantheon.
That distinction belonged primarily to [[Shiva]] and Kali. Both possessed Divine authority connected to the extremely high-ranking Concept of Destruction, and neither behaved as a conventional subordinate of Indra.[2]
By Chapter 970, the narration directly described Shiva and Kali as effectively autonomous Beings who rarely followed Indra's orders. Indra understood that simply possessing the title of God-King did not give him the power necessary to force either God of Destruction to obey him.[2]
Shiva's influence was particularly overwhelming. Although Indra continued functioning as the official ruler for thousands of years, Shiva later explained that his own influence had existed within the Pantheon from the beginning. He had simply allowed someone else to perform the administrative responsibilities associated with ruling it.[3]

### Hindu Underworld
The Hindu [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] is the infernal side of the Pantheon's cosmological system and was ruled by the title-bearing Demon King known as Yama.
Unlike Svarga's Gods, the Underworld contained Asuras, Demons and other Negative-aligned Beings. Its Ruler possessed independent authority over the Realm and its inhabitants.
The position of Yama was not permanently attached to one individual. By the Demonic War, the current Yama was the grandson of a previous Yama whom he had replaced after taking the throne.[8]
During the conflict surrounding [[Diablo]], the Hindu Underworld became politically separated from the Heavenly Pantheon. Yama sided with the demonic coalition and severed the proper connection between his Hell and the Hindu Divine system, creating problems for [[Indra]] and the Pantheon.[6]
[[Shiva]] eventually began taking measures to correct the instability caused by Indra's administration, with even Yama becoming aware that Shiva had begun moving to prevent the God-King's incompetence from further affecting the Pantheon.[7]
The Underworld's political identity changed further during the Imperial era. Yama ultimately chose to pledge loyalty directly to Violet Elderblood and the wider Dragon's Nest structure rather than remain stagnant beneath his old arrangements. He accepted beginning from the bottom of the Imperial hierarchy in exchange for the opportunity to continue progressing.[9]

### Power Structure

#### Indra's Rule
[[Indra]] served as the recognized God-King of the Hindu Pantheon for thousands of years. He administered Svarga and represented the Pantheon during international Divine affairs.
His authority, however, was structurally unusual because the faction contained Gods considerably stronger than himself.
By the era following Ragnarok, the Pantheon's effective leadership had become divided between Indra and [[Shiva]]. Indra possessed the official title and administrative position, while Shiva possessed the greater strength, prestige and respect among the Gods.[2]
The imbalance increasingly frustrated Indra. He believed that Shiva's influence had reduced him to a God-King in name only, and he repeatedly sought opportunities to increase his own authority.
Indra's political calculations were additionally constrained by Kali. Like Shiva, Kali was sufficiently powerful and independent that Indra could not simply order her to act according to his wishes.[2]

#### Shiva's Influence
[[Shiva]] was one of the oldest and strongest Gods associated with the Hindu Pantheon and historically possessed far greater actual military power than its nominal ruler.
His presence served as an important stabilizing factor. Even hostile forces familiar with the Pantheon believed that [[Indra]]'s mistakes would not necessarily destroy the faction because Shiva would eventually intervene if the situation became sufficiently dangerous.[7]
Shiva himself later confirmed that he had never truly lacked influence. He had simply allowed Indra to perform the administrative work associated with God-Kingship because he had little interest in dealing with those responsibilities personally.[3]
This changed as Earth's political situation deteriorated and [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s expansion accelerated.

#### Kali
Kali represented the Pantheon's second major pillar of military strength. Her mastery of Destruction became sufficiently advanced that by Chapter 1063 [[Shiva]] recognized that she possessed the necessary Power to take another major step in her existence if she chose to do so.[3]
Kali remained highly loyal to her home even after developing a comparatively friendly relationship with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] and receiving training that dramatically strengthened her. She understood, however, that the gap between Dragon's Nest and the Hindu Pantheon had become so great that conventional military or political leverage no longer mattered.[3]

### Military Strength
Before [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] completely surpassed Earth's existing Pantheons, the Hindu Pantheon possessed the greatest publicly known military firepower among the conventional Pantheons primarily because of [[Shiva]] and Kali.[1]
This position did not mean every Hindu God individually stood above their equivalents in other Pantheons. The faction's exceptional status depended disproportionately upon its two Gods of Destruction.
During his discussion with Kali, Shiva summarized the Pantheon's military assets as including:
- two top-tier Gods of Destruction;
- hundreds of high-level Warrior Gods;
- and a substantial Divine army.[3]
Shiva believed this military strength could make the Pantheon valuable enough to negotiate an alliance with Victor.
Kali disagreed with the assumption. By that period Victor already possessed overwhelming military forces, enormous political influence and the capacity to create or develop additional powerful Beings. The only meaningful asset the Hindu Pantheon could truly offer was loyalty.[3]

### Demonic War
The Pantheon was indirectly affected by the large-scale instability produced during the Demonic War.
The Hindu [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] had become separated from the Heavenly administration under Yama, while [[Indra]] failed to properly bring the Realm back under unified Pantheon control.[6]
During the war, [[Diablo]] regarded the Hindu Gods as unreliable because of these internal problems but acknowledged that [[Shiva]]'s presence made complete collapse unlikely. Shiva was expected to eventually take action if Indra allowed the situation to deteriorate too far.[7]
Yama independently participated in the conflict through his own alliances and confronted [[Vlad III Tepes|Vlad Tepes]]. His activities further demonstrated that the Hindu Underworld could act separately from Svarga rather than operating as a completely obedient extension of Indra's government.[8][7]

### Acquisition of the Norse Pantheon
Following the destruction of [[Odin]]'s regime, Hela obtained ownership of the remaining [[Norse Pantheon]] and decided to sell its entire Divine territory rather than rule it herself.[10]
The surviving major Pantheons gathered to negotiate for ownership.
[[Indra]] initially considered simply attacking Hela and taking the Pantheon by force, reasoning that the Hindu Pantheon possessed [[Shiva]] and Kali. He immediately abandoned the idea after remembering that neither God of Destruction was obligated to follow his orders and that violating the supernatural non-aggression agreement would have brought consequences upon his entire faction.[2]
Shiva approached the acquisition from a longer-term perspective. He did not immediately need the Norse territory, but understood that additional Pantheon territory could become extraordinarily valuable once Earth's Sector evolved and its civilizations began interacting with more powerful external societies.[2]
After negotiations, Shiva ultimately exchanged several extraordinarily valuable Divine resources for ownership of the Norse Pantheon's key. Hela handed him control after the Contract was completed.[11][12]
As a result, the Hindu Pantheon acquired the former Norse territories, including [[Asgard]]. By Chapter 986, the Hindu Pantheon was explicitly described as having become the second Pantheon in Authority after Dragon's Nest.[13]

### Decline of Indra
Despite the Pantheon's growing territorial influence, [[Indra]]'s personal political position continued deteriorating.
By Chapter 986, [[Shiva]]'s popularity and authority had become so great that when members of the Pantheon were asked who their God-King was, many would answer Shiva instead of Indra.[13]
Indra attempted to recover influence through [[Ganesha]]. After [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s actions began restoring Earth's environment, Ganesha was involved in an attempt to claim Divine credit for accomplishments actually produced by Victor.
[[Kaguya Elderblood Blank]] responded by entering the Hindu Pantheon directly and destroying Ganesha in front of its Gods.[13]
Neither Indra nor Shiva intervened because attacking one of Victor's wives would have brought the entirety of Dragon's Nest against the Pantheon.
The incident further damaged Indra's political position. Shiva correctly understood that Victor's faction had deliberately left the Pantheon intact partly because doing so strengthened Shiva's argument for taking greater internal control.[13]

### Rise of Shiva
The transformation from [[Indra]]'s nominal rule to [[Shiva]]'s direct leadership became official in Chapter 1063.
Recognizing how rapidly [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s influence was spreading across Earth, Shiva decided to stop remaining a mostly passive power within his own Pantheon.
Indra officially presented the transition as voluntarily abandoning his position in favor of a more experienced God. The narration immediately clarified that this was merely political language: Indra had effectively been expelled from power.[3]
The transfer occurred quickly and quietly. Only the Pantheon's upper leadership initially knew that Indra had lost his authority, allowing Shiva to conceal the political transition from foreign spies.[3]
Shiva subsequently explained that he had never really lost control. His influence had always been present; Indra had simply handled the inconvenience of formal leadership on his behalf.[3]
From that point onward, Shiva became the Pantheon's effective and official God-King.

### Attempted Alliance with the Elderblood Empire
After consolidating the Pantheon, [[Shiva]] sought diplomatic contact with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s representatives.
His reasoning was simple: Victor's expansion had reached a point where the existing Pantheons could no longer realistically prevent Earth from falling under his rule. Shiva concluded that resisting the process would only create unnecessary losses.[3]
Rather than oppose Victor, he wanted the Hindu Pantheon to enter the coming political order early enough to minimize the cost of submission and benefit from the future expansion of the Empire.
Shiva initially believed an alliance remained possible because the Hindu Pantheon possessed significant military strength.
Kali correctly understood the deeper problem: Victor no longer needed their army, political influence or resources. Dragon's Nest already possessed all of those advantages on a vastly greater scale.[3]
The only thing Shiva could genuinely offer Victor was the Pantheon's loyalty.

### Temple of Destruction
The Temple of Destruction is the personal Divine Temple belonging to Kali. It exists upon a floating island surrounded by a vast celestial ocean and clouds.[3]
Despite belonging to a Goddess embodying Destruction, the Temple possesses an unusually peaceful and sacred appearance.
It is constructed primarily from gleaming white marble and contains enormous columns supporting a vaulted ceiling decorated with Divine carvings and sacred imagery. Multicolored light enters through large stained-glass windows, while Divine Flowers fill the halls with a subtle fragrance.[3]
At the center of the Temple is a large crystal altar radiating [[Divine Energy]]. Behind it stands a fountain containing clear, luminous water.[3]
Kali used the Temple as a place for meditation and Divine development. When [[Shiva]] visited her shortly before his takeover of the Pantheon, she was floating approximately thirty centimeters above the ground in meditation while continuing to refine her Destruction.[3]
The Temple should not be confused with the central governmental seat of the Hindu Pantheon. It is specifically identified as Kali's personal sacred Temple.

### Imperial Conquest
[[Shiva]]'s attempt to negotiate cooperation ultimately became irrelevant once [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] began the final unification of Earth.
The [[Blood God Religion]] and Imperial forces systematically eliminated organizations and Divine institutions that remained outside Victor's authority. Hindu-controlled territories were not exempt from this process.[4]
Shiva correctly recognized the campaign as an organized purge intended to destroy potential future opposition before the creation of the unified Empire.
Victor eventually entered the Hindu Pantheon's Divine territory personally.
His unrestricted presence was powerful enough that the Pantheon's defensive barriers provided almost no meaningful resistance. High-Level and [[Primordial Gods]] struggled beneath his presence, while weaker Gods died simply from being exposed to the pressure of his unrestrained existence.[4]
Victor arrived supported by overwhelming Imperial forces, including his Heralds, Demon armies, Dragon Goddesses and enormous Leviathans. The smallest visible Leviathans were already comparable in size to mountains.[4]
He offered Shiva and Kali two choices:
- Surrender and allow the Hindu Pantheon to be annexed into the Empire;
- or fight, die and have their Divine authorities repurposed for the Empire.[4]
Kali attempted to raise the possibility of their previous alliance proposal, but Victor rejected it. In his view an alliance required relatively equal parties, and the Hindu Pantheon no longer possessed enough power or political standing to meet that condition.[4]
Shiva ultimately surrendered.

### Annexation
Following [[Shiva]]'s surrender, [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] formally incorporated the Hindu Pantheon into the [[Elderblood Empire]].
Victor explicitly declared:
- all Hindu Gods would be annexed;
- all resources belonging to the Pantheon would belong to the Empire;
- its history would become part of the Empire;
- and all of its territories would be incorporated under Imperial sovereignty.[5]
The Pantheon was not completely dismantled.
Victor instead ordered it to receive the same treatment as the [[Shinto Pantheon]]. The Hindu Gods were permitted to retain internal self-government, but were required to answer the Empire whenever summoned.[5]

### Imperial Era
After the annexation of the Hindu Pantheon, [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] continued the unification of every major supernatural organization on Earth.
The first year of the [[Imperial Calendar]] formally marked the end of Earth's old political order. All major Pantheons and supernatural races were incorporated into the [[Elderblood Empire]].[5]
The Hindu Pantheon consequently ceased functioning as an independent international faction.
[[Shiva]] remained its internal God-King, but the ultimate political and military authority belonged to Victor Elderblood and his Empire.
The Pantheon's former autonomy therefore became administrative autonomy rather than sovereignty.

### Relationship with Dragon's Nest
The Hindu Pantheon's relationship with [[Dragons Nest Pantheon]] developed through several stages.

#### Rival Power
Before [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] completely eclipsed Earth's Gods, the Hindu Pantheon was one of the few factions possessing enough military strength to be considered a serious global power. [[Shiva]] and Kali made direct aggression against it exceptionally dangerous.[1]

#### Cooperation
[[Shiva]] supported [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] during several major supernatural crises and increasingly recognized him as the central force determining Earth's future.
By the era following the fall of [[Asgard]], Shiva no longer considered a direct war with Dragon's Nest a realistic option.[14]
When [[Indra]] finally witnessed the magnitude of Victor's later Dragon form, Shiva compared their position to Mortals confronting a force of nature: Victor was something they needed to adapt around rather than fight.[14]

#### Attempted Alliance
After officially assuming leadership, [[Shiva]] attempted to secure a formal alliance with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]].[3]

#### Submission
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] rejected equal diplomatic treatment and annexed the Pantheon into the Empire.[4][5]

#### Current Status
The Hindu Pantheon now exists beneath Dragon's Nest and the [[Elderblood Empire]] while retaining its own God-King and internal Divine administration.

### Known Members

#### Current Leader
- [[Shiva]] — Current God-King of the Hindu Pantheon and one of its oldest and most powerful Gods. Holder of the Divine Concept of Destruction. Shiva historically allowed [[Indra]] to manage the Pantheon before formally taking direct control in Chapter 1063.[3]

#### Former Leader
- [[Indra]] — Former God-King and Lord of Svarga. God associated with the Skies, Lightning, Thunder, Storms, Rain and War. His political authority gradually diminished as [[Shiva]]'s influence increased, and he was eventually removed from office.[2][3]

#### Major Gods
- Kali — Goddess of Destruction and one of the Pantheon's two greatest historical military assets. Her Divine authority encompasses Destruction and other Negative-associated powers. Her mastery became sufficiently advanced that [[Shiva]] acknowledged she possessed the power necessary to advance even further if she chose.[3]
- [[Parvati]] — Goddess associated with Marriage and Love and Shiva's principal wife.[15]
- Brahma — Ancient Creator God associated with Creation. His exact administrative role within the modern Hindu Pantheon has not been directly established in the currently available chapters.
- [[Ganesha]] — God associated with Protection and the removal of obstacles. One incarnation was destroyed by [[Kaguya Elderblood Blank]] after participating in an attempt to take credit for [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s restoration of Earth's environment.[13]

#### Hindu Underworld
- Yama — Ruler of the Hindu [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]], King of the Asuras and bearer of the title of Yama. The current Yama historically separated the Underworld from the main Pantheon and later pledged direct loyalty to Dragon's Nest during the Imperial era.[8][9]

### Former and Acquired Territories

#### Svarga
The Pantheon's original principal Heavenly Realm and traditional seat of its God-King.

#### Hindu Underworld
The corresponding Hell associated with Hindu Souls and Asuras. Although cosmologically part of the larger Hindu Divine system, it repeatedly demonstrated political independence from Svarga.

#### Former Norse Pantheon
[[Shiva]] acquired ownership of the surviving [[Norse Pantheon]] from Hela following Ragnarok.[12]
The acquisition significantly increased Hindu Divine territory and authority. Two years afterward, the Hindu Pantheon was described as second only to Dragon's Nest in overall Pantheon Authority.[13]
Following [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s later annexation of Earth, these territories ultimately became part of the [[Elderblood Empire]] together with the rest of the Hindu Pantheon's holdings.[5]
