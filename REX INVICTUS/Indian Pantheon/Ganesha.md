### Appearances

#### Debut
Chapter 986
Ganesha is a God of the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]] and a son of [[Shiva]] and [[Parvati]]. His previous incarnation was a Major God associated with protection and the removal of obstacles and was politically aligned with the former God-King [[Indra]]. During the restoration of Earth, Ganesha attempted to take credit for work performed by [[Victor Elderblood]] and [[Yggdrasil]] in order to increase his influence among Mortals. This resulted in his execution by [[Kaguya Elderblood Blank]], who destroyed his previous incarnation with Black Dragon Fire.[1]
Although Kaguya destroyed the individual existence that had been Ganesha, her attack did not employ the Concept of END. Consequently, the underlying Divine Essence associated with Ganesha was not absolutely terminated and remained capable of eventually reforming. The God was subsequently reincarnated, although the reincarnated Ganesha should not be treated as psychologically identical to his previous incarnation, as Chapter 986 establishes that the original personality and individual continuity were destroyed.[1]
Following the integration of the Hindu Pantheon into the [[Elderblood Empire]], the reincarnated Ganesha exists beneath the new Imperial order alongside the other surviving and reformed Hindu Gods.

### Physical Appearance
The novel has not provided a sufficiently detailed description of Ganesha's physical appearance.
It has also not been explicitly established whether the novel's Ganesha possesses the elephant-headed appearance traditionally associated with him. Consequently, his existing artwork should be regarded as an artistic interpretation rather than a confirmed canonical depiction.
The appearance of his reincarnated form has likewise not yet been described in sufficient detail.

### Personality

#### Previous Incarnation
Ganesha's previous incarnation was ambitious and greedy for influence. During [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s restoration of Earth, several Gods attempted to exploit Humanity's limited understanding of supernatural events by claiming responsibility for improvements they had not actually caused. Ganesha became one of the most significant offenders.[1]
His actions were motivated by the benefits that Mortal recognition could provide to a God. By presenting Victor and [[Yggdrasil]]'s achievements as his own, Ganesha could strengthen his reputation, Faith and influence among Humans.[1]
His behavior was also connected to the deteriorating political situation within the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]]. [[Indra]] was steadily losing influence to [[Shiva]], and Ganesha belonged to the political side surrounding the former God-King. Increasing Ganesha's religious influence would consequently also strengthen Indra's position against Shiva.[1]
Ganesha nevertheless badly underestimated how seriously Victor's family treated insults against the Emperor. Victor himself was occupied with matters far greater than a God stealing credit for environmental restoration, but his wives considered attacks upon his reputation equivalent to attacks upon their family. Kaguya therefore chose to make an example of Ganesha.[1]

#### Reincarnated Ganesha
The personality of the reincarnated Ganesha has not yet been sufficiently explored.
Chapter 986 establishes that the destruction of his former incarnation eliminated the previous individual's personality and continuity. The Divine Essence could eventually produce Ganesha again, but the resulting Being would possess a different mindset rather than simply restoring the same person who had been killed.[1]
The reincarnated Ganesha should therefore be treated as a new incarnation of the same Divine identity rather than the literal resurrection of the previous Ganesha.

### Synopsis

#### God Emperor Victor Elderblood
Several years after [[Victor Elderblood]] began transforming Earth, the planet underwent an extraordinary environmental restoration. Pollution was disappearing, vast quantities of waste were being removed and damaged ecosystems were recovering through Victor's efforts alongside the World Tree [[Yggdrasil]].[1]
While supernatural factions understood the source of these changes, ordinary Humanity possessed comparatively little knowledge of the forces responsible. Several Gods consequently attempted to claim the restoration as their own achievement in order to increase their religious influence.
Ganesha was one of those Gods.[1]
By this period, the political situation within the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]] had already become unstable. [[Indra]] remained the official God-King, but [[Shiva]] had accumulated considerably greater practical influence and was increasingly regarded as the true dominant authority of the Pantheon. Indra had become desperate to restore his political relevance, and Ganesha's attempt to obtain additional Mortal influence became connected to that struggle.[1]
The Elderblood family eventually learned that Ganesha was taking credit for Victor's work.
[[Kaguya Elderblood Blank]] personally entered the Hindu Pantheon accompanied by members of the evolved [[Blank Clan]]. By this point, Kaguya was not merely one of Victor's maids but one of his wives and a powerful Dragon whose authority came directly from the Emperor's family.[1]
Ganesha was captured in front of the Hindu Gods.
Kaguya explained that Victor might be willing to ignore petty beings attempting to steal credit for his achievements because such matters had become insignificant compared with the responsibilities he carried as Emperor. His wives, however, would not permit others to damage his reputation or appropriate his accomplishments.[1]
Kaguya seized Ganesha by the head and transformed her arm into the scaled limb of a Black Dragon. She then unleashed Black Dragon Fire directly through him. The attack destroyed Ganesha's head at an atomic level and continued consuming his Divine existence.[1]
Shiva and Indra both wanted to respond, but neither acted.
Kaguya was one of Victor's wives. Attacking her would therefore transform the execution of one God into a direct conflict with Victor, his family and the entire military power surrounding the [[Elderblood Empire]]. Neither Hindu leader considered Ganesha valuable enough to risk the destruction of their Pantheon.[1]
Ganesha's previous incarnation was consequently destroyed.
The method of his death, however, created an important distinction. Kaguya had not employed the Concept of END. Her Black Dragon Fire possessed destructive properties capable of eliminating Ganesha as an individual, but it did not absolutely terminate the deeper Divine Essence from which the God could eventually emerge again.[1]
The original Ganesha could therefore no longer simply return with the same personality and memories. That individual had ceased to exist. Nevertheless, his remaining Divine Essence retained the capacity to reform, eventually allowing a new incarnation of Ganesha to emerge.[1]
Ganesha's execution was only the beginning of the confrontation.
After Kaguya departed, War, one of Victor's [[Heralds of Chaos]], appeared before the Hindu Gods. War delivered a warning from Victor directly to Shiva: out of respect for Shiva, the Emperor would allow the incident to end with Ganesha's death. If another Hindu God provoked the Elderblood faction in the same manner, the next visit would result in the destruction of the Pantheon.[1]
War's presence alone overwhelmed numerous Hindu Gods. Even Indra was left struggling to breathe after the Herald disappeared. When another God questioned whether they intended to tolerate the humiliation, Indra immediately rejected the idea of attempting retaliation.[1]
Shiva then confronted Indra.
Although Ganesha's own greed had been responsible for his decision, Shiva understood that Indra's political influence had contributed to the situation. Indra had become increasingly desperate as Shiva's authority grew, and increasing Ganesha's Mortal influence would also strengthen Indra's political faction.[1]
Rather than restoring Indra's authority, Ganesha's death produced the opposite result.
Victor's warning had addressed Shiva as the person responsible for controlling the Hindu Pantheon rather than Indra, effectively recognizing Shiva as the Pantheon's true authority. Shiva consequently concluded that allowing Indra to continue operating without restriction could eventually provoke an even greater conflict with Victor.[1]
The incident became one of the final catalysts for Indra's removal from power. Shiva later openly exercised the political influence he had maintained throughout the Pantheon and became its official God-King.[2]
The Hindu Pantheon was subsequently incorporated into the Elderblood Empire, placing its Gods beneath Victor's greater Imperial structure.[3]
During the enormous periods of development that followed, the Divine systems of Earth and the Empire continued functioning across accelerated time. The Divine Essence that had previously constituted Ganesha ultimately reformed, resulting in the reincarnated Ganesha currently associated with Shiva and the Hindu Pantheon.
The reincarnated Ganesha is nevertheless not identical to the God executed by Kaguya. Chapter 986 establishes that the personality and individual existence of the previous incarnation were destroyed; what survived was the deeper Divine Essence capable of eventually producing a new incarnation.[1]

### Death and Reincarnation
Ganesha's case demonstrates the distinction between destroying a Divine individual and absolutely terminating the Divine principle from which that individual exists.
- Destruction of the Previous Incarnation: Kaguya's Black Dragon Fire completely destroyed the Ganesha who existed during Chapter 986.[1]
- Loss of Personal Continuity: The previous Ganesha's individual consciousness and personality did not survive his destruction.[1]
- No Conventional Resurrection: His later existence should not be interpreted as the old Ganesha simply returning from death.
- Surviving Divine Essence: Because Kaguya did not use END, the underlying Divine Essence was not absolutely erased.[1]
- Divine Reformation: That Essence remained capable of reforming and eventually producing another incarnation of Ganesha.[1]
- Different Mindset: The reincarnated Ganesha possesses a different individual mindset from his predecessor rather than continuing the former incarnation's personality.[1]
- Same Divine Identity: Despite the loss of psychological continuity, the reformed God continues to be recognized as Ganesha and remains associated with [[Shiva]], [[Parvati]] and the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]].

### Skills & Abilities
Ganesha is a Major God of the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]]. However, neither his previous incarnation nor his reincarnated existence has received a substantial dedicated combat scene. His abilities should therefore remain limited to powers and Divine associations established by the story rather than expanding his traditional mythology into unsupported combat abilities.
- God Physiology: Ganesha is a genuine Hindu God whose existence is fundamentally Divine. As a God, he possesses [[Divine Energy]] and the supernatural characteristics inherent to members of his race. His specific physical statistics have not been demonstrated sufficiently to quantify them individually.
- Divine Energy: Ganesha possesses the Divine Energy necessary to maintain his existence and exercise his Divine authorities.
- Protection Divinity: Ganesha is identified as a God associated with Protection. The novel has not yet demonstrated the exact mechanics or combat applications of this Divinity.
- Protection Manipulation: Through his Divine association with Protection, Ganesha should possess authority over protective phenomena. No named technique or specific defensive feat has yet been demonstrated.
- Obstacle Removal: Ganesha is associated with removing obstacles from the paths of others. The exact conceptual mechanics of this Divine authority have not yet been explained.
- Faith Empowerment: Like other Gods, Ganesha can benefit from Mortal recognition and Faith. His previous incarnation's attempt to take credit for [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s restoration of Earth was specifically motivated by the opportunity to increase his influence among Humanity.[1]
- Divine Reincarnation: Ganesha's Divine Essence survived the destruction of his previous incarnation because Kaguya did not use the Concept of END. The Essence was therefore capable of reforming into another incarnation.[1]
- Divine Essence Persistence: Destruction of Ganesha's physical and individual existence was insufficient to erase the deeper Divine Essence associated with him.[1]
- Identity Reformation: His Divine Essence can produce a new Ganesha after sufficient time, although the resulting incarnation does not retain the exact personality of the previous one.[1]

### Relationships

#### Shiva
[[Shiva]] is Ganesha's father and one of the central figures of the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]].
Shiva was present when Kaguya executed Ganesha. Although he initially wanted to respond to the death of one of his Pantheon's Gods, he understood that attacking Kaguya would immediately provoke [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] and the Elderblood family.[1]
After Ganesha's death, Shiva acknowledged that Ganesha had ultimately become a victim of his own greed for greater Mortal influence. He nevertheless placed part of the responsibility upon [[Indra]], whose declining political authority had created the environment in which schemes such as Ganesha's were being encouraged.[1]
Ganesha's death consequently became one of the incidents that convinced Shiva to take direct control of the Hindu Pantheon.
The reincarnated Ganesha continues to be recognized as Shiva's son despite representing a new incarnation rather than the restoration of the original personality.

#### Parvati
[[Parvati]] is Ganesha's mother.
Little direct interaction between the two has currently been shown in the novel. Nevertheless, the reincarnated Ganesha remains associated with [[Shiva]] and Parvati's Divine family after the destruction of his previous incarnation.

#### Indra
[[Indra]] was the God-King of the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]] during the previous Ganesha's life and the political figure most closely connected to the circumstances leading to his execution.
By Chapter 986, Indra's authority had deteriorated severely. [[Shiva]] possessed greater strength, respect and practical influence, leaving Indra increasingly desperate to rebuild his own political faction.[1]
Ganesha's attempt to claim [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s work would have increased his Mortal influence and, by extension, strengthened the side of the Pantheon associated with Indra.[1]
The strategy ended catastrophically. Ganesha was killed, Victor publicly treated Shiva as the authority responsible for controlling the Hindu Gods and Shiva gained further justification for restricting Indra.
Ganesha's death therefore contributed indirectly to the end of Indra's reign.[1][2]

#### Kaguya Elderblood Blank
[[Kaguya Elderblood Blank]] killed Ganesha's previous incarnation.
Her execution of him was not motivated by a personal conflict. Kaguya acted because Ganesha attempted to appropriate [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s accomplishments, something she regarded as an insult to her husband and their family.[1]
Kaguya killed him using Black Dragon Fire. The attack destroyed the previous Ganesha as an individual but, because it lacked the absolute termination produced by END, did not prevent his underlying Divine Essence from eventually reforming.[1]
The reincarnated Ganesha therefore exists only because Kaguya's attack, despite its overwhelming destructive power, was not absolute conceptual termination.

#### Victor Elderblood
Ganesha never directly fought [[Victor Elderblood]].
The conflict was created when his previous incarnation attempted to take credit for Victor and [[Yggdrasil]]'s environmental restoration of Earth. Victor himself considered such comparatively minor political problems beneath the level requiring his personal intervention, but his family responded in his place.[1]
Kaguya executed Ganesha while War subsequently delivered Victor's warning to the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]].
Ironically, Ganesha's attempt to benefit from Victor's accomplishments resulted in his death and helped accelerate the political collapse of the faction surrounding [[Indra]].
The reincarnated Ganesha currently exists within the Hindu Pantheon after that Pantheon became subordinate to Victor's [[Elderblood Empire]].

### Current Status
Ganesha is currently alive through reincarnation.
The individual killed by Kaguya in Chapter 986 remains dead. His consciousness and personal identity were destroyed and did not simply return.[1]
What survived was his Divine Essence. Because the Essence was not erased through END, it could eventually reform into a new incarnation of Ganesha with a different mindset.[1]
The reincarnated Ganesha is therefore recognized as the current Ganesha and as the son of [[Shiva]] and [[Parvati]], while remaining distinct in personal continuity from his previous incarnation.
He is currently associated with the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]], which itself exists beneath the authority of the [[Elderblood Empire]].
