Parvati is an ancient Hindu Goddess, the Goddess of Marriage and Love, a Major God of the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]], and the wife of [[Shiva]]. Unlike Shiva's eleven additional manifestations, each of which lives an independent life and possesses its own wife or romantic partner, Parvati is the sole wife of Shiva's principal manifestation.[1]
Despite possessing comparatively little direct combat exposure in the story, Parvati is one of the most personally important people in Shiva's life. Shiva's attachment to her is strong enough to influence even his pursuit of power: when discussing why he had not followed Kali into increasingly extreme conceptual evolution, Shiva acknowledged that the isolation required to advance his Destruction further would separate him from Parvati for potentially thousands of years, something he was unwilling to accept.[2]
Following the incorporation of the Hindu Pantheon into the [[Elderblood Empire]], Parvati became an Imperial subject alongside the rest of her Pantheon. She later appeared directly during the aftermath of Shiva's unauthorized experiment involving [[Veylan Valentin Walker]], where her reaction revealed considerably more of her personality and relationship with her husband.[3][4]

### Physical Appearance
Parvati's physical appearance has not yet received a sufficiently detailed canonical description.
The novel has not clearly established her hair color, eye color, height, clothing or any unique Divine transformation. Consequently, existing artwork depicting Parvati should be treated as an artistic interpretation rather than a definitive representation of her appearance.

### Personality
Parvati is generally described as a kind, helpful and devoted woman. When [[Shiva]] first spoke about her, he compared her behavior to [[Scathach Elderblood Scarlett|Scathach Scarlett]] after witnessing the latter's frightening possessiveness toward [[Victor Elderblood]]. Shiva explained that Parvati was normally an excellent wife, but that her demeanor changed dramatically whenever another woman became romantically involved with him.[1]
Her jealousy is sufficiently intense that even Shiva—one of the strongest and most fearless Gods on Earth—admitted that the expression she assumes in such situations intimidates him. This does not mean that Parvati is normally hostile or spiteful. The comparison in Chapter 515 specifically contrasts her with [[Hera]]: despite possessing similar Divine associations with marriage, Parvati is characterized as kind and helpful during ordinary circumstances rather than habitually vindictive.[1]
Parvati is extremely devoted to Shiva but does not blindly support everything he does. She is willing to criticize him harshly when she believes he has acted foolishly, and her familiarity with his personality allows her to recognize his bad decisions long before their consequences become unavoidable.[4]
This became particularly evident following Shiva's experiment with [[Veylan Valentin Walker]]. Parvati had repeatedly warned Shiva that interfering with Victor Elderblood and his bloodline in such a manner was an unnecessarily dangerous idea. When Shiva ignored her warnings and was subsequently punished by both Victor and Kali, Parvati displayed very little sympathy and immediately reminded him that she had warned him repeatedly.[4]
Her anger does not diminish her confidence in Shiva's capabilities. After learning that Victor had sentenced Shiva to fight on the most dangerous front lines of the coming war, Parvati considered the punishment comparatively lenient. She trusted Shiva's strength so completely that a battlefield assignment which would amount to a death sentence for most beings was, in her view, simply an opportunity for her husband to train, grow stronger and once again demonstrate why he was known as the God of Destruction.[4]
Parvati is therefore simultaneously affectionate, possessive, pragmatic and demanding. She loves Shiva deeply enough to support him without hesitation, but her affection does not prevent her from confronting him whenever his pride or recklessness causes unnecessary problems.

### Synopsis

#### Demonic War
Parvati was first mentioned during the [[Supernatural Gathering]] while [[Shiva]], [[Vlad III Tepes|Vlad Tepes]] and [[Volk Fenrir]] observed [[Scathach Elderblood Scarlett|Scathach Scarlett]]. [[Scathach]]'s threatening reaction toward women interacting with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] caused the three men to think about the possessive women in their own lives.[1]
Shiva remarked that Scathach reminded him strongly of Parvati. He described his wife as normally kind and helpful, but stated that she became extremely dangerous whenever other women became involved with him. Her jealous expression was intimidating enough that even Shiva felt uncomfortable when confronted by it.[1]
The discussion also clarified Shiva's unusual family structure. His primary manifestation has Parvati as his sole wife, while each of his eleven additional manifestations possesses its own independent wife or romantic partner. Parvati therefore occupies a unique position as the spouse of Shiva's principal existence rather than one of the partners belonging to his other manifestations.[1]

#### King Among Kings
Parvati did not participate directly in [[Shiva]]'s battle against the Elder God Nur, but her importance to Shiva became clear during his explanation of his own development.
Kali had pursued an extraordinarily deep relationship with Destruction, approaching a state where her existence itself increasingly embodied the Concept. Shiva possessed superior control over his Destruction but had deliberately chosen not to undergo the same extreme evolution.[2]
One of the primary reasons was Parvati.
Pursuing that path would require Shiva to isolate himself and meditate for enormous periods of time, potentially spending thousands of years separated from ordinary relationships. Shiva admitted that he had no desire to abandon his life with Parvati merely to obtain greater power.[2]
His decision demonstrates the unusual importance Parvati holds in the life of a Being otherwise defined by strength, wisdom and Destruction. Even the possibility of advancing his fundamental Divinity was insufficient to make Shiva willingly sacrifice his relationship with his wife.

#### God Emperor Victor Elderblood
Parvati was present beside [[Shiva]] during the confrontation created by [[Ganesha]] in Chapter 986.[5]
After [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] and [[Yggdrasil]] began restoring Earth's environment, several Gods attempted to take credit for the improvements in order to increase their influence among Mortals. Ganesha became one of the most prominent offenders and was consequently targeted by [[Kaguya Elderblood Blank]].[5]
Kaguya entered the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]] accompanied by the evolved [[Blank Clan]] and publicly destroyed Ganesha using Black Dragon Fire. Shiva and [[Indra]] both wanted to intervene but understood that attacking Victor's wife would immediately transform the situation into a war with the entire Elderblood faction.[5]
Parvati remained beside Shiva during the aftermath. Although Shiva maintained a neutral outward demeanor while discussing the situation with the other Gods, the narration notes that his wife could recognize that he was irritated, demonstrating her familiarity with emotions he normally concealed from others.[5]
Ganesha's death ultimately contributed to a major restructuring of the Hindu Pantheon. Shiva recognized Indra's political influence behind the incident and concluded that allowing the increasingly desperate God-King to operate freely could eventually provoke Victor into destroying their Pantheon. Shiva later removed Indra and became God-King himself.[5][6]
The Hindu Pantheon was eventually incorporated into the [[Elderblood Empire]], making Parvati and the other Hindu Gods subjects of Victor's growing Imperial structure.[3]

#### Those Who Rule Reality
Parvati's most substantial direct appearance occurred after [[Shiva]]'s experiment involving [[Veylan Valentin Walker]] was exposed.
Shiva had secretly obtained information from the [[Akashic Records]] through a transaction involving a Primordial Being and used that information to reconstruct one of [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s distant Human ancestors. He then performed a ritual that accepted Veylan into his bloodline as an adopted [[Demigod]] son, hoping to reproduce even a fraction of the extraordinary hereditary potential demonstrated by Victor's descendants.[7][8]
Parvati had opposed the idea from the beginning. According to her later argument with Shiva, she had warned him repeatedly that interfering with Victor in this manner was dangerous and unnecessary.[4]
Her concerns proved correct.
After discovering the experiment, Victor considered Shiva's actions a serious breach of trust. He removed Shiva from his position as God-King, placed [[Kali Elderblood]] in control of the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]] and sentenced Shiva to fight on the front lines during the Empire's future war against the civilizations beyond their Sector.[8]
Kali then spent several hours physically punishing Shiva for the entire incident. The destructive confrontation inside Shiva's temple was severe enough that explosions of Energy and Shiva's screams could be heard by the Gods waiting outside.[4]
Parvati eventually arrived and encountered [[Rudra]], Shiva's close friend and representative. When she asked what had happened, Rudra simply answered that the obvious consequences of Shiva's behavior were occurring. Parvati immediately understood and expressed frustration, once again remarking that she had repeatedly warned Shiva.[4]
Rudra advised her not to enter the temple while Kali was still furious, and Parvati agreed to wait.[4]
After Kali finally exhausted her anger, Parvati entered and immediately began reprimanding Shiva herself. Shiva attempted to argue that it was not the correct moment, but Parvati rejected the excuse and reminded him that she had warned him dozens of times. She mocked his confidence in being the ancient and supposedly wise God of Destruction and demanded to know why, after witnessing countless examples of what happened to those who challenged Victor, he had still chosen to provoke the Emperor.[4]
At the height of her frustration, Parvati compared Shiva to [[Indra]]. The comparison affected Shiva so severely that it was effectively treated as emotional damage, forcing Parvati herself to admit that she may have gone too far.[4]
Despite her anger, Parvati understood Shiva's underlying motivation. He had wanted to prepare another powerful warrior for the increasingly dangerous future facing the Empire, and the rapid expansion of Victor's military made the desire for additional trump cards understandable. The problem was not Shiva's desire to strengthen their side but the reckless method he had chosen without considering the consequences of being discovered.[4]
When Parvati learned that Victor's punishment required Shiva to serve on the front lines, she regarded the sentence as lenient. For almost anyone else, being sent into the most dangerous battles of the coming Upper-Sector war would effectively be a death sentence. Parvati, however, possessed complete confidence in Shiva's combat ability.[4]
Rather than pitying him, she ordered Shiva to train, push his limits and become the foremost warrior once the war began. She wanted him to burn down their enemies' planets and once again demonstrate the destructive power of the God she had fallen in love with.[4]
Shiva accepted her demand seriously.
Parvati was also present when Kali ordered Shiva to help transfer full administrative control of the Hindu Pantheon to her. Shiva accepted the change, privately acknowledging that he was considerably more suited to fighting than ruling.[4]

### Skills & Abilities
Parvati is an ancient Major Goddess of the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]]. However, she has not yet participated in a dedicated battle, meaning her abilities should remain limited to Divine authorities and characteristics directly established by the novel rather than automatically importing abilities associated with Parvati in external mythology.
- God Physiology: Parvati is a genuine Third Generation Hindu Goddess and has existed for an immense period of time. Her existence is Divine rather than Mortal, although the novel has not provided enough individual combat feats to quantify her physical strength, speed, durability or regenerative capabilities.
- Divine Power: As a Major Goddess, Parvati possesses [[Divine Energy]] and the conceptual authorities associated with her Divine identity.
- Love Divinity: Parvati is explicitly identified as the Goddess of Love.[1] She therefore possesses a major Divine authority connected to Love. The novel has not yet demonstrated the precise supernatural applications, range or limitations of this Divinity.
- Love Authority: Parvati possesses conceptual authority over aspects of Love as part of her Divine identity.[1] Abilities such as forced affection, emotion manipulation or supernatural attraction should not be attributed to her until specifically demonstrated.
- Marriage Divinity: Parvati is explicitly identified as the Goddess of Marriage.[1] Marriage therefore constitutes another of her major Divine authorities.
- Marriage Authority: Parvati possesses conceptual authority associated with Marriage and marital bonds. The novel has not yet shown a named technique or detailed mechanical application of this Divinity.
- Divine Longevity: Parvati has existed across an immense span of supernatural history and remains physically active despite her extraordinary age.
- Emotional Insight: Parvati knows [[Shiva]] sufficiently well to notice emotional reactions that he conceals from other observers. During the aftermath of [[Ganesha]]'s execution, she recognized that Shiva was irritated despite his outwardly neutral behavior.[5]
- Strategic Judgment: Parvati recognized the danger of Shiva's experiment involving [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s ancestry long before it was exposed and repeatedly warned him against continuing it.[4] Her assessment proved correct when the experiment resulted in Shiva losing his position as God-King.
- Fearlessness: Parvati is not intimidated by Shiva's reputation or power and openly reprimands him when necessary. Even after Kali had spent hours beating him, Parvati immediately continued criticizing his decisions rather than comforting him.[4]

### Relationships

#### Shiva
[[Shiva]] is Parvati's husband and the most important relationship shown for her.
Despite Shiva possessing eleven additional manifestations with separate romantic lives, Parvati is explicitly identified as the sole wife of his principal manifestation.[1]
Their marriage is extremely close. Shiva described Parvati as normally kind, helpful and an excellent wife, although he admitted that her jealousy becomes frightening whenever another woman becomes involved with him.[1]
Parvati's importance to Shiva extends beyond ordinary affection. Shiva deliberately refused to pursue the same extreme conceptual evolution as Kali because doing so would require potentially thousands of years of isolation away from Parvati. He valued continuing his life with his wife more than maximizing his own strength.[2]
Their relationship is nevertheless not one in which Parvati blindly agrees with Shiva. She frequently challenges his judgment and appears comfortable reprimanding him despite his age, status and overwhelming power. The Veylan incident showed that she had warned him repeatedly before the plan failed and had no hesitation in reminding him of those warnings afterward.[4]
Parvati also possesses complete confidence in Shiva as a warrior. When [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] sentenced him to fight on the most dangerous front lines of the coming war, she treated the punishment as relatively mild and encouraged Shiva to use it as an opportunity to push his limits and once again display his destructive strength.[4]

#### Ganesha
[[Ganesha]] is associated with [[Shiva]] and Parvati's Divine family.
Parvati was present during the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]] confrontation in which Ganesha's previous incarnation was executed by [[Kaguya Elderblood Blank]].[5] Kaguya's Black Dragon Fire destroyed Ganesha as an individual, although his Divine Essence remained capable of eventually reforming because the attack did not employ the Concept of END.[5]

#### Kali Elderblood
Parvati and Kali are both ancient members of the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]] and possess longstanding relationships with [[Shiva]].
Parvati did not interfere while Kali punished Shiva for the Veylan incident. Instead, she accepted [[Rudra]]'s advice to remain outside the temple until Kali's anger had subsided.[4]
Her decision demonstrates that Parvati understood both Kali's temperament and the legitimacy of her anger. Once Kali finished, Parvati proceeded to reprimand Shiva herself rather than objecting to what had happened.[4]
Parvati also accepted the political consequences of the incident, including Kali becoming the new ruler of the Hindu Pantheon and Shiva agreeing to assist in transferring control to her.[8][4]

### Current Status
Parvati remains alive and continues to belong to the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]].
Following [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s annexation of Earth's supernatural powers, the Hindu Pantheon became part of the [[Elderblood Empire]], making Parvati an Imperial subject together with [[Shiva]] and the other Hindu Gods.[3]
After the Veylan incident, [[Kali Elderblood]] replaced Shiva as the principal ruler of the Hindu Pantheon. Parvati remained beside Shiva while he accepted his new position as a frontline warrior and agreed to support Kali's restructuring of the Pantheon.[8][4]
