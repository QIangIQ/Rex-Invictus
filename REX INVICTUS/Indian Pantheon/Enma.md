### Appearances

#### First Mention
Chapter 667
Enma is a God associated with the [[Buddhist Pantheon]] and the legitimate Ruler of the Buddhist [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]]. He is referred to by [[Diablo]] as the Buddhist Hell King, placing him in the same broad Underworld hierarchy as figures such as Yama, [[Hades]], Hela, [[Izanami-no-Mikoto]], [[Apep]] and the successive Rulers of [[Biblical Hell]].[1]
Unlike a Pantheon's God-King, whose authority normally governs its celestial and political structure, a Ruler is responsible for its Hell or Underworld and the local portion of the greater supernatural system through which dead Souls are judged, processed and routed. [[Buddha]] stood as the principal Divine authority of the Buddhist Pantheon, while Enma governed its Underworld.[2][3]
Enma became indirectly involved in the Demonic War through an alliance with Diablo. When Diablo's wider coalition began collapsing, Enma remained one of the few Underworld rulers still politically aligned with him. Diablo considered asking Enma for assistance against the increasingly disastrous situation surrounding [[Victor Elderblood]], but acknowledged that Enma was occupied with restructuring his own Hell and was therefore unlikely to respond immediately.[1]

### Physical Appearance
Enma has not directly appeared on-page in the currently established material, and the novel has not provided a canonical physical description of him. His height, build, hair color, eye color, clothing, Divine regalia and any physical manifestation associated with his position as Ruler remain unknown.
The image currently used for Enma on the wiki should consequently be treated as an artistic interpretation rather than a confirmed canonical design.

### Personality and Traits
Enma has not received direct dialogue or sufficient individual characterization for a detailed personality profile. What can be established comes primarily from his actions and political position during the Demonic War. He was pragmatic enough to cooperate with [[Diablo]] despite belonging to a separate Divine civilization and maintained control over the Buddhist [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] independently enough that Diablo treated him as an allied ruler rather than merely another subordinate God.[1]
His decision to focus upon restructuring his Hell while the wider supernatural world was collapsing around him also suggests that maintaining the stability of his own Underworld took priority over immediately answering Diablo's military problems. Diablo believed Enma might have been the only available ally capable of providing useful assistance at that moment, but simultaneously recognized that Enma was unlikely to abandon the internal reorganization of his own realm merely because [[Biblical Hell]] was experiencing a crisis.[1]
The novel has not established whether Enma personally shares Diablo's ideology, hatred of Humanity or larger ambitions. Their relationship is better understood as a strategic alliance between supernatural rulers than proof that Enma possessed the same beliefs as the Demon King.

### Synopsis

#### Demonic War
Enma was first mentioned during the later stages of the Demonic War, at a point when [[Diablo]]'s carefully constructed network of alliances had begun collapsing. [[Victor Elderblood]] had entered [[Biblical Hell]] and systematically defeated its military forces, killing and consuming three of the Four Horsemen, defeating War, permanently damaging Hell itself and beginning the conquest of Diablo's own territory. Diablo could not personally return to deal with Victor because abandoning Earth would undermine the invasion and leave the Demonic army vulnerable to Heaven.[1]
While discussing the worsening situation with [[Bael]], Diablo reviewed the supernatural allies and Pantheons that might still be capable of supporting him. The Greek alliance was unavailable because of [[Hades]]' unpredictability, the Norse side had become unreliable because of Hela's actions, and the [[Shinto Pantheon]] was already struggling with the instability of its own [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]]. The [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]] was likewise undergoing internal problems involving [[Indra]], [[Shiva]] and its Hell.[1]
Despite those failures, Diablo still possessed several allies. He explicitly identified the Ancient [[Chinese Pantheon]], Yama as the Hindu Hell King, and Enma, the Buddhist Hell King, as forces that remained on his side. Yama had succeeded in placing his Hell behind Diablo's coalition, while Enma likewise remained politically aligned with him.[1]
Diablo briefly considered Enma the most realistic remaining individual who might be capable of assisting him. Yama was away in South Africa creating additional problems and was about to become involved with Vlad Dracul Tepes, leaving Enma as one of the few major allied authorities still potentially available. However, Enma was in the process of restructuring his Hell, and Diablo concluded that he would therefore be unlikely to respond quickly enough to solve the immediate crisis in Biblical Hell.[1]
The chapter does not establish that Enma personally entered the battlefield, fought Victor or abandoned the [[Buddhist Pantheon]]. His known contribution to the Demonic War was political and infernal: he remained one of Diablo's allied Underworld rulers while reorganizing the Buddhist afterlife system under his authority.[1]
The Buddhist Pantheon itself suffered catastrophic losses during the wider Demonic War. Buddhism possessed an enormous Human following and therefore an important supernatural relationship with Faith, Souls and the afterlife, but the conflict severely damaged its former Divine structure.[4] Enma is not identified among the Gods killed during the Pantheon's destruction, and no chapter currently confirms his death.

### Role as Ruler
Enma's most important confirmed characteristic is not an individually demonstrated combat ability but his position as the legitimate Ruler of the Buddhist [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]]. Within the cosmology, a Ruler is fundamentally different from a conventional God who merely possesses authority over Death.
The greater system surrounding Souls is maintained by mechanisms and entities above ordinary Pantheons, particularly [[The Judges of The Abyss]]. Individual Pantheons possessing sufficiently developed afterlives maintain their own local Underworld infrastructure, with a Ruler acting as the principal authority responsible for that realm.[2][3]
The general hierarchy is:

This means Enma's position should not be confused with a conventional God of Death. Gods of Death operate beneath the legitimate Ruler and perform many of the practical duties associated with locating, transporting and processing Souls, while the Ruler occupies the superior administrative position over the Underworld as a whole.[3]
The Ruler likewise does not create or own the universal Soul System. The office administers a local portion of an already existing cosmological structure. The Judges of The Abyss and the greater systems connected to death and reincarnation remain above the authority of any individual Hell King.[2]

### Skills & Abilities
Enma has never participated in an on-page battle, meaning his complete personal power set remains unknown. His confirmed abilities and authorities are derived primarily from his status as a genuine God and legitimate Ruler of the Buddhist [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]]. Abilities unique to mythological versions of Yama, Enma or Yanluo should not automatically be imported into the character without novel evidence.

#### Divine Physiology
- God Physiology: Enma is a genuine Divine Being belonging to the [[Buddhist Pantheon]] and therefore possesses the supernatural body, Soul and Energy associated with Gods.
- Divine Soul: Enma possesses a Divine Soul capable of sustaining Divinity and the supernatural Authority required of an [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] Ruler.
- [[Divine Energy]] Manipulation: As a God, Enma can generate and manipulate Divine Energy.
- Superhuman Physical Characteristics: Enma possesses supernatural strength, speed, durability, stamina, reflexes and senses beyond ordinary mortal beings. Their exact level has never been individually demonstrated.
- Divine Regeneration: Gods possess supernatural recovery beyond mortal species, although sufficiently severe Soul or conceptual damage can still permanently kill them.
- Agelessness / Divine Longevity: Enma has existed for an enormous period and is not subject to ordinary biological aging.
- Godly [[Immortality]]: Conventional natural death through age is ineffective against him. This should not be confused with absolute immortality against Soul destruction or sufficiently powerful Divine attacks.

#### Ruler Authority
- Ruler Authority: Enma is the recognized ruler of the Buddhist [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] and possesses the supernatural authority necessary to administer that realm.[1][2]
- Underworld Authority: His office grants him authority over the Buddhist Hell and its local supernatural infrastructure.
- Soul-System Connection: Legitimate Rulers are connected to the local afterlife system through which Souls entering their domain are processed.[2]
- Soul Administration: Enma possesses administrative authority over Souls passing through the Buddhist Underworld.
- Soul Judgment: Judgment is one of the principal functions associated with legitimate Rulership. A Ruler is expected to evaluate Souls impartially when direct judgment is necessary rather than simply acting according to personal preference.[2]
- Soul Evaluation: His office necessarily involves assessing Souls under the jurisdiction of his Underworld and determining how the local system should process them.
- Afterlife Routing: As the highest authority of Buddhist Hell, Enma oversees the systems responsible for directing Souls through the appropriate stages of the afterlife.
- Soul Processing: The local Underworld system under a Ruler can process, punish, purify or otherwise prepare Souls for later stages of the greater cycle.[2]
- Reincarnation-System Interaction: Enma's Underworld participates in the greater Soul cycle leading eventually toward reincarnation. This does not mean Enma possesses unrestricted personal control over reincarnation throughout Creation.
- God-of-Death Authority: Gods of Death and equivalent psychopomp agents within a developed afterlife operate beneath the Ruler rather than independently of the Underworld's sovereign authority.[3]
- Territorial Authority: As the Buddhist Hell King, Enma possesses authority within his own Underworld that would not necessarily apply at the same level outside his jurisdiction.
- Underworld Administration: Enma is capable of reorganizing the systems and structure of Buddhist Hell. Chapter 667 specifically states that he was restructuring his Hell during the Demonic War.[1]
- Large-Scale Soul Administration: Buddhism possessed an enormous number of worshippers, making its afterlife a major system responsible for dealing with the Souls associated with a very large mortal population.[4]

#### Judgment and Afterlife Knowledge
Enma's position requires substantially greater knowledge of Souls and afterlife mechanisms than an ordinary combat-oriented God.
- Afterlife Expertise: Enma possesses extensive knowledge of the operation and administration of an [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]].
- Soul-System Knowledge: His responsibilities require familiarity with how Souls enter, move through and leave his local afterlife system.
- Judicial Authority: As Ruler, Enma occupies the highest local position responsible for maintaining appropriate judgment rather than leaving every decision to subordinate Gods of Death.
- Administrative Reorganization: His restructuring of Buddhist Hell demonstrates that he can modify or reorganize the operational structure of his Underworld when circumstances require it.[1]
- Crisis Administration: Enma prioritized stabilizing and restructuring his own Hell even while [[Diablo]]'s alliance was undergoing a major military crisis, demonstrating the scale of responsibilities attached to maintaining an afterlife realm.[1]

#### Soul Manipulation (Ruler-System Based)
Enma's status gives him legitimate authority to interact with Souls processed by his [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]], but this should not be exaggerated into unrestricted control over every Soul in existence.
- Soul Interaction: A Ruler can directly interact with Souls passing through the supernatural system under their authority.
- Soul Perception: Ruler responsibilities require the ability to perceive and evaluate spiritual beings within the afterlife system.
- Soul Regulation: Enma can participate in regulating the movement and treatment of Souls under Buddhist Hell's jurisdiction.
- Soul-Judgment Resistance: A legitimate Ruler possesses a high-order connection to their Underworld's Soul system, making ordinary spiritual interference considerably less effective than it would be against a normal mortal Soul.
Enma remains subordinate to the greater cosmological Soul System. [[The Judges of The Abyss]] possess authority beyond that of an individual Pantheon's Ruler and should not be treated as Enma's equals or subordinates.[2][3]

#### Political and Strategic Authority
- Divine Leadership: Enma governs an entire [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] rather than serving merely as a specialist God within another ruler's territory.
- Infernal Diplomacy: Enma maintained a strategic alliance with [[Diablo]], demonstrating that he operated independently in relations between major supernatural realms.[1]
- Cross-Pantheon Alliance Building: His alliance placed him alongside authorities from other Pantheons, particularly Yama and forces belonging to the Ancient [[Chinese Pantheon]].
- Strategic Independence: Enma did not immediately abandon his own responsibilities when Diablo required assistance. His continued restructuring of Buddhist Hell suggests that his allegiance did not supersede his duties as Ruler.[1]

### Power
Enma's exact combat strength is unknown because he has never directly fought on-page. His position as a Ruler nevertheless places him significantly above the level of an ordinary minor God in terms of supernatural authority and institutional importance.
Ruler status can provide enormous advantages inside the corresponding [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]]. [[Persephone]], after becoming the recognized Ruler of the Greek Underworld, received a tremendous increase in power and authority because the realm itself supported her position.[5] This establishes that legitimate Rulership is more than a political title and can possess significant supernatural consequences.
However, Persephone's individual awakening should not be copied wholesale onto Enma. The exact extent of his personal Divinities, combat techniques, artifacts and synchronization with Buddhist Hell have never been shown. He should therefore be classified primarily through his confirmed position as a Major God and Underworld Ruler rather than assigned an unsupported precise combat tier.
The older classification of Enma as a Second Generation Primordial God is not established by the checked novel material. Being a Ruler does not automatically make an individual a Primordial God, and the terms should remain separate unless the story directly confirms Enma's Divine generation.

### Relationships

#### Diablo
[[Diablo]] was Enma's principal known political ally during the Demonic War. When Diablo reviewed the supernatural factions still supporting him in Chapter 667, he explicitly included Enma among the remaining allied authorities and identified him as the Buddhist Hell King.[1]
Diablo believed Enma might be one of the only remaining allies capable of providing meaningful assistance against his deteriorating situation, but Enma's ongoing restructuring of Buddhist Hell made an immediate intervention unlikely.[1] Nothing in the chapter establishes that Enma was Diablo's subordinate; their relationship is more accurately described as an alliance between independent supernatural rulers.

#### Yama
Yama was another [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] ruler aligned with [[Diablo]] during the Demonic War. Diablo discussed Enma and Yama together as two Hell Kings whose realms remained politically favorable toward his coalition.[1]
The two occupy comparable offices in different religious systems: Yama rules the Hindu Hell, while Enma governs the Buddhist Underworld. Their exact personal relationship has never been depicted.

#### Buddha
[[Buddha]] served as the principal God-King associated with the [[Buddhist Pantheon]], while Enma governed the Pantheon's [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]]. Their positions represent the distinction between celestial and infernal leadership: Buddha stood at the head of the Pantheon's broader Divine structure, whereas Enma exercised authority over its Hell and Soul-processing infrastructure.
No direct conversation between Buddha and Enma has been shown, and the nature of their personal relationship remains unknown.

#### The Judges of The Abyss
[[The Judges of The Abyss]] occupy a cosmological position above individual [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] rulers. They are associated with the greater system governing Souls, while Rulers such as Enma administer local Hells connected to that larger structure.[2][3]
Enma therefore possesses enormous local authority but is not the ultimate source of Soul judgment or reincarnation throughout Creation.

### Current Status
Enma's current location has not been revealed. The [[Buddhist Pantheon]] suffered catastrophic destruction during the Demonic War and lost much of the power and infrastructure it possessed before the conflict, but Enma himself has never been confirmed dead.
The last direct information concerning him identifies him as alive, governing and restructuring Buddhist Hell during the Demonic War.[1] Until a later chapter explicitly establishes otherwise, his status should remain Alive rather than being inferred from the destruction suffered by the wider Pantheon.
