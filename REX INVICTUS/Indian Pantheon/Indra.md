Indra, also known as Sakra, is an ancient Hindu God, the God of the Skies and Lightning, and the former God-King of the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]]. He formally governed the Pantheon for thousands of years, but his reign was unusual because the political authority attached to the throne never translated into complete control over its strongest members. [[Shiva]], despite deliberately avoiding most of the administrative burden of rulership, retained enormous influence throughout the Pantheon, while beings such as Kali were individually powerful enough that Indra could not force them to obey him merely because he possessed the title of God-King.[5][4]
This imbalance gradually became the defining problem of Indra's reign. By the time of the [[Norse Pantheon]]'s collapse, the Hindu Pantheon effectively possessed a divided leadership structure: Indra held the official throne while Shiva was the strongest, most respected and politically influential God. Indra deeply resented the situation and increasingly attempted to recover the authority he believed should naturally belong to the Pantheon's King.[5]
His attempts ultimately accelerated his downfall. After [[Ganesha]] tried to take credit for the environmental restoration being performed by [[Victor Elderblood]] and [[Yggdrasil]], [[Kaguya Elderblood Blank]] entered the Hindu Pantheon and publicly killed him. Shiva recognized Indra's political influence behind Ganesha's behavior and concluded that Indra's desperation had become dangerous enough to threaten the survival of the entire Pantheon.[2] Shiva subsequently exercised the influence he had maintained for millennia and forced Indra from the throne. The official explanation described the transition as a voluntary resignation, but the narration explicitly states that Indra had effectively been expelled from his position.[4]
Indra remained alive after his removal and later became a subject of the [[Elderblood Empire]] when Victor annexed the Hindu Pantheon.[6] Shiva himself was later removed as ruler following the [[Veylan Valentin Walker]] incident, with [[Kali Elderblood]] becoming the current leader of the Hindu Pantheon.[7]

### Physical Appearance
The novel has not yet provided a sufficiently detailed canonical description of Indra's normal appearance. His established form is male, and he had lived as a man for thousands of years by the time of his first direct appearance.[5]
Unlike a Human body, however, a God's manifested form is not necessarily biologically immutable. During the negotiations surrounding the former [[Norse Pantheon]], Indra seriously considered altering himself into a woman after observing the political advantages [[Amaterasu]] Elderblood and Haruna had obtained through their relationships with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]. The possibility itself was treated as something he could accomplish; what stopped him was primarily the psychological strangeness of changing his sex after existing as a man for thousands of years.[5]
His current artwork should therefore be treated as an artistic interpretation rather than a confirmed representation of his canonical hair color, eye color, height or Divine attire.

### Personality
Indra is proud, ambitious, status-conscious and deeply attached to the authority associated with being a God-King. The central frustration of his character is that he possessed the official throne without ever possessing unquestioned supremacy over the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]]. He believed that the Gods should listen to him because he was their King, yet [[Shiva]]'s strength, history and influence repeatedly caused others to defer to the God of Destruction instead.[5]
This produced a considerable inferiority complex concerning Shiva. By Chapter 970, Indra openly resented the fact that his authority was constantly undermined despite his royal position. The situation had deteriorated to the point where the Pantheon was described as effectively divided between the two: Indra held the position, while Shiva possessed the louder voice and greater respect. Indra lacked the strength necessary to force either Shiva or Kali to acknowledge his authority absolutely.[5]
Indra is also impulsive and prone to selfish or poorly considered ideas. During the auction of the [[Norse Pantheon]], his initial reaction was to consider attacking Hela and simply taking the territory without sacrificing the Hindu Pantheon's resources. He reasoned that their side possessed Shiva and Kali, but immediately recognized the obvious flaw: neither was his obedient subordinate, and breaking the meeting's non-aggression agreement would place the entire Pantheon in danger without guaranteeing their support.[5]
The narration specifically distinguishes this impulsiveness from complete stupidity. Indra may regularly entertain foolish ideas, but surviving as God-King for thousands of years gave him enough political experience to evaluate situations from several perspectives and abandon a plan once the consequences became clearly disastrous.[5]
He is extremely opportunistic. After seeing how close relationships with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] had strengthened the positions of [[Amaterasu]] and Haruna, Indra briefly considered changing his sex and attempting to attract Victor himself. His thought process was primarily political rather than romantic: becoming personally important to the Dragon Emperor could potentially give Indra enough outside support to counterbalance Shiva and Kali.[5]
His obsession with recovering influence became progressively more dangerous. After his reputation declined to the point where people increasingly identified Shiva rather than Indra when discussing the ruler of the Hindu Pantheon, Indra became desperate for opportunities to reassert himself.[2] This desperation contributed directly to the [[Ganesha]] incident, which instead gave Shiva the justification necessary to remove him.
Despite his pride, Indra possesses a strong instinct for self-preservation. He does not insist upon fighting simply because his honor has been challenged. He abandoned the idea of attacking Hela once the political consequences became clear, refused to confront War after experiencing the Herald's pressure, and ultimately stepped aside rather than engage Shiva in a conflict he understood he could not win.[5][2][4]

### Background
Indra had ruled the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]] for thousands of years before the events of the main story. His reign was not created through the complete defeat or disappearance of [[Shiva]]. Shiva later clarified that his influence had always remained throughout the Pantheon; he had simply allowed another God to handle the tedious responsibilities associated with formal government.[4]
Consequently, Indra's kingship depended upon Shiva choosing not to act directly. This arrangement functioned while the Pantheon remained relatively stable, but the increasing number of planetary, Divine and interdimensional crises gradually exposed its weaknesses.
The most serious early example occurred when Yama deliberately disconnected the Hindu Hell from the Pantheon's normal system during the Demonic War. The disruption interfered with the normal processing and recycling of Souls, leaving Indra furious and forcing the Hindu leadership to deal with functions normally handled automatically through the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]].[8]
By Chapter 670, even outsiders were aware that Shiva had begun taking direct action because he was no longer willing to tolerate the consequences of Indra's leadership.[9] Indra nevertheless retained the official throne until [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s rise completely changed the political balance of Earth.

### Synopsis

#### Demonic War
Indra did not make a direct on-page appearance during most of the Demonic War, but the breakdown of the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]]'s [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] placed his administration under considerable pressure.
Yama, the ruler responsible for the Hindu Hell, joined the conspiracy surrounding [[Diablo]] and deliberately separated his Underworld from the Pantheon. This interrupted the normal management of the enormous number of Souls passing through the Hindu system. Yama openly mocked Indra's resulting frustration, remarking that he could practically hear the thunder of the God-King's anger from Hell.[8]
The disruption was strategically important because the normal afterlife systems handled far more than sinful Human Souls. Dead living things continuously contributed small traces of Soul material that were recycled through these cosmic systems. Once Yama intentionally stopped the automatic process, enormous quantities of work fell upon the Pantheon's Divine authorities instead.[8]
The political consequences extended beyond the Underworld itself. By Chapter 670, information reaching [[Merlin]] indicated that [[Shiva]] had finally started acting more directly because he was unwilling to continue allowing Indra's incompetence to damage the Hindu Pantheon.[9] This represented the first major indication that the God-King's formal position was beginning to lose practical meaning.

#### Dragon God of Chaos
Indra made his first direct appearance following the destruction of the [[Norse Pantheon]]. In less than a decade, the Greek, Egyptian and Norse Pantheons had all collapsed, creating legitimate fear that the wave of Divine civilizations being destroyed might eventually reach the surviving factions. The situation was serious enough that Indra personally attended the gathering convened after Ragnarök alongside [[Shiva]].[1]
His attendance also exposed how far the balance of power had shifted. During older meetings, Shiva had commonly served as the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]]'s principal representative despite not being its official King. This time, Indra himself appeared because the disappearance of multiple ancient Pantheons directly threatened the future of his own position.[1]
The meeting became an auction when Hela announced that she intended to sell the former Norse Divine territory. She planned to retain the Gods, treasures and resources she considered hers while transferring the dimension and its remaining institutional value to another Pantheon.[1]
Indra witnessed the resources being presented by the rapidly developing Dragons Nest. Advanced constructions such as the Tower of Nightmare and enormous technological Divine Artifacts demonstrated that [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction had already begun surpassing the conventional limitations of ancient Pantheon civilization despite existing for only a comparatively short period.[10]
As the bidding continued, Indra initially considered solving the problem through violence. Since the Hindu Pantheon possessed both Shiva and Kali, he briefly imagined attacking Hela, defeating her forces and simply taking the Norse territory without paying for it.[5]
The idea collapsed almost immediately. Shiva and Kali were autonomous beings who rarely obeyed rules simply because Indra issued them. Furthermore, the meeting was governed by a non-aggression agreement whose violation would punish not merely Indra personally but his entire faction. Indra therefore abandoned the thought before attempting to act upon it.[5]
The narration used this moment to explain his political position directly. Indra was God-King in title, but leadership had effectively become divided between himself and Shiva. Shiva was stronger, more respected and possessed far greater personal influence throughout the Pantheon. Indra hated the arrangement but lacked the power required to force Shiva or Kali beneath his authority.[5]
Hela's behavior reinforced the humiliation. While considering potential buyers and politically relevant parties, she concentrated primarily upon Shiva, [[Amaterasu]] and Haruna while giving Indra comparatively little attention. The slight angered him, yet Indra possessed enough self-control to avoid creating a confrontation in a room filled with beings he could not afford to provoke.[5]
Indra became particularly irritated with Kali because she had developed close relationships with Victor and several members of his family without providing the Hindu leadership with the amount of strategic information he believed those connections should have produced. From Indra's perspective, Kali's social proximity to Dragons Nest represented a political resource that was being wasted.[5]
Watching Amaterasu Elderblood and Haruna also produced one of Indra's strangest ideas. Both women had achieved exceptionally advantageous positions by becoming personally connected to Victor. Indra therefore contemplated changing his sex, becoming a woman and attempting to "charm the Dragon" himself. He acknowledged that the idea felt extremely strange after living as a man for thousands of years, but he was opportunistic enough not to reject it solely on principle if the potential reward became sufficiently valuable.[5]
The actual Hindu bid for the Norse territory further revealed Indra's Divine specialization. Shiva's offer contained an Essence of Indra's Storm and Indra's own written records describing discoveries he had accumulated concerning his Divinities, alongside Shiva's Destruction knowledge and other rare objects.[3] This establishes that Indra had developed substantial understanding of Storm-related Divine authority even though the novel has still not shown him personally fighting with it.
The Hindu Pantheon ultimately acquired the former Asgardian territory. By Chapter 986, the acquisition had elevated the Hindu Pantheon into the position of the second most influential Pantheon on Earth after Dragons Nest.[11][2]
Ironically, this institutional expansion did not strengthen Indra's personal position. Shiva's role in obtaining the territory and his already greater prestige continued pushing Indra further into the background.

#### God Emperor Victor Elderblood
Two years after Ragnarök, [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] and [[Yggdrasil]] were actively restoring the planet. Pollution, buried garbage and other damage created through Human civilization were being removed on a global scale. Although most knowledgeable supernatural factions understood who was responsible, some Gods attempted to claim the achievement for themselves to increase their own Faith and influence.[2]
One of those Gods was [[Ganesha]].
[[Kaguya Elderblood Blank]] entered the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]] accompanied by evolved members of [[Blank Clan]] and publicly executed Ganesha with Dragon Fire. [[Shiva]] and Indra both considered reacting, but neither moved because striking Victor's wife would cause the entire Elderblood faction to descend upon the Pantheon.[2]
Kaguya then made the purpose of the demonstration clear: Victor might personally ignore small insults while concentrating upon larger Imperial affairs, but his wives would not tolerate others stealing his accomplishments or damaging the pride of their family.[2]
The confrontation escalated further with the appearance of War. The Herald arrived carrying a direct message from Victor addressed specifically to Shiva: control the Hindu Gods, because there would be no second warning.[2]
War's pressure alone was enough to leave Indra and several Gods collapsed and desperately trying to breathe after the Herald departed. When another God questioned whether the Hindu Pantheon intended to tolerate the humiliation, Indra remembered the Being that had just stood before them and immediately rejected the possibility of confrontation.[2]
Shiva subsequently focused his attention upon Indra. Ganesha had acted partly from his own greed, but Shiva understood that Indra's political influence was behind the incident. Indra's status had deteriorated so severely that many beings asked who ruled the Hindu Pantheon and answered Shiva rather than Indra. This loss of recognition had made Indra increasingly desperate to regain control.[2]
The Ganesha incident therefore had the opposite result from what Indra wanted. Instead of restoring his authority, it gave Shiva the perfect justification to restrict him further. Victor's warning had also publicly acknowledged Shiva—not Indra—as the individual responsible for controlling the Hindu Pantheon, unintentionally or deliberately strengthening Shiva's legitimacy at the King's expense.[2]
Shiva concluded that Indra could no longer be allowed to operate freely. In his view, Indra's desperation might eventually produce another mistake severe enough to provoke Victor into destroying the Hindu Pantheon altogether.[2]
Shiva finally moved to take formal control. His influence had never disappeared; he possessed supporters throughout the Pantheon and merely had not previously chosen to occupy the throne personally.[4]
Indra understood immediately that attempting to oppose him would only result in being consumed politically—and potentially physically—by Shiva's movement. He therefore stepped aside.
Publicly, the event was described as a voluntary resignation in which Indra nobly surrendered authority to a more experienced God during an unstable era.[4]
The narration immediately exposes the explanation as political theater.
Indra had been expelled from his position.[4]
The transition was initially known primarily within the highest levels of the Hindu Pantheon, allowing Indra to retain some dignity while Shiva became its official God-King. Indra's millennia-long reign effectively ended without a civil war because he possessed enough political awareness to recognize that resisting was pointless.[4]
Shiva's rule did not preserve Hindu independence for long. Victor subsequently began systematically dismantling or absorbing every supernatural political structure on Earth capable of threatening the emerging [[Elderblood Empire]].[12]
When Victor arrived in the Hindu Pantheon, the decision was ultimately made by Shiva and Kali rather than Indra. Victor offered them two outcomes: integration into the Elderblood Empire or destruction, with the Divinities of the fallen Gods being repurposed for Imperial use.[12]
The Hindu Pantheon surrendered and became part of the Elderblood Empire while retaining a degree of internal administration.[6]
Indra therefore entered the Imperial era not as a king or independent sovereign but as one of the ancient Gods belonging to a Pantheon now subordinate to Victor.

#### Those Who Rule Reality
Indra has not played a major direct role during the current Upper-Sector preparations, but the political hierarchy above him changed again.
After [[Shiva]] secretly reconstructed [[Veylan Valentin Walker]] in an attempt to reproduce a fraction of [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s hereditary potential, Victor stripped Shiva of the position of God-King and appointed [[Kali Elderblood]] as the new principal ruler of the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]].[7]
Shiva accepted the punishment and agreed to help Kali bring the Pantheon completely under her administration.[13]
Consequently, Indra is currently a former God-King serving within a Hindu Pantheon governed by Kali and subordinated to the [[Elderblood Empire]]. There is presently no indication that Victor or Kali restored any portion of Indra's former governmental authority.

### Skills & Abilities
Indra is an ancient God who ruled one of Earth's major Pantheons for thousands of years. However, unlike Gods such as [[Shiva]], Kali or [[Thor]], Indra has not yet received a dedicated combat scene. His ability section should therefore distinguish confirmed Divine associations from powers that are merely associated with Indra in external mythology.
- God Physiology: Indra is a genuine Divine Being rather than a Mortal or [[Demigod]]. His existence is sustained through Divine nature and Energy, and he has remained active across thousands of years.[5]
- [[Divine Energy]]: As an ancient God and former God-King, Indra possesses Divine Energy and conceptual authorities associated with his identity. The novel has not yet quantified his reserves or shown his full combat output.
- Sky Manipulation: Indra is explicitly introduced as the God of the Skies.[1] His authority therefore extends to the Divine Concept associated with the sky, although no complex application has yet been demonstrated on-page.
- Lightning Manipulation: Chapter 986 explicitly refers to Indra as the Lightning God.[2] The exact scale, techniques and combat applications of his Lightning Divinity remain unrevealed.
- Storm Manipulation: A Divine Essence associated with Indra's Storm existed within the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]]'s resources and was offered during the auction for the former Norse territory.[3] This confirms Storm as part of Indra's Divine authority, although he has not personally demonstrated a named Storm technique.
- Storm Essence: The Hindu Pantheon possessed an Essence containing Indra's Storm authority.[3]
- Divinity Research: Indra maintained written records documenting discoveries and insights accumulated through studying his own Divinities. Shiva considered those records valuable enough to include in a Pantheon-scale Divine transaction.[3]
- Divine Body Transformation: Indra considered altering his manifested sex from male to female as a realistic option rather than an impossible hypothetical.[5]
- Sex Transformation: Indra specifically contemplated transforming into a woman in order to improve his chances of obtaining [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s personal support. He chose not to pursue the idea because changing after thousands of years as a man felt strange to him.[5]
- Political Authority (Formerly): As God-King, Indra possessed formal authority over the Hindu Pantheon and the institutions governed through its throne. His authority was institutional rather than absolute, as Shiva and Kali remained powerful enough to act independently of him.[5]
- Pantheon Administration (Formerly): Shiva deliberately allowed Indra to carry much of the administrative burden of formal rulership for thousands of years.[4] Whatever his shortcomings, Indra therefore possesses extensive practical experience managing a Divine civilization.
- Political Experience: The narration explicitly notes that Indra's long time in power gave him enough experience to view situations from multiple perspectives despite his impulsive personality.[5]
- Risk Assessment: Indra can recognize when an idea would expose himself or his Pantheon to disproportionate danger. He abandoned the plan to attack Hela after accounting for the treaty and the independence of Shiva and Kali, and later immediately rejected confronting War after experiencing the Herald's presence.[5][2]
- Self-Preservation: Indra consistently chooses survival over pointless pride once the difference in power becomes undeniable. This trait ultimately prevented a destructive civil conflict when Shiva moved to remove him.[4]
Note: Rain, War and Thunder are traditional attributes associated with Indra and appeared in older versions of this article, but the currently indexed novel chapters do not provide sufficient Indra-specific feats or explanations to build separate ability trees for them. They should not be expanded into unsupported powers until the novel directly demonstrates or explains those authorities.

### Relationships

#### Shiva
[[Shiva]] is the single most important political figure in Indra's life and the primary reason his reign never became absolute.
Indra formally occupied the throne for thousands of years while Shiva avoided routine administration. However, Shiva never surrendered his actual influence. He possessed supporters throughout the Pantheon, remained its strongest and most respected major God, and could intervene whenever he considered the situation sufficiently serious.[5][4]
Indra deeply resented this arrangement. From his perspective, the Gods should have listened to their God-King rather than another member of the Pantheon. Unfortunately for him, he lacked the strength or political weight required to force Shiva into submission.[5]
Their relationship deteriorated as Indra's mistakes accumulated. Shiva began moving more actively during the Demonic War, then concluded after the [[Ganesha]] incident that Indra had become actively dangerous to the Pantheon's survival.[9][2]
When Shiva finally consolidated his influence, Indra stepped aside rather than challenge him. His reign ended through political expulsion rather than battle.[4]
Even after Shiva himself later lost the throne, there is no indication that Indra was considered as a candidate for restoration. Leadership passed directly to Kali.[7]

#### Kali Elderblood
Kali was another major obstacle to Indra exercising absolute authority. Like [[Shiva]], she was powerful and independent enough that Indra could not simply command her despite being God-King.[5]
Indra was especially frustrated by Kali's relationship with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] and the Elderblood family. He believed that such close connections should have produced more strategic information and political advantages for the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]].[5]
The power imbalance between them only increased over time as Kali developed through her association with Victor and eventually became a True Dragon Goddess. By Chapter 1152, she had risen far beyond Indra politically as well, becoming the ruler of the Pantheon he once governed.[7]

#### Ganesha
[[Ganesha]] was one of the Gods associated with Indra's attempts to recover lost political influence.
During [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s environmental restoration of Earth, Ganesha claimed credit for work actually being performed by Victor and [[Yggdrasil]]. Mortal recognition and worship would have increased Ganesha's influence while indirectly strengthening the political faction surrounding Indra.[2]
The plan ended catastrophically when Kaguya entered the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]] and killed Ganesha in front of its Gods. [[Shiva]] immediately recognized Indra's influence behind the situation.[2]
Rather than restoring Indra's authority, Ganesha's death became the incident that finally convinced Shiva to remove him from power.

#### Victor Elderblood
Indra's relationship with [[Victor Elderblood]] is defined primarily by politics and the continually expanding difference in status between them.
At the auction following Ragnarök, Indra quickly recognized that Victor's personal relationships created enormous political advantages. The realization became so extreme that he briefly considered changing his own sex and attempting to become romantically involved with Victor if it would strengthen his position.[5]
He never attempted the plan.
His actual indirect conflict with Victor occurred through the [[Ganesha]] incident. The attempt to benefit politically from Victor's accomplishments provoked Kaguya and War, while Victor's message publicly treated [[Shiva]] rather than Indra as the responsible authority of the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]].[2]
The result unintentionally helped Shiva remove Indra from power.
Victor later absorbed the entire Hindu Pantheon into the [[Elderblood Empire]], leaving Indra subordinate to the same ruler whose rise had accelerated the collapse of his kingship.[6]

#### Hela Elderblood
Indra encountered Hela directly during the auction of the former [[Norse Pantheon]].
Hela showed little concern for his formal title and focused her attention upon figures she considered more politically relevant, particularly [[Shiva]], [[Amaterasu]] and Haruna. Being ignored visibly angered Indra.[5]
He briefly considered taking her territory by force, but abandoned the idea once he accounted for the non-aggression agreement and the fact that Shiva and Kali were not guaranteed to support him.[5]

#### Yama
Yama was nominally responsible for the Hindu [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] but deliberately separated his Hell from the Pantheon during the Demonic War as part of the plans surrounding [[Diablo]].[8]
The action created an enormous administrative crisis for Indra by disrupting Soul processing and forcing the Pantheon to compensate for functions normally handled automatically by its Hell. Yama openly found Indra's resulting frustration amusing.[8]
The incident became one of the earliest major demonstrations that Indra lacked complete control even over essential systems belonging to his own Pantheon.

### Current Status
Indra is alive and remains a member of the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]].
He no longer possesses the position, institutional authority or political privileges of God-King. His "voluntary resignation" was a diplomatic fiction used to conceal the fact that [[Shiva]] had forced him from office.[4]
After [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] annexed the Hindu Pantheon, Indra became a subject of the [[Elderblood Empire]] alongside the rest of its Gods.[6]
The Hindu Pantheon's current ruler is [[Kali Elderblood]], who replaced Shiva after the Veylan incident.[7] Indra therefore currently exists beneath both Kali's Pantheon administration and Victor's supreme Imperial authority.
