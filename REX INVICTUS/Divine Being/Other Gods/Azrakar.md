### Appearances

#### Debut
Chapter 1192
I am Azrakar, ruler of the void between galaxies!
—Azrakar to War, Chapter 1193
Azrakar is the name given to two different Gods associated with the Black Eclipse in [[MTWABV Series|My Three Wives Are Beautiful Vampires]]. The original Azrakar was a Primordial God of the Void and one of the faction's Ten Chief Gods, serving [[Vharzath]], the Great Darkness. His Divine authority allowed him to distort and compress space, confine opponents within spatial distortions, and launch attacks from unexpected locations. He could also spread Darkness across enormous astronomical distances and release concentrated attacks capable of devastating star systems. Despite his considerable power and the additional power he drew from his patron, the original Azrakar was killed and erased from existence by War and the other Four Horsemen of the Apocalypse.[1][2][3]
The original Azrakar supervised an operation involving Black Eclipse's attempts to introduce fragments of Divine power into the [[Elderblood Empire]]'s territory. When War attacked his vessel, Azrakar engaged him in a battle whose destruction spread across star systems and affected neighboring galaxies. [[Kaelith]], [[Nerath]], and [[Vhalor]] eventually joined the confrontation, turning it into a battle between four Chief Gods and the Four Horsemen. The Horsemen ultimately overcame the Gods by fighting together within an Eldritch domain associated with [[Gol-goroth]]. Death immobilized Azrakar, Pestilence weakened him, Famine severed his limbs, and War decapitated him and pierced his heart before the Horsemen erased him from existence. The original Azrakar was not among the Gods subsequently restored by [[Vaelthar]].[3][4]
The name was later assigned to another God through a ritual ordered by Vharzath. The successor abandoned his former name, received Darkness, and emerged resembling the original Azrakar. Vharzath described him as a threat capable of endangering an entire galaxy, while explicitly acknowledging that he was weaker than his predecessor. His individual techniques and combat abilities have not yet been demonstrated in the available chapters, and his creation does not constitute the resurrection of the original Azrakar.[5]

### Physical Appearance

#### Original Azrakar
The original Azrakar's Divine form stood over six meters tall and possessed an extremely slender, skeletal physique. His narrow torso was marked by black, rib-like structures beneath a surface resembling solidified emptiness rather than ordinary flesh. His elongated head lacked a visible nose, ears, or conventional human facial features. Six white eyes, arranged in three pairs, occupied his face without visible pupils. His arms extended almost to his knees and ended in hands with six thin fingers each. He had no conventional legs; instead, his lower body tapered into a mass of formless Darkness that allowed him to hover in space without touching a surface.[1]
Broken rings revolved behind his head, each turning independently rather than forming a conventional halo. Matter approaching the rings disappeared, although the precise nature and mechanism of this effect were not explained. His presence also suppressed the surrounding light and color. The battlefield became increasingly gray as his Darkness spread, obscuring the stars and making his surroundings appear as though illumination itself had been swallowed.[1]
Although his physical form remained recognizable during combat, the scale of his power extended far beyond his immediate body. His Darkness spread across vast regions of space, while his spatial techniques affected opponents and structures at considerable distances. His body could also undergo limited structural reorganization to prevent an existing wound from worsening. This did not heal the injury: wounds inflicted by War remained open and accumulated throughout the battle. The original Azrakar was eventually dismembered, decapitated, mortally wounded, and erased from existence.[1][2][3]

#### Successor Azrakar
The successor is described as resembling the original Azrakar after [[Vharzath]]'s ritual. The available scene does not provide a complete physical description, however, and does not independently confirm that he possesses the original's six eyes, revolving rings, elongated arms, formless lower body, or exact height. Those traits therefore remain specifically associated with the original's demonstrated appearance until the successor is described in greater detail.[5]

### Personality

#### Original Azrakar
The original Azrakar was proud, domineering, and deeply conscious of his age and status. He expected others to recognize his authority as a Primordial God and resented opponents who challenged him without showing the deference he considered appropriate. During his confrontation with War, he repeatedly emphasized his identity and ancient existence, expecting his title and power to intimidate the warrior. War's refusal to respond to these declarations unsettled and angered him, particularly because the warrior continued to challenge him despite the enormous destructive power Azrakar displayed.[1][2]
Azrakar preferred to defeat War personally and initially regarded [[Kaelith]]'s intervention as an insult to his strength. His objection disappeared when she explained that the [[Vharzath|Great Darkness]] had ordered her to intervene. Although his pride made him reluctant to accept assistance, his loyalty to Vharzath ultimately took precedence over his personal preference. He accepted the additional Chief Gods when they arrived and agreed to [[Nerath]]'s instruction that War be captured alive.[1][2]
His resentment was more pronounced toward Niklaus, whose influence over the Great Darkness exceeded what Azrakar believed his subordinate status should permit. Azrakar could not simply order Niklaus around, despite his own position among Black Eclipse's Chief Gods. This frustrated him, but he continued to obey the faction's higher authority and carry out the assignment he had been given.[6][1]
In combat, Azrakar initially relied on overwhelming force, using spatial confinement, immense Darkness, and concentrated attacks to overpower War. When these methods failed to produce a decisive victory, he adapted by changing the origins of his spatial attacks, combining his distortions with Kaelith's illusions, and exploiting apparent openings to catch War's weapon. His tactical adjustments demonstrated that he could learn from an opponent's counters, but War repeatedly recognized and exploited the limitations of his techniques. Azrakar's adaptability could prolong the fight, but it could not overcome the Horsemen's eventual coordination and Eldritch advantage.[2][3]

#### Successor Azrakar
The successor's personality has not been sufficiently established. [[Vharzath]] orders him to abandon his former name, accept the name Azrakar, and remain ready for further orders. He responds to his master's instruction and leaves after the ritual. The available scene does not establish whether he shares the original's pride, temperament, resentment toward Niklaus, or personal attitude toward combat.[5]

### Synopsis

#### Those Who Rule Reality
Black Eclipse is an ancient faction operating in the Upper Sectors. Intelligence recovered by [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] describes a hierarchy headed by the [[Vharzath|Great Darkness]], followed by seven Thrones and additional ranks of Gods and subordinate forces. The recovered information is incomplete and partly secondhand, so its estimates of the faction's age, hierarchy, and strength cannot be treated as a complete account of its leadership. Later information identifies ten Chief Gods beneath the Seven Thrones, with Azrakar among them. He was not one of the seven Thrones himself.[7][2][8]
As the barrier separating Victor's territory from the Upper Sectors weakened, Black Eclipse attempted to introduce fragments of Divine power into the Empire, seeking potential hosts. The Empire discovered and destroyed these fragments before they could establish themselves successfully. Niklaus warned the Great Darkness against underestimating Victor, but Azrakar was still assigned to supervise the operation and force the infiltration forward. The mission placed him directly in the path of War, Victor's Herald, who entered Black Eclipse's territory to confront the faction's forces.[9][6][1]
Azrakar travelled aboard a vessel resembling a living organism. When a subordinate reported that the Divine fragments continued to fail to find suitable hosts, Azrakar remained silent. He then sensed a spatial disturbance and searched the surrounding Solar System, attempting to identify the intruder. Before the search could reveal the source, War attacked the vessel and destroyed it, killing those aboard. Azrakar responded by spreading Darkness through the surrounding region, suppressing the light of stars and engaging the intruder directly.[1]
The battle rapidly escalated. Azrakar manipulated distances to impede War's movement, confined him within distorted space, and hurled masses of Darkness that propelled him through star systems and astronomical debris. War repeatedly broke through these attacks with [[Gol-goroth]], returning to the fight despite the destruction surrounding them. Azrakar also drew on additional power that War recognized as originating from the Great Darkness. This foreign power increased the threat he posed, although the precise nature and limits of the enhancement remained unexplained.[1]
As the fight continued, Azrakar compressed space and launched concentrated attacks. One of his black spheres, dense enough to bend nearby starlight, was deflected by War and travelled into a region containing numerous star systems. The resulting destruction included stars collapsing, planets being torn from their orbits, and black holes consuming the debris. The episode demonstrated the destructive consequences of the projectile in that environment, rather than establishing that every use of the technique must produce an identical outcome.[2]
Azrakar grew increasingly frustrated that War continued to counter his attacks. He attempted to seize the advantage by affecting a vast region of space instead of concentrating only on the warrior. War nevertheless identified and attacked the structures supporting the spatial effect, breaking the technique without needing to overpower every part of the affected region simultaneously. The exchange exposed an important limitation: Azrakar's complex spatial constructions could be disrupted when an opponent identified how they were maintained.[2]
[[Kaelith]] eventually arrived under orders from the Great Darkness. Azrakar initially objected to her interference, but accepted her assistance after learning that their master wanted the confrontation brought under control. The two combined Azrakar's spatial distortions with Kaelith's perceptual interference and illusions. War's attacks were diverted toward false images, allowing Azrakar to strike from unexpected directions. However, War adapted to their tactics, attacking during the intervals when Azrakar had to adjust his spatial domain and preventing him from maintaining every distortion while simultaneously defending himself.[2][3]
Azrakar also began opening spatial rifts around War and changing the points from which his attacks originated. These techniques forced War to defend against attacks coming from unusual angles. War countered by reading the pattern of the distortions, attacking at close range, and exploiting the moments when Azrakar's control was interrupted. Gol-goroth wounded Azrakar's arm, and the God reorganized his body's structure to prevent the cut from deepening. The wound nevertheless remained open. Further attacks damaged his torso and head, progressively reducing his ability to maintain control over the battlefield.[3]
[[Nerath]] and [[Vhalor]] subsequently arrived, bringing the number of Chief Gods present to four. Azrakar introduced them and accepted Nerath's statement that the Great Silence wanted War alive. The confrontation became a battle between Azrakar, Kaelith, Nerath, and Vhalor on one side and War and the other Horsemen on the other. War revealed the additional Eldritch manifestations connected to Gol-goroth, bringing Famine, Pestilence, and Death into the battle alongside him.[2][3]
The Horsemen eventually established an Eldritch domain that isolated the battlefield and undermined the Gods' effective control over the surrounding space. Azrakar's spatial authority no longer gave him the advantage it had earlier in the confrontation. Death immobilized him with miasma, Pestilence weakened him, and Famine severed his limbs. War then decapitated Azrakar and pierced his heart. The Four Horsemen unleashed their combined power and erased him from existence, ending the original Azrakar's life permanently.[3]
The Great Darkness reacted to the defeat by ordering the Horsemen to be captured or killed and calling for the remaining Chief Gods. [[Vaelthar]] later entered the aftermath and restored Kaelith, Nerath, and Vhalor. The original Azrakar was not restored, and the available account does not show his body or existence returning after the Horsemen's attack.[3][4]

#### The Replacement
The name Azrakar returned after the original had been erased. In a later scene, [[Vharzath]] ordered another God to abandon his previous name and become Azrakar, God of the Void. Darkness entered the new God during the ritual, after which he emerged resembling the original. Vharzath described him as a threat capable of endangering an entire galaxy, but explicitly stated that he was not yet as strong as his predecessor, who had lived far longer.[5]
[[Xytherion]], identified as the Throne of the End, entered after the ritual and confirmed that the process had succeeded. Vharzath stated that nine further creations were still required. The successor was instructed to remain ready and then departed. He was not shown fighting, and the available scene did not establish that he had inherited every technique, physical characteristic, or combat experience possessed by the original Azrakar. The ritual therefore introduced a replacement bearing the same name, not a resurrection of the God erased by the Four Horsemen.[5]

### Skills & Abilities
The original Azrakar was a Chief God and Primordial God of the Void whose demonstrated abilities centered on spatial manipulation, Darkness, and concentrated energy attacks. He could distort distances, compress or confine regions of space, open rifts to alter attack trajectories, and spread Darkness across vast astronomical distances. War also recognized additional power in Azrakar that originated from the Great Darkness. These feats establish Azrakar as an exceptionally dangerous Divine combatant, but do not mean that every individual technique could independently destroy a galaxy.[1][2][3]
The successor was created through Vharzath's ritual and is described as a galaxy-level threat who remains weaker than the original. His personal techniques have not been demonstrated in the available chapters. The abilities listed below therefore refer primarily to the original Azrakar unless otherwise specified.[5]

#### Primordial Divinity and Cosmic Power
- Divinity of the Void: Azrakar's primary Divine authority is associated with the Void between galaxies and the manipulation of space. He identifies himself as the ruler of that Void and demonstrates the ability to alter spatial relationships during combat. The full conceptual nature and ultimate limits of his Divinity have not been explained.[1][2]
- Chief God-Level Power: As one of Black Eclipse's Ten Chief Gods, Azrakar possesses extraordinary Divine power and is identified as a threat capable of endangering an entire galaxy. This classification describes his overall threat level; it does not establish that every attack he performs has galaxy-destroying output.[2][8]
- Empowerment from the [[Vharzath|Great Darkness]]: During the battle, War recognizes that Azrakar is drawing on power belonging to his patron. The additional power allows Azrakar to intensify his attacks, but its exact mechanism, duration, and maximum output are not established.[1]
- Energy Accumulation: Azrakar gathers energy before releasing concentrated attacks. His preparation of a dense black sphere and his later accumulation of power for a beam demonstrate that he can concentrate his output rather than relying exclusively on immediate, low-preparation attacks.[1][2]

#### Void and Spatial Manipulation
- Spatial Manipulation: Azrakar alters spatial relationships around his opponents, interfering with movement, distance, positioning, and the structure of the battlefield. His spatial authority forms the foundation of many of his offensive and defensive techniques.[1][2][3]
- Distance Distortion: He can distort the relationship between movement and distance so that War's attempts to advance fail to bring him closer as expected. The technique interferes with ordinary spatial movement rather than merely creating a physical barrier.[1]
- Spatial Confinement: Azrakar encloses an opponent within a distorted region of space to restrict movement and prevent escape. War counters the technique by attacking the spatial structure itself rather than simply attempting to force his way through it.[1]
- Spatial Compression: He compresses space around his opponent, applying pressure and attempting to immobilize or crush the target. War repeatedly disrupts these constructions by identifying their structure and striking the spatial effect directly.[1][2]
- Spatial Displacement: Azrakar can alter his position during combat and appear behind or beside War to deliver attacks. The scenes demonstrate spatially assisted repositioning, but do not establish that he can teleport without limitation under all circumstances.[1]
- Spatial Rifts: Azrakar opens rifts around War and uses them to launch attacks from unexpected angles. These attacks can originate from locations that do not correspond to Azrakar's apparent position, complicating the opponent's defense.[3]
- Alteration of Attack Origins: Rather than simply distorting the distance between two points, Azrakar can manipulate the points from which his attacks emerge. This makes it more difficult to predict the direction of an incoming strike and allows him to exploit openings created by other spatial distortions.[3]
- Spatial Interference: Azrakar can distort an opponent's spatial awareness and interfere with the relationship between perceived positions and the actual battlefield. When coordinated with [[Kaelith]]'s illusions, this makes it harder for War to distinguish a genuine target from a deceptive image.[3]
- Spatial [[Radar Warning Receiver (RWR)|Interception]]: Azrakar can catch part of [[Gol-goroth]] within a spatial distortion, obstructing the weapon's movement and attempting to create an opening for a counterattack. War eventually counters his techniques by maintaining close pressure and disrupting the structures supporting the distortions.[3]
- Spatial Domain Manipulation: Azrakar can maintain a distorted region in which distances and spatial orientation become unreliable. This allows him to shape the battlefield and coordinate attacks with Kaelith. The domain requires adjustments, however, and War exploits the moments when Azrakar must modify it to keep its effects active.[2][3]
- Spatial Perception: Azrakar senses a spatial disturbance and searches the surrounding Solar System for its source. Although he does not immediately locate the intruder, his perception alerts him to an abnormality that ordinary observation has not revealed.[1]
- Spatially Assisted Offense: He combines spatial manipulation with direct strikes and energy attacks, using disrupted movement or misleading positioning to create openings. The technique becomes more effective when Kaelith's illusions further interfere with the opponent's perception.[3]
- Limitations Against Eldritch Domains: Azrakar's spatial authority is not absolute. Once the Four Horsemen establish their Eldritch domain through Gol-goroth, the Chief Gods lose their effective control over the affected battlefield. Azrakar's earlier spatial advantage consequently fails to protect him from their coordinated assault.[3]

#### Darkness Manipulation
- Expansive Darkness: Azrakar releases Darkness that spreads across enormous regions of space, including thousands of star systems. He can intensify the darkness as the battle escalates, obscuring increasingly large portions of the surrounding region.[1]
- Light Suppression: His presence and Darkness suppress visible illumination, causing stars to fade and the surrounding region to lose its color. The battlefield becomes gray and then disappears beneath the spreading darkness.[1]
- Stellar Concealment: Azrakar's Darkness obscures stars and celestial bodies, concealing portions of the battlefield within a vast dark region. This effect accompanies his offensive techniques and spatial interference.[1]
- Darkness Projection: He launches concentrated masses of Darkness at his opponent. These attacks can propel War through star systems, asteroid belts, and other astronomical obstacles.[1]
- Darkness Waves: Azrakar releases broad waves of Darkness and energy capable of driving War backward and scattering astronomical debris. The destructive force of these attacks contributes to the devastation surrounding their confrontation.[1]
- Darkness-Based Energy Attacks: Azrakar concentrates gathered power into beams and releases it against War's attacks. Their collision produces a large explosion and a destructive wave through the surrounding region.[1]
- Darkness Restraints: He wraps Darkness around War's arm and attempts to restrain him and take away his weapon. War breaks through the restraint by overpowering the Darkness around his body and continuing his attack.[1]
- Darkness-Enhanced Defense: Azrakar surrounds himself with Darkness during combat and uses it alongside his spatial techniques to interfere with incoming attacks. This does not make him invulnerable; War repeatedly penetrates his defenses and inflicts lasting wounds.[1][2]
- Darkness and Spatial Synergy: Azrakar combines Darkness with spatial manipulation, obscuring the battlefield while altering distance and positioning. When [[Kaelith]] adds her illusions, the combined techniques can cause attacks to miss their intended targets and create openings for counterattacks.[2][3]
- Vulnerability to Superior Attacks: His Darkness can be penetrated or overcome by sufficiently powerful attacks. War repeatedly cuts through his defenses with [[Gol-goroth]], and the Four Horsemen eventually defeat him through coordinated Eldritch power.[1][3]

#### Void Sphere and Concentrated Energy
- Void Sphere: Azrakar gathers a tremendous quantity of energy in his palm and compresses it into a dense black sphere. The sphere bends the light of nearby stars and travels through space along a path where distance appears to have been erased.[2]
- Large-Scale Gravitational Destruction: After War deflects the Void Sphere, it enters a region containing numerous star systems. Stars collapse, planets are torn from their orbits, and black holes consume the resulting debris. The event demonstrates the devastating consequences of the projectile in that region, but does not establish that every Void Sphere must produce the same outcome in every circumstance.[2]
- Concentrated Energy Blasts: Azrakar releases concentrated energy at close and long range. These attacks can strike War, force him backward, and contribute to the large-scale destruction surrounding the battle.[1]
- Energy Accumulation and Release: He can gather power before unleashing a larger attack. His concentrated beam clashes with War's sword attack and triggers an explosion that sends a destructive wave through the surrounding region.[1]
- Explosive Counterattacks: Azrakar releases bursts of energy to separate himself from War or disrupt an incoming assault. Although these attacks can throw War backward, they do not prevent him from returning to close combat.[1]

#### Physical Attributes and Combat
- Superhuman Strength: Azrakar can fight War in close combat, deliver physical blows, and throw his opponent through astronomical obstacles. His physical attacks remain dangerous even when his spatial techniques are being challenged.[1]
- Superhuman Durability: He survives numerous powerful attacks from War and continues fighting despite accumulating injuries. His durability is substantial but not absolute, as repeated precision strikes eventually leave him severely wounded and unable to withstand the Horsemen's finishing sequence.[1][2][3]
- Flight: Azrakar moves through space without conventional legs or visible propulsion. His lower body forms a mass of Darkness that hovers above the surrounding environment.[1]
- High-Speed Combat: Azrakar exchanges attacks with War at high speed, repositioning himself, defending against incoming strikes, and launching counterattacks during rapid exchanges. The confrontation demonstrates his ability to participate in combat at War's level of speed, though it does not establish a precise numerical speed.[1]
- Long-Range Combat: His Darkness, concentrated projectiles, spatial distortions, and energy attacks allow him to engage opponents across substantial distances rather than relying exclusively on physical contact.[1][2]
- Close-Quarters Combat: Azrakar uses direct strikes and physical counterattacks, sometimes combining them with spatial distortions to obstruct [[Gol-goroth]] or exploit War's position.[1][3]
- Combat Adaptation: After War repeatedly counters his initial methods, Azrakar changes his tactics. He coordinates spatial distortions with [[Kaelith]]'s illusions, alters the origins of his attacks, and uses apparent openings to attempt to trap War's weapon. War eventually recognizes and counters these tactics.[2][3]
- Structural Reorganization: When Gol-goroth cuts his arm, Azrakar reorganizes his body's structure to prevent the wound from deepening. This is an injury-containment technique rather than confirmed regeneration: the wound remains open, and the damage continues to accumulate.[3]
- Pain and Injury Tolerance: Azrakar continues fighting despite substantial wounds to his body. His endurance allows the battle to continue, but does not prevent his injuries from compromising his ability to maintain his spatial techniques or survive the coordinated assault that follows.[3]

#### Battlefield Awareness and Tactical Abilities
- Spatial Detection: Azrakar senses a spatial disturbance and uses his Divinity to search the surrounding Solar System. His initial search does not reveal the intruder, but he recognizes that something has disturbed the region.[1]
- Tactical Adjustment: Azrakar adapts his approach after War defeats or counters his earlier attacks. He changes how he maintains his spatial domain, redirects the origins of his attacks, and makes use of [[Kaelith]]'s illusions to interfere with War's perception.[1][3]
- Deceptive Openings: Azrakar deliberately presents an apparent weakness to encourage War to attack. He attempts to use the resulting opening to catch the warrior's weapon within a spatial distortion. War eventually recognizes the tactic and changes his approach.[3]
- Coordinated Offense: Azrakar works with Kaelith, using spatial distortions to exploit the perceptual confusion caused by her illusions. Their combined tactics can misdirect War's attacks and create openings for strikes from unexpected directions.[2][3]
- Command Authority: Azrakar supervises an operation involving Black Eclipse's Divine fragments and acts as a senior authority aboard the vessel he commands. He introduces [[Nerath]] and [[Vhalor]] when they arrive and accepts the order to take War alive. His authority does not extend to freely commanding Niklaus, whose relationship with the [[Vharzath|Great Darkness]] places him outside Azrakar's ordinary command.[6][1][2]
- Operational Cooperation: Azrakar cooperates with other Chief Gods when they join the confrontation and adjusts his tactics to accommodate their involvement. The available chapters show him working with allies, but do not establish that he can control their powers or override their individual abilities.[2][3]

#### Weaknesses and Limitations
- Vulnerability to Precision Attacks: War's precise strikes penetrate Azrakar's defenses and inflict wounds that remain open. Azrakar can reorganize his body's structure to contain some injuries, but this does not heal them or prevent further damage.[3]
- Spatial Techniques Require Adjustment: Maintaining his complex spatial distortions requires Azrakar to make continued adjustments. War exploits the intervals between those adjustments, attacking the structures supporting the techniques and forcing Azrakar to divide his attention between offense, defense, and spatial control.[3]
- Deception Can Be Countered: Azrakar's use of apparent openings and misleading attack positions can initially catch War off guard. However, War recognizes the pattern and changes his response, limiting the effectiveness of the same trick when Azrakar attempts to use it again.[3]
- Dependence on Spatial Control and Perceptual Interference: Azrakar's combined use of spatial distortions, Darkness, and [[Kaelith]]'s illusions is effective at disrupting War's positioning and perception. War gradually adapts, attacks during the intervals in Azrakar's control, and prevents the God from maintaining his advantage indefinitely.[2][3]
- Eldritch Domain Suppression: The Four Horsemen establish an Eldritch domain through [[Gol-goroth]], undermining the Chief Gods' control over the affected battlefield. Azrakar's spatial authority no longer protects him from the Horsemen's coordinated assault once the domain takes hold.[3]
- No Confirmed Resurrection: The original Azrakar is killed and erased by the Four Horsemen. Although [[Vaelthar]] later restores Kaelith, [[Nerath]], and [[Vhalor]], the original Azrakar is not among those restored. The later appearance of a successor under the same name is not evidence that the original returned.[3][4]

#### Defeat by the Four Horsemen
The following abilities belong to the Four Horsemen and describe the techniques used to defeat Azrakar. They are not abilities possessed by Azrakar himself.
- Eldritch Domain Through [[Gol-goroth]]: Gol-goroth manifests its Eldritch nature and establishes a domain isolating the affected battlefield. Within this domain, the Four Horsemen gain the advantage over the Chief Gods, whose control over the space becomes ineffective.[3]
- Coordinated Eldritch Assault: War and his brothers synchronize their attacks and combine their power, preventing Azrakar from responding to each opponent independently. The coordinated assault overwhelms his remaining defenses.[3]
- Miasma Paralysis: Death immobilizes Azrakar with miasma, preventing him from escaping the final assault.[3]
- Weakening Through Pestilence: Pestilence weakens Azrakar during the coordinated attack, helping prevent him from resisting the other Horsemen.[3]
- Limb Severing: Famine severs Azrakar's limbs, removing his ability to fight effectively or escape the immediate assault.[3]
- Decapitation and Heart Impalement: War decapitates Azrakar and pierces his heart after the other Horsemen have immobilized and weakened him.[3]
- Existential Erasure: The Four Horsemen unleash their combined Eldritch power and erase Azrakar from existence. This is the finishing attack used against him, not a demonstrated ability belonging to Azrakar.[3]

#### The Successor Azrakar
- Ritual-Based Transformation: [[Vharzath]] orders another God to abandon his former name and assume the name Azrakar. Darkness enters the new God during the ritual, after which he emerges resembling the original.[5]
- Galaxy-Level Threat: Vharzath identifies the successor as a being capable of threatening an entire galaxy. This establishes his overall threat level, but does not demonstrate that he can reproduce every feat of the original Azrakar.[5]
- Explicitly Weaker Than the Original: Vharzath states that the successor is not yet as strong as the previous Azrakar, who had lived far longer. The successor's strength should therefore not be treated as equal to that of the original.[5]
- Unconfirmed Individual Techniques: The successor has not yet demonstrated the original's spatial confinement, spatial rifts, Darkness attacks, Void Sphere, or combat adaptations in the available scene. These remain unconfirmed for the successor until he demonstrates them himself.[5]
- Unconfirmed Physical Details: Although the successor resembles the original, the scene does not establish that every detail of the original's anatomy and appearance has been reproduced. His six eyes, revolving rings, and formless lower body should not be treated as independently confirmed traits of the successor.[5]
- Unconfirmed Resistance to Eldritch Powers: The successor has not demonstrated an ability to resist [[Gol-goroth]]'s Eldritch domain, survive existential erasure, or withstand the finishing sequence that killed the original Azrakar.[5]

### Trivia
- The original Azrakar was the Primordial God of the Void and one of Black Eclipse's Ten Chief Gods. He was not one of the Seven Thrones.[2][8]
- Azrakar's claim that his existence predates countless civilizations is part of his own declaration to War; the precise length of his lifespan has not been established.[2]
- The incomplete intelligence [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] recovered concerning Black Eclipse did not provide a complete roster of its highest-ranking members. Its account of the faction's hierarchy should not be treated as exhaustive.[7]
- The recovered intelligence lists the divinities Night, Void, Silence, Shadows, Death, Fear, the Eclipse, and Oblivion. These names are not presented as a confirmed list of the Seven Thrones.[7]
- Azrakar's Darkness can spread across thousands of star systems and suppress their visible light.[1]
- His Void Sphere bends the light of nearby stars. When War deflects it, the projectile causes a chain of stellar collapses and the formation of black holes in the region it reaches.[2]
- Azrakar can reorganize the structure of his body to prevent a wound from worsening, but this does not constitute confirmed healing or regeneration.[3]
- The original Azrakar was erased after Death immobilized him, Pestilence weakened him, Famine severed his limbs, and War decapitated him and pierced his heart.[3]
- [[Vaelthar]] later restored [[Kaelith]], [[Nerath]], and [[Vhalor]], but the original Azrakar was not among them.[4]
- The successor bearing Azrakar's name is a different God created through Vharzath's ritual, not the original Azrakar resurrected.[5]
- Vharzath explicitly states that the successor is weaker than the original and that nine more creations are still required.[5]
- The successor's individual combat abilities and full physical appearance have not yet been demonstrated in the available chapter.[5]
