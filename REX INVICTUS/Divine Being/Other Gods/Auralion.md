Auralion is a god and one of the ten chief gods of [[Aetheryon]], the leader the System records as <Emperor> and also as <Kaiser>. The ten are public because their work is tied to the justice that faction preaches, and each of them stands for one aspect of it. Auralion is one of those ten. Which aspect is his is not said.[1]
He does not serve <Law>. That faction is a different power, the herald of the Primordial of Balance, and it moves only when that Primordial allows it. He is also not Justice. Justice is the envoy of the same patron, the one who came for War, and this name is not given to that envoy. He is not [[Laelzorith]], [[Seraphel]], or [[Dravakar]]. Those three are the judges Aetheryon sends later, and each of them names a domain of his own. Law, Truth, and Retribution are not read out as seats among the ten.[2][1][3][4][5]
What is known of him is the financing. Conan names him over the ruins of a [[Varkhess League]] capital, after the League has already lost. Kaleb is the one who places the office: one of the chief gods of the being known as <Emperor>. Auralion is not in the room. No body, no voice, and no attack are his.[1]

### Physical Appearance
Nothing of him is shown. Height, eyes, hair, skin, and race beyond the word god are unknown.
The body on the ground when the name is spoken is not his. That captive is the leader of the League, pale, with octopus-like eyes and six arms, and he is unconscious. Conan throws that man at Kaleb's feet and then says who paid them.[1]

### Personality
He does not speak. No order, no meeting, and no defense of the League are shown.
The justice attached to his office is the justice the faction preaches. Kaleb does not take the word as a virtue. He treats it as the image they keep, and he counts them as enemies of the Emperor. The Emperor in that thought is [[Victor Elderblood]]. The word itself, as the narration gives it, depends on who speaks it and for whom it is supposed to hold. For these factions it is a way to keep a good name while they pursue their own interest.[1]

### Synopsis

#### Volume 9: Those Who Rule Reality
The Upper Sector is held by four factions, and none of them will let a fifth stand. While those leaders measure [[Victor Elderblood]], smaller powers read the new Empire as a reason to move. Some want protection from one of the four. Some only want what the expansion leaves unguarded. [[Aetheryon]] has already told [[Vharzath]] that he will not spend his main fleet for a small share, and that lesser factions are the ones he uses to test the Empire. The bargain that follows is narrower. He will hit Elderblood forces. He will not send his elites into Victor's galaxy.[6][7][3]
The [[Varkhess League]] is the first of the small powers to act. It is an alliance of rulers near an imperial border. Its leaders take three worlds the Empire has only just incorporated, for the ore and the industry, while imperial attention is on driving Darkness off. The Empire takes the worlds back. Two days later Conan leads elites who can erase solar systems, and the League's own system is finished in under thirty minutes. There is hardly any resistance. A foolish decision, driven by fear, costs them everything.[1]
In the ruins of the capital, Conan and Kaleb stand over the League's leader. Conan says the one who financed them is a god named Auralion, and he throws that leader down. Kaleb adds the rank. Auralion serves as one of the chief gods of one of the four enemy factions. Asked which, Kaleb answers: the being known as <Emperor>.[1]
The Empire rewrites a record when a real name is learned. Victor learning the name of Darkness was enough to force an update. No one yet knows this leader's personal name, so the record keeps the codename the System assigns on its own. <Emperor> is that codename. The chief gods are the exception. Their names are already public, because their activity stands in connection with justice. The personal name his own elite later answers with is Emperor Aetheryon. That name is not in the League's hands, and it is not what Conan reports here.[1][5]
Conan does not treat the League as informed. They were being used. Their database has nothing on the faction. What they have are the names of the ten chief gods, each standing for one aspect of justice. Kaleb's answer is not a correction of the count. The justice they preach is still only a word. In the end they are enemies of the Emperor.[1]
The name did not come from a file on Auralion. Conan says they know who paid the League only because his mind is weak, and he is looking at the unconscious alien when he says it. The weak mind is the captive's. The captive is sent to the girls, the B.H.E. under [[Kaguya Elderblood Blank]], the third legion, who handle the dirtier work. Auralion is not among the prisoners.[1]
The League's ruin does not stop the other small powers. The [[Nharok Consortium]] steals shipments. The [[Order of Sythra]] buys separatists on worlds whose incorporation is not finished. Pirates take an abandoned mine and tax a route. [[Scathach Elderblood Scarlett]] reads the report and calls them vermin that smell an opportunity. The losses in it are the trap. The cargo does not matter. The politics of those worlds do not matter. The dead are clones, already back in Victor's galaxy. What the Empire wanted was the list of who would move against the Dragon Emperor without the strength to survive it. Most of them are shortsighted. A few act on the commission of the higher factions, and those few have real influence. Kaguya, once she has the result, names the patrons who are actually moving: <Emperor>, Darkness, and Light. The first two are the active ones. Light makes the situation worse and does not spend itself the same way.[1]
Auralion is not seen again. The ten stay the public face of that justice. When Aetheryon later spends hundreds of [[World Trees]] to keep his elite alive, seven rays open and that elite comes out, each one an aspect of justice. Auralion is not named in that number, and nothing on the page says the seven are the ten. The three judges who meet [[Scathach]] before that blast are named, and he is not one of those names either.[4][5]

### Skills & Abilities
No technique of his is shown. He does not fight, he does not speak, and he does not appear. The only act attached to the name is that he financed the [[Varkhess League]].[1]
- Chief god of justice: The office is one of ten. Each of the ten stands for one aspect of the justice that faction preaches. His own aspect is not named. No domain is spoken the way [[Laelzorith]] speaks Law, [[Seraphel]] speaks Truth, and [[Dravakar]] speaks Retribution. Those three claims are not seats in this count.[1][4]
- Financing: The League moves while the Empire is busy with Darkness, takes three new worlds, and loses them. Conan is the one who says Auralion paid them. No sum, no order, and no meeting are given. The League does not know the faction. It only knows the ten names.[1]

#### Power and Threat Level
- Chief god: That is the rank Kaleb gives him, under the being known as <Emperor>. No feat of his is measured. He is not shown at the level of the four leaders, and he is not one of Darkness's Thrones.[1]
- Not the envoy: Justice, who comes for War, has a Throne-level presence and serves the same patron. That presence is not given to Auralion, and the envoy is not given this name.[2][1]
- Not an Enforcer: Aetheryon tells Vharzath he will deploy his Enforcers. Auralion is not called one of them. He is also not a subordinate who can answer END with BEGIN. Aetheryon has no such subordinate.[7]

### Trivia
- The spelling on the page is Auralion. Conan is the one who says it. Kaleb is the one who names the office and the faction.[1]
- He is one of ten. Each stands for one aspect of the justice that faction preaches. The League knew the names and nothing else about the faction. His own aspect is not one of the names given.[1]
- He serves [[Aetheryon]]. At the time of the report the Empire still keeps the System codename, because no one knows the personal name. The shape that leader uses is a gigantic skull of pure fire. The name the elite later answer with is Emperor Aetheryon.[6][1][5]
- <Law> is another leader's faction, the herald of the Primordial of Balance, and is not his master.[3]
- The six-armed captive is the leader of the [[Varkhess League]], not Auralion. Conan's remark about a weak mind is aimed at that captive. The captive is sent to the B.H.E. Auralion is not taken.[1]
- The same patron sends the envoy Justice to claim War. Justice is not identified as Auralion.[2][1]
- [[Laelzorith]], [[Seraphel]], and [[Dravakar]] serve the same Emperor and name Law, Truth, and Retribution. Aetheryon calls those three his judges. They are not given as three of the ten public chief gods, and Auralion is not named with them.[1][4][5]
- Seven rays of light open after Heptagrammakos, and the elite that comes out is called aspects of justice. Auralion is not named among them.[5]
- The League's defeat is not his defeat. No later appearance is shown.[1]
