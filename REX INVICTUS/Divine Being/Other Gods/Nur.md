Nur was an Elder God of [[Nightingale]], a member of the [[Elder Gods Pantheon]] and the holder of the Divinity of the Elements. He referred to himself simply as the God of the Elements, while the narration further identified him as Nightingale's God of the Elements. His Divine authority gave him control over the known elements and their combinations, including Water, Fire, Air, Earth, Steam, Lava, Ice, Light and Darkness.[1]
Nur was one of only five Elder Gods remaining when the Pantheon openly entered the final war for Nightingale. Like the other surviving Elder Gods, his gigantic body exceeded one thousand metres in height and contained an extraordinarily large Soul described collectively with those of his companions as resembling clusters of Gods.[2]
During the war, Nur was confronted by Shiva, whose Divinity of Destruction represented an overwhelmingly unfavorable matchup for him. Although Nur unleashed practically every element available to him and even attempted direct physical combat, none of his attacks could penetrate Shiva's surrounding field of Destruction. Shiva ultimately released his Divinity through his presence alone and completely destroyed Nur, reducing the Elder God's physical existence into particles that would eventually return to Creation.[1]

### Physical Appearance
Nur possessed the colossal form characteristic of the five surviving Elder Gods who appeared during the final war. Each of them stood well above 1,000 metres and possessed radically different, alien anatomies. Some possessed mouths and eyes, others lacked recognizable facial structures entirely, and their bodies ranged from strange white forms to forms apparently composed from materials such as earth.[2]
Nur specifically was described as possessing an appearance so grotesque that the narration compared him to a creature taken directly from a horror movie. His appearance was intimidating enough that his emotionless speech alone was stated to frighten weak-hearted observers.[1]
The chapter does not provide a complete anatomical description of Nur, so exact details such as his facial structure, number of limbs, skin texture or coloration should not be treated as established unless revealed elsewhere. He nevertheless retained an enormous humanoid-scale arm capable of physically attacking [[Shiva]], with his fist moving at a speed that appeared abnormal even relative to his gigantic body.[1]

### Personality
Nur initially presented himself with an emotionless and intimidating demeanor. When [[Shiva]] asked for his name, Nur responded suspiciously rather than immediately introducing himself, questioning why the God of Destruction cared about knowing who he was.[1]
Shiva's answer—that he wished at least one person to remember Nur after destroying him—immediately angered the Elder God. Nur responded by firing an enormous blast of Fire from his mouth rather than continuing the conversation, demonstrating both his pride and how easily Shiva's casual dismissal could provoke him.[1]
Nur was nevertheless capable of understanding unfavorable combat circumstances. He quickly recognized that physically touching Shiva was dangerous because entering the God of Destruction's immediate sphere of influence would cause his body to be destroyed. He consequently relied primarily upon ranged Elemental attacks before frustration eventually caused him to attempt direct physical combat anyway.[1]
He was also fully aware of the Elder Gods' larger strategy. When [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] suddenly disappeared from [[Nightingale]], Nur explained to Shiva that the intervention of Shiva and Velnorah had disrupted their original plans, but that the primary objective had always remained the elimination of Victor Elderblood. Nur regarded Victor as an abomination that needed to be removed.[1]
Even immediately before his death, Nur refused to accept Shiva's moral justification for participating in the war. He accused Shiva of hypocrisy, arguing that despite presenting himself as though his actions were justified, the God of Destruction was ultimately no different from other Gods who acted according to their own interests.[1]

### Synopsis

#### Volume 6: King Among Kings
Nur was among the five gigantic Elder Gods who openly emerged during the final battle for [[Nightingale]]. The five Beings towered beyond the clouds at heights exceeding one thousand metres and possessed enormous Souls that [[Shiva]] compared to clusters of Gods. Although Nur was not identified by name during their initial appearance, he was one of the remaining Elder Gods participating in the Pantheon's final war against [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s coalition.[2]
The Elder Gods subsequently divided among the exceptionally powerful combatants who had joined Victor's side. While [[Ikor-Kar-VI]] and [[Var-Kar-VI]] confronted Victor directly and Shur fought [[Vlad III Tepes|Vlad Tepes]] and [[Otsuki Elderblood Haruna]], Nur became the opponent of Shiva, the Hindu God of Destruction.[1]
Their confrontation was extraordinarily one-sided. Shiva began by casually asking Nur for his name. Nur initially questioned the reason, only for Shiva to explain that he wanted someone to remember the Elder God after his destruction. Offended by the casual certainty of Shiva's statement, Nur's eyes shone and he immediately released a gigantic blast of Fire from his mouth.[1]
Shiva merely raised an eyebrow. He released a small amount of the Energy of Destruction surrounding his body, and Nur's enormous flame was completely destroyed before reaching him.[1]
Nur finally introduced himself as the God of the Elements. Shiva acknowledged the name without changing his relaxed demeanor, further angering Nur. The Elder God subsequently began attacking with the full breadth of his Elemental authority.[1]
Water, Fire, Air, Earth, Steam, Lava, Ice, Light, Darkness and combinations of those forces were repeatedly launched toward Shiva. The attacks caused extensive destruction throughout the surrounding environment, demonstrating that Nur's Elemental attacks themselves possessed tremendous destructive capability. None of them, however, penetrated Shiva's immediate sphere of influence. Anything entering the region protected by his Destruction was simply erased before reaching him.[1]
The difference between their Divine Concepts made the matchup particularly severe. Shiva's Destruction was one of the highest destructive Divine authorities and could reduce ordinary matter, Energy and supernatural phenomena into material that would later return to Creation. Nur's Elemental authority possessed no demonstrated ability capable of overcoming it.[1]
Shiva consequently paid remarkably little attention to the battle. While Nur continuously bombarded him with different elements, Shiva spent much of the confrontation thinking about Victor, The Heavenly Father and the limitations of his own Destruction rather than concentrating upon his opponent.[1]
Nur eventually became sufficiently frustrated to attempt direct physical combat. His gigantic fist moved toward Shiva at a speed completely disproportionate to the enormous size of his body. The moment his arm entered Shiva's Destruction field, however, the entire limb was destroyed.[1]
Shiva openly questioned why Nur continued attacking when the difference between them had become obvious. Before Nur could fully answer, both combatants noticed that Victor's presence had suddenly disappeared from Nightingale.[1]
Nur revealed that Victor's disappearance was part of the Elder Gods' wider strategy. He admitted that Shiva and Velnorah joining the conflict had caused the plan to deviate from its intended course, but explained that Victor had always been the Elder Gods' primary target. To Nur, Victor was an abnormal Being who needed to be eliminated before he became an even greater threat.[1]
Shiva privately considered the plan unlikely to succeed. He understood that Victor's talent caused him to grow dramatically whenever confronted by severe adversity and believed that if the Elder Gods failed to kill him during their trap, Victor would inevitably return considerably stronger.[1]
After briefly observing the destruction occurring throughout Nightingale and considering Victor's increasingly powerful faction, Shiva decided to end his own battle. His attention returned completely to Nur, causing the Elder God to instinctively feel as though his entire existence was being judged.[1]
Shiva offered Nur the small mercy of making his death painless and asked whether he had any final words. Nur responded by condemning him as a hypocrite. In Nur's view, Shiva portrayed himself as though he represented justice, but ultimately behaved according to the same self-interested reasoning as other Gods.[1]
Shiva did not deny the accusation. He admitted that he had chosen to sacrifice the Elder Gods because an alliance with Victor's rapidly expanding faction would benefit both his Pantheon and the inhabitants of Earth against powerful extraterrestrial threats. From Shiva's perspective, sacrificing a smaller hostile group for the survival of a much greater population was the practical decision.[1]
After wishing that Nur would make a different decision if given another opportunity in the future, Shiva covered himself in the Power of Destruction and released it in Nur's direction. The attack was produced merely by Shiva deliberately flexing his presence rather than through an elaborate Divine technique.[1]
The Destruction encompassed Nur's entire enormous body and completely overwhelmed the Elder God. His existence was evaporated, leaving only particles that Shiva observed would eventually return to Creation.[1]
Nur did not resurrect afterward. This distinguished his destruction from the ordinary physical deaths normally negated by the immortality supplied by Ikor-Kar-VI. Shiva's Concept of Destruction completely overcame the Elder God's continued survival and permanently removed Nur from the battle.[1]

### Skills & Abilities
Nur's combat capabilities revolved almost entirely around his Divinity of the Elements. Unlike Gods restricted to a single classical element, Nur possessed authority over all known elements available within [[Nightingale]]'s system and could freely employ both individual elements and combinations between them.[1]

#### Elder God Physiology
- Supernatural Physical Characteristics: As an Elder God, Nur possessed physical strength, speed and durability vastly beyond ordinary supernatural beings. Despite exceeding one thousand metres in height, his gigantic fist could move at a speed [[Shiva]] specifically noted as disproportionate to the size of his body.[1]
- Large Size: Nur belonged to the group of five Elder Gods whose bodies exceeded 1,000 metres in height during their appearance on [[Nightingale]].[2]
- Enhanced Strength: Nur attempted to directly strike Shiva with his gigantic fist after his Elemental attacks repeatedly failed.[1]
- Enhanced Speed: Despite his colossal size, Nur could launch physical attacks at extremely high speed.[1]
- Divine Power: As a God, Nur possessed [[Divine Energy]] through which his Elemental Divinity manifested. His attacks were powerful enough to inflict extensive environmental destruction even though Shiva personally ignored them.[1]
- Enhanced Soul: Nur possessed the enormous spiritual existence characteristic of the surviving Elder Gods. When Shiva observed the five Elder Gods collectively, their Souls appeared so immense that they resembled clusters of multiple Gods.[2]

#### Divinity
- Divinity — Elements: Nur was the God of the Elements and represented Elemental authority within [[Nightingale]]. His Divinity allowed him to create, manipulate and attack with all known elements and their combinations.[1]
- Elemental Manipulation: Nur possessed broad control over Elemental phenomena rather than being limited to one particular Element. He continuously shifted between different Elemental attacks while fighting [[Shiva]].[1]
- Elemental Generation: Nur could generate enormous quantities of the Elements he controlled directly through his Divine Power rather than requiring naturally occurring sources nearby.[1]
- Elemental Combination: Nur could combine known Elements together and employ the resulting derivative Elements. The narration explicitly states that he attacked Shiva with both individual Elements and their combinations.[1]
- Fire Manipulation: Nur could create and manipulate Fire on an enormous scale.[1]
- Fire Breath: His first demonstrated attack was a gigantic blast of Fire released directly from his mouth toward Shiva.[1]
- Water Manipulation: Water was explicitly among the Elements Nur used against Shiva.[1]
- Air Manipulation: Nur attacked Shiva using Air as one of his controlled Elements.[1]
- Earth Manipulation: Earth was included among Nur's directly demonstrated Elemental authorities.[1]
- Ice Manipulation: Nur possessed control over Ice and used it among the numerous attacks directed at Shiva.[1]
- Lava Manipulation: Nur could create and manipulate Lava, demonstrating authority over derivative Elements formed through combinations of more fundamental Elemental properties.[1]
- Steam Manipulation: Nur could generate and manipulate Steam as part of his Elemental arsenal.[1]
- Light Manipulation: Light was explicitly included among the Elements under Nur's control.[1]
- Darkness Manipulation: Nur likewise possessed Elemental control over Darkness and employed it offensively against Shiva.[1]
- Ranged Combat: Nur could employ practically his entire Elemental arsenal from a distance, allowing him to avoid direct contact with opponents whose abilities made close combat dangerous.[1]
- Area-of-Effect Attacks: Although his attacks could not penetrate Shiva's Destruction field, the surrounding environment suffered significant damage from Nur's continuous Elemental bombardment.[1]

#### Borrowed Immortality
Like the other members of the [[Elder Gods Pantheon]], Nur benefited from the supernatural immortality maintained by [[Ikor-Kar-VI]]. This should not be confused with Nur's personal Divinity; his innate Divine authority was Elements, while the mechanism preventing the Elder Gods from permanently dying originated from Ikor.[3][4]
- [[Immortality]] (Granted by Ikor): Ikor maintained a Blessing that prevented members of his Pantheon from permanently dying while sufficient Positive Energy remained available to sustain it.[4]
- Resurrection (Granted by Ikor): Ikor could expend Energy to restore supported members after fatal damage. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] later identified Ikor himself as the key responsible for preventing his followers from remaining dead.[4]
- Soul Immortality (Granted): Earlier information concerning the Elder Gods identified immortality of the Soul as their defining survival advantage. This protection originated from the leader of the Elder Gods rather than from Nur's Elemental Divinity.[3][4]
- Immortality Dependence: The protection depended upon Ikor and his reserves of Positive Energy. Ikor could prioritize, withdraw or become unable to sustain the immortality of members elsewhere when his Energy became insufficient.[4]

### Weaknesses
- Superior Conceptual Authorities: Nur's control over the Elements did not allow him to overcome Divine Concepts inherently superior to the phenomena he controlled. [[Shiva]]'s Destruction erased every Elemental attack the moment it entered his sphere of influence.[1]
- Destruction: Shiva's Divinity represented a direct method of permanently destroying Nur. The God of Destruction enveloped Nur's complete body and reduced him to particles, after which the Elder God did not resurrect.[1]
- Close-Range Vulnerability to Destruction: Nur understood that physically touching Shiva would result in his destruction and initially avoided direct contact for this reason. When he eventually attempted a physical strike, his arm was immediately erased upon entering Shiva's destructive field.[1]
- Borrowed [[Immortality]]: Nur's immortality was dependent upon [[Ikor-Kar-VI]] rather than being inherent to his Divinity of the Elements.[4]
- Overwhelming Power Difference: Despite possessing an exceptionally versatile Divinity, Nur was incapable of meaningfully threatening Shiva because none of his demonstrated Elements possessed sufficient conceptual authority to bypass Shiva's Destruction.[1]
