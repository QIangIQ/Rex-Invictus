#### Debut

Leave this matter to me. I'll make sure the destruction stops—this is my realm, after all.
—Vharzath to Equilibrium
Vharzath is the name [[Victor Elderblood]] speaks when he addresses the leader of the Black Eclipse and declares his extinction. The System lists that ruler as <Darkness>, position 8. His own side calls him the Great Darkness, the Great Leader, and the Great Being. He is the Primordial God of Darkness. He sits above the seven Thrones and above the Ten Chief Gods. He is not <Light>, not <Emperor>, and not <Law>. Those are the other three names on that tier of Upper-Sector ruler.[1][2][3]
His principal galaxy is not a capital he rules from a distance. He transformed his own body into that territory. The place is grotesque, it is permeated by his power, and it is his entire body. Any force that is not at his level is extinguished there. Inside a domain of that rank, an upper-faction ruler is in essence omniscient. Nothing escapes those senses unless a being of the same level intervenes. [[Scathach]] looked at it, recognized a being of her husband's kind, and turned the invasion back. Only the Emperor, in her judgment, can enter.[4][5]
He is cautious where that body is not, and the caution is a revision, not his first instinct. He orders the sector flooded, hears [[Niklaus Horseman]], and pulls the order back to traps that are watched rather than rushed. He still sends the fragments, then [[Azrakar]], then the other Chief Gods, then [[Vaelthar]]. When the fight starts tearing galaxies that are his, he appears once, stops their motion, buys the Regulators off with the dead, and does not stay to trade blows. His support is still in Azrakar's power, his attention is still on the field, and his Darkness is what he later forces through [[Nerath]] hard enough to drive War back one time. When Victor appears holding a galaxy, Vharzath turns toward him and is pleased. He has found Negativity. He does not take the invitation to leave his territory. His planets are already under attack, and he cannot call his army home.[6][7][8][3][9]
The war after that does not pull him out either. Vaelthar is the greatest loss he names, and the loss is the seat, not a corpse he left on the field. The sword stopped. The living were taken. Territory and the Ten Chief Gods can be replaced. The Throne of Origin cannot. He raises a weaker Azrakar under the old name, still owes himself nine more, and bargains with <Emperor>: half the spoils, the Abominations against that rival's main fleet. The weapon he then spends is the [[Nihilvore]]. [[Hela Elderblood]] erases it with <END> joined to a ship. <Emperor> appears as a skull of fire and says the faction has to disappear. Vharzath refuses the blind great offensive. The refusal lasts until the conquests themselves start to go. Within days he is looking at one galaxy, his own body, as the thing he would have left. He sends two of the Thrones and tells them to put their own safety first. The two who then take the field are [[Aetherys]], Throne of Time, and [[Oryndal]], Throne of Space. He does not fight that battle. When they are taken, a roar carries across countless galaxies, and a vast mass of pure darkness, larger than a galaxy, forms into a humanoid with hundreds of arms. He demands the subordinates back or he will destroy the empire. Victor meets that size and will not give the subordinates back. <Emperor> comes with him, larger than galaxies. Vharzath is furious and wants to attack at once. He does not. It is too strange, and too simple. He says it is not over, turns, and vanishes. The extinction is still only a declaration. The full potential is still not shown.[10][11][12][13][14][15][16]

### Physical Appearance
On the principal throne he is an older man, and a raised hand is enough to stop the room. That is the shape he uses among his own. It is not a measurement of his age, and it is not the whole of him.[6]
The rest is the galaxy. Mortals looking at his principal base see a galaxy-shaped form without stars, sunk in the deepest darkness. [[Scathach]] sees what it is. The leaders of the four factions stand on a level of their own, and [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] is one example of that level. Vharzath is another. He is grotesque by the standard of the other three. <Light>, <Emperor>, and <Law> immerse a galaxy in their power and make it their domain. He transformed his own body into the territory. That place is his entire body. It is a death zone for every being who does not stand at his level. Within the domains of the upper factions the ruler is, in essence, omniscient, and nothing escapes those senses unless a being of the same level intervenes. His body is that kind of domain. The senses are not the whole universe. A Hell warded so that he has no power there is a place he cannot perceive. Scathach will not take an army into it. She says the Emperor is the only one who can enter, because any other force would be extinguished there. Entering it would be the same kind of act as an enemy trying to walk into the planet Victor keeps inside his Soul.[4][5]
On a battlefield that is still only a galaxy under his command, and not that body, pure Darkness flows from him at a gesture and spreads until the surrounding Solar Systems and galaxies fall under it and stop in their course. Equilibrium addresses that presence as Darkness. He is not [[Azrakar]]'s skeletal void, not [[Kaelith]]'s starry night, not [[Nerath]]'s white elongation, and not [[Vhalor]]'s faceless shadow.[8]
When Victor arrives holding a galaxy, nothing new is added to the form. Light, Vharzath, and the patron aligned with <Emperor> all turn toward the dragon. The recognition is the turn, and the pleasure. He does not take a shape for that meeting, because he does not join it. Later, when <Emperor> comes to him, the other party is a gigantic skull of pure fire. Vharzath is not described as matching it.[3][10]
The shape he uses when the two Thrones are taken is neither the older man nor the starless galaxy. A vast mass of pure darkness, larger than a galaxy, forms into a humanoid monster with hundreds of arms. The roar that comes with it carries across countless galaxies. He demands his subordinates and threatens the empire. Victor grows to that size in his colossal Dragon form. Vharzath does not strike. He turns and vanishes. The form is not called a projection. It is not said to have replaced the body that is the galaxy, and it is not described as the whole of his potential.[16]

### Personality
He listens when the rest of the room laughs, and he changes his own order because of it. Niklaus is a recent servant. Darkness covers that body, the eyes are no longer a Vampire's, and the sclera is black and the pupils red. The others mock him for thinking a Middle Sector can threaten a ruler who, they say, has reigned since before those sectors existed, over an empire of hundreds of galaxies. They also resent that the Great Darkness saw value in him. Vharzath does not join the laughter. He had already told them to flood the sector and not to stop. After the warning, that the man they call <[[Chaos]]> will destroy them and devour the faction, he narrows his eyes and revises it. The traps continue. Nothing is to be rushed. He is watching how it develops. The narration is plain about why. A ruler does not stay at the top by being stupid or impulsive, and Niklaus's words had weight. Caution is the method. It is not a withdrawal.[6]
The same habit shows in what he spends and what he will not spend. The Souls he gives Soul are a bargain, not kindness. They would have returned to him, and he would rather lose the dead than have Equilibrium and a Judge of the Abyss inside a war between Gods, because once those two step in, the matter stops being simple. He also will not tolerate chaos in a galaxy that is his. The deaths of his ordinary people do not move him, for the same reason. Their Souls return to him. Passivity does. [[Azrakar]]'s death ends the restraint he had left on the Horsemen. The order that wanted War alive does not survive that death. He wants all four, alive or dead, and he still does not come to take them. The power he puts in [[Nerath]] arrives as an order that her duty is not yet fulfilled. The quantity is more than she can hold, and she screams for it to leave. [[Vaelthar]]'s restoration of the three comes with the rebuke that they disappointed him. They bow.[8][17][18][3][10]
The caution drops into recognition when [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] appears, and it does not drop into a duel. Vharzath is pleased. He has found Negativity. He does not answer the declaration of extinction by walking out. Victor had meant the appearance as help for his herald and as a lure, so the army could destroy the territory while he dealt with its ruler. The leader's pride is not enough to make him take it. Victor's own reading, afterward, is that the leaders of these factions are not fools. They would not have lasted eons if they were.[3][9]
What he cannot replace, he says himself. Territory is nothing. The Ten Chief Gods are nothing. Even the death of one who had served him for millennia only pains him. Vaelthar is the loss. He regrets having sent that Throne out, and the regret does not make him reckless. He still wants to understand the Dragon Emperor before he spends what he has left. He will make a bargain with <Emperor> and expect both of them to break it. When the [[Nihilvore]] is reported lost, the answer is "Indeed," without heat. When <Emperor> tells him the faction must disappear, he will not throw the Thrones into a great offensive he has not studied. He will do it his way. His way, once the map fails, is still not himself. Individual deaths he can bear, because the Souls come home. Losing everything he will not. Two Thrones go, and the order he gives them is to live. The man Violet later reads out of that Throne's memories is the same man. Cautious, a born leader, and arrogant. He does not sit through losses without end. When the two are taken, the same caution is what stops the blow. He is furious. He wants to attack at once. He does not, because what stands in front of him is too strange and too simple. The Emperor's inward word for that withdrawal is cowardice. That judgment is the Emperor's, not his own.[16][10][11][12][13]

### Synopsis

#### Dragon God of Chaos
The System prints the highest Authorities after <CHAOS> rises. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] reads 0 as <????>, then Negativity and Positivity, Infinity, Death, Life, Soul, Balance, Light, Darkness, Emperor, and Law. He takes the names through Balance as the Primordials, and everything under that as higher beings. His own entry sits at the bottom of the list, not in that block. <Darkness> is the eighth name. No face, no faction, and no personal name come with it.[1]

#### Those Who Rule Reality
A clone of [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] has already clashed with an Upper-Sector faction, and a weapon he does not know has annihilated the Solar System he arrived in. The four names set on that class of ruler are Light, tied to that attack, Darkness, tied to the rescue of Niklaus, Emperor, and Law. <Light> is the proof that the type exists. Victor will not subordinate himself to one of them. His reading of a perceptive ruler in that class is that Victor will be treated as something to control, something to use, or a threat that has to disappear. Velnorah treats the four as the main factions of the Upper Sectors, beings who have ruled there for millions of years, possibly billions, and who can afford to burn a Solar System to remove one enemy. The recording she shows of the rescue is Niklaus being taken through a portal by dark tentacles. The soldiers of the other faction call themselves Light. A third force, the one Velnorah herself met, is neither of them.[2]
Before the Empire is shut inside its own time, Victor looks at the seventh name on the list, not the eighth. <Light> will not leave him alone, and he will not leave Light alone. Something in both of them wants completion. Peace, he says, was never an option, not while so many rulers remain. The throne of the universe has been empty too long. The only ones qualified to put that house in order are the holders of <Negativity> and <Positivity>, and only their merger would birth <[[Chaos]]>. His reading of Light is that Light already senses this, and may be waiting for Negativity's chosen to absorb him and take the empty seat. That reading is about Light. It is not a meeting with Vharzath, and it does not make the ruler of Black Eclipse the Representative of Negativity.[19]
Around Imperial Year 170,000, War brings in Nikolai, a [[Demigod]] of [[Dagda]]'s line and the son of [[Juliette Dubois]], from a moon in the Sagittarius Dwarf, about eighty thousand light-years from [[Yggdrasil]]. The foreign interest wanted Victor's Blood, whether it could be reproduced, and where the anomaly of the Emperor came from. His Soul is being eaten by an Upper-Sector fragment. Victor will not bring that corruption near the family. He isolates it in a rune-sealed laboratory at the edge of the Sector and forces the fragment to manifest. It knows him as the emerging <Chaos>, knows he holds <Negativity>, and says <Light> is already searching. It also says several rulers above mean to kill him, take his Authorities, or use him for the empty throne. When it tries to speak on <Darkness>, <Emperor>, and <Law>, an existential Soul Contract destroys the body and the Soul before the protected part can be said. Victor does not accept the limit. He rebuilds the prisoner, the contract kills it again, and the cycle runs 623,596,777,174 times across subjective years. What survives is an elite operative's memory, rumor and incomplete intelligence, not the ruler's. It names Black Eclipse, Darkness at the top of it, and Niklaus with the faction, and it ties earlier corruption inside the Sector to that faction. Prolonged Eldritch exposure then destroys the foreign god linked to Nikolai. Victor erases what is left rather than absorb it. That god is not identified as Vharzath. Akasha locks the report to the adult family.[20][21]
Victor gives the ladder anyway. Darkness, the Primordial God of Darkness, position 8, stands at the top of a pantheon whose majority is from the Negativity side. Seven generals, the [[Seven Black Thrones]], stand under him, then Greater Gods, Lesser Gods, and servants. Sasha's firm conclusion is only that Niklaus is with them. Violet names Night, Void, Silence, Shadows, Death, Fear, the Eclipse, and Oblivion, and she calls those the seven main gods below him. That is eight names. She wants them as food for the Empire or for subordinates, and the overlap is also why a dead god of this pantheon could reinforce someone in the family who already holds a matching Concept. The Leader, in that memory, is galactic. The seven sit slightly under him. Elites are placed at the destruction of Solar Systems. Medium and low soldiers sit between a continent and a planet, and Velnorah calls those soldiers mindless, closer to organic machines. Victor and [[Amaterasu]] treat every one of those figures as unverified. The wives are mostly galactic themselves. Velnorah says the enemy is not weak, that the Empire is the anomaly, and that she still fears it is not enough. The Empire has had 200,000 years. From the same memory she predicts the pantheon has existed for more than two million years, and that even after losses to other factions the number left would still be substantial. [[Aphrodite]] answers that age alone did not save the Greeks. The memory does not contain the Ten Chief Gods. The same meeting is where the fragments of [[Abhoth]] are set as a counter, so a later attempt to corrupt an Imperial body can be felt and turned back. Victor's own figure, given to his daughters, is larger than the memory's age: the higher sector has been active for billions of years longer than the middle sector they stand in.[21][22]
Fragments of Divine Concepts start attaching to Souls inside the Empire. One is taken off Ryujin. It carries a Light-related trace, and the god it came from is not given a name. In the same hour, in the principal hall, a woman reports that her power has been neutralized again. The barrier has grown thinner, and something on the far side is holding. The man beside her names <Chaos>. She treats that as a jackpot. Devouring the sector would grow the faction further, and if he is the one they are looking for, they would finally stand level with the pests she despises. Whether the justice-obsessed worms interfere does not matter to the older man on the throne. His first order is to send more traps, not to stop, and to flood the sector, because the barrier could fall at any moment. The man and the woman call him Master.[23][6]
Niklaus is quiet until he is asked. He does not care about the old sector. His revenge is on Vlad, and a straight invasion is foolish. Victor will destroy them and devour the faction. They laugh, and one of them tells him that the Great Darkness seeing value in him does not give him the right to talk. Vharzath raises a hand, lets him finish, and then changes the order. The traps continue, and nothing is rushed. He is watching how it develops. They obey even where they disagree. The footholds do not stop. Victor braces the barrier himself, feels the strain in his Soul, and gives the Empire a year before the war.[6]
[[Azrakar]] is sent because of that caution, an elite to see that the work proceeds. He cannot command Niklaus. Niklaus is the Great Leader's direct servant, and the leader who used to decide everything alone now listens to him. Serving that leader is Azrakar's greatest joy, so the resentment is not aimed upward. It is aimed at the servant whose standing seems to outrank a Chief God. Victor sends War through the barrier with [[Gol-goroth]]. War kills the assistant, destroys the living ship and everyone on it, and fights Azrakar across star systems until the shock reaches neighboring galaxies. One of Azrakar's attacks is not entirely his. War recognizes his patron's power in it. Even with his own power and the Great Leader's support, Azrakar cannot put the herald down. [[Kaelith]] arrives because an unanswered attack would make the other powers test them. Those are the Great Leader's instructions. Azrakar objects, then obeys.[7]
The galaxies of that sector are enormous, up to ten thousand times the Milky Way, and they are not empty. This one is directly under Darkness, and the collision can already be felt in distant galaxies. Equilibrium and a Judge of the Abyss are about to intervene, because more galaxies will be destroyed if it continues. Vharzath appears before the decision is made. He calls Equilibrium by name. Equilibrium answers him as Darkness. He says the realm is his, and he takes the containment. Pure Darkness flows from his body at a gesture. Through that Authority the surrounding Solar Systems and galaxies halt. He gives Soul the lives lost, who would otherwise have returned to him, so the two will not step in. The sacrifice is political. Azrakar can still spread darkness across hundreds of star systems. It deforms the stars and no longer breaks them. Gol-goroth tells War that someone else is watching. The fight continues, and War can feel the heavier space.[8]
[[Nerath]] and [[Vhalor]] arrive, and Azrakar counts four of the Ten Chief Gods on the field. He introduces Nerath as the Goddess of Silence held in the highest esteem by the Seven Thrones, and Vhalor as the God of Shadows, and he tells War to feel honored. The Great Leader is giving him his attention. War does not care. The number of heads he owes his Emperor has gone up. Nerath's own order is different from the later one. The Great Silence wants War alive, and Azrakar accepts that they will take him alive. Vharzath's hold on the region is what keeps the clash from wiping the galaxy. Had he not kept the battle's effects to a minimum, eight beings capable of threatening galaxies would already have destroyed it. The hold does not stop four pillars of Energy from erasing nearby stars, and it is also why the spheres stop entering the Empire. There is no room left to send them.[8][17]
Inside Gol-goroth's domain, Death locks Azrakar, Pestilence weakens him, Famine takes the arms and legs, and War takes the head and the heart. The four erase him. The other great factions are already watching and beginning to move. Vharzath knows. He is displeased. The order to take War alive does not survive Azrakar. The new order is to bring the Horsemen in, all of them, alive or dead. One of the seven Thrones answers, "Yes, Great Darkness," and summons the remaining Chief Gods. That Throne is not named, and it is not [[Vaelthar]]'s entrance.[17]
The three who remain are not finished. Their patron's energy is what keeps the Horsemen from ending them. Nerath is pinned, the corruption seated in the wounds. His voice tells her the duty is not yet fulfilled, and more of his Darkness enters than she can hold. She screams for it to get out. What leaves her is pure Darkness, and it drives War back. Pestilence calls it the patron protecting her more actively. Famine says promoting beings of her level is not easy, and that he is trying to conserve his strength. The four set the killing strokes anyway. The domain is hit from outside before they land. War drops the execution.[18]
Vaelthar, Throne of Origin, comes through at the head of the relief. Kaelith greets him as Great Origin. Ten stand with the wounded three. Nine are galactic-level threats. One is a true God at a Throne's level. His presence strains what is left of the domain. War judges him stronger than himself, sees no way out, and shuts the domain down. Vaelthar restores Kaelith, Nerath, and Vhalor with a beam of pure Darkness and tells them they have disappointed the Great Darkness. They bow and accept any punishment. Justice and [[Elarion]] arrive for patrons of their own. Justice is faceless, suited, sharp-toothed, and at the level of a Throne. Elarion, God of Goodness, wears golden armor. Each says his patron wants War. A divinity of <BEGIN> flares with them. It is Vaelthar's. War recognizes it as kin to the Emperor's, and corrupted. War refuses to be portioned out. He names himself the incarnation of War and the Dragon Emperor's herald.[3]
Victor answers, "Well said, my herald," and appears as a giant dragon holding the galaxy. Light, Vharzath, and the patron aligned with <Emperor> turn toward him. Vharzath is openly pleased. He has found Negativity. Niklaus, watching, judges that Victor has become absurdly stronger. In his God Emperor form Victor praises War. War sees that his father has fully recovered. Victor calls the Gods who tried to take his herald a disappointment and kills the restored Kaelith by the Soul. The body is erased. He holds the Soul. Only the patrons perceive the method. He then says the name Vharzath and declares extinction for the leader of Black Eclipse.[3]
A God reports that their planets are under attack from every side. Vharzath senses more than twenty elite fighters, all women, each at a Throne's level or beyond it. He cannot recall his forces and he cannot send relief. Victor has already isolated the region. Vaelthar orders the attack anyway. He places Victor at the level of the four faction leaders and puts their chance of surviving him under one percent. The being who meets that attack is a projection. The true body is the dragon, still holding the galaxy, and it hardly moves. A plain horizontal cut of [[Junketsu]] slices what stands in front of it, Gods and planets and stars, and takes one of Vaelthar's arms. Nerath looks at allies cut in two and at the Throne missing an arm, and understands they cannot win. They are alive, in her reading, only by the goodwill of the Great Darkness, or because the enemy is deliberately sparing them. Only the Great Darkness, she judges, can keep up with him. Vaelthar heals them and sends them in again. Victor lets it happen. He is waiting for the larger prey. The projection walks out of the prison, reduces a fleeing God to particles, and leaves Vaelthar under a foot with the sword at his head. The sword does not come down. The only mark on the projection is a crack in its gauntlet. He is alive when the living are ordered taken. Vharzath will call the loss of that seat the greatest of the war. What happens to the body is later, and it is not this stroke.[3][9][10][13]
Justice, still there, calls this faction among the weakest and the most isolated. Victor says that soon it will no longer exist, and that he will do Justice the favor. The lure does not work. Vharzath's arrogance and his ego are not strong enough to pull him into that stupidity. Victor treats the refusal as a test passed. They are not fools, and they would not have survived eons if they were. He orders the living taken prisoner and the remains of the fallen collected. The projection leaves with War and the fallen Gods. The true body lets the galaxy go, and the dragon drifts between the galaxies again.[9]
The invasion does not stop because he refused the duel. Imperial fleets under John take the outer lines of his galactic territory. The ground where his influence is greatest is an almost perfect circle at the center of the galaxy. The surrounding galaxies are extensions, and they do not belong directly to the domain. Both sides are still spending low-rank soldiers. His elites and his Thrones have not appeared. [[Scathach]], on the main field, is shown the principal galaxy and recognizes his body. She turns the army back. Only Victor could enter, and entering would be suicide even for the wives. The principal objective is postponed, not failed. Elsewhere the count of his real strength is settled against the Empire's. The Empire has more than forty beings who can threaten a galaxy. Black Eclipse has seven who are genuinely at that level, and those seven are the Thrones. The Ten Chief Gods are galactic threats only because his blessing forces them up to it. In the lower and middle ranks the Eclipse outnumbers the Empire three to one. Scathach's answer is that quality beats cannon fodder, and that machines and clones can fill the ranks the Empire lacks. She also intends to hunt a Throne. She does not name which.[4][24]
Victor, home in body and still present in the Upper Sectors so that no one will dare his territory, sees the mistake in his own map. His worlds are scattered across smaller galaxies. The four great factions keep their centers close, or inside one galaxy. Each has a principal galaxy that is its body or its realm. Vharzath is the example, and the grotesque one. The other three bathe a galaxy in their power. He became his. Scathach withdrew for that reason. The advance is stopped by agreement. He already understands the war better than the reports do.[5]
Inside his palace he spends the dead rather than himself. He tells a god to abandon his old name and become Azrakar, God of the Void. Darkness enters that body. What stands up resembles the predecessor and can again threaten a galaxy, and is not yet as strong as the one who died and had lived far longer. He sends the new Azrakar out to hold himself ready. [[Xytherion]], Throne of the End, enters as that god leaves, dressed as a medieval warrior in black armor with red accents, and says the ritual succeeded. Vharzath agrees, and says he must still create nine more.[10]
The greatest loss is not the territory and not those ten. They are replaceable. It is Vaelthar. Vharzath had made him to be a possible Chosen One of Negativity, put a portion of his own power into him, and still was not the one who chose. The divinity that Throne carried was <BEGIN>, stained by this Darkness. He regrets having sent him out. The death of a Chief God who served for millennia pains him, and is not an excuse.
