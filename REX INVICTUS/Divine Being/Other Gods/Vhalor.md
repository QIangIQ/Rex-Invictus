### Appearances

#### Debut
Chapter 1193
Even standing directly before him, one seemed unable to truly see or comprehend him.
—Narration describing Vhalor, Chapter 1193
Vhalor is the God of Shadows and one of the Ten Chief Gods of the [[Black Eclipse Pantheon]]. He serves the [[Vharzath|Great Darkness]] and belongs to the hierarchy beneath the [[Seven Black Thrones]]. His designated area of responsibility is Shadow, one of the ten positions personally bestowed by the Black Eclipse's leader. Unlike an ordinary humanoid deity, Vhalor manifests as a faceless, depthless black figure whose lower body consists of moving bands of Shadow. His Shadows move independently of ordinary light, bind and strike opponents, and gradually dissolve matter they touch.[1][2]
Vhalor first appears during the battle between War and [[Azrakar]], arriving alongside [[Nerath]], the Goddess of Silence. He fights Pestilence in coordination with Nerath, using his extensions and multiple Shadow manifestations to restrain and attack the Horseman. After [[Gol-goroth]] establishes an Eldritch domain that suppresses the Gods' powers, Vhalor attempts to escape and counterattack, but the Four Horsemen repeatedly injure him. He subsequently joins [[Kaelith]] and Nerath in creating a small field that resists the domain's influence, although their combined efforts cannot restore their full powers.[1][3][4]
When [[Vaelthar]], the Throne of Origin, arrives with the other Chief Gods, he restores Vhalor, Kaelith, and Nerath with a beam of pure Darkness. The three bow and accept his reprimand. During the subsequent confrontation with [[Victor Elderblood]], Victor kills Kaelith by attacking her Soul directly, but Vhalor is not identified as one of Victor's individual victims. Later information confirms that Vhalor is among the Black Eclipse Chief Gods captured and imprisoned by Victor's side.[5][6][2]

### Physical Appearance
Vhalor is a tall, disproportionate figure covered in a black surface that appears to have no depth. His shoulders are narrow, while his arms extend below his knees and end in long, pointed fingers that can move independently of one another. His head has no visible eyes, nose, or mouth. Instead, it resembles an elongated, deformed helmet split by a vertical opening, from which Darkness deeper than the black covering the rest of his body emerges.[1]
Black growths resembling unfinished arms cover his back. They become difficult to perceive when someone attempts to look directly at them. His lower body has no conventional legs; it divides into moving bands of Shadow that carry him through space without any visible supporting structure.[1]
The Shadows surrounding Vhalor do not behave like ordinary shadows. They spread in directions that cannot be explained by the position of nearby objects or the light of surrounding stars. Matter that touches them gradually disappears, seemingly drawn toward a place outside ordinary space. Even when viewed directly, his manifestation is difficult to comprehend fully.[1]
His body is not fixed in a single shape during combat. Vhalor can disperse his manifestation, reappear elsewhere, and extend his Shadows into bands, pointed appendages, or multiple hands. These manifestations can restrain opponents and maintain contact long enough to begin dissolving the matter they touch. When Pestilence's corruption spreads along an extension, Vhalor can discard the affected portion of his manifestation, preventing that particular contamination from spreading further through it.[3]
However, these properties do not make him invulnerable. [[Gol-goroth]]'s attacks tear through his manifestation, and Death's scythe strikes him as he reappears from dispersal. Vhalor repeatedly reconstructs his body, but the damage and energy expenditure make the process increasingly difficult. [[Vaelthar]] eventually restores his damaged manifestation with pure Darkness.[4][5]

### Personality
Vhalor is quiet and reserved. When he arrives alongside [[Nerath]], he does not introduce himself or explain his authority; [[Azrakar]] identifies him as the God of Shadows. Nerath delivers the Great Silence's demand that War be taken alive, while Vhalor remains silent. His lack of dialogue does not prevent him from responding decisively when his allies are threatened.[1]
During the battle against Pestilence, Vhalor intervenes when Nerath is injured. He pulls her away from danger and attacks from the Horseman's blind spots, repeatedly changing his approach when Pestilence destroys his restraints. Rather than relying on a single overwhelming attack, he combines restraint, repositioning, and repeated strikes with Nerath's ability to suppress an opponent's powers.[3]
His composure deteriorates when [[Gol-goroth]]'s Eldritch domain suppresses the Gods' abilities and Azrakar is killed. Vhalor recognizes that the Horsemen move with extraordinary coordination, describing their movements as though they were cast from a single mold. He then argues that they must pursue the incarnation of War, whom he identifies as the key to their survival. Even while severely wounded, he attempts to counterattack rather than simply surrender.[3][4]
Vhalor nevertheless acknowledges the hierarchy above him. When [[Vaelthar]] restores him and reprimands the surviving Gods for disappointing the [[Vharzath|Great Darkness]], Vhalor bows alongside [[Kaelith]] and Nerath and accepts the punishment. His actions demonstrate loyalty to his superiors and a willingness to cooperate with other Chief Gods when their survival depends on it.[5]

### Synopsis

#### Those Who Rule Reality
The [[Black Eclipse Pantheon]] is an ancient faction governed by the [[Vharzath|Great Darkness]], with the [[Seven Black Thrones]] occupying positions beneath its leader and the Ten Chief Gods serving below them. Information gathered later establishes that the ten positions are personally bestowed by the leader rather than automatically attained through strength. Each title corresponds to a particular area of responsibility, and Vhalor's is Shadow.[7][8][2]
War enters the Sector with [[Gol-goroth]] and confronts [[Azrakar]], the God of the Void. The battle escalates when the Four Horsemen of the Apocalypse reveal their full manifestations and fight together. As the conflict continues, more Chief Gods arrive to support the Black Eclipse side. Among them are [[Nerath]], Goddess of Silence, and Vhalor, God of Shadows. Azrakar introduces the two Gods, while Nerath states that the Great Silence wants War alive. Vhalor remains silent as the combatants prepare for a battle involving four Gods against four opponents.[9][10][1]
Vhalor joins the fight when Pestilence pressures Nerath. He pulls her out of danger and attacks Pestilence from unexpected directions. A band of Shadow forms beneath the Horseman's feet and wraps around his legs, interrupting his charge. Vhalor appears beside him and strikes with his pointed fingers before additional Shadow extensions seize Pestilence's arms. When the Horseman releases corruption that destroys the restraints, Vhalor disperses his body and manifests several black hands around the target. These hands attack from different directions, with some strikes reaching Pestilence's shoulder and back.[3]
Nerath then touches Pestilence's chest, briefly suppressing his powers through Silence. Vhalor closes his Shadows around the Horseman and attempts to immobilize him, but Pestilence concentrates corruption at the points of contact and spreads it along the extensions. Vhalor dissolves the contaminated portion of his manifestation, then joins Nerath in restraining Pestilence again. Their combination allows Vhalor to keep attacking while Nerath disrupts the enemy's techniques, although Pestilence's corruption continues to threaten their control of the battlefield.[3]
The situation changes when War calls upon his brothers and Gol-goroth opens its eye. The Four Horsemen attack Azrakar together, combining their Eldritch powers with the sword's influence. The surrounding space becomes subject to the Eldritch domain, suppressing the Gods' influence over it. Nerath's abilities and Vhalor's Shadows become ineffective within the altered conditions. The Horsemen overwhelm Azrakar, and War finishes the attack that kills the God of the Void.[3]
With Azrakar dead, War immediately attacks the remaining Gods before they can form a new defensive line. Vhalor is the first target. He attempts to disperse his body and escape, but Famine's blades pierce the Shadow Realm and force him to gather his manifestation together again. War reaches him during that interruption, and Gol-goroth's vertical strike shatters a Shadow structure Vhalor raises in defense, tearing open his shoulder.[4]
Vhalor withdraws and spreads his influence across the area, causing Shadows to appear around War and Famine. His domain is too weakened to affect the Horsemen properly, however, and Gol-goroth cuts through the remaining defense. When Vhalor finally vanishes and reappears farther away, Death anticipates his trajectory and strikes him with a scythe of pure miasma as he materializes, piercing his abdomen and interrupting his escape.[4]
Vhalor attempts to create distance while War and Famine approach from different directions. He gathers his Shadows into a compact barrier and launches an Energy wave that pushes both opponents back for a short time. The interruption gives him a brief opportunity to recover strength, but Pestilence has already spread corruption throughout the region. That corruption reaches the structures supporting Vhalor's defense, destabilizing the barrier and allowing Gol-goroth to break through. Vhalor dodges the killing blow but suffers another deep wound, leaving Death an opportunity to finish him.[4]
[[Kaelith]] and Nerath attempt to intervene, but their powers are suppressed and they can no longer manipulate the Horsemen's senses as effectively as before. They draw upon their sovereign's power, producing a pillar of Darkness that weakens Gol-goroth's influence enough to create an opening. Kaelith's Night and Nerath's Silence then pull Vhalor away from Death. Nerath explains that their powers are weakened and that they must create an area of effect. Vhalor attunes himself to their powers, and a pillar of Darkness rises from his body as the three Gods establish a small field that attempts to resist the Eldritch conditions.[4]
The field does not restore Vhalor's full Shadow Realm, and the fight continues to deteriorate. While the three Gods struggle to keep their defensive area intact, Death attacks Kaelith and the other Horsemen continue to pressure Nerath. Vhalor observes that the knights move as though cast from a single mold and identifies the incarnation of War as the key to their survival. He argues that they must launch a counteroffensive, but War breaks through space and appears among the Gods before they can carry out the plan. Famine breaks through Vhalor's incomplete defense, and Gol-goroth tears open his flank. Famine then attacks from behind, leaving a large wound in his back.[4]
Vhalor gathers his Shadows for another attack, but War drives his sword through the manifestation and forces him to abandon the technique. Vhalor falls to his knees, and restoring his body becomes increasingly difficult. The Horsemen explain that their opponents' patron is sustaining the Gods and preventing their deaths from being completed. War prepares to strike Vhalor while Death and Famine close in on Kaelith and Pestilence continues attacking Nerath. Before the executions can be completed, an external force strikes the Eldritch domain and interrupts the battle.[4]
The arrival is led by [[Vaelthar]], the Throne of Origin, accompanied by the other Chief Gods. Vaelthar's presence places additional pressure on the domain, and War eventually ends it rather than remain trapped between the restored faction and the forces already surrounding him. Vaelthar then restores Vhalor, Kaelith, and Nerath with a beam of pure Darkness. The three bow before him as he reprimands them for disappointing the Great Darkness and accept their punishment.[4][5]
The confrontation subsequently escalates when [[Victor Elderblood]] arrives and declares that the Gods have disappointed him. Victor kills Kaelith by attacking her Soul directly, erasing her body while retaining her Soul. He then declares his intention to extinguish Vharzath and orders the confrontation to proceed. Vhalor is not identified as one of Victor's individual victims in this sequence. The ensuing battle demonstrates the enormous disparity between Victor and the assembled Gods, with the Gods' attacks failing to force him back and Victor cutting through their defenses with [[Junketsu]].[5][6]
Later, Violet and Lily discuss the captured Black Eclipse Gods. Their findings identify Vhalor as one of the ten titled Chief Gods and specify his area of responsibility as Shadow. The captured group includes the named Chief Gods whose former positions can be passed to successors selected and empowered by the Black Eclipse leader. This confirms that Vhalor survives the confrontation and is subsequently held captive by Victor's side.[2]

### Skills & Abilities
Vhalor is a Chief God whose demonstrated abilities center on the manipulation of Shadows, the dispersal and reconstruction of his manifestation, and coordinated combat with other Gods. The following abilities are based on his recorded actions.

#### Shadow Manipulation
- Independent Shadows: Vhalor's Shadows spread in directions that cannot be explained by the position of surrounding objects or the available light. They do not follow the behavior of ordinary shadows.[1]
- Shadow Extensions: He extends his Shadows into bands and elongated appendages capable of striking, grasping, and restraining opponents. Against Pestilence, he forms a band beneath the Horseman's feet, wraps his legs, pulls him backward, and strikes with pointed fingers.[3]
- Multiple Shadow Hands: After dispersing his manifestation, Vhalor can produce several black hands around an opponent. The hands attack in succession and from different directions, making it difficult for a target to avoid every strike.[3]
- Shadow Binding: His extensions can seize an opponent's limbs and hold the target in place. These restraints are vulnerable to sufficient force or countermeasures, as Pestilence destroys them by spreading corruption through the points of contact.[3]
- Shadow-Induced Dissolution: Matter that enters his Shadows gradually disappears, seemingly drawn toward a place outside ordinary space. When his Shadows maintain contact with an opponent, the affected matter begins to dissolve. The process is gradual rather than an immediate, unconditional erasure.[1][3]
- Blind-Side Attacks: Vhalor attacks from positions his opponent is not watching, using his mobility and extensions to punish attacks directed at his allies. This is especially effective when coordinated with [[Nerath]]'s suppression of an opponent's powers.[3]

#### Manifestation and Shadow Realm
- Manifestation Dispersal: Vhalor can dissolve his physical manifestation to evade restraints or attacks. He uses this method against Pestilence before producing multiple Shadow hands elsewhere on the battlefield.[3]
- Reappearance: After dispersing, he can reform at a different position and continue attacking. His reappearance is not inherently safe, however; Death anticipates his movement and strikes him as he materializes.[4]
- Shadow Realm: Vhalor can disperse his body into a Shadow Realm to escape an opponent's reach. Famine's blades pierce that realm and force him to gather his manifestation together again, interrupting his escape.[4]
- Shedding a Contaminated Portion: When Pestilence's corruption spreads along one of his extensions, Vhalor dissolves the affected part of his manifestation to prevent the contamination from spreading further through it.[3]
- Manifestation Stabilization: Vhalor can reconstruct his body after dispersal and injury, but doing so costs energy. Repeated wounds and the suppression imposed by [[Gol-goroth]]'s domain make it increasingly difficult for him to keep his manifestation together.[4]

#### Shadow Structures and Energy
- Shadow Structure: Vhalor forms a structure of Shadows to defend against [[Gol-goroth]]'s vertical strike. It initially holds, but the sword eventually shatters it and wounds his shoulder.[4]
- Compact Shadow Barrier: He gathers Shadows into a compact barrier that briefly protects him from War and Famine. Pestilence's corruption reaches the structures supporting the defense, weakening it until Gol-goroth breaks through.[4]
- Energy Wave: Vhalor launches a wave of energy at War and Famine while his compact barrier is active. The attack pushes them back temporarily, allowing him a brief opportunity to recover strength, but it does not defeat either opponent.[4]
- Area-Wide Shadow Influence: Vhalor can spread his influence across an area and cause Shadows to appear around his enemies. This ability is severely limited inside Gol-goroth's Eldritch domain, where his weakened realm no longer affects the Horsemen as it did before.[4]

#### Cooperation with Other Chief Gods
- Combined Restraint with [[Nerath]]: Vhalor restrains opponents with his Shadows while Nerath suppresses their powers through Silence. Their combined attacks force Pestilence to contend with both Gods and allow Vhalor to exploit openings created by Nerath.[3]
- Rescue Through Night and Silence: When Death threatens Vhalor, [[Kaelith]] and Nerath combine Night and Silence to pull him out of the Horseman's reach. Their intervention depends on finding an opening in [[Gol-goroth]]'s influence.[4]
- Joint Field of Resistance: Vhalor attunes himself to Kaelith and Nerath, after which a pillar of Darkness rises from his body. The three create a small field intended to resist the Eldritch domain's influence. The field offers limited resistance rather than restoring their full abilities.[4]
- Battlefield Assessment: Vhalor recognizes that the Horsemen move with exceptional coordination and identifies the incarnation of War as the key to their survival. He advocates a counteroffensive based on that observation, but the plan is interrupted by War's attack.[4]

#### Limitations
- Eldritch Domain Suppression: [[Gol-goroth]]'s domain suppresses Vhalor's control over the battlefield. His Shadows and realm become ineffective against the Horsemen, forcing him to rely on physical evasion and limited defensive structures instead.[3][4]
- Vulnerability to Corruption: Pestilence can spread corruption through Vhalor's Shadow extensions. Vhalor can discard an affected portion, but the countermeasure does not prevent the Horseman from repeatedly threatening his restraints and defenses.[3]
- Vulnerability During Reassembly: Vhalor's dispersal is not an absolute escape. Famine can pierce his Shadow Realm and force him to reassemble, while Death can anticipate where he will reappear and attack at that moment.[4]
- Energy Expenditure: Reconstructing his manifestation after repeated injuries requires increasing amounts of energy. By the end of the fight, Vhalor struggles to keep his body stable and cannot reliably sustain a counterattack.[4]
- Patron-Sustained Survival: Death states that the Gods' patron is keeping them alive inside the domain. This survival is not presented as a self-sufficient ability unique to Vhalor.[4]
- Restoration by [[Vaelthar]]: Vhalor is restored by Vaelthar's beam of pure Darkness after the battle. This is an act performed by Vaelthar, not a demonstrated healing technique belonging to Vhalor himself.[5]
