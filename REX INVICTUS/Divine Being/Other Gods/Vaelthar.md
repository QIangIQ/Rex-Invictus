### Appearances

#### Debut
Chapter 1195
Great Origin, Vaelthar.
—[[Kaelith]], greeting him while severely injured
Vaelthar, known as the Great Origin, was the Throne of Origin and one of the Seven Thrones of the Black Eclipse. Unlike the faction's Chief Gods, he was a true God at Throne level, placing him above them but beneath [[Vharzath]], the Great Darkness. He was artificially created by Vharzath as a possible Chosen One of Negativity. Although the creation succeeded, Vaelthar was not chosen. Vharzath gave him a portion of his own power, and Vaelthar's divinity of <BEGIN> became stained by the Great Darkness.[1][2][3]
Vaelthar entered [[Gol-goroth]]'s domain at the head of a reinforcement force after [[Azrakar]] was erased. His arrival interrupted the Horsemen's attempt to finish off the wounded Kaelith, [[Nerath]], and [[Vhalor]]. He restored the three Gods and reprimanded them for disappointing their ruler. When [[Victor Elderblood]] appeared, Vaelthar recognized that his forces faced an opponent whose power exceeded their expectations. Nevertheless, he ordered the attack, estimating that their chance of surviving was below one percent.[1][2]
Vaelthar and the accompanying Gods attacked Victor's projection with coordinated divine attacks, spatial restraints, concentrated force, and a prison formed from black constructs. None of these techniques could meaningfully stop the projection. Vaelthar continued fighting after losing an arm and suffering extensive damage to his armor and energy structures. Victor ultimately defeated him and left him incapacitated beneath his foot, with [[Junketsu]] held at his head. The sword did not fall in that moment, but this scene was not evidence of Vaelthar's survival. Vharzath later treated the loss of the Throne of Origin as the greatest loss of the war and regretted sending him out.[4][3]

### Physical Appearance
Vaelthar is a humanoid God nearly three meters tall. He has four golden eyes, four arms, and reverse-jointed legs. His body is covered in ivory armor veined with violet, while black energy flows from his back like a cloak. During combat, this energy spreads around his arms and forms additional structures across the battlefield. Other structures hover behind his shoulders until they are disrupted by damage.[2][4]
[[Junketsu]]'s initial cut removes one of Vaelthar's arms along with the material in its path. By the time Vaelthar later blocks with all four arms, the missing arm has returned. His armor is eventually torn, revealing flickering violet lines beneath his skin, and the energy structures behind his shoulders lose their shape. These changes occur as he sustains damage from [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s projection.[4]
His title and name are distinguished when [[Kaelith]] greets him as "Great Origin, Vaelthar." Great Origin is his title, while Vaelthar is his name. His defining unusual trait is not simply his appearance, but his divinity of <BEGIN>, which was stained by the Darkness of the being who created him.[1][2][3]

### Personality
Vaelthar demonstrates strict loyalty to [[Vharzath]]. After restoring [[Kaelith]], [[Nerath]], and [[Vhalor]], he tells them that they have disappointed the Great Darkness. The three accept the reprimand and bow before him without protest.[2]
His command style is uncompromising. Even after determining that the reinforcement has less than a one-percent chance of surviving [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]], Vaelthar orders his forces to attack. He continues committing his own power and that of his subordinates despite the overwhelming difference in strength demonstrated during the battle. When the Gods are cut down, he orders them to stand firm and channels his power into their bodies to restore their wounds. He later gathers the survivors for another attack even after his own armor and energy structures have been badly damaged.[2][4]
Vaelthar does not withdraw when the battle turns against him. His final recorded actions show him continuing to resist Victor's projection, even after his attacks and defensive techniques have failed. His loyalty and refusal to retreat do not change the outcome: Vharzath later regards him as a loss that cannot be replaced.[4][3]
Although he was created as a possible Chosen One of Negativity, Vaelthar was not selected. The supplied chapters do not establish that he possessed the powers or status of the actual Chosen One merely because he was created as a candidate.[3]

### Synopsis

#### Those Who Rule Reality
Following [[Azrakar]]'s erasure, the Horsemen continue attacking the remaining Chief Gods of the Black Eclipse. War attacks [[Vhalor]] with [[Gol-goroth]], while Death, Famine, and Pestilence pressure [[Kaelith]] and [[Nerath]]. The wounded Gods are close to being defeated when an outside force strikes Gol-goroth's domain. The pressure of the incoming power disrupts the battlefield and forces the Horsemen to abandon their immediate executions.[5][1]
A breach opens in the domain, and the reinforcement enters. The group's arrival forces the Horsemen back and changes the balance of the engagement. Kaelith recognizes its leader and greets him as "Great Origin, Vaelthar." He is one of the Seven Thrones, a true God at Throne level rather than one of the Chief Gods. His presence further strains Gol-goroth's domain. War judges the new arrival stronger than himself and ends the domain rather than remain trapped against the reinforcement.[1][2]
Vaelthar restores Kaelith, Nerath, and Vhalor with a beam of pure darkness. He then tells them that they have disappointed [[Vharzath]]. The three accept the rebuke and submit to whatever punishment their ruler might impose. Vaelthar's power draws attention from War, who notices the divinity of <BEGIN> and recognizes a similarity to the Emperor's power, although Vaelthar's divinity feels corrupted. The power belongs to Vaelthar, not to the other beings arriving on the battlefield.[2]
Justice and [[Elarion]] arrive with their own intentions toward War. Before the situation can be settled, [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] appears in his God Emperor form, with his true dragon body holding the galaxy. Vharzath recognizes Victor as Negativity and reacts with excitement. Victor praises War, then kills Kaelith by striking her Soul, erasing her body while retaining her Soul. He subsequently declares his intention to extinguish Vharzath and the Black Eclipse. The faction's territories are already under attack, and Vharzath cannot readily rescue or recall the forces isolated on the battlefield.[2]
Vaelthar nevertheless orders the reinforcement to attack Victor's projection. He estimates their chance of surviving at below one percent, but insists that they must try. The accompanying Gods launch attacks capable of destroying stars, create dimensional spears, and attempt to pin Victor from several directions. When the attacks clear, Victor remains standing without having raised his sword. He dismisses the assault and challenges them to do better.[2][4]
One of the Gods then uses a spatial distortion to reappear in front of Victor with a sword capable of cutting dimensional barriers. Victor catches the blade between two fingers, overcomes the attacker's additional power, and throws him through the debris of ruined planets. Vaelthar watches the exchange and realizes that the Gods have encountered an entirely different level of power, but he does not call off the attack.[4]
Vaelthar spreads black energy from his back and commands spatial restraints to surround Victor while the other Gods combine their divinities to restrict the projection. He follows with a concentrated strike to Victor's chest. The impact sends a shock through the surrounding space, but Victor does not move. Victor catches Vaelthar by the wrist, pulls him forward, and strikes his stomach, breaking his defense and scattering part of his black energy. Vaelthar is thrown into the Gods rushing toward Victor, disrupting their attack.[4]
Victor then draws [[Junketsu]] and uses a plain horizontal cut. The attack splits the Gods and the surrounding material in its path, cutting through their defenses and continuing through planets and stars. Vaelthar loses an arm during the exchange, while the surviving Gods recognize that they cannot win. Vaelthar orders them to stand firm and channels his power through their bodies, restoring their wounds within seconds. His missing arm has also returned by the time he next blocks with all four arms.[4]
The Gods attack again, using beams capable of destroying solar systems. Victor gathers their attacks into a sphere with one hand and makes the energy disappear by closing his fist. He then releases an aura that sweeps the Gods away and disrupts their control of their abilities. Vaelthar raises his domain around the area, darkening the space and enclosing Victor inside a prison of black constructs. The other Gods pour their attacks into the prison while Vaelthar burns immense energy to prevent the projection from escaping. Cracks appear in the structure, however, and Victor simply walks out. The attacks fade near him, and the prison collapses.[4]
The remaining Gods make further attempts to stop Victor, including an energy wave, a dimensional weapon, and a chain intended to bind his Soul. Their attacks fail to halt his advance. Victor kills one of the Gods who attempts to flee, then turns his attention back to Vaelthar. Junketsu cuts through the remaining attacks and defenses, scattering the surviving combatants. Vaelthar blocks with all four arms, but the impact breaks his defense. Victor strikes his chest, sending him backward and breaking his black energy into large fragments.[4]
Vaelthar rises with his armor torn, violet lines flickering beneath his skin, and the energy structures behind his shoulders damaged. He calls the remaining Gods for one last attack and raises another barrier. Victor breaks through it with a sword stroke and hits him again before he can renew the defense. Vaelthar attempts a counterattack, but Victor catches his arm and throws him against space itself, opening a rift. Victor places a foot on Vaelthar's chest and aims Junketsu at his head. Vaelthar is still moving, and the sword does not fall in that instant.[4]
Victor then notices a small crack in his gauntlet. The damage is negligible and required close inspection to see. The battle had been fought against a projection; Victor's true body remained the dragon holding the galaxy and had barely moved. Victor loses interest in the engagement and orders the living taken prisoner and the fallen collected. Vaelthar is not shown surviving the encounter. Later, Vharzath identifies the loss of the Throne of Origin as the greatest loss of the war and regrets having sent him out.[4][3]

### Skills & Abilities
Vaelthar was one of the Seven Thrones of the Black Eclipse and possessed the divinity of <BEGIN>, stained by [[Vharzath]]'s Darkness. The following abilities are based on his demonstrated actions in Chapters 1195–1197 and the later explanation of his creation and death in Chapter 1201. His battle against [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] was fought against a projection, not Victor's true dragon body.[1][2][4][3]

#### Divinity and Nature
- Divinity of <BEGIN>: Vaelthar possesses the divinity of <BEGIN>. During the confrontation, War recognizes that it resembles the Emperor's divinity but feels corrupted. [[Vharzath]] later explains that Vaelthar was artificially created as a possible Chosen One of Negativity and given a portion of his own power, staining Vaelthar's <BEGIN> with Darkness. The text does not establish that Vaelthar was himself chosen as the Chosen One of Negativity.[2][3]
- Throne-Level Divinity: As the Throne of Origin, Vaelthar is a true God at Throne level and stands above the Black Eclipse's Chief Gods. His presence is powerful enough to strain [[Gol-goroth]]'s domain upon entering it. This does not establish that he is stronger than every other Throne or faction leader.[1][2]
- Darkness-Imbued Power: Vaelthar's power is associated with the Darkness placed within him by Vharzath. He manifests black energy during combat and uses it to form restraints, a prison-like domain, and a beam that restores his subordinates. The chapters do not explicitly identify each of these techniques as a separate application of the <BEGIN> concept.[2][4][3]

#### Physical Abilities
- Superhuman Physical Characteristics: Vaelthar possesses a humanoid divine body nearly three meters tall, with four arms, four golden eyes, and reverse-jointed legs. He can engage the Horsemen's forces and fight [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s projection directly, although the latter overwhelms him.[1][2][4]
- Four-Arm Combat: Vaelthar uses all four arms to block an attack from [[Junketsu]]. His defense fails under the impact. His missing arm, removed during an earlier exchange, is present again when he later blocks with all four arms; the transcript does not explicitly identify the mechanism behind this restoration.[4]
- Energy Mantle: Black energy pours from Vaelthar's back like a cloak. During combat, it spreads around his arms and forms additional structures across the battlefield. These structures are disrupted as he sustains damage.[2][4]
- Damage Resistance: Vaelthar withstands multiple exchanges with Victor's projection, including a direct blow to the chest and attacks that damage his armor and energy structures. His resistance does not prevent Victor from breaking his defenses and incapacitating him.[4]

#### Presence and Command
- Powerful Presence: Vaelthar's arrival strains [[Gol-goroth]]'s domain. The reinforcement's combined energy forces the Horsemen back and interrupts their attempt to finish off the wounded Chief Gods. War judges Vaelthar stronger than himself and chooses to end the domain rather than continue fighting within it.[1][2]
- Tactical Command: Vaelthar leads the reinforcement and directs the Gods accompanying him. He orders them to coordinate their attacks, use their divinities against [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]], and concentrate their power while he raises his domain. He also rallies the survivors after their initial defeat.[2][4]
- Risk Assessment: Vaelthar estimates that the reinforcement has less than a one-percent chance of surviving Victor but orders the attack anyway. This is a judgment he makes about their prospects, not a supernatural ability to determine exact probabilities.[2]
- Restoration Through Power Transfer: After [[Junketsu]] cuts through the Gods, Vaelthar orders them to stand firm and channels his own power into their bodies. Their wounds close within seconds, allowing the survivors to fight again. This technique is demonstrated on his subordinates; it is not proof that he can restore himself from any injury or resurrect the dead.[4]

#### Restoration Techniques
- Beam of Pure Darkness: Vaelthar releases a beam of pure darkness that restores [[Kaelith]], [[Nerath]], and [[Vhalor]] after they have been severely injured by the Horsemen. The technique restores their bodies sufficiently for them to continue, but it does not protect Kaelith from [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s subsequent attack on her Soul.[2]
- Healing and Recovery Support: Vaelthar can use his power to close the wounds of his subordinates during battle. After he calls out "Stand firm!", their injuries heal within seconds. This is distinct from the earlier beam of pure darkness, although both techniques serve to restore the wounded Gods.[4]

#### Spatial Techniques
- Spatial Restraint Generation: Vaelthar commands countless restraints to appear around [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s projection. The other Gods combine their divinities with the restraints in an attempt to restrict Victor's movement. The restraints fail to hold him. The transcript shows Vaelthar commanding the technique, but does not fully explain whether he personally creates every restraint or coordinates their combined attack.[4]
- Domain Expansion: Vaelthar envelops the surrounding region in his domain during the later stages of the battle. The area darkens as the domain spreads around Victor's projection, providing the basis for his prison technique.[4]
- Domain Prison Construction: Vaelthar forms black constructs around Victor, closing them together like a prison until the projection is hidden from view. He maintains the structure by expending immense energy to prevent Victor from escaping while the other Gods attack the enclosed target. The prison cracks, Victor walks out, and the structure collapses. It therefore does not demonstrate the ability to permanently seal an opponent of Victor's level.[4]
- Barrier Generation: After sustaining substantial damage, Vaelthar raises another barrier during the final rally. Victor breaks through it with a sword stroke and lands another attack before Vaelthar can renew the defense. The chapter demonstrates a temporary defensive barrier but does not establish that it is an independent spatial concept or an absolute defense.[4]

#### Offensive Techniques
- Concentrated Power Strike: Vaelthar concentrates his power into a single blow aimed at [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s chest. The impact sends a shock through the surrounding space, but Victor remains in place and catches Vaelthar by the wrist. The attack fails to inflict meaningful damage on the projection.[4]
- Energy Manipulation: Vaelthar can direct and shape the black energy flowing from his body, using it to surround his arms and form structures across the battlefield. These manifestations are disrupted when Victor strikes him. The chapter does not establish that every attack made by the accompanying Gods is one of Vaelthar's abilities.[2][4]

#### Limitations
- Inferior to [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s Projection: Vaelthar's restraints, concentrated strike, domain prison, and final barrier all fail to stop Victor's projection. Victor defeats him in direct combat and leaves only a negligible crack in his own gauntlet. This result applies to the projection shown in the fight; Victor's true body remains the dragon holding the galaxy.[4]
- Finite Defensive Output: Vaelthar must expend immense energy to maintain the domain prison and prevent Victor from escaping. The structure nevertheless cracks and collapses. His defensive techniques are therefore not shown to be cost-free or unbreakable.[4]
- Not the Chosen One of Negativity: Although created as a possible candidate, Vaelthar was not chosen. His artificial origin and Darkness-stained <BEGIN> divinity do not establish that he possessed the actual status or full power of the Chosen One of Negativity.[3]
- Death: Vharzath later treats Vaelthar as a loss that cannot be replaced. The scene in which Victor stands over him with [[Junketsu]] at his head does not depict a confirmed escape or survival, and no later passage in the supplied chapters shows Vaelthar returning to life.[4][3]
