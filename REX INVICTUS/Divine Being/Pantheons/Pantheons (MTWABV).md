**[[Pantheons (MTWABV)]]** are the Divine civilizations, supernatural factions and higher-dimensional territories in My Three Wives Are Beautiful Vampires. (Lore adapted from *My Three Wives Are Beautiful Vampires*.)

> **Rex Invictus canon.** This material is adapted into the Rex Invictus world at the lowest priority: Rex Invictus wins any conflict, then the Flamel material, then MTWABV. Events of the MTWABV timeline (Victor's conquests, the fall of the pantheons, the [[Elderblood Empire]]) are distant background or rumor unless a chapter puts them on the page. Pantheons are the Divine civilizations, supernatural factions and higher-dimensional territories in [[MTWABV Series|My Three Wives Are Beautiful Vampires]]. The word is considerably broader than the ordinary meaning of "a collection of Gods". Within the cosmology, a Pantheon can simultaneously describe a Divine faction, the Gods and supernatural species belonging to it, the higher-dimensional territory under its control, its Heaven and Hell or [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]], its afterlife infrastructure, and the network of Concepts represented by the Gods sustaining that Reality.[1][2]
Traditional Pantheons do not normally exist as hidden countries somewhere on the surface of a Mortal planet. Their principal territories are supernatural dimensions existing separately from ordinary material space and reached through the [[Dimensional Void]], an endless interdimensional medium through which the dimensions belonging to a Sector remain separated while still forming part of the same greater Reality.[3][4]
A sufficiently developed Pantheon may therefore constitute an entire dimensional civilization: a celestial Realm inhabited by Gods, an infernal Realm responsible for the dead, independent supernatural geography, its own local temporal progression, Divine Laws and Conceptual authorities, while remaining metaphysically connected to the Mortal civilization and [[World Trees]] from which the system developed.[5][6][7]

### Definition
A Pantheon is best understood as a complete Divine system rather than merely a religious organization.
Pantheon = a Divine civilization and its supernatural jurisdiction, potentially encompassing its Gods, Conceptual hierarchy, celestial and infernal dimensions, afterlife systems, subordinate supernatural populations, Faith network and political government.
The exact meaning depends upon context.
Usage
Meaning
Pantheon as a faction
The Gods, supernatural species, servants, armies and subordinate populations organized beneath a Divine authority.
Pantheon as a civilization
A complete society possessing government, laws, rulers, military forces, cities, resources, traditions, Faith and political interests.
Pantheon as a dimension
The supernatural dimensional territory controlled by the Divine civilization.
Pantheon as a mythological complex
The complete collection of celestial, infernal and spiritual territories associated with one mythology, potentially including Heaven, Hell, Underworlds, Divine kingdoms and afterlife infrastructure.
Pantheon as a Conceptual system
The network of Gods whose Divinities represent and maintain Concepts within the Divine Reality.
Pantheon as territory
A possession that can be conquered, annexed, inherited, transferred or even sold independently of many of its former inhabitants.[8]
These meanings frequently overlap. When characters speak of the [[Greek Pantheon]], for example, they may mean the Greek Divine civilization, the Gods belonging to it, or the complete dimensional territory containing [[Olympus]] and its connected supernatural structures. Context determines which meaning is being used.

#### Not Simply a Religion
A mortal religion and a Pantheon are related but are not identical.
Religion
The Mortal system of Faith, stories, worship and cultural traditions surrounding Divine beings.
Pantheon
The actual supernatural civilization, Gods and dimensional structure behind those traditions.
The [[Shinto Pantheon]], [[Biblical Pantheon]], [[Greek Pantheon]] and other Divine civilizations continued to exist as supernatural powers regardless of whether ordinary Humans directly understood the true structure of their Realms.

#### Not Simply a Race
A Pantheon does not need to consist exclusively of Gods.
The clearest example is Dragon's Nest. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s faction contained Gods, Dragons, Vampires, Demons, Angels, [[Werewolves]], Youkai, Witches, Humans and numerous other species, yet the greater System formally recognized Dragon's Nest as a legitimate Pantheon once Victor established it.[9]
Pantheon status is therefore associated with a sufficiently developed Divine civilization and authority structure, not racial purity.

### Cosmological Structure
Pantheons are part of the higher-dimensional architecture surrounding inhabited worlds and their Sectors.
The material Universe seen by ordinary civilizations represents only one layer of existence. Outside normal astronomical space are supernatural dimensions separated by the [[Dimensional Void]]. Pantheons, Heavens, Hells, spiritual Realms and numerous other dimensions occupy this greater dimensional network.[3][4]

#### The Dimensional Void
The [[Dimensional Void]] is an endless space flowing through a Sector and serving as the medium within which or through which dimensions are separated and connected.[3]
A dimension normally cannot simply be reached by walking far enough through ordinary space. Dimensional travel requires bypassing or traversing the Void separating the material layer from the target Realm.[4]
This means [[Olympus]] is not physically located somewhere above Greece, [[Takamagahara]] is not an undiscovered geographical region of Japan, and [[Asgard]] is not merely a distant astronomical planet accessible through ordinary terrestrial travel.
Greece ≠ Olympus
Japan ≠ Takamagahara
Mortal Scandinavia ≠ Asgard
Their religions and histories are connected to Mortal civilizations, while the Divine territories themselves occupy different planes of existence.[2]

#### Dimensional Void ≠ Void Between Cosmologies
The [[Dimensional Void]] should not be confused with the much greater Void used by beings travelling between separate Universes, Realities and Cosmologies.
Dimensional Void
Exists as part of the internal structure of a Sector/Reality.
Separates and connects Pantheons, Hells, Paradises and other dimensions.
Remains part of the native Creation.
Void Between Cosmologies
Exists beyond the barriers separating entire cosmological systems.
Is traversed by sufficiently powerful Outsiders, Multiversal Beings and comparable entities.
Is not the ordinary location of Earth Pantheons.
Pantheon dimensions can therefore be described as existing beyond the ordinary material Reality without implying that they exist outside their entire native Cosmology.

### World Trees and Pantheon Dimensions
[[World Trees]] are fundamental to the dimensional ecosystems developing around inhabited worlds.
A developed world does not consist solely of the physical planet. Its World Tree system can support additional supernatural planes associated with Life, Souls, Gods and the afterlife.[7]

World Tree roots function as cosmological anchors for attached dimensions. If the connection sustaining a Realm is sufficiently damaged or abandoned, the associated dimension can become unstable and eventually collapse. The deterioration of Norse dimensional structures during Ragnarök demonstrated this dependency.[10]
Consequently, a Pantheon dimension is neither an arbitrary pocket space nor an ordinary physical continent. It forms part of a larger supernatural ecosystem connecting:

- and the greater dimensional network of the Sector.

### Pantheon Space-Time
Pantheon dimensions are genuine Realms with internal spatial and temporal structures rather than static rooms detached from causality.
Dimensions can possess their own local flow of Time.[11] The [[Greek Pantheon]] provides the clearest demonstration that a Pantheon's Reality possesses a meaningful past, present and future that can be affected by the Divine Concepts sustaining it.

#### The Greek Pantheon and Time
[[Kronos]] was the Divine representative of Time within the [[Greek Pantheon]]'s structure.
When [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] permanently killed Kronos, the temporal system of [[Olympus|Mount Olympus]] immediately stopped advancing. With the Divine authority responsible for Time removed, the Pantheon's Reality became stagnant: the past and future ceased progressing and only the present remained.[6]
Kronos' death did not erase universal Time. It disrupted Time inside the Divine Reality in which Kronos functioned as its representative. This distinction is important when separating a Primordial God from an actual Primordial Entity.
This event establishes several important properties of Pantheon dimensions:
- the Realm possesses an internal temporal progression;
- its history includes a meaningful past and future;
- Divine Concepts can directly govern the operation of that local Reality;
- removing a sufficiently fundamental Divine representative can damage the Realm's metaphysical functions;
- and the Pantheon is sufficiently independent that its local Time can fail without Time immediately disappearing from every other Sector or dimension in Creation.[6]

#### The Three Sisters and the Greek Timeline
The Greek system had already demonstrated this local temporal structure much earlier through the Three Sisters of Fate.
In Chapter 247, [[Kairos]] explains that the Sisters continually remain at their station preserving the past and future. If they abandoned their work, the past, present and future of beings belonging to their mythology would fall apart.[5]
Their function should not be interpreted as proof that every individual Pantheon automatically contains an independent infinite multiverse. What it directly demonstrates is that the Greek Divine system possessed a metaphysically regulated history in which the past, present and future of its mythology were real structures requiring supernatural maintenance.
The Fate system therefore operated alongside the Pantheon's other Conceptual functions:

The broader Force of Fate was later described as a flow containing branching possibilities. Individuals could enter different branches through their choices rather than being mindlessly forced along a single path.[12] Gods associated with Fate possessed considerably greater access to this structure than ordinary prophetic beings.[13]

#### Local Time and the Greater Timeline
Pantheon-local space-time should be distinguished from the greater timeline structure of Creation.
Before the events surrounding Destiny, Creation possessed a much larger branching system of alternative timelines and possible Realities. The later collapse of those alternatives into the Main Timeline affected the cosmology on a vastly greater scale than the local temporal structure of one Pantheon.[14][15]
Therefore:
Pantheon timeline
The internal historical and temporal progression of a Divine Realm.
Sector / Reality timeline
The historical progression of the larger Reality containing the Pantheon.
Former Creation-wide timeline system
The greater branching structure once governed by Destiny.
They are related but should not automatically be treated as identical structures.

### Gods and Conceptual Reality
Gods are not simply biologically powerful superhumans.
They are explicitly described as Higher Existences whose nature is substantially more spiritual and Conceptual than that of conventional Mortal species. Their existence is closer to that of extremely advanced spirits, with their Divine bodies fundamentally connected to their Souls rather than functioning like ordinary mortal flesh.[16]
Their defining characteristic is Divinity: Conceptual authority embedded within the core of a Divine Soul.

#### Conceptual Representatives
When a Being becomes a God, the Concept represented by their Divinity becomes a fundamental component of that God's existence.
A [[Ares|God of War]] does not merely possess "War [[Magic]]".
War forms part of the God's Divine identity.
A Goddess of Beauty does not merely cast Beauty-related abilities.
Beauty is represented through her Divinity.
Gods therefore function as Divine embodiments, representatives or anchors of Concepts within their corresponding Reality.[17]
The setting establishes that if a Concept lacks a Divine representative within the relevant Pantheon/Sector structure, that Concept can cease functioning correctly in that Reality.[17]
[[Kronos]] provides the most direct example: once the Greek Divine representative of Time died, Time inside the Pantheon stopped progressing.[6]

#### Pantheons as Conceptual Realities
A developed Pantheon can therefore be understood as more than geography.
Its Reality may be maintained through a network of Divine Concepts:

#### Divine Embodiment ≠ Universal Embodiment
The Conceptual importance of Gods should not be exaggerated into the claim that every God is the absolute universal Concept they represent.
[[Kronos]] embodied and represented Time within the Greek Divine system, but he was not the universal metaphysical source of all Time throughout Creation.[6]
Likewise, different Sectors can possess separate Divine representatives of apparently identical Concepts without automatically competing with one another. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] discovered this when Gods of another Sector possessed Concepts such as Night and Darkness despite Nyx never having sensed them as rivals.[18]

This is why the death of Kronos could devastate Time inside [[Olympus]] without destroying Time throughout every Reality in existence.

#### Celestial Side
The celestial side of a Pantheon usually contains its principal Divine government, God-King and Gods traditionally associated with the "Heavenly" portion of that mythology.
This does not mean every celestial God is morally benevolent.
"Positive" and "Negative" in the cosmology concern metaphysical alignment and Energy, not simplistic morality.

The infernal side can contain:
- Hell;
- Underworlds;
- dead Souls;
- a Ruler;
- Gods of Death;
- psychopomps;
- punishment systems;
- reincarnation infrastructure;
- and other Soul-related authorities.
A Pantheon's Hell can be an enormous independent dimension. [[Biblical Hell]], for example, expanded to approximately 100 times the size of Earth before later mergers drastically increased the scale of [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s infernal territories.[20]

### Great Pantheons and the Soul System
Earth historically possessed numerous Pantheons, but only seven received the traditional classification of Great Pantheons.
Their status was not determined solely by how strong their God-King was.
Chapter 704 explains that the critical distinction involved the development of the Pantheon's Hell and its integration into the greater system of Souls supervised by [[The Judges of The Abyss]].[1]

#### Minor Pantheons
Smaller Pantheons can still possess:
- powerful Gods;
- a God-King;
- Divine territory;
- Faith;
- supernatural populations;
- an [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]];
- and considerable military power.
They simply do not necessarily possess the complete Soul infrastructure that defines the Great Pantheon system.
The [[Celtic Pantheon]] is an explicit example. It possessed a Hell, yet its infernal system was not sufficiently developed to receive the complete Ruler structure possessed by the Great Pantheons.[1]

### The Seven Great Pantheons
The seven Divine civilizations traditionally indexed as Earth's Great Pantheons are:
1. [[Greek Pantheon]]
2. [[Norse Pantheon]]
3. [[Shinto Pantheon]]
4. [[Egyptian Pantheon]]
5. [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]]
6. [[Biblical Pantheon]]
7. [[Chinese Pantheon]]
Chapter 704 establishes the seven-Pantheon structure and the Heaven/Hell qualification mechanism. The classification should therefore not be treated as a simple "top seven strongest Gods" ranking.[1]
The [[Buddhist Pantheon]] was nevertheless an extremely significant Divine civilization with [[Buddha]] and the [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] authority [[Enma]]. Its absence from the traditional seven-member index does not mean it was an insignificant faction.

### Pantheon Dimensions as Territory
The word "Pantheon" can refer directly to dimensional property.
Following Ragnarök, [[Hela Elderblood]] announced that she intended to sell the [[Norse Pantheon]]. Much of the population and portable resources had already been removed, meaning the object being sold consisted primarily of the surviving dimensional territory, infrastructure and political value of the fallen Divine civilization.[8]
This demonstrates that:
Pantheon ≠ only its living Gods
A Pantheon can remain as a territorial structure after the government that once ruled it has collapsed.
Pantheon territories can therefore be:
- conquered;
- inherited;
- annexed;
- reorganized;
- merged into another dimensional system;
- transferred to another ruler;
- or sold.[8]
[[Victor Elderblood]] similarly obtained authority over former Pantheon territories and later incorporated several Divine Realms into his expanding dimensional civilization.

### Scale and Structural Stability
Pantheon dimensions vary enormously in size and stability.
They should not be treated as ordinary magical rooms.
The [[Greek Pantheon]] possessed sufficient dimensional scale that [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s battle against its Gods took place across both [[Olympus]] and the [[Greek Underworld]]. During Chapter 799, Victor created an attack whose accumulated Energy was judged sufficient to destroy the Pantheon twice over.[21]
The statement establishes that "destroying a Pantheon" can mean destroying an actual large-scale Divine Realm rather than merely defeating the Gods occupying it.
Likewise, Victor and War shook the entire [[Biblical Hell]] during their battle and permanently damaged its geography without casually causing the dimension itself to disappear.[22]
However, Pantheon dimensions are not absolutely indestructible.
Their stability depends upon factors such as:
- the World Tree roots anchoring the Realm;
- its Divine infrastructure;
- the Concepts functioning within it;
- the Gods maintaining fundamental aspects;
- dimensional barriers;
- accumulated [[Divine Energy]];
- and direct damage suffered by the Realm.
The Norse crisis demonstrated that damaged or abandoned World Tree roots can cause attached dimensions to deteriorate.[10]
The Greek conflict demonstrated that removing Conceptual anchors such as [[Kronos]] can destabilize the laws functioning inside a Pantheon without immediately physically annihilating the whole Realm.[6]

### Pantheon Authority
Pantheons are not neutral environments to their rulers.
[[God-Kings]] and other high-ranking Divine beings can obtain substantial advantages while operating inside the territories connected to their authority.
Ra provides a later example. During his confrontation with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]], Ra fought while supported by the power and authority of his own Pantheon.[23][24]
Pantheon-based advantages can include:
- additional [[Divine Energy]];
- greater environmental control;
- access to Divine infrastructure;
- territorial authority;
- amplification of Divinities;
- Faith;
- support from subordinate Gods;
- and control over the Realm's supernatural systems.
The exact benefits depend upon the Pantheon and individual God.

### Earth Pantheons

#### Greek Pantheon
The [[Greek Pantheon]] was one of Earth's most structurally important Divine civilizations.
Its Divine history included primordial deities such as [[Ouranos]], [[Gaia]], Nyx, [[Erebus]] and [[Tartarus (Realm)|Tartarus]], followed by the [[Titans]] and later Olympian Gods.
[[Olympus|Mount Olympus]] represented its principal celestial territory while the [[Greek Underworld]] formed its infernal counterpart.
[[Zeus]] ruled Olympus as God-King while [[Hades]] historically governed the Underworld before the position eventually passed to [[Persephone Elderblood]].
The Greek Pantheon is particularly valuable for understanding Pantheon cosmology because it directly demonstrates:
- Gods functioning as Conceptual anchors;
- a Pantheon possessing its own temporal progression;
- the destruction of local Time following [[Kronos]]' death;[6]
- the Three Sisters of Fate maintaining the history of beings belonging to Greek mythology;[5]
- separate celestial and infernal Divine territories;
- and sufficient dimensional scale that attacks capable of destroying the entire Pantheon required exceptional amounts of power.[21]
The Pantheon was eventually dismantled during [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s war against its rulers.

#### Norse Pantheon
The [[Norse Pantheon]] developed around [[Asgard]] and the wider Norse Divine system, with Helheim functioning as its principal [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]].
[[Odin]] served as God-King while [[Hela Elderblood]] governed Helheim.
Ragnarök destroyed Odin's political order and severely damaged the Pantheon's supernatural infrastructure. Following the conflict, Hela inherited effective control over what remained but had no desire to rebuild the old system.
She later auctioned the surviving Pantheon itself, demonstrating that a Pantheon can remain a valuable dimensional territory after its former government collapses.[8]

#### Shinto Pantheon
The [[Shinto Pantheon]] is the Divine civilization associated with Japan.
Its celestial Realm is [[Takamagahara]], while Yomi forms its infernal counterpart.
[[Amaterasu]] Elderblood serves as its principal celestial ruler.
The Pantheon is notable for its enormous Divine population; more than one million Gods are stated to belong to the Shinto system.[27][28]
This demonstrates that the named Gods appearing in the story represent only a tiny fraction of the actual population of major Pantheons.

#### Egyptian Pantheon
The [[Egyptian Pantheon]] was one of Earth's Great Pantheons.
Ra served as its principal God-King while [[Apep]] possessed major authority associated with its infernal side.
Its supernatural territories included Egyptian celestial and [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]] dimensions.
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] eventually defeated Ra and destroyed the Pantheon's independent political authority. Its surviving population and territories were subsequently incorporated into his expanding civilization.

#### Hindu Pantheon
The [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]] is one of Earth's surviving ancient Divine civilizations.
[[Indra]] historically held its God-King position before [[Shiva]] became its principal ruler, while Yama governs its [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]].
The Pantheon was eventually placed beneath the Elderblood political order while retaining a separate Pantheon identity.
This distinction remains important during the Imperial Era. Political subordination to [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] does not automatically mean every Hindu God becomes a member of Dragon's Nest.[29]

#### Biblical Pantheon
The [[Biblical Pantheon]] possesses one of Earth's largest supernatural infrastructures.
[[Heavenly Father]] stands at the center of its celestial system, which includes the Seven Heavens and enormous Angelic populations.
The Pantheon's negative side contains [[Biblical Hell]], whose rulership changed repeatedly between [[Lucifer]] Morningstar, [[Diablo]], [[Victor Elderblood]] and later administrators serving beneath Victor's authority.
The Biblical system differs from Pantheons such as Shinto because many functions performed elsewhere by individual Gods are instead carried out by immensely powerful Angels.

#### Chinese Pantheon
The [[Chinese Pantheon]] was one of Earth's ancient Great Pantheons and was ruled by the [[Jade Emperor]].
The Pantheon was destroyed during the Demonic War.
Real-world Chinese mythology should not be used to populate the faction with Gods that have never been confirmed within the novel.

#### Buddhist Pantheon
The [[Buddhist Pantheon]] was a major Divine civilization headed by [[Buddha]], while [[Enma]] governed its [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]].
Its enormous population of believers granted the system substantial spiritual importance.
The Pantheon was devastated during the Demonic War.

#### Aztec Pantheon
The [[Aztec Pantheon]] was another smaller Earth Divine civilization.

### Non-Earth Pantheons
Pantheons are not unique to Earth.
Any sufficiently developed world, Sector or Divine civilization can develop its own Pantheon structures.

#### Elder Gods Pantheon
The [[Elder Gods Pantheon]] was the principal native Divine faction associated with [[Nightingale]].
It developed beneath [[Ikor-Kar-VI]], a Primordial God possessing the Concepts of [[Immortality]] and Predation.
Its known Divine members included:
- Ikor-Kar-VI
- [[Var-Kar-VI]]
- [[Lucifer]] Morningstar
- Shur
- Nur
The Pantheon was destroyed during [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s war against Ikor.
Its structure demonstrates that foreign Pantheons do not need to reproduce Earth's exact Heaven/Hell political model.

#### Magic World Pantheon
The civilization encountered through the Witches' dimensional experiment possessed its own Pantheon.
Unlike Earth's frequently divided Divine factions, its Gods operated with unusual political unity.
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] considered that unity particularly dangerous because coordinated Gods could achieve far more collectively than stronger individual deities continuously undermining one another.[30]
The Pantheon's proper name remains unrevealed.

### Pantheons and Sectors
Pantheons exist within the larger framework of Sectors.
Divine Concepts are not automatically universal across all Sectors.
A God possessing a Concept in one Sector does not necessarily compete with a God representing the same apparent Concept in another Sector. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] directly discovered this while analyzing the Conceptual structure of foreign Gods.[18]
A Sector therefore possesses its own local Divine hierarchy and Conceptual relationships.
Pantheons form important higher-dimensional components of that Reality.

### Pantheons and Faith
Pantheons historically maintain connections with Mortal civilizations through Faith.
Mortal belief can strengthen Gods and influence Divine development, but the Pantheon itself is an objectively existing supernatural civilization rather than a fictional realm created solely by Human religion.
Greek religion was connected to the [[Greek Pantheon]].
Shinto worship was connected to [[Takamagahara]].
Biblical Faith connected Mortals to [[Biblical Heaven]] and its Divine authorities.
The mortal civilization and its Divine Pantheon can therefore influence each other while occupying separate planes of existence.

### Pantheon Population
Pantheon populations vary enormously.
The [[Shinto Pantheon]] alone contains more than one million Gods.[27][28]
Major Pantheons can additionally contain:
- Angels;
- Demons;
- spirits;
- Divine servants;
- magical creatures;
- mortal or semi-mortal populations;
- armies;
- Souls;
- and other supernatural species.
Consequently, the "Known Gods" listed on individual wiki pages represent only characters confirmed by the narrative, not the complete population of those Pantheons.

### Notes
- Pantheons are actual supernatural dimensions and civilizations, not merely collections of names from Mortal mythology.[2]
- Pantheon dimensions exist outside ordinary material space and are connected through the endless [[Dimensional Void]].[3][4]
- The Dimensional Void should not be confused with the extracosmological Void separating entire Universes and Cosmologies.
- Major Pantheons commonly encompass both celestial and infernal components, although smaller or unusual Pantheons do not necessarily possess a complete Heaven/Hell structure.[1]
- Pantheon dimensions can possess their own temporal progression. The [[Greek Pantheon]]'s past and future ceased functioning when [[Kronos]] died and Time stopped advancing inside [[Olympus]].[6]
- The Three Sisters of Fate were responsible for maintaining the past, present and future of beings associated with Greek mythology, further demonstrating that a Pantheon possesses metaphysical historical structure rather than existing as a static location.[5]
- Gods are Higher Existences whose nature is closer to Conceptual or high spiritual beings than conventional Mortal lifeforms.[16]
- Gods can function as Conceptual anchors within their Divine Reality, but ordinary Gods and [[Primordial Gods]] should not be confused with Creation-wide [[Primordial Entities]].[17][6]
- A Pantheon's existence can continue territorially after its government falls. The surviving [[Norse Pantheon]] was eventually sold as dimensional territory.[8]
- Pantheon dimensions are not inherently indestructible. Extremely powerful attacks can destroy them, while loss of World Tree support can destabilize attached Realms.[21][10]
- Dragon's Nest demonstrates that a Pantheon does not need to consist only of Gods.[9]
- Political conquest does not necessarily erase Pantheon identity. A Pantheon can remain a distinct Divine system while existing beneath the [[Elderblood Empire]].[29]
- The [[Eldritch Pantheon]] is primarily a Conceptual lineage and should not automatically be assumed to possess the Heaven/Hell infrastructure of a conventional Pantheon.[26]
- Known Pantheon members listed on the wiki are not exhaustive. Major Pantheons can contain enormous unnamed Divine populations.
- Real-world mythology should not be used to add Gods, realms, artifacts or historical events unless those elements are confirmed within the novel.
