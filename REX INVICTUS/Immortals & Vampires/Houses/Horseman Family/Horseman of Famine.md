Hunger is one of the most horrible ways to die. So tell me, you ever get hungry, Alucard?
—Famine to [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]
Famine was one of the Four Horsemen of the Apocalypse, an elite Demon who served [[Diablo]] and represented supernatural Hunger. With War, Death and Pestilence, he was one of Diablo's executioners, a class feared even by Pillar-ranked Demons, and one of the Beings the Bible would have usher in the Apocalypse.[1]
His work in Hell was support. The Glaive and the Authority put a hunger on the target that the body did not have, and the other three used the opening. The Glaive was not an ordinary spear. A piece of his Soul sat in it, and that piece carried the Hunger. Touching a Horseman's weapon without leave was dangerous enough that even the Demon King would not do it.[2]
He is the first of the four to die for good. Victor Elderblood put [[Junketsu]] through the heart, the only one of them without armor, and ate the existence. An average value on that Soul was enough to take the whole martial art and the Authority. The value was not the one Victor had put on Adonis. What came with the three consumed Horsemen was a Minor Authority, the remnant of a [[Demigod]]'s Divinity, and the battle memories tied to it.[2][3][4]
He does not climb back out of that. War later draws an Eldritch Famine incarnate out of his own body through [[Gol-goroth]], in the same breath as Death and Pestilence. That fighter is blades, not the Hunger Victor ate. He puts a knife of pure power through [[Kaelith]]'s stomach, pierces [[Vhalor]]'s shadow realm so the god has to pull himself back together, takes the arms and legs off [[Azrakar]], and is the one who says they cannot finish the three gods for good. He is set to stop Kaelith escaping when [[Vaelthar]] arrives. He does not land her death. Victor does. The recreation is not the Soul from Hell walking back out intact.[5][6][7]

### Physical Appearance
Famine was a knight in a cloak, and that was the difference. War and Death came in full armor. He did not. On his back was a slashing spear that looked plain, the sort of spear that could have been found anywhere. In the fight it is his Glaive.[1][2]
The horse looked as if it had not been fed in years. Frail as it was, the face still carried a fierce glow, and that glow was swallowed by the blue flames coming out of its eyes.[1]
The missing armor is why the heart-thrust goes in without difficulty.[2]
The incarnate is still a knight, and he does not bring that glaive back. [[Junketsu]] ate it. What he uses afterward is a knife of pure power, and then blades, more than one, spun until the space around [[Kaelith]] runs out. When [[Gol-goroth]]'s domain wakes what was dormant, he changes with Death and Pestilence. The three become increasingly like Death. The face disappears. The armor turns pitch-black. Wings of dark energy grow from the back and devour the light. The unarmored Horseman of the Hell fight is not the shape that cuts [[Azrakar]]'s limbs off.[8][9]

### Personality
He expected fear. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s smile was the wrong answer, and Famine said so: he had come to see the man shaking and hiding. The Power Victor then let out left Famine struggling to breathe. He still followed War. The older brother forced his own Power out, and Death, Pestilence and Famine did the same.[1]
Hunger, to him, was one of the worst ways to die, and he was sure the instinct would open a man who could not be opened any other way. The effect did not need a real lack of food. Roxanne was still fueling Victor. The body stayed strong. The mind was told it had gone several thousand years without feeding, and that the stomach was a bottomless pit. Training under [[Scathach]] to endure hunger did not cover it.[2]
He still read Victor wrong. Few beings, he said, escaped the brothers, and he did not expect the speed to matter once it was stopped. Victor's answer was that Lightning was one aspect, and that sealing it meant sealing thousands of other Powers as well. Famine's eyes went wide. He also could not make sense of a man laughing while all four of them were on him. War could. Famine called it crazy.[2]
The attachment to the brothers is not a performance. When the existence is eaten, War, Death and Pestilence scream for him and come back in hatred, not in a king's order.[2]
The incarnate does not talk like a man selling Hunger. He fights like someone who has stopped looking for the elegant answer. When [[Kaelith]]'s copies will not resolve into one body, he quits the search and hits every presence that seems real. When Death cannot break her compressed Night, Famine puts his blades on the same point until it fails. He is also the practical voice later. He is the one who reports that they cannot finish the gods for good. After [[Nerath]] drives War back with a flood she did not choose, his first comment is that the power is infuriating. Pestilence says the patron is protecting her more actively. Famine is the one who then explains it: promoting beings of her level is not easy, and the [[Vharzath|Great Darkness]] is trying to conserve his strength.[9][6]

### Synopsis

#### Demonic War
While [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] was still cutting through Hell, Demon rulers kept asking why [[Diablo]] had not yet sent the Horsemen.[10] Vepar, who had seen the top Pillars fight, put the same ceiling on the realm from the other side: past the Pillar ranks, the beings who might still fight Alucard were the Seven Deadly Sins and the Horsemen.[11]
They came after the conquest could no longer be ignored. Four horses. The Demons on Victor's side went tighter than they had against whole armies. Helena screamed for them to take wing and get clear, and [[Vine]] and Vepar drove the rest of the field off. Victor stayed where he was.[1]
Famine was the cloak, the plain spear, and the starved horse with blue fire in the eyes. He told Victor this was not the reaction he had expected. Victor asked what reaction a Horseman of the Apocalypse had come for. Fear, and a man in hiding.[1]
Victor's Power hit them hard enough that Famine had trouble breathing. War got up on will alone. The other three followed their older brother. Death drew the two scythes. Pestilence took a whip that looked able to part flesh. Famine took the Glaive. Victor dropped the pillar of Power, pointed, and told the Horsemen to dance.[1]
War met him first. Death's scythe missed the neck. Famine came in at a thunderous speed. Victor knew the weapon at once as a spear style. The basics sat close to his master's, even where the style itself did not.[2]
The Hunger arrived on the next clash. Overwhelming bloodlust, an insatiable hunger, as if he had not fed in several thousand years. The lapse was enough for Famine to press. He spun the Glaive, put a dark-blue Power over it, and asked if Alucard had ever been hungry. Enduring hunger under [[Scathach]] did not reach this. Victor felt like a man who had never eaten and was about to starve. He also knew the body was lying to him. The strength seemed to leave, and did not. Roxanne was still there. He was not hungry. It was a psychological attack on the most basic nature of a living Being.[2]
He laughed in the middle of it. Famine did not understand that. The four still fitted together, and the pressure stayed on him.[2]
Trying to leave the range of the Technique with Lightning, the Lightning vanished. The Power was still in him. He could not call it. Famine came through the gap, said few beings escaped the brothers, and drove the Glaive into the ground. The question of whether Victor could feel hunger at all went through his whole existence. A bottomless pit opened in the stomach. Pestilence's whip took the legs. War and Death struck together and thought they had him.[2]
They had not. Victor caught weapons, caught Famine's Glaive in his teeth, and turned their own momentum back on them. Pestilence lost an arm to the exchange and seated it again, slower than his regeneration should have allowed. Victor then looked at Famine and smiled. The Horseman winced. [[Junketsu]] went through the heart before he could answer. No armor. The blade went in easily.[2]
Victor spat the Glaive out and told him that stopping the speed changed nothing. Lightning was one aspect. Sealing a Power was not how this fight was won, because thousands of other Powers would have to be sealed with it. Famine's eyes opened. Victor's body went dark with crimson in it. He said he was hungry, and that it was Famine's fault. Half of him became the head of a Demonic Beast and ate the existence whole.[2]
War, Death and Pestilence screamed for their brother. Victor belched. The Hunger on him was gone. For a Horseman of Hunger, he said, the man had tasted really good.[2]
The Glaive was still on the ground. Vepar had already said what those weapons were, while the four were still alive: not even the Demon King touched them without permission. Each one held a small part of its Horseman's Soul, and the weapons were semi-sentient. The Power in the blade was that Soul's foremost nature. War's was Hellfire. Death's was dark Miasma. Famine's was Hunger. Pestilence's was Disease. Junketsu followed the meal. Branches became a mouth of sharp teeth, the Glaive was gone in less than two seconds, and the Odachi stood up again as that Glaive.[2]
From the edge of the field, the death was already being called permanent. Victor fought the other three with their brother's weapon, and Death knew the handling at once. That way of using a Glaive was theirs. Victor's answer was that he was a Progenitor, the one who set the worth of Souls. He had not done with Famine what he had done with Adonis. The existence was not worth that. An average value on the Soul had been enough to learn the whole martial art, and enough for this.[3]
He asked the three if they had ever been hungry. They tried to meet Famine's Authority with their own. They were late. Death went down first, a hand on his stomach, then War, then Pestilence.[3]
Death and Pestilence followed him. War did not. Victor left the last brother alive.[3][4]
What the meal had actually been was named afterward, inside Victor. The Authorities and the battle experience had come across, and the memories that belonged to that experience, without those existences being set at the highest value. The powers were a Minor Authority, remnants of a [[Demigod]]'s Divinity, an aspect of Horsemen who would have become Lesser Deities and did not, because they died. Remnants of that order only pushed the Divinity Victor already had. The memories were hundreds and thousands of years of nothing but fighting, and putting a value on those Souls to take the techniques and the Authority cost him. Roxanne felt the tax.[4]

#### Those Who Rule Reality
The Horseman stays dead. What comes back is not a soul climbing out of Hell, and it is not shown using Hunger on the gods. War, sent ahead into the Upper Sectors with [[Gol-goroth]], releases his power into the sword. A massive eye opens. Three figures come out of his body: Eldritch Famine incarnate, Eldritch Pestilence incarnate, and Eldritch Death incarnate. War himself is war personified. He tells [[Kaelith]] he was never alone. His brothers, and his Emperor, are with him. Each of the three is only slightly weaker than War. Alone, they can threaten solar systems. With War, the sword, and the Emperor behind that power, they are galactic threats. The meeting is six against two: the four brothers, the cosmic horror in the sword, and the Emperor, against [[Azrakar]] and Kaelith.[5]
War says "Together." The three answer with him. They charge the Goddess of the Night like coordinated clones, while War stays on Azrakar. She tries to hide inside her Concept. Death does not allow it. He throws an enormous sword of pure Miasma. She dodges and wastes a blast of Night. Then they reach her. Death slashes her face. Famine drives a knife of pure power into her stomach. Pestilence severs her limbs. Nothing in the sequence is wasted. She snarls. Pure Darkness pours out of her, the wounds close, the night darkens, and her power rises. She throws a wave and tells them to die. They dodge it and come again. To the Horsemen she is a child with too much power and too little command of the fundamentals. She is visibly less experienced than Azrakar.[8]
He is on her with Death while War is cutting the God of the Void apart. When she puts an illusion in Azrakar's place, that punishment is Death's. Famine's work is the copies. He goes forward. His blades pierce the images and force her to keep changing position, while Death's scythe cuts the lines she would use to get closer. It works until she twists their sense of distance. What they are looking at is no longer the ground. Famine strikes an image that is suddenly in front of him, and it is gone in time. She is in his blind spot, hits his abdomen, and tears him away from Death.[9]
She deepens the night. The images are no longer only something to look at. Each of them can hit, and the hits are real. Famine stops looking for the original. He starts destroying every presence that seems solid, and the whirling blades eat the room she has. Some images burst. Some stay. One of them pins his arm, and she uses it for a fierce blow to his face. Death is there before it finishes. The scythe goes through the image and into the real shoulder, and the projections fail.[9]
Famine gets free and ignores the copies that are still standing. He hits the dark places where the energy holding the illusions is gathered, and she has to spend herself putting the area back. She answers by compressing Night around her own body into a harder defense. Death hits that barrier and does not go through. Famine puts his blades on the same point. The protection gives. Death uses the hole. She gets out of the killing stroke and still takes a deep cut that stops her regeneration for a moment. The two of them do not give her the fight back.[9]
War sees that Pestilence cannot handle [[Nerath]] and [[Vhalor]] together, leaves the god in front of him, and calls the Horsemen. Famine answers with the others. Together they fight. Light takes them off the field and puts them at War's side. Four pillars of raw energy rise. Nearby stars are erased by the pressure, not by explosion. Gol-goroth opens its eye and isolates an Eldritch domain. The laws that were holding the space stop applying, and Eldritch beings have the advantage. Vhalor snarls that they are the abominable brood of Death. The three brothers become increasingly like Death: the face goes, the armor turns pitch-black, and dark wings devour the light. War gives them his power and synchronizes their senses with his own. They are late to save Azrakar. The four bend space and hit him together. Death paralyzes him with Miasma. Pestilence weakens him. Famine severs his arms and legs. War cuts off the head and pierces the heart. All four raise their hands, and Azrakar is erased.[9]
War does not let the three survivors form a line. Vhalor is first. The God of Shadows tries to scatter his body to get out of reach. Famine cuts that path off. His blades pierce the shadow realm and force Vhalor to pull the manifestation back together, and that pause is enough for War to arrive. A vertical blow of Gol-goroth breaks a shadow structure and tears the shoulder. Vhalor spreads his influence. The realm is already too weak to touch the knights. When he vanishes and comes back far away, Death is the one who has read the path and pierced the abdomen. They do not give him room after that. Famine is in front. War is at the side. A compact barrier and a wave buy Vhalor a few moments. Pestilence's corruption is already under the defense. Gol-goroth breaks it. Vhalor dodges the killing stroke, takes a deep wound in the upper body, and Death comes in to finish him. Kaelith and Nerath spend their sovereign only far enough to dull Gol-goroth, and that moment is enough to pull Vhalor out of Death's hands.[6]
Death, disappointed, goes to Kaelith. Her images no longer work on him. He breaks them by force, cuts her side, and makes her spend what she has on defense because the alien energy will not come out. Famine takes that opening. He is at her side, and he tears a huge hole in her body. She screams. Nerath cries her name. Kaelith tells her she is fine, and to stay on the fight.[6]
The three gods try to gather. Kaelith says the knights move as if cast from a single mold. Vhalor says they have to go after the incarnation of War or they die. It has already turned. War breaks through space with Gol-goroth and appears among them. Vhalor tries to meet him with shadows. Famine breaks that defense before it is complete. Death intercepts Kaelith when she tries to reach Vhalor. Pestilence turns his corruption onto the space around Nerath so she cannot act. The counteroffensive fails.[6]
War still reaches Vhalor, cuts through the last defense, and opens the flank when the god pulls back. Famine is the one behind him. There is no safe way out, and the back takes a huge wound. Famine reports that they cannot finish the gods for good. Death answers that the patron's energy is keeping them alive, that they have to act quickly, and that something is already approaching. That warning is Death's. The report that the kill will not stay is Famine's.[6]
Death destroys the last layer of Kaelith's illusions and opens her belly, nearly splitting her in two. She tries to retreat. Famine is already behind her and blocks the way. With nowhere to go she packs Darkness into a barrier. Death breaks it and throws her into deep space. The form tries to come back. The corruption is in too deep. Death aims the last stroke. Nerath tries to reach her. Pestilence hits Nerath first.[6]
Nerath says the Horsemen swap places and are not four fighters but one being. From where she stands she cannot tell War from Death from Famine. War is then in front of her with Gol-goroth raised, and Famine and Death place themselves so there is no way out. Her Silence dies before it reaches them. A combined attack pins her. Famine is one of the four in that pinning. He is not the one who seats the corruption in the wounds. That is Pestilence. The Great Darkness forces more power into Nerath than she can hold, and the Darkness that leaves her drives War back. Famine's comment on that burst is that it is an infuriating power. Pestilence says the patron is protecting her more actively now. Famine answers that promoting beings of her level is not easy, and that he is trying to conserve his strength. Pestilence says that does not make it any less infuriating.[6]
They have the three at the edge. None of them has a stable defense. The patron is what is still in the way. War raises Gol-goroth against Vhalor. Death aims the scythe to finish Kaelith. Famine is set to keep her from escaping. Pestilence is on the last of what is keeping Nerath conscious. The domain is hit from outside before any of those strokes land. Death growls that they were almost dead. The brothers put distance between themselves and the gods. [[Vaelthar]], Throne of Origin, comes through the gap.[6]
War shuts the domain down. Vaelthar restores Kaelith, Nerath, and Vhalor. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] arrives and kills Kaelith by striking her Soul. The body is erased. He holds the Soul. Famine's knife, his blades, and the hole in her are not the hand that ends her.[7]

### Equipment

#### Famine's Glaive
Famine's Glaive looked, at a distance, like an ordinary slashing spear. It was the weapon on his back, and it was the weapon he fought [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] with.
A small part of his Soul was in it. The weapon was semi-sentient, and the nature seated in the blade was Hunger. A dark-blue Power could be laid over it while that Hunger was pushed harder. Without his permission the weapon was hazardous to touch. Not even [[Diablo]] was described as willing to lay a hand on the Horsemen's weapons.[1][2]
He used it as a spear and as the mouth of the Authority. He spun it, he drove it into the ground, and the question in it went through Victor's existence. Victor caught the blade in his teeth before the end.[2]
After the body was gone, the Glaive stayed. [[Junketsu]] grew a mouth out of branches and ate it in less than two seconds, then stood as that Glaive. Death recognized his brother's art in the way Victor used it.[2][3]
The knife of pure power and the later blades belong to the incarnate. They are not that glaive. The glaive stayed eaten.[8][9]

#### Emaciated Horse
The horse looked unfed for years. Blue flames came from the eyes and swallowed the fierce glow of the face. The incarnate is not shown bringing it back, and [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] is not shown taking it and renaming it.[1]

### Combat Style
He was built to fight as one of four. Hunger took concentration. Pestilence took the body. Death took the life. War took the front.[2][3]
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] beat that pattern because losing one Power did not empty him. Stopping Lightning still left thousands of others, and eating Famine handed the Hunger to the man it had been used on.[2]
The incarnate keeps the support role and drops the method. He does not starve [[Kaelith]]. He opens her, he makes her copies expensive, he hits the energy holding them, and he breaks the point Death cannot enter. Against [[Vhalor]] he is the reason a scattered body has to become one body again, and the blade in the back once War has the front. Against [[Azrakar]] he is the limbs. He still does not take the head, and he still does not take Kaelith's death. His own reading of the fight, once the patron is involved, is that they cannot finish them for good.[9][6]

### Relationships

#### War
Older brother, and the one the other three followed when [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s Power had them struggling to stand. Famine's Hunger was the opening War hit. War screamed for him when he was eaten, and War is why an Eldritch Famine stands on a field again. The incarnate comes out of him through [[Gol-goroth]], answers "Together," answers the later call, and takes the limbs on [[Azrakar]] while War takes the head and the heart. On [[Vhalor]], the pause in the shadow realm is what lets War arrive, and Famine is in front of him and then behind him. The domain, the pillars, and the choice to shut the field when [[Vaelthar]] comes are War's.[1][2][5][9][6]

#### Death
Death was the kill to Famine's distraction. After the Soul was valued, Death was the first brother the Hunger put on the ground, and Death was the one who knew the Glaive art as theirs.[2][3]
As incarnates they are a pair on [[Kaelith]]. Famine forces the position, hits every copy, and breaks the point the scythe cannot enter. Death reads the real body, takes the shoulder through the image, and uses the hole. When an image pins Famine's arm and she is hitting his face, Death is the one who gets there. Famine is the one who says the gods cannot be finished. Death is the one who says the patron's energy is why, and that something is already coming.[9][6]

#### Pestilence
The other support. Hunger and disease were the two ways the formation took a fighter apart without matching him blow for blow. Pestilence's whip used the pit Famine opened. Pestilence roared when the brother was gone, and he died the same way.[2][3]
They are recreated together. Pestilence's war is [[Nerath]] and the corruption under [[Vhalor]]'s barrier. Famine's war is the blades on [[Kaelith]] and the shadow realm. They share the reading of the patron. Famine calls the burst infuriating and explains the conservation. Pestilence is the one who says the more active protection is no less infuriating, and the one who seats corruption in Nerath's wounds. Famine does not.[5][6]

#### Diablo
King and master. The four were his executioners, above the fear even Pillar Demons had of them. Famine died on that service. The incarnate is not shown going back to that court. He fights where War fights.[1][5]

#### Victor Elderblood
Famine came to see him hide. He got a smile, then a Power that made breathing hard, then a hunger [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] could feel and Roxanne could prove was false. Victor ate him, set an average worth on the Soul, took the spear art and the Hunger, and used both on the brothers before the Glaive had been in [[Junketsu]] for more than a few seconds.[1][2][3]
Victor is also why an incarnate of him can be sent into the Upper Sectors. War was spared, and [[Gol-goroth]] was given to War. When Victor arrives on that field, the goddess he erases is the one Famine had already opened and failed to finish.[5][7]

#### Junketsu
She ate the weapon after [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] ate the man. The Glaive's form became one of hers. The Hunger in the blade and the Hunger in the Soul did not stay in the same place. He kept the Authority. She kept the shape.[2]

#### Kaelith
His principal opponent after the recreation, shared with Death. The knife in the stomach is his and does not stay. The blades make her copies costly, and they make her spend power rebuilding the area. She still hits his abdomen, pins his arm, and strikes his face. The huge hole is his, taken while Death holds her on the side wound. He is behind her when the belly is already open, so the barrier she raises is the only door left, and Death breaks that door. At the execution he is the one set to stop her leaving. [[Vaelthar]] restores her. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] erases her.[8][9][6][7]

#### Vhalor
Not a long duel. Famine is the blades that pierce the shadow realm and force a scattered god to become one body, the pressure from the front while War is at the side, the break in a shadow defense that has not finished forming, and the huge wound in the back after [[Gol-goroth]] has already opened the flank. He does not take the abdomen. That is Death's scythe. He does not take the last stroke on [[Vhalor]]. That was War's sword, and it does not land.[6]

#### Azrakar
Famine's hands in that death are the arms and the legs. He does not paralyze him, weaken him, take the head, or pierce the heart.[9]

#### Nerath
He does not fight her the way he fights [[Kaelith]]. He is one of the two, with Death, who close her escape when War is in front of her, and he is one of the four in the pinning. The corruption in her wounds is Pestilence. His words about her are the report that they cannot be finished, and the explanation that a patron does not promote a being of her level cheaply.[6]

#### The Great Darkness
Not a master he serves. Famine is the one who says the three cannot be finished for good, calls the burst that drives War back an infuriating power, and then explains it as the patron conserving strength because promoting beings of her level is not easy. The voice that floods [[Nerath]] is that patron. The relief that arrives is [[Vaelthar]]. Death is the one who feels that relief coming.[6]

### Death
First of the four to die for good. The heart, then the head in [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s hand, then half of Victor's body as a Demonic Beast's head, and the existence gone in one bite. Hell's ordinary way back needed a Soul. That Soul had been eaten. The men watching already called it permanent. His brothers understood it before anyone explained it.[2][3]

### Return
What fights [[Kaelith]] is an Eldritch incarnate drawn out of War by [[Gol-goroth]], with Death and Pestilence, only slightly weaker than War. He has a knife of pure power, then blades. He pierces a shadow realm, severs a god's limbs, and tears a hole in a goddess without being the hand that ends her. None of that undoes the consumption in Hell, and none of it is shown giving him the Hunger back. That Authority stayed with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]. The glaive stayed with [[Junketsu]].[2][5][7]

### Legacy
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] used the Hunger on the three survivors in the same battle, after pricing the Soul at an average value and taking the martial art with it. The memories of the technique and the Authority cost his own Soul something. Roxanne felt that cost. The power itself was a remnant, not a full Divinity, and it only pushed what he already was.[3][4]
[[Junketsu]] took the Glaive and its shape.[2]
The incarnate is a different remainder. The role can be put back on a field without the Soul, and the role on that field is the blade in the stomach, the cut through a shadow realm, and the man who says the patron will not let the kill stay. [[Kaelith]] still dies by Victor's hand.[5][6][7]
