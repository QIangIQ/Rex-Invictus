No Being, whether Mortal or God, can escape disease.
—Pestilence to [[Victor Elderblood]]
Pestilence was one of the Four Horsemen of the Apocalypse, the corrupted knight who represented disease among [[Diablo]]'s executioners. With War, Death, and Famine, he belonged to a class Pillar-ranked Demons feared. His place in the formation was support. Disease did not need to win the first exchange. On an ordinary opponent it accumulated, multiplied into disabilities, and became more dangerous the longer the fight lasted. Victor Elderblood later judged him the most dangerous of the four for that reason, and still killed him before the accumulation could finish, because Roxanne's pure Energy kept the poison off his body.[1][2]
The original died in [[Biblical Hell]]. [[Junketsu]] pierced his heart, Absolute Zero froze him into an ice statue covered in blood, and Victor consumed that blood. Junketsu ate the whip. An average value on the Soul, not the value used on Adonis, was enough to take the Authority, the battle experience, and the memories tied to it. What came across was a Minor Authority, the remnant of a [[Demigod]]'s Divinity.[2][3] The fighter who later stands with War is an Eldritch incarnate drawn out of War through [[Gol-goroth]], together with Famine and Death. He is not that consumed Soul returning. On that field he severs [[Kaelith]]'s limbs, weakens [[Azrakar]], and spends the rest of the battle pushing [[Nerath]] to the edge of annihilation. [[Vaelthar]] restores her before the last stroke. Victor's kill, when he arrives, is Kaelith.[4][5][6]

### Physical Appearance
Pestilence appeared as a corrupted knight, as though he had come out of a foreboding depth of Hell, and he was marked as the representation of disease among the four. He rode a pale horse full of worms and covered in white flames. The white fire belongs to the horse. He himself is not described as wreathed in it. His weapon was a whip that looked able to cut flesh easily. When he spun it, the wind off the whip took on a dark-orange hue.[1][7]
The incarnate keeps a knight's shape until [[Gol-goroth]]'s domain wakes what the three brothers had left dormant. Their faces disappear, the armor turns pitch-black, and wings of dark energy grow from their backs and devour the light, because they become increasingly like Death. War already carried a darker form of his own and had kept this side of his power hidden until the fight was turning. The change in the three is his doing. The domain is the sword's.[8]

### Personality
[[Diablo]] trusted him with work that was not a duel. Once the alliance of Humans and Angels was already strained, he told War to contact Pestilence, asked for doppelgangers that would quietly harm that relationship, and left the method to him, calling him an expert in the matter. That is political judgment, not a scene of Pestilence carrying the plan out.[9]
In battle he was certain of the Authority. No Mortal and no God, he said, escapes disease. The certainty did not leave him careless. After [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s cut took an arm, Death told him to stop playing, and Pestilence answered that he had not been messing around. The arm went back on, the regeneration was late, and he recognized anti-Soul properties in the weapon. He was serious after that. The same attachment shows when the brothers die. Famine's consumption and then Death's left him shivering and swearing that Victor would pay, and he stayed in the fight with War rather than breaking.[7][2]
The incarnate keeps that patience. When Silence fades a wide spread, he stops using the wide spread. He packs the corruption, infects single pieces, and does not give a deformed body time to finish restoring itself. His irritability is narrow and practical. After [[Nerath]]'s patron drives War back, he is the one who says the patron is protecting her more actively, and the one who answers Famine's explanation of that conservation by saying it does not make the power any less infuriating.[8][5]

### Synopsis

#### Demonic War
The alliance between Humans and Angels had cracked before Pestilence was called. Humans had struck one of the seven Virtues, the [[Heavenly Father]] had used [[Diablo]]'s own scheme to clean his house, and Diablo wanted the breach kept open. His order to War was specific. Contact Pestilence. Use doppelgangers to harm the relationship subtly. Leave the method to him, because that work was his expertise. The page gives the order and does not show the operation being carried out.[9]
He next appears with the other three Horsemen, sent against [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] in [[Biblical Hell]]. Four riders were enough to tighten Victor's own Demons more than whole armies had, and Helena cleared them off the field. Pestilence was the corrupted knight on the worm-filled pale horse, whip in hand. Victor's released Power left Famine struggling to breathe until War forced himself up, and the younger three followed. Death took his scythes, Pestilence his whip, Famine his Glaive.[1]
The opening exchange cut all four. Victor's lightning-assisted strike left deep wounds, and Pestilence lost an arm. Death told him to stop playing around. He put the arm back, felt the regeneration take longer than it should have, and understood that [[Junketsu]] harmed the Soul. From that point he fought without the earlier ease. He spun the whip until its wind turned dark orange and named the Technique Disease Field. Worms rose out of the ground, the air turned toxic, and the battlefield became an area of disease. A slash through the air left a whip-mark on Victor's armor while Pestilence declared that neither Mortals nor Gods could escape disease. Victor did not yet know what the Technique did and treated standing inside it as foolish, so he tried to leave on Lightning. The Lightning vanished at the boundary. He could still feel the Power. He could not use it.[7]
Famine's Hunger then opened a bottomless pit in Victor's stomach, and Pestilence used the opening. The whip wrapped Victor's legs and yanked him to his knees so War and Death could strike together. Victor still broke the hold. He caught their weapons, said he had learned the four styles, hauled Pestilence in by his own whip, and struck him in the face. Vepar's explanation of those weapons covers the whip as well as the others. Each holds a small part of its Horseman's Soul, the weapons are semi-sentient, and the nature in Pestilence's is Disease. Not even the Demon King touched them without permission.[7]
Famine died first, eaten after Junketsu took his heart, and Victor turned that Hunger and that spear art back on the three who remained. They tried to meet the Authority with their own and were late. Death fell with a hand on his stomach, then War, then Pestilence. Victor threw Junketsu through Death and consumed him, and Junketsu ate the scythes. Pestilence shivered at the death, roared that Victor would pay, and was told not to cry, because he would join his brother soon. Victor's line as he closed was that the weak had no right to interfere where the strong were fighting. He appeared in front of Pestilence, pierced the heart, and spoke Absolute Zero. The Horseman became an ice sculpture, the ice covered itself in blood, and that blood rushed into Victor's mouth. Junketsu ate the whip in the same way she had eaten the Glaive and the scythes.[7][2]
What Victor understood from the consumed Authority was why the brothers had been confident, and why that confidence had been early. Through disease, Pestilence could harm someone by using something he knew. The effect was poisonous and killed the body slowly. A second effect was already being negated by Roxanne's pure Energy, which is why Victor felt nothing from it. Famine and Pestilence were the support. Death and War were the ones who struck. Hunger drained strength, and illness incapacitated, so together they attacked the basic condition of a Being. In a fight of attrition against someone ordinary, Pestilence would have won, because the disease accumulated and multiplied into disabilities. Without Roxanne keeping the body at its best, Victor's own judgment was that the fight would have become dangerous. They had also underestimated him. Speed was not his main power.[2]
The three Souls were not given the highest value, the one used on Adonis. The Authorities, the battle experience, and the memories belonging to that experience still crossed, and Roxanne felt the tax on Victor's Soul. The powers were a Minor Authority, remnants of a [[Demigod]]'s Divinity, and they only pushed a Divinity he already had. War alone remained, ascended during the fight, and was beaten and spared. After War's horse had carried him off, Victor turned Junketsu into a whip because that shape was easier to carry.[3][10]

#### Those Who Rule Reality
Pestilence stays dead until War, fighting in the Upper Sectors with [[Gol-goroth]], releases his power into the sword. An eye opens, and three figures come out of War's body: Eldritch incarnates of Famine, Pestilence, and Death, with War himself as war personified. Each of the three is only slightly weaker than War. Alone they can threaten solar systems. With War, the sword, and the Emperor behind them, they are galactic threats, and the meeting is six against two rather than a duel. These are new fighters wearing the old roles. They are not the Souls [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] ate.[4]
While War stayed on [[Azrakar]], the three charged [[Kaelith]] together. She tried to hide inside her Concept. Death stopped that disappearance with a thrown sword of pure Miasma, and the blast of Night she answered with was wasted. They reached her in one rush. Death cut her face, Famine drove a knife of pure power into her stomach, and Pestilence severed her limbs. Darkness poured out of her and closed every wound, the night deepened, and her power rose, but the wave she threw missed. To the Horsemen she was a child with too much power and too little command of the fundamentals.[11]
Once [[Nerath]] and [[Vhalor]] joined the field, Pestilence was the one left against two Chief Gods. Nerath's Silence did not need a parry. Her presence cut vibrations and interrupted the propagation of energy, so his blows faded when they reached her zone, and corruption spread across the battlefield died on arrival. She touched his chest before a defense was set and briefly cut his powers off, then struck his face and abdomen. He answered by packing all of the corruption into a small part of his body and discharging it at once. The shock broke part of the zone and forced her arms back. He seized a floating structure near her torso and let corruption eat across it, and she detached that piece and threw it away before the infection reached the rest of her. From then on he stopped trying to flood the whole body. Small doses on separate points forced her to isolate or discard each one, while she expanded Silence and began cutting his techniques off at the moment they started. He took several hits and his control weakened, but he still built a charge just beneath her defensive layer and, when she moved to attack, discharged it into her center. Part of her body lost its shape. She regenerated it.[8]
Vhalor turned that exchange into a net. Every attack on Nerath gave him an opening, he pulled her clear, and she struck Pestilence so his blows could not land at full force. Silence covered the fight again. Corruption sent through her territory arrived too weak, so Pestilence gathered it in his own body and charged. Shadow wrapped his legs and hauled him back. Vhalor's pointed fingers came in beside him. Pestilence turned part of the blow, Nerath knocked him away, and more extensions seized his arms. A wave of corruption destroyed those first restraints, but Vhalor had already left them. Black hands struck in succession. One took the shoulder and another the back. Nerath's touch cut his powers off again, the Shadows closed, and parts of his body began to dissolve. He concentrated power at the points of contact and sent corruption back along the extensions until Vhalor had to dissolve part of his own manifestation. The two gods tightened the net anyway. War, seeing that his brother could not hold both, left his own opponent and called the Horsemen. The three answered together, vanished in light, and appeared at his side. Four pillars of raw energy rose, and nearby stars were erased by the pressure itself, not by an explosion and not by a plague.[8]
Gol-goroth then isolated an Eldritch domain in which the previous laws stopped and Eldritch beings held the advantage. The Chief Gods' hold on the space ended with that change. Vhalor's shadows did nothing there, and Azrakar's void did nothing there. The three brothers took on Death's likeness, War gave them his power and synchronized their senses with his own, and the four struck Azrakar together before the others could pull him clear. Death paralyzed him with Miasma, Pestilence weakened him, Famine took the arms and legs, and War cut off the head and pierced the heart. All four then erased him. The Great Darkness, displeased, ordered the Horsemen brought in alive or dead.[8]
War did not let the survivors form a new line. Pestilence had already seeded corruption across the region, and some of it reached the structures holding Vhalor's barrier, so the defense lost stability and Gol-goroth broke through. Vhalor dodged the killing stroke and still took a deep wound. Kaelith and Nerath could only spend their sovereign far enough to dull the sword and drag him out of Death's reach. Pestilence's fight with Nerath continued under worse conditions for her. Silence still interrupted some of his manifestations, but the corruption was already seated at many points, and whenever she removed one concentration another appeared elsewhere, splitting her control. He used that split to advance, carried the corruption inside his body, and crossed the Silence before it could suppress him. She struck his chest and broke the technique for a moment. He used that contact to infect one of the floating structures of her form. She severed it at once, and the severance itself was the gap he fired through. The discharge deformed her upper body and forced her to stop and reassemble. He did not give her the time. He advanced again, spread more concentrations, and closed the routes she could escape by.[5]
She tried to wipe every manifestation at once. The pressure eased in places. It did not clear the corruption already inside her body. His remaining power, gathered to a single point and released as she attacked, went through her defense and threw her back. When she expanded her domain to drive the knights off Kaelith, Gol-goroth's influence resisted her laws. War then broke into the middle of the three gods, and Pestilence turned his corruption onto the space around Nerath so that she could not act while the others were separated. Famine is the one who reported that the gods could not be finished for good. Death is the one who named the patron's energy as the reason, and who felt something approaching. Pestilence's own comment comes later.[5]
Nerath tried to reach Kaelith and was struck first. Corruption spread over the damaged upper body and forced her to spend everything on containing it. From where she stood, the Horsemen were swapping places and no longer read as four fighters. A combined attack pinned her, pierced the body, and took its stability. Pestilence let the corruption concentrate in the wounds so she could not take the body back, and she was at the edge of annihilation when the Great Darkness forced in more power than she could hold. What left her drove War back. Pestilence called that a more active protection. Famine explained it as a patron conserving strength, because promoting a being of her level is not easy. Pestilence answered that the explanation did not make it less infuriating. She could barely stand. He gathered the corruption on her specifically, ready to destroy the last foundation still keeping her conscious, while War raised Gol-goroth against Vhalor, Death aimed at Kaelith, and Famine was set to stop Kaelith from escaping.[5]
None of those strokes landed. A blow from outside interrupted the knights, Gol-goroth held through one wave and began to fail on the second, and [[Vaelthar]], Throne of Origin, came through with the other Chief Gods. War shut the domain down. Vaelthar restored Kaelith, Nerath, and Vhalor. Victor then arrived and killed Kaelith by striking her Soul. The body was erased, and he held the Soul. Pestilence is still among the Horsemen at that point, and the page does not show him acting in the standoff that follows.[5][6]

### Skills & Abilities
His Hell Authority is disease that gets worse with time, delivered through a whip and through a named field. The incarnate is not shown reusing Disease Field. What he uses on the gods is corruption, changed to fit whatever is suppressing the spread.
- Demon Physiology: An executioner of [[Diablo]]'s, of the class Pillar Demons feared. He took a lightning-assisted cut that removed an arm, put the arm back, and kept fighting. The regeneration of that wound was real and late, because [[Junketsu]] harms the Soul. That is not a regeneration that ignores Soul damage.[1][7]
- Partial Deflection: Against [[Vhalor]] he turned part of a strike made with pointed fingers. [[Nerath]] still knocked him away, and the Shadows still closed on him afterward.[8]
- Demonic Return, Then None: Ordinary demonic death left a way back through Hell if the Soul remained. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] consumed this Soul. The later body is War's incarnate, not that return.[2][4]
- Pestilence Manipulation: Authority over disease. Victor, after taking it, described the working as simple and, against a normal opponent, decisive in a long fight.[2]
- Knowledge-Tied Harm: Through disease he can harm a target by using something that target knows.[2]
- Slow Poison: The shown effect is poisonous and kills the body slowly.[2]
- Unnamed Second Effect: The Authority had a second effect beyond that poison. On Victor it was already being cancelled by Roxanne's pure Energy, which is why he felt nothing. The page does not name the second effect.[2]
- Disability Multiplication: As time passes, the disease accumulates and multiplies into various disabilities. In an attrition fight against someone ordinary, Victor judged that Pestilence would win. Without Roxanne, his own fight would have become dangerous.[2]
- Minor Authority: Classed, with the other consumed Horsemen, as a Minor Authority and the remnant of a [[Demigod]]'s Divinity. An average value on the Soul carried the Authority, the battle experience, and the related memories. It was not the highest value, and taking it taxed Victor's Soul.[2][3]
- Disease Field: Named by him. Spinning the whip turns its wind dark orange, worms come out of the ground, and the air becomes toxic until the battlefield is an area of disease.[7]
- Remote Whip-Mark: A slash through the air left a whip-mark on Victor's armor at the moment he declared that no Mortal or God escapes disease.[7]
- Dark-Orange Channeling: The Power on the whip shows as a dark-orange hue in the wind it raises.[7]
- Boundary Suppression: Victor could still feel his Lightning inside the field and could not use it when he tried to leave the Technique's range. This is not shown as a general nullification, and the incarnate is not shown using it on the gods.[7]
- Supernatural Whip: Cut, bound, and carried the Disease. He wrapped it around Victor's legs and yanked him to his knees once Hunger had opened a gap. A small part of his Soul sat in the weapon. Junketsu ate it. The later whip-shape of Junketsu is chosen because it is easier to carry, not because the sword is shown casting Disease Field.[1][7][2][10]
- Limb Severing: His part of the first coordinated rush on [[Kaelith]] was her limbs. Death took the face and Famine the stomach. Darkness closed all three wounds.[11]
- Corruption, Packed: Wide corruption fades when it enters an intact Silence, and his own blows fade there as well. He answers by putting all of it into a small part of his body, or carrying it inside his body, and releasing it before the suppression can finish. One such shock broke part of Nerath's zone. Another, built just beneath her defensive layer, discharged into her center and briefly took the shape out of part of her body. A later single-point burst threw her back.[8][5]
- Sub-Layer Charge: Even while she was cutting techniques off at their onset and his control was weakening, he built energy just under the defensive layer and fired it into the center as she attacked.[8]
- Zone Crossing: When a spread arrived too weak, he concentrated the power in himself and charged through the Silence before his powers were suppressed. He did the same inside the domain.[8][5]
- Corruption, Placed: When flooding the whole body failed, he set small doses on separate points so Nerath had to isolate or discard each infected piece.[8]
- Contact Infection: A blow she landed on his chest became the contact he used to infect one of the floating structures of her form.[5]
- Severance Gap: She cut that structure away to stop the spread. He fired the concentrated discharge through the gap the cut had opened, and the hit deformed her upper body.[5]
- Replacement Contamination: Corruption already seated at many points did not end when she removed one. Another concentration appeared elsewhere, and she had to split control of the domain to chase them.[5]
- Persistent Internal Corruption: Wiping manifestations with Silence eased the pressure in places and did not clear corruption that had already reached her body.[5]
- Wound Saturation: On the pinning, he let the corruption gather in the wounds so she could not take the body back. At the execution he gathered it on her again, aimed at the last foundation still keeping her conscious. [[Vaelthar]] arrives before that stroke.[5]
- Corruption, Returned and Seeded: A wave of corruption destroyed the first Shadow restraints on his arms, without catching Vhalor, who had already left them. When the Shadows began dissolving his body, he drove corruption back along those extensions and forced Vhalor to shed part of his own manifestation. Separately, concentrations spread across the region ahead of time reached the structures holding Vhalor's barrier and took its stability out from under [[Gol-goroth]]. Pestilence did not swing that sword.[8][5]
- Spatial Lock: When War appeared among the three gods, Pestilence turned the corruption onto the space around Nerath so she could not act.[5]
- Escape Restriction: After the deformation, he spread further concentrations and closed the routes she could use to get away, and he did not let her finish reassembling.[5]
- Political Work: Diablo called him an expert at harming relations and left him the method of a doppelganger campaign against Humans and Angels. The campaign is ordered, not shown.[9]
- Apocalyptic Mount: A pale horse, full of worms, covered in white flames. The incarnate is not shown calling it back.[1]
- Eldritch Incarnation: Drawn out of War through Gol-goroth with Famine and Death. Slightly weaker than War alone, a solar-system threat by himself, and a galactic threat with War, the sword, and the Emperor. Inside the domain he shares the change toward Death's likeness. Place-swapping, shared senses, the light that moves the three to War's side, and the four pillars are the group's, under War and the sword. They are not teleports or plagues he is shown using alone. The domain is Gol-goroth's.[4][8][5]
- Coordinated Combat: Support beside Famine in Hell, the limbs on Kaelith, the weakening on [[Azrakar]], and the hold on Nerath while the others split the remaining gods. He answers War's call with the others.[2][11][8]

### Equipment

#### Pestilence's Whip
The whip looked able to part flesh, took on a dark-orange hue when spun, bound [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s legs, and was the instrument of Disease Field. Like the other Horsemen's weapons, it held a small part of its owner's Soul and embodied his foremost Power, which Vepar named as Disease. [[Junketsu]] devoured it after the ice statue was consumed. Victor later gave Junketsu the whip's shape because a whip was easier to carry.[7][2][10]

#### Pale Horse
He arrived on a pale horse infested with worms and covered in white flames. Nothing in the Upper Sector fight shows that mount.[1]

### Relationships

#### Diablo
[[Diablo]] was his king, and the one who knew him as the Horseman to call when the work was a quiet worsening of someone else's alliance rather than a charge. The doppelganger order went through War. Pestilence later died on the other kind of order, the one that sent all four into Hell after [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]. The incarnate fights where War fights, not back at Diablo's court.[9][2][4]

#### War
War is the older brother and the direct half of the formation Pestilence supported. After Famine and Death were gone, the two of them were what remained, and [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] still refused to eat War. The incarnate exists because that refusal left War alive to receive [[Gol-goroth]]. When Pestilence cannot hold [[Nerath]] and [[Vhalor]] together, War leaves his own opponent and calls the Horsemen, and Pestilence answers. On [[Azrakar]], the weakening is Pestilence's part of a kill whose head and heart belong to War.[2][10][4][8]

#### Famine
Famine was the other support. His Hunger opened the moment the whip used, and they died in the same battle. They are drawn back out of War together. On [[Kaelith]], Famine's knife and Pestilence's severing are two parts of one rush. Later, Famine is the one who says the gods cannot be finished and who explains the patron's conservation. Pestilence's line beside that explanation is that the more active protection is no less infuriating.[7][11][5]

#### Death
Death told him to get serious, and Death was the kill paired with Pestilence's disease. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] ate Death in front of him, and Pestilence's answer was rage rather than withdrawal. As incarnates, Death is the one who names the patron's energy when Famine reports that the kills will not stick. The likeness the three take inside the domain is Death's, shared, not a form Pestilence puts on by himself.[7][2][8][5]

#### Victor Elderblood
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] is the man Disease Field could not keep inside its boundary, the man Roxanne kept clear of the poison, and the man who ended the attrition with Absolute Zero before it could finish. He priced the Soul at an average value, kept the Authority, and left the whip's shape with [[Junketsu]]. He is also the reason an incarnate of Pestilence can be sent out at all, because he spared War and armed him. When Victor reaches the Upper Sector field, the god he erases is [[Kaelith]].[7][2][10][6]

#### Nerath
[[Nerath]] is his real opponent after the recreation. Her Silence fades his spread, cuts his powers off by touch, and forces him off the wide attack. He answers with packed discharges, infection of single pieces of her body, a charge carried through the zone, corruption that reappears when she clears a point, and corruption seated in the wounds of the pinning. He has her at the edge of annihilation, and at the execution he is aimed at the last of what is keeping her conscious. [[Vaelthar]] restores her. The Darkness that drives War off her belongs to her patron.[8][5][6]

#### Vhalor
[[Vhalor]] is the second half of the net, not a separate duel. He restrains Pestilence, strikes from the side, and starts dissolving the body where the Shadows close. Pestilence turns part of one blow, breaks the first arm-restraints, and sends corruption back along the extensions until Vhalor has to shed them. The corruption already seeded across the field is what later takes the stability out of Vhalor's barrier. War and [[Gol-goroth]] do the cutting.[8][5]

#### Kaelith
His part of her first clash is the severed limbs, closed again by her Darkness in the same moment she closes the face and the stomach. After that he is on [[Nerath]]. He hits Nerath when she tries to reach [[Kaelith]]. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] kills Kaelith after [[Vaelthar]] has restored her.[11][5][6]

#### Azrakar
Pestilence weakened him inside the domain. Death paralyzed him, Famine took the limbs, and War took the head and the heart. The erasure that followed was all four.[8]

#### The Great Darkness
The displeasure at [[Azrakar]]'s death, the order to bring the Horsemen in, and the power forced into [[Nerath]] belong to that patron. Pestilence recognizes the later burst as more active protection and refuses to treat Famine's explanation as comfort. He is not the one who feels [[Vaelthar]] coming. That is Death.[8][5]

### Death
Pestilence died third, after Famine and Death. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] appeared in front of him with the line that the weak had no right to interfere where the strong fought, pierced his heart with [[Junketsu]], and froze him with Absolute Zero. The ice statue covered itself in blood, Victor consumed the blood, and Junketsu devoured the whip. A return through Hell needed a Soul. That Soul had been eaten and priced. The incarnate is a role put back on a field, not that Soul walking out of Victor.[2][3][4]

### Return
[[Gol-goroth]] draws him out of War with the other two, only slightly weaker than War, carrying corruption rather than the old Disease Field. He severs a goddess's limbs, weakens a Primordial God, and drives [[Nerath]] to the point where only her patron and [[Vaelthar]]'s arrival keep her alive. None of that restores the consumed Soul, and none of it is shown handing the Authority back. The Authority stayed with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]. The whip stayed with [[Junketsu]].[2][4][6]

### Legacy
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] kept the disease Authority and the battle memories that came with the Soul, at a cost Roxanne could feel. [[Junketsu]] kept the weapon, and later the whip's shape. The incarnate is the other remainder: proof that the role can fight without the Soul, and that the role on that field is the limbs in the first rush, the corruption [[Nerath]] cannot simply fade, and the stroke aimed at the last of her consciousness. She lives because [[Vaelthar]] restores her. [[Kaelith]] dies by Victor's hand.[2][3][10][5][6]
