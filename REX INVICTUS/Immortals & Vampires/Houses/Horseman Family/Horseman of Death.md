Death was one of the Four Horsemen of the Apocalypse and one of [[Diablo]]'s executioners, paired with War as a direct fighter while Famine and Pestilence supported them. The four belonged to a class that Pillar Demons feared. His weapons were two black scythes that could be joined into one, coated in Dark Miasma he could lengthen and shorten past the metal, and each weapon held part of his Soul. Vepar named the power in them as the Dark Miasma of Death.[1][2]
He and War were the pair sent against Alexander. Death took both of the Pope's arms. War took the heart. Afterward Diablo gave Death his own order, to have [[Asmodeus]] make another weapon of corruption from the dead of Helheim, and Death accepted it while saying the materials were scarce.[3][4] In [[Biblical Hell]] he died second. Hunger put him down first of the three who were left, [[Junketsu]] pierced his heart, and he was gone before War and Pestilence broke the ice. Victor said he now had the Authority of Death, Junketsu ate the scythes and took their shape, and the unnamed horse was called Despair because that is what beings feel when they are about to die.[5]
He does not come back by the ordinary return of a Demon. An Eldritch incarnate of Death is later drawn out of War through [[Gol-goroth]], with Famine and Pestilence. That fighter will not let [[Kaelith]] hide inside Night, stops chasing her copies once he has seen how they move, paralyzes [[Azrakar]], and has a scythe of pure Miasma aimed at her when the domain breaks. He does not land the stroke. Victor is the one who later erases Kaelith. The incarnate is not treated here as the Soul eaten in Hell walking back out intact. Despair stays Victor's.[6][7][8][9]

### Physical Appearance
Death appeared as a knight in black armor, with a hood that hid his face in supernatural darkness. War could be seen once the helmet was off. Death could not. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] was sure he had seen a smug smile under that hood after the cheek cut, and he also knew the hood was only absolute darkness. The armor was Horseman equipment. War called it the work of the best blacksmiths in Hell, and it stopped most of the damage from the strike that still cut all four of them and took Pestilence's arm. The self-repair Victor watches in that fight, fed by the Miasma of Hell, is shown on War's armor.[10][3][2]
He carried two black scythes, used apart or joined into one much larger scythe, and he rode in with them connected on his back. The horse was large, black, and demonic, with green flames at the eyes, hooves, and tail, and he called it up out of shadow by a nod. It had no name until Victor claimed it. The incarnate keeps a knight's shape until [[Gol-goroth]]'s domain wakes what the brothers had left dormant. The three then become increasingly like him: the face disappears, the armor turns pitch-black, and wings of dark energy eat the light around them. War already had a darker form of his own. The scythe of pure Miasma used on [[Vhalor]] and [[Kaelith]] is a weapon of that later fight, not the pair [[Junketsu]] ate.[3][1][5][11][7]

### Personality
In the field he deferred to War without being silent. At the Vatican, once the [[Heavenly Father]] had pulled Alexander's strength and three Seraphim were ready to fight, he came to War's side and asked what they should do, then left when War ordered the withdrawal so [[Diablo]] could be told. He shared the irritation at having been used to clean the Inquisition, and he shared the anger when Ariel said they would not be able to run next time. Both of them controlled it and went. In Hell it is War who speaks first. Death's own voice is the disbelief under [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s pressure, and the roar at Pestilence to stop playing and get serious.[3][1][2]
He took orders from Diablo directly as well as through War. The mission against Alexander was given to War with Death attached to it. The next order, contacting [[Asmodeus]] for another weapon of corruption, was his, and he accepted it while pointing out that the materials were scarce. Loss moved him the same way it moved the others. After Famine was eaten, the three who remained attacked with the fury of someone who had lost a loved one, and Death was the one who recognized their brother's Glaive in Victor's hands and demanded to know what had been done.[10][4][5]
The incarnate has less patience for a trick once he has named it. Piercing a body that turns out to be another image gets one inward line, that damned ability, and after that he stops chasing every copy. The scythe stays close, he watches which movements do not match, and he waits until the real [[Kaelith]] has to spend herself to hold the area. Losing [[Vhalor]] disappoints him, and he goes to her rather than arguing with the rescue. He is also the one who says the patron's energy is what keeps the three alive, that they have to act quickly, and that something is already approaching because he can feel it. When the blow from outside stops the execution, the reaction is irritation. They were almost dead.[11][7]

### Synopsis

#### Demonic War
Death is first named after [[Sitri|Duke Sitri]]'s death, when War reports the failure of the Demon Dukes against the Vampires. [[Diablo]] changes the plan. The march on the Vatican is pulled back, the [[Aztec Pantheon]]'s demonic troops are recalled to Hell's defense, [[Asmodeus]]'s factories are to turn captured Supernaturals and Lesser Gods into more Demons, and Sitri is bait for the Angels. War is told to take his brother Death, and Diablo's mount if he thinks it necessary, and to make the deaths of Alexander and the Inquisition's Generals permanent. War accepts. The Dragon that comes to mind when the mount is mentioned is Diablo's, not Death's horse.[10]
He next appears at the Vatican, lying in wait with War, when [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s pressure forces both of them to stop and look. Victor, recognized in that moment as the second progenitor, is there because a traitor in the Inquisition has put a demon-metal bullet into Ariel. From a distance he watches the two Horsemen on the golden-haired man in paladin armor, the Pope, while the three Seraphim do not intervene. Death cuts off both arms with the two scythes. War puts the greatsword through the heart. The [[Heavenly Father]] then withdraws Alexander's strength, ages him, and refuses him Heaven. A door opens, and thousands of hands from the abyss tear the Soul out. Ariel announces the rest of the punishment: the Inquisition loses its free will and autonomy, it will be investigated, and the Souls responsible, Alexander's among them, go to Limbo.[12][3]
War understands that the Demons were used to remove the most influential man in the Inquisition so the house could be cleaned. Death shares the irritation. With the Seraphim now willing to fight them to the death, he asks what they should do, and War pulls them back so the King can be told. War's red horse rises out of the ground. Death nods, and shadows leave the ground as a black horse with green fire at the eyes, hooves, and tail. Their parting line is that they will see the Angels on another battlefield. Ariel's answer, that next time they will not be able to run, puts anger in both their eyes, and they still leave.[3]
The report is given on the outskirts of ancient China, with Lilith and [[Baal]] present. War calls the plan a success and Alexander dead, and says they were used. Diablo's answer splits the work. War is to contact Pestilence and harm the relations between Humans and Angels without noise. Death is to contact Asmodeus and have the spirits of Helheim's dead made into another weapon of corruption. He takes the order and notes that the materials are scarce.[4]
He is not seen again until the four ride against Victor in Hell, after several Pillar territories have already fallen. Helena, [[Vine]], and Vepar know them on sight and go tense. Death is the black-armored rider on the green-flamed horse, the two scythes already joined on his back. War greets Victor as Alucard. The pillar of red Power drops all four from their horses and makes kneeling a fight. Death's word for it is that it is impossible. War forces himself up anyway, and the other three follow. Death draws the two scythes apart.[1]
War leaps first. Victor leans back, and a scythe covered in Dark Miasma misses the neck by the width of the dodge. The attack was stealthy. The kick throws Death off, and the same motion gives Victor the leverage to push War away. A lightning-fast iai then cuts all four deeply. Pestilence loses an arm. War's armor and Death's armor stop most of the rest, which Victor remarks on, and War answers that armor from Hell's best smiths is not breached so easily. Once the fight is in the air, Death roars at Pestilence to get serious, joins the scythes into one, and flies in while War presses from the other side.[2]
Victor parries War, puts a compressed sphere of Blood through the armor without reaching flesh, and watches that armor rebuild itself on the Miasma of Hell. He turns into Death's swing, slows his own time, and punches the Horseman into a cobweb crater. The blade was avoided. The cheek is still cut. Under the hood he is sure he sees the smugness, and he is also sure the hood is only darkness. The reading he makes is that the Miasma on the scythe can be extended and shortened, and that the control of that Energy is incredible. He appears behind Death, cuts horizontally, and Death retreats in time for the metal. Victor extends his own blade and opens a gigantic gash, with the line that he can do that too.[2]
The four then fight as one set. Famine opens a bottomless hunger, Pestilence's whip takes the legs, and War and Death come in from either side already thinking the hit has landed. Victor catches the greatsword and the giant scythe in hands of ice, slams their heads together, kicks Death in the face, and says he has learned the four styles. He pierces Famine's heart and eats him. [[Junketsu]] eats the Glaive and becomes one. The three who remain come back with the fury of a lost brother. Victor dodges and answers all of it. When the Glaive knocks Death back, Death is the one who says that way of using it is their brother's, and demands to know what was done.[2][5]
Victor's answer is the Progenitor's pricing of Souls. He did not do with Famine what he had done with Adonis. An average value was enough to take all of that martial art, and the Authority with it, and he asks the Horsemen whether they have ever been hungry. The three try to meet Famine's Authority with their own and are late. Death goes down first, a hand on his stomach, then War, then Pestilence. Junketsu, still a Glaive, is thrown through Death's heart. Victor is holding him in the next moment, and asks whether Death can be afraid of his own death. The body shakes as that end arrives. War and Pestilence charge into a wall of ice. War breaks it. They are late. Death is gone, and the Glaive is feeding on the scythes.[5]
What shows on Victor is the Authority he says he now has. The skin pales as if he were dead, the hair releases dark Miasma, and Junketsu becomes two small scythes he raises with an obvious familiarity, then joins into one large black scythe with red details and Miasma on the blade. The horse had never been given a name. He calls it by the feeling beings have when they are about to die, Despair, and a shadow on the ground gives the same horse back, bigger, more muscular, and healthier, still black, still burning green at the eyes, the tail, and the hoof. He tells it to play and wait until called. Pestilence dies next, to Absolute Zero, and the fight that remains is War's.[5]

#### Those Who Rule Reality
The Horseman stays dead through the years after Hell. What comes back is not a Soul climbing out of him. War, sent ahead into the Upper Sectors with [[Gol-goroth]], releases his power into the sword. A massive eye opens, and three figures come out of War's body: Eldritch incarnates of Famine, Pestilence, and Death, with War himself as war personified. Each of the three is only slightly weaker than War. Alone they can threaten solar systems. With War, the sword, and the Emperor behind them, they are galactic threats, and the meeting is counted as six against two rather than a duel. These are new fighters in the old roles. They are not the Souls [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] ate, and Despair is not among them.[6]
While War stays on [[Azrakar]], the three charge the Goddess of the Night together. She tries to retreat and hide inside her Concept. Death does not allow the disappearance. He forms an enormous sword of pure Miasma and throws it. She dodges and answers with a blast of Night that is wasted, and then they are on her. Death cuts her face, Famine drives a knife of pure power into her stomach, and Pestilence severs her limbs. Darkness pours out of her and closes every wound, the night deepens, and her power rises, but the wave she throws misses. To the Horsemen she is a child with too much power and too little command of the fundamentals, visibly less experienced than Azrakar, and an attack that misses does not threaten them.[13]
He remains her fight while War is cutting the God of the Void apart. When Azrakar is too wounded to breathe, she puts an illusion in his place and buys him a moment. Death takes that opening and pierces the body, and the body is another image. The thought he has about it is short. That damned ability. She wraps herself in the deepest night, hides the movement behind dark veils, and uses the dark to close what is already hurt. He does not chase every picture. The scythe stays close to his body, he watches which copies do not move like the others, and he waits until the real [[Kaelith]] has to spend herself to keep the area up.[11]
Famine's blades go through the images and force her to keep changing place, while Death's scythe cuts the lines she would use to get closer. That pairing holds until she twists their sense of distance, so that what they are looking at is no longer the ground. Famine strikes an image that vanishes in time. She is in his blind spot, hits his abdomen, and tears him away from Death, then tries to use the gap on Death himself. The scythe takes her arm and sweeps diagonally across the body. She dissolves into darkness and is behind him before the return swing is ready, and the blow lands on his back. He answers in the same motion, with the weapon and with a thrust.[11]
She deepens the night around the three of them until the images are no longer only something to look at. Each of them can hit, and the hits are real. Famine stops looking for the original and starts destroying every presence that seems solid. Some images burst and some stay. One pins his arm, and she uses it for a blow to his face. Death arrives before it finishes. The scythe goes through the image and into the real shoulder, and the projections fail. When she compresses Night around her own body into a harder defense, his scythe hits that barrier and does not go through. Famine puts his blades on the same point, the protection gives, and Death uses the opening. She gets out of the killing stroke and still takes a deep cut that stops her regeneration for a moment. The two of them do not give her the fight back. On the other side of that same moment, Pestilence is the one facing two Chief Gods.[11]
War calls the Horsemen when he sees that Pestilence cannot hold both. Death vanishes in the same light as the others and is at his side. Four pillars of raw energy rise, and nearby stars are erased by the pressure itself. Gol-goroth then isolates an Eldritch domain in which the previous laws stop and Eldritch beings hold the advantage, and the Chief Gods' hold on the space ends with that change. The three brothers take on Death's likeness, War gives them his power and synchronizes their senses with his own, and the four strike Azrakar together before the others can pull him clear. Death paralyzes him with Miasma. Pestilence weakens him. Famine takes the arms and legs. War cuts off the head and pierces the heart. All four erase him. The Great Darkness, displeased, orders the Horsemen brought in alive or dead.[11]
War does not let the survivors form a new line. [[Vhalor]] tries to scatter, is forced back together, and is wounded by Gol-goroth. When he finally vanishes and reforms far away, Death has already read the path. The scythe of pure Miasma is there as he becomes solid, stops the flight, and pierces the abdomen. Death comes in to finish him after the next wound and is pulled off that prey when Kaelith and [[Nerath]] spend their sovereign far enough to dull the sword and drag Vhalor clear. The loss disappoints him. He goes straight to Kaelith.[7]
She shrouds the region in night and sets images at different moments so he will not know which body is real. That no longer works on him inside the domain. He destroys the illusions by force, and the clash itself weakens the area the three gods are trying to hold. She uses a gap to retreat. He follows the movement and cuts her side before she can disappear. Regeneration tries to close it and has trouble removing the alien energy. He keeps the pressure on and forces her to spend herself on defense, which is the opening Famine uses to tear a huge hole in her body. When Pestilence's discharge throws Nerath back, Death and Famine are already there, and Kaelith's routes away from them are closed.[7]
War then breaks into the middle of the three gods. Vhalor tries to meet him with shadows, Famine breaks that defense before it is finished, and Death is the one who stops Kaelith when she tries to reach Vhalor. Famine reports that the gods cannot be finished for good. Death is the one who says why. Their patron's energy is keeping them alive, they have to act quickly, and something is approaching, because he can feel it. He destroys the last layer of her illusions, opens a wound in the belly deep enough to nearly split her in two, and then breaks the Darkness barrier she raises when Famine has already cut off the retreat. The blow throws her into deep space. The body tries to regenerate, the alien corruption is in too deep, and he aims the scythe for the last stroke.[7]
Nerath tries to reach her and is hit by Pestilence first. From where the goddesses stand, the Horsemen are swapping places and no longer read as four fighters. A combined attack pins Nerath, and Famine and Death are the ones set so she cannot escape while War is in front of her. At the execution itself he aims at Kaelith again, War raises Gol-goroth against Vhalor, Famine is set to stop Kaelith leaving, and Pestilence is aimed at the last of what is keeping Nerath conscious. None of those strokes land. A blow from outside interrupts them, Gol-goroth holds through one wave and starts to fail on the second, and [[Vaelthar]] comes through with the other Chief Gods. Death's reaction is that they were almost dead. War shuts the domain down. Vaelthar restores Kaelith, Nerath, and Vhalor. Victor then arrives and kills Kaelith by striking her Soul. The body is erased, and he holds the Soul. Death is still among the Horsemen at that point, and the page does not show him acting in the standoff that follows.[7][8]

### Skills & Abilities
He is one of the two direct fighters of the four. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s reading in Hell was that Famine and Pestilence supported him and War. The incarnate keeps that place. He is the scythe and the Miasma, and after one false body he stops chasing copies. Nothing below is a power of the Primordial Being called Death. That is a different existence.
- Demon Physiology: An executioner of [[Diablo]]'s, of the class Pillar Demons feared. War's armor and his stopped most of a lightning-fast iai that still cut all four and took Pestilence's arm. He also took a blow in the back from [[Kaelith]] and answered in the same motion. He is not shown as immune to either.[1][2][11]
- Demonic Return, Then None: An ordinary demonic death left a way back through Hell if the Soul remained. Victor consumed this Soul. The later fighter is War's incarnate through [[Gol-goroth]], not that return.[4][5][6]
- Authority of Death: Named by Victor after the consumption, not by Death as a Technique in the duel. Victor says that since he now has it, the horse should be called by the same name beings feel when they are about to die. That name is Despair. What shows on him with the claim is a dead pallor, dark Miasma coming off the hair, and [[Junketsu]] taking the scythes' shape, which he raises with an obvious familiarity. The average-value speech, and the line about learning a martial art that way, was said about Famine. It is not repeated for this Soul.[5]
- The Feeling of the End: While the heart is pierced, Victor asks whether Death can be afraid of his own death, and the body shakes as that end arrives. The page shows the question and the shaking. It does not show Death casting fear on anyone else during the fight.[5]
- Dark Miasma Manipulation: The coating on the scythes, and the power Vepar names as his foremost one. Victor called the control incredible.[2]
- Variable Reach: The Miasma can be extended and shortened past the metal. Victor dodged the blade and still took a cut on the cheek. When Victor got behind him, Death retreated from the metal, and Victor's own extended blade opened a gigantic gash in return.[2]
- Miasma Sword: Against Kaelith he formed an enormous sword of pure Miasma and threw it to stop her hiding inside Night. She dodged it. The throw is not the hit. The rush that follows is, and his part of that rush is her face.[13]
- Paralysis: On [[Azrakar]], his part of the four-man kill is Miasma that paralyzes the god. Pestilence weakens him, Famine takes the limbs, and War takes the head and the heart. The erasure after that is all four.[11]
- Scythe of Pure Miasma: The weapon that meets [[Vhalor]] as he becomes solid again is a scythe of pure Miasma. It stops the flight and pierces the abdomen. This is not the pair Junketsu ate.[5][7]
- Stalled Regeneration: A deep cut on Kaelith halts her regeneration for a moment. The cut on her side leaves alien energy that the regeneration has trouble removing. The belly wound, deep enough to nearly split her in two, leaves corruption too deep for her to overcome. He is not shown eating the Soul. He is shown leaving a body that cannot put itself back while he is still on it.[11][7]
- Scythe Mastery: Two black scythes, used apart or joined into one much larger scythe. He carried them connected on his back, joined them to fly at Victor while the fight was in the air, and opened the Hell fight with a stealthy cut at the neck under the Miasma. Victor leaned back, the blade missed, and the kick sent Death away. Junketsu ate both, then took the form of two small scythes that connect into a large black one with red details and Miasma on the blade.[3][1][2][5]
- Soul in the Weapon: Like the other Horsemen's weapons, the scythes are semi-sentient, hold a small part of his Soul, and embody his foremost Power. Vepar named that Power as the Dark Miasma of Death. Touching them without the Horseman's permission was considered extremely hazardous.[2]
- Reading a Copy: After piercing a body that was only an image, he stops hunting every copy. The scythe stays close. He watches the differences in how the copies move and waits until the real Kaelith has to act to keep the area alive. Her twisting of distance still works on him once, long enough for the blow to his back.[11]
- Cutting the Approach: While Famine's blades force her to change position, his strikes cut the paths she would use to get closer. When she later tries to leave a clash, he follows the movement and cuts the side before she disappears.[11][7]
- Through the Image: When an illusion has Famine's arm pinned and she is hitting his face, the scythe goes through the image and into the real shoulder. The projections fail. An arm she throws at him is intercepted, and the diagonal sweep crosses the body, before she gets behind him.[11]
- A Barrier, Then Not: A compressed Night barrier around her body stops his scythe. He does not go through it alone. Famine breaks the same point, and Death uses the hole. She escapes the kill and still takes the cut that stalls her regeneration. Inside the domain he destroys the last layer of her illusions by force, opens the belly, and breaks the next Darkness barrier himself, throwing her into deep space.[11][7]
- A Path Read in Advance: Vhalor vanishes and reforms far away. Death has already foreseen where he will become solid. The Miasma scythe is there as he does.[7]
- Shadow Mount: At the Vatican, shadows leave the ground and become the black horse, eyes, hooves, and tail burning green. He nods, and it is there. Victor later calls that horse out of a shadow on the ground himself. It comes up bigger, more muscular, and healthier, still burning green, and it answers to the name Death never gave it. This is the summoning of that horse. It is not a general control of darkness shown anywhere else.[3][5]
- Eldritch Incarnation: He comes out of War's body when the sword's eye opens, with Famine and Pestilence. Slightly weaker than War alone, a solar-system threat by himself, and a galactic threat with War, the sword, and the Emperor. Inside the domain the other two become increasingly like him, because that is the change War's power wakes. Vhalor calls all four the abominable brood of Death. The likeness is the transformation. It is not a Technique he is shown casting on his brothers. Place-swapping and the shared senses are the four under Gol-goroth, not a teleport he uses alone. Her illusions, which still fooled him before the domain, no longer work on him once he starts destroying them by force inside it. That is not an immunity he is shown carrying back out.[6][11][7]
- What He Can Feel Coming: After Famine says the gods cannot be finished, Death says the patron's energy is keeping them alive, that they must act quickly, and that something is approaching because he can feel it. What arrives is [[Vaelthar]]'s relief. The sense is one warning, not a map of the future.[7]
- With the Others: Direct combatant beside War at the Vatican and in Hell, where Victor caught both blades in a hand of ice and slammed their heads together. On Kaelith he is paired with Famine: Famine forces the position and breaks the point, and Death cuts the exits, takes the real body out of the images, and uses the hole. He also arrives in time to stop her finishing Famine. He answers War's call with the others. When Kaelith tries to reach Vhalor after War appears among them, Death is the one who stops her. The stars erased when the four pillars rise are pressure, not his scythe.[3][2][11][7]

### Equipment

#### Death's Scythes
A pair of black scythes, used apart or joined into one large scythe, and carried connected on his back when the four rode in. They held part of his Soul, were semi-sentient, and carried the Dark Miasma of Death. [[Junketsu]] consumed both after he vanished, then took their shape: two small scythes that connect into a larger one with red details and Miasma on the blade. The scythe of pure Miasma he uses on [[Vhalor]] and [[Kaelith]] as an incarnate belongs to that later fight. The original pair stayed eaten.[3][2][5][7]

#### Black Armor
Horseman armor, made by the best blacksmiths in Hell, which stopped most of the damage from [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s iai. The rebuilding Victor sees, using the Miasma of Hell as Energy, is shown on War's armor in that exchange.[2]

### Despair
Main article: Despair
The black horse he rode, with green flames at the eyes, hooves, and tail, called up out of shadow. It had no name while it was his. After he was gone, [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] brought the same horse out of a shadow on the ground, larger, more muscular, and healthier, and named it Despair, for the feeling beings have when they are about to die. He told it to play until called.[3][1][5] Later the horse comes out of the ground at Victor's side and goes to Hell, where Hell's Miasma changes the flames from green to deep black and then to neon violet as it evolves and grows wings of pure darkness. The incarnate is not shown taking it back.[9]

### Relationships

#### War
War is the older brother and the one [[Diablo]] told to take Death on the mission against Alexander. They fought that battle together, and Death asked him what to do when the Seraphim turned. In Hell they were the two direct fighters, and War was the one who broke the ice after Death had already been taken. The incarnate exists because [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] spared that brother and armed him. Death comes out of him through [[Gol-goroth]], answers when he calls the Horsemen, takes his power and his senses for the attack on [[Azrakar]], and puts distance with the others when War turns to face [[Vaelthar]]. The head and the heart of the God of the Void are still War's. The paralysis is Death's.[10][3][5][6][11]

#### Famine
Famine was the support paired with Pestilence, and his death is what sent the other three back in with the fury of a lost brother. Death is the one who recognized the Glaive art in [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s hands. As incarnates they are a pair against [[Kaelith]]. Famine forces her to move and breaks the barrier the scythe cannot enter. Death keeps her from finishing Famine when an image has the arm pinned, and he is the one on the real shoulder. Famine is the one who says the gods cannot be finished. Death is the one who says why.[2][5][11][7]

#### Pestilence
Pestilence supported the two direct fighters with disease that got worse over time. Death is the one who told him to stop playing and get serious, after the severed arm had already shown what [[Junketsu]] did. Pestilence saw this death and was consumed himself a moment later. They are drawn back out of War together. Pestilence's fight is [[Nerath]], and the corruption under [[Vhalor]]'s barrier. Death's fight is [[Kaelith]], and for one stroke it is also Vhalor's abdomen.[2][5][6][7]

#### Diablo
[[Diablo]] was his King. The order to take Death against Alexander went through War. The order to contact [[Asmodeus]], and to have the dead of Helheim made into another weapon of corruption, was given to Death himself. The incarnate is not shown going back to that court. He fights where War fights.[10][4][6]

#### Alexander
Death cut off both of the Pope's arms. War stabbed the heart. The Soul was then torn out by the hands beyond the door the [[Heavenly Father]] opened, and the wider punishment, including Limbo, was announced by Ariel. The two Horsemen understood afterward that they had been used to remove him.[3]

#### Victor Elderblood
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s pressure is what first made Death and War stop and look, at the Vatican, and Victor then watched them kill Alexander from a distance. In Hell, Death cut his cheek with Miasma that outran the blade, and Victor answered by copying the extension and opening a gash of his own. Hunger, taken from Famine at an average value, put Death down first. The Authority of Death, the scythes inside [[Junketsu]], and the horse are what Victor kept. He is also the reason an incarnate of Death can be sent into the Upper Sectors, because he spared War and gave him [[Gol-goroth]]. When Victor arrives on that field, the god he kills is the one Death had already opened and failed to finish.[12][3][2][5][6][8]

#### Kaelith
His principal opponent after the recreation. He will not let her hide in Night. The thrown sword misses, the rush does not, and his part of it is her face. He is fooled once, hit in the back once, and stopped by a compressed barrier once. After that he waits, hits the real shoulder through the image, and inside the domain he stops reading the copies and breaks them. The side cut, the pressure that lets Famine tear her open, and the belly that nearly splits her are his. He aims the last stroke twice. [[Vaelthar]] restores her. [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] erases her.[13][11][7][8]

#### Vhalor
Not his duel for most of the fight. He is the scythe already on the path when [[Vhalor]] reforms, the abdomen that gets pierced, and the finish that [[Kaelith]] and [[Nerath]] pull the god out of. Vhalor is also the one who calls the awakened four the abominable brood of Death.[11][7]

#### Azrakar
[[Kaelith]] drags this fight onto him for one moment, by putting an illusion in his place. Death pierces that body and gets an image. In the kill itself, Death's part is the Miasma that paralyzes him. He does not take the head.[11]

#### Nerath
She is half of the rescue that takes [[Vhalor]] off his scythe, and she tries to reach [[Kaelith]] when the last stroke is aimed. Pestilence stops that. Death and Famine are the pair who close [[Nerath]]'s escape when War is in front of her. He does not fight her the way he fights Kaelith.[7]

#### The Great Darkness
Not a master he serves. Death is the one who identifies the patron's energy as the reason the three gods will not stay dead, and the one who feels something coming. The voice that then floods [[Nerath]] belongs to that patron. The relief that answers the warning is [[Vaelthar]].[7]

### Death
He died second, after Famine and before Pestilence. Hunger put him on the ground with a hand on his stomach. [[Junketsu]], in the form of Famine's Glaive, was thrown through his heart, and [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] asked whether he could be afraid of his own death while the body shook at the end of it. War broke the ice. Pestilence came with him. Both were late. Death was already gone, and the Glaive was eating the scythes. A Demon who dies in the ordinary way can return through Hell if the Soul remains. This Soul was consumed. The incarnate is a role put back on a field, not that Soul leaving Victor.[4][5][6]

### Return
What fights [[Kaelith]] is an Eldritch incarnate drawn out of War by [[Gol-goroth]], in the same manifestation as Famine and Pestilence. He has a scythe of pure Miasma, he paralyzes a Chief God, and he brings another to the edge of death without being the hand that ends her. None of that undoes the consumption in Hell, and none of it gives him Despair back. The horse stayed [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s, and later evolved under Hell's Miasma from green to deep black to neon violet, with wings of pure darkness.[5][9][6][8]

### Legacy
[[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] kept the Authority of Death, the scythes inside [[Junketsu]], and the horse he named for the feeling of an ending. The incarnate is the other remainder, and the shape his brothers take when the domain wakes them. They become increasingly like him, and [[Vhalor]] names the four for him. On that field he does the work the title suggests, short of the last stroke. [[Kaelith]] dies by Victor's hand, after Death has already failed to finish her twice.[5][9][11][7][8]
