## Which Version Is This? (Disambiguation)
He has a single present-day version. **The Triumvir** (Robespierre's masters) and **Roman Triumvirate Holdings** (the bounty-setters Asmodeus names) are never explicitly said to be the same group. They are named in the same chapter, and both are linked to the questers and to Rome, so treat them as *very probably* one faction. *Canon baseline (not story):* in Riordan's *Trials of Apollo*, Triumvirate Holdings is the company of the evil emperors Nero, Commodus and Caligula.

## Appearance
"A small gray man" with "gray eyes," fastidious, brushing dust off his coat sleeve in the middle of the wreckage. He gives "an old-fashioned, courtly" bow. His hands glow with "purple-violet energy." Brimstone and rotten-egg stench surrounds the scene, though he says it isn't his.

## Personality
- **Courtly menace.** He bows twice and pays Perenelle compliments while threatening her.
- **Theatrical.** He likes brimstone because "It is so" "So dramatic."
- **Smug and contemptuous.** "Humans are so predictable."
- **Patient and persistent.** The Triumvir has gathered "many other treasures," and only the Stone has stayed "perpetually out of reach."
- **Cunning.** He let the questers lead him: "they led me straight to your door."

## Voice & Speech Patterns
Polished, polite and dryly mocking. He uses formal address ("Madame Perenelle") and speaks in neat, finished sentences. He jokes about "British wand whippers."
- "Ah, Madame Perenelle. I was wondering where you were" (Ch17)
- "I must congratulate his guests, they led me straight to your door." (Ch17)
- "You haven't aged a day." (Ch17)
- "I rather like the smell of brimstone." (Ch17)
- "I've come for the Philosopher's stone, Perenelle." (Ch17)
- "My masters will." (Ch17)
- "Humans are so predictable". (Ch17)
- Asmodeus on the Triumvirate: "The Roman Triumvirate Holdings is hunting you five. A King's ransom. Each." (Ch17)

## Abilities, Powers & Equipment
- Destructive magic: the shop's windows crack and explode, green and yellow smoke pours out, and the shelves are splintered and shot through with holes.
- Purple-violet energy projection.
- Tracking: he followed the demigods to the Flamels.
- **The Triumvir/Triumvirate's resources:** a bounty of "One singular ten-tens of gold Talents" on each quester, which Asmodeus converts to "Roughly four-hundred-thirty million dollars." A collection of "many other treasures."

## Backstory
He is the historical leader of the French Revolution's Terror (d. 1794), still alive. He is an old enemy of the Flamels (Nicholas: "Bah, bad memories of him"). The text never explains how he survived. "Of Free France" is an odd label, since "Free France" usually refers to de Gaulle's WWII movement. That may be an author's slip or deliberate.

## Story Arc So Far
- **Ch17:** Asmodeus warns the five questers (Percy, Zoë, Bianca, Grover and Eleni) about the Triumvirate bounty. In San Francisco, Robespierre follows them to the Small Book Shop. After they leave, he wrecks it, confronts Perenelle, demands the Stone and attacks her. The chapter cuts away.
- **Possible link:** Manannán mac Lir says the Morrígan is "contracted elsewhere, bound to serve some man's schemes" in the Americas (Ch17). In Michael Scott's books the Morrigan is allied with Dee, Robespierre's equivalent here, so this may point to the Triumvir. That is speculation, not confirmed.

## Continuity Notes
- He is "Robespierre of Free France" (see Backstory).
- **Triumvir vs Roman Triumvirate Holdings:** named in the same chapter (Ch17) and very probably one faction, but the text never equates them, and it never says whether the faction is mortal or otherwise. Do not call it a mortal Roman faction.
- **Author's vault:** there are no vault notes on Robespierre, the Triumvir or the Flamels; everything here comes from Ch17.
- **Era:** *present day* is c. 2021 per the author's timeline (R2), so the "Free France" label (a WWII-era name) and his 1794 death date are both well in his past.
- The bounty is stated in Greek-style "gold Talents" while the bounty-setter has a Roman corporate name. This mix of Greek and Roman is unexplained.
