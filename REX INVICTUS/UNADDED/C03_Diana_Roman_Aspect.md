## Which Version Is This? (Disambiguation)
- Diana is **not** simply another name for Artemis in this story. The text treats Greek and Roman aspects as **distinct minds in one divine domain**. Artemis can feel Diana "surge forward in her consciousness" and can suppress her (Ch11). Diana can be exhausted while Artemis still endures (Ch18).
- For the Greek aspect see `C01_Artemis_Present.md`. For the far-future Director see `C02_Artemis_Future_The_Director.md`. No future version of Diana appears in the text.
- **Parallels in the text:** Ares's form "flickered momentarily at the mention of that name, between his alternate forms" (Ch10). The name that triggers it is the AI's "Designation: Minerva", after Percy has already needled him as "Huitzilopotchli" [sic] and "Mars Ultor"; Ares then says "You sound like Sister." This is the closest parallel to Diana's surge. Eleni senses Huitzilopochtli as "similar to Lord Mars, his counterpart" (Ch16). The AI Minerva takes the Roman name of Athena, calling it "my poor roman aspect" (Ch6). Aspects are real, partly separate identities here.

## Appearance
No physical description. Diana exists only as an inner presence inside Artemis.

## Personality
- **Impulsive and affectionate toward family.** When Chiron asks Artemis whether her visit concerns "the bolt? Or Minerva?", Diana surges: *Sister! Where?* (Ch11). Whether she reacts to the name itself (Minerva is the Roman name of her sister Athena) or to the AI Minerva, who is aboard the plane inside Percy, is unstated.
- **Subordinate to Artemis.** She "attempted to surge forward" and "was suppressed brutally" (Ch11). Artemis keeps control.
- **Loyal and self-spending.** During Artemis's days under the sky she "had long exhausted her strength" (Ch18), which implies she bore part of the load.

## Voice & Speech Patterns
Only one line, rendered as italic inner thought:
- "Sister! Where?" (Ch11)

Her voice is urgent and exclamatory, unlike Artemis's controlled formality. Anything beyond this must be extrapolated with care.

## Abilities, Powers & Equipment
- Shares Artemis's divine power and domain. When she has "exhausted her strength", Artemis has less to draw on.
- In Roman contexts, her name carries authority: Percy introduces Zoë at New Rome as "agent of Diana" (Ch17), adapting the party's titles to Roman hierarchy (Percy is "son of Neptune", Bianca "daughter of Pluto", Eleni "of Jupiter's").

## Backstory
Not given. In myth Diana is the Roman goddess of the hunt, the moon and childbirth, identified with Artemis. The story keeps her as a separate aspect but says nothing of her history.

## Continuity Notes
- Diana appears only as a name and an inner voice. The Ch18 line is the only proof that she bore part of the sky's weight.
- The trigger for Diana's Ch11 surge is not stated explicitly. "Minerva" is the last word Chiron speaks before her line. She may be reacting to the name (her Roman sister's) or to the AI Minerva, who is physically aboard inside Percy; the text supports either reading. Other profiles (the primer, B01) should use the same wording (*name or AI presence unstated*).
- **Present-day year** (00a_CANON_RULINGS.md R2): c. 2021 per the author's timeline; nominally the canon PJO era. Do not give a bare 2005/2006.
- "Agent of Diana" is used once, for Zoë alone, in Percy's New Rome introduction (Ch17). It is not a general title of the Hunters.
- In Ch10 the AI Minerva has a mirror moment: on seeing the "PROJECT ARTEMIS" file she thinks "Her Sister's name? No… Her creator's sister." This suggests the story deliberately plays on Roman-aspect kinship.
