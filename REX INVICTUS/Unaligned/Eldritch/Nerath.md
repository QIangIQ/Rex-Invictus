### Appearances

#### Debut
Chapter 1193
The Great Silence wants him alive.
—Nerath to the assembled Chief Gods, Chapter 1193
Nerath is the Goddess of Silence and one of the Ten Chief Gods serving the [[Black Eclipse Pantheon]]. She possesses Divine Authority associated with Silence and holds an unusually esteemed position among the [[Seven Black Thrones]]. Her supernatural manifestation suppresses surrounding sound and vibrations, while her combat abilities allow her to interfere with certain forms of Energy, interrupt an opponent's powers through physical contact, and disrupt techniques before they fully manifest.[1][2]
Nerath enters the confrontation between Black Eclipse and the [[Elderblood Empire]] alongside [[Vhalor]], the God of Shadows. Her arrival brings the number of Chief Gods confronting War and the other three Horsemen to four. She announces that the Great Silence wants War captured alive, and [[Azrakar]] accepts the objective on behalf of the assembled gods. Nerath subsequently fights Pestilence, initially suppressing his spreading corruption and interrupting his techniques. Although she can regenerate some of the damage inflicted on her, Pestilence eventually exploits the limitations of her Silence and lands concentrated attacks.[1][2]
After [[Gol-goroth]] establishes an Eldritch domain, Nerath's Silence becomes ineffective against the Horsemen's coordinated assault. She survives the initial confrontation with the aid of power supplied by the [[Vharzath|Great Darkness]] and is later restored by [[Vaelthar]], the Throne of Origin. During the subsequent battle against [[Victor Elderblood]]'s projection, Vaelthar channels his power through his subordinates to close their wounds. Nerath recognizes that the gods cannot defeat the projection, but her ultimate fate after Victor orders the surviving forces taken prisoner is not confirmed in the available account.[2][3][4][5]

### Physical Appearance
Nerath's Divine manifestation is extremely elongated and slender, with a smooth white surface that does not resemble ordinary skin. Her proportions are stretched far beyond those of a human body. She has a narrow torso, an unusually long neck, and an oval head without a visible mouth, nose, or ears. Three black openings are arranged vertically where conventional eyes would be. They have no visible pupils or irises, and remain directed toward her opponent during combat.[1][2]
Disconnected, rib-like structures float around her torso rather than joining into a conventional skeleton. Her arms are exceptionally long and end in thin fingers capable of reaching an opponent before a conventional defense is established. She has no recognizable legs. Instead, her lower body narrows into white threads that fade into the surrounding void, allowing her segmented form to move without relying on ordinary locomotion.[1][2]
Small black symbols float around her head. Their precise purpose and nature have not been identified. They make no audible sound, consistent with the supernatural silence surrounding her manifestation.[1]
Despite having no visible mouth, Nerath can speak in a calm, cold voice that is clearly heard by those present. Her introduction demonstrates this unusual quality when she announces that the Great Silence wants War alive, with her words carrying as though she had spoken loudly despite her sound-suppressing presence.[1]
Her body can be damaged by concentrated attacks. When Pestilence contaminates one of the floating structures near her torso, Nerath detaches and discards it before the corruption spreads further. A separate concentrated discharge causes part of her body to lose its shape, but she retreats and regenerates the damaged area. These feats demonstrate that she can isolate some damage and restore certain structures, not that she is immune to corruption or possesses unlimited regeneration.[2]

### Personality
Nerath is restrained and difficult to read. She does not introduce herself with threats or lengthy declarations. When she arrives alongside [[Vhalor]], she acknowledges [[Kaelith]] and observes the battlefield before delivering the objective: the Great Silence wants War alive. Her calm manner contrasts with the destructive battle surrounding her, and she does not explain the reason behind the order or the identity of the authority that issued it.[1]
Her reserved demeanor does not indicate passivity in combat. Against Pestilence, she attacks directly and repeatedly adapts when her initial techniques stop working. When he concentrates corruption into a localized discharge, she changes how she protects her body, discarding contaminated structures rather than allowing the infection to spread. She subsequently expands her Silence and begins interrupting his powers at the moment they form. Her actions show an ability to adjust her methods in response to an opponent rather than relying on a single technique.[2]
Nerath also cooperates effectively with Vhalor. She takes advantage of the openings created by his Shadows, while her own interference with Pestilence's powers makes it more difficult for the Horseman to respond to both opponents. Their coordinated attacks force Pestilence to divide his attention, although neither god can completely overcome his corruption.[2]
She demonstrates concern for her fellow Chief Gods during the Eldritch battle. When Kaelith is badly injured, Nerath cries out her name and attempts to reach her, even as Pestilence continues attacking. She also recognizes that the Horsemen are coordinating their movements in a way that makes them appear in multiple places. Her conclusion that their apparent separation is misleading helps her understand the danger, but it does not provide a way to defeat them.[3]
Nerath remains loyal to the [[Vharzath|Great Darkness]] even when the power supplied by her patron becomes more than her body can contain. She screams for the incoming power to leave her, but the scene does not establish that she rejects her ruler or abandons her allegiance. After [[Vaelthar]] restores her, she bows when told that the surviving Chief Gods have disappointed the Great Darkness.[3][4]
During the subsequent confrontation with [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s projection, Nerath recognizes the difference between the gods' collective strength and the overwhelming power before them. After seeing her allies cut apart and Vaelthar lose an arm, she concludes that they cannot win and that only the Great Darkness can match their opponent. She does not voice this conclusion aloud.[5]

### Synopsis

#### Those Who Rule Reality
Black Eclipse is one of the major factions of the Upper Sectors, ruled by the [[Vharzath|Great Darkness]] and organized beneath the [[Seven Black Thrones]]. The Ten Chief Gods serve beneath the Thrones and represent some of the faction's most powerful Divine combatants. Nerath is the Goddess of Silence among these ten. When she arrives on the battlefield, [[Azrakar]] identifies her as a Chief God who is held in particularly high esteem by the Seven Black Thrones, although the specific reason for this distinction is not explained.[6][1]
War enters Black Eclipse's territory and battles Azrakar. The confrontation escalates when [[Kaelith]] joins the fight and War manifests the Eldritch incarnations of Death, Famine, and Pestilence through [[Gol-goroth]]. The ensuing battle threatens the surrounding astronomical region, prompting the Great Darkness to contain its effects.[7][1]
Nerath arrives with [[Vhalor]] while the confrontation is still underway. Kaelith recognizes her and remarks on her emergence from the silence associated with her existence. Azrakar introduces Nerath as the Goddess of Silence and Vhalor as the God of Shadows, emphasizing their exceptional standing within Black Eclipse. War responds without intimidation, treating their arrival as an increase in the number of enemies whose heads he intends to bring before his Emperor.[1]
Nerath then announces that the Great Silence wants War alive. Azrakar agrees to take him prisoner, and the four Chief Gods prepare to fight the four Horsemen. Nerath engages Pestilence, whose corruption initially loses much of its effectiveness when it enters her Silence. She approaches him and touches his chest, briefly interrupting the manifestation of his powers before striking him and forcing him to retreat.[1][2]
Pestilence adapts by concentrating his corruption into a smaller area and releasing it in a sudden discharge. The shock wave breaks through part of Nerath's Silence and gives him an opportunity to seize one of her floating structures. Corruption begins eating across its white surface, but Nerath detaches the affected component before the infection can spread throughout her body. Pestilence then applies smaller points of contamination, forcing her to keep isolating the affected areas. Nerath responds by expanding her Silence, eliminating new points of corruption before they can connect and attacking Pestilence's powers as his techniques begin to form.[2]
Pestilence eventually builds energy beneath Nerath's defensive layer and discharges it into her center. Part of her body loses its shape, forcing her to retreat and regenerate. Vhalor intervenes, pulling her out of danger and attacking Pestilence from unexpected directions with his Shadows. Nerath resumes attacking the Horseman while Vhalor restrains him, and her contact-based interruption briefly cuts off Pestilence's powers again. Together, the two gods repeatedly tighten their attack, although Pestilence's corruption also damages Vhalor's shadow extensions.[2]
The confrontation changes when War summons his brothers to fight together. The four Horsemen synchronize their attacks and awaken the Eldritch properties of Gol-goroth. The sword opens an Eldritch domain that isolates the battlefield and gives the Eldritch beings the advantage. Within this domain, the Chief Gods' influence over the space becomes ineffective: Nerath's Silence, Vhalor's Shadows, and Azrakar's Void can no longer control the battlefield as they did before.[2]
The four Horsemen attack Azrakar simultaneously. Death immobilizes him, Pestilence weakens him, Famine severs his limbs, and War decapitates him and pierces his heart. They then erase him through their combined Eldritch power. Nerath and Vhalor attempt to reach Azrakar but arrive too late to intervene.[2]
The surviving Chief Gods are forced to continue fighting inside the Eldritch domain. Nerath and Kaelith channel the Great Darkness's power, but their combined effort only weakens Gol-goroth's influence temporarily. Nerath and Vhalor use that opening to pull Vhalor out of Death's reach, and Nerath calls for a broader area of effect to counter the Horsemen's overwhelming battlefield control. The two gods coordinate their abilities to create a limited field, but they cannot fully restore their former advantages.[3]
Nerath continues fighting Pestilence as the battle deteriorates. Although her Silence can interrupt some of his manifestations, the corruption already spread across the battlefield and is repeatedly replaced when she suppresses individual points. Pestilence eventually lands another concentrated attack that throws her down. Nerath attempts to reach Kaelith when the latter is threatened by Death and Famine, but Pestilence intercepts her and leaves her struggling to contain the corruption within her damaged body.[3]
As the Horsemen continue their assault, Nerath realizes that their movements are coordinated so closely that the individual knights appear to exchange positions. She recognizes that they are acting as a single being rather than as separate combatants. Before she can turn that realization into an effective counterattack, the Horsemen overwhelm her defenses, pin her, and corrupt the wounds they inflict so that she cannot easily regain control of her body.[3]
At the point of defeat, Nerath hears the Great Darkness declare that her duty is not yet fulfilled. An overwhelming quantity of Darkness enters her, exceeding what her body can comfortably contain. She screams for the power to leave her, but its release forces War to retreat. The intervention saves her from immediate defeat, although she is left barely able to stand and is not in a condition to resume the battle at full strength.[3]
[[Vaelthar]], the Throne of Origin, subsequently enters the domain with the other Chief Gods. His intervention disrupts the Horsemen's attack long enough for him to restore Nerath, Kaelith, and Vhalor with a beam of pure Darkness. He then tells the three that they have disappointed the Great Darkness. Nerath bows alongside the others and accepts the reprimand. The restoration does not prevent the next confrontation from developing against [[Victor Elderblood]]'s projection.[4]
The gods attack Victor's projection, but their combined efforts fail to overcome it. Vaelthar establishes restraints and barriers, while the other gods combine their Divine powers against the projection. The attacks do not meaningfully move it. Victor then manifests [[Junketsu]], whose horizontal cut severs the gods and astronomical bodies in its path. Nerath sees her allies cut apart and Vaelthar lose an arm, recognizing that their force cannot win the confrontation and that only the Great Darkness can match the enemy.[5]
Vaelthar orders the remaining gods to stand firm and channels his own power through his subordinates, closing their wounds within seconds. Nerath is among those affected by this second restoration. The projection allows the gods to attack again, but their combined energy is crushed without difficulty. Vaelthar eventually attempts to contain the projection within a domain, but it walks out of the prison. After the confrontation, Victor orders the living to be taken prisoner and the remains of the fallen to be collected. Nerath's exact fate after that order is not confirmed in the available account.[5]

### Skills & Abilities
Nerath is a Chief God whose abilities are associated with the Concept of Silence. Her demonstrated powers primarily involve suppressing sound and vibration, interfering with certain Energy flows, interrupting supernatural techniques, and coordinating with [[Vhalor]]'s Shadows. Her abilities are effective against Pestilence's spreading corruption under favorable conditions, but concentrated attacks can break through her Silence, and the Eldritch domain created through [[Gol-goroth]] prevents her from exerting the same control over the battlefield.[1][2]

#### Divine Authority and Silence
- Divine Authority over Silence: Nerath possesses supernatural authority associated with Silence. Its effects are manifested through her presence and the area she controls, allowing her to suppress sound and interfere with certain forms of Energy propagation. The full metaphysical scope of her Concept has not been established.[1][2]
- Sound Suppression: Her presence suppresses surrounding sound. Despite this effect, she can communicate in a calm, cold voice that remains clearly audible to others.[1]
- Vibration Suppression: Nerath's presence eliminates vibrations in the surrounding area, interfering with the normal propagation of sound and certain effects that depend on energy travelling through the affected space.[2]
- Silence-Controlled Area: Nerath can extend her Silence across a broader area by extending her arms. This restricts Pestilence's ability to spread corruption freely and weakens manifestations that enter the zone. Expanding the area does not make the effect absolute, and it can be breached by concentrated power.[2][3]
- Energy Flow Interference: Certain forms of Energy weaken as they enter her Silence. Pestilence's spreading corruption loses effectiveness when it reaches the zone, forcing him to change his approach and concentrate his power instead of relying on unrestricted spread.[2]
- Contact-Based Power Interruption: Nerath can touch an opponent and briefly interrupt the manifestation of their powers. She demonstrates this by touching Pestilence's chest, penetrating his guard and cutting off his abilities for a short period. The effect is temporary and does not permanently remove his powers.[2]
- Technique Interruption: Nerath can strike at Pestilence's powers as his techniques begin to form, disrupting them before they fully manifest. This weakens his control over the corruption and creates openings for follow-up attacks, but Pestilence eventually adapts by accumulating energy beneath her defensive layer.[2]
- Defensive Suppression: Her Silence reduces the effectiveness of Pestilence's attacks when they enter her controlled area. It does not prevent him from attacking through concentrated discharges or from using corruption already established within the battlefield.[2][3]
- Limited Effect Against Concentrated Power: Pestilence can compress his corruption into a small part of his body and release it with enough force to break through part of Nerath's zone. This demonstrates that her suppression can be overcome by concentrated power rather than functioning as an unconditional negation of supernatural abilities.[2]
- Limitations Within an Eldritch Domain: When [[Gol-goroth]] opens its Eldritch domain, the Four Horsemen's power changes the conditions governing the battlefield. Nerath's Silence becomes ineffective against their coordinated Eldritch assault, and she cannot restore her previous level of control simply by expanding the zone.[2][3]

#### Physical Structure and Regeneration
- Non-Humanoid Divine Body: Nerath possesses an elongated, segmented manifestation with floating rib-like structures and a lower body formed from white threads. The body can move without conventional legs, and its separate components remain suspended around her as she fights.[1][2]
- Extended Reach: Her arms and fingers are exceptionally long, allowing her to reach Pestilence and touch his chest before he can establish an effective defense. This reach supports her contact-based power interruption and close-range attacks.[2]
- Detachable Anatomy: Nerath can detach and discard a contaminated component of her body before corruption spreads further. She also isolates smaller infected areas when Pestilence applies corruption to different points. The discarded structures are not shown growing back individually.[2]
- Regeneration: After Pestilence discharges concentrated corruption into her center, part of Nerath's body briefly loses its shape. She retreats and regenerates the damaged area. This establishes an ability to restore certain bodily damage, but not immunity to corruption or unlimited regeneration.[2]
- Regeneration Under Pressure: Nerath can recover from structural damage when given an opportunity, but Pestilence's continued attacks prevent her from doing so safely. Later concentrated attacks leave her severely compromised, and corruption seated in her wounds prevents her from easily regaining control of her body.[3]
- Limits of Personal Regeneration: Nerath's regeneration is distinct from restoration performed by another being. [[Vaelthar]] restores her with a beam of pure Darkness and later channels his power through his subordinates to close their wounds. These acts are Vaelthar's interventions, not feats of Nerath's own regenerative ability.[4][5]

#### Combat and Tactical Abilities
- Close-Range Combat: Nerath fights at close range, using her extended arms and fingers to strike Pestilence and make contact with his chest. Her attacks are often timed to interrupt his powers or exploit openings created by [[Vhalor]].[2]
- Tactical Adaptation: When Pestilence changes from spreading corruption across a wide area to targeting individual parts of her body, Nerath responds by detaching contaminated structures and expanding her Silence. She later interrupts his techniques at their onset, demonstrating that she can adjust her approach as an opponent adapts.[2]
- Battlefield Control: By extending the Silence over a larger area, Nerath restricts Pestilence's options and forces him to concentrate his power to break through the suppression. The larger field requires her to manage several threats simultaneously and does not stop attacks that have already bypassed the zone.[2][3]
- Threat Recognition: During the Eldritch battle, Nerath recognizes that the Horsemen's movements are coordinated to the point that their apparent individual positions become unreliable. She concludes that they are acting as a single being rather than as four independent opponents. This insight identifies a key feature of their combat behavior but does not provide an effective countermeasure by itself.[3]
- Combat Cooperation: Nerath works alongside Vhalor to attack Pestilence. She disrupts his powers and creates openings while Vhalor uses his Shadows to restrain and attack him from unexpected directions. Their combined attacks force Pestilence to defend against both combatants at once.[2]

#### Combined Abilities with Vhalor
- Silence and Shadows: Nerath's Silence and [[Vhalor]]'s Shadows complement one another during their fight against Pestilence. Nerath reduces the effectiveness of his powers, while Vhalor uses shadow extensions to obstruct his movements and restrain his body. Their combined pressure prevents Pestilence from focusing exclusively on either opponent.[2]
- Opening Creation: Nerath's interruption of Pestilence's powers creates brief opportunities for Vhalor to land attacks or tighten his restraints. Conversely, Vhalor's interventions give Nerath time to recover from concentrated attacks and return to the offensive.[2]
- Coordinated Restraint: Vhalor attempts to immobilize Pestilence with his Shadows while Nerath interrupts the Horseman's powers through contact. Pestilence can still spread corruption through the shadow extensions and force Vhalor to dissolve parts of his manifestation, so their restraint is not absolute.[2]
- Cooperation Against the Eldritch Domain: Nerath and Vhalor attempt to support the other surviving gods after the Horsemen establish [[Gol-goroth]]'s Eldritch domain. Nerath calls for an area-of-effect approach, and the gods coordinate their powers to create a limited field. Their effort does not restore their original advantage or overcome the Horsemen's control of the domain.[3]

#### Patron Power and Restoration
- Patron-Supplied Darkness: When Nerath is close to defeat, the Great Darkness tells her that her duty is not yet fulfilled. A large amount of Darkness enters her, exceeding what her body can comfortably contain. Its release forces War to retreat, but Nerath is left barely able to stand. This is a temporary intervention by her patron, not evidence that she can freely produce the same level of power at will.[3]
- [[Vaelthar]]'s Restoration: Vaelthar restores Nerath, [[Kaelith]], and [[Vhalor]] with a beam of pure Darkness after entering the Eldritch domain. This returns them to the battle but does not prevent Kaelith from subsequently being killed by [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s projection or guarantee that the remaining gods can defeat it.[4]
- Vaelthar's Shared Power: During the later confrontation, Vaelthar channels his power through his subordinates, closing their wounds within seconds. Nerath benefits from this second intervention. The feat belongs to Vaelthar and should not be treated as evidence that Nerath can independently restore other gods in the same way.[5]

#### Weaknesses and Limitations
- Concentrated Corruption: Nerath's Silence weakens Pestilence's spreading corruption, but a concentrated discharge can break through part of the zone and damage her body directly.[2]
- Targeted Contamination: Pestilence can apply small points of corruption to separate parts of Nerath's body, forcing her to detach or isolate the affected structures. This places pressure on her ability to maintain control over the whole battlefield.[2]
- Limited Suppression: Nerath can interrupt certain powers and techniques, but her interference is temporary. Pestilence can build energy beneath her defensive layer and attack when she moves to engage him.[2]
- Regeneration Requires an Opening: Nerath can restore damaged body structures, but repeated attacks can prevent her from recovering. Severe damage and corruption within her wounds leave her increasingly vulnerable.[2][3]
- Area-of-Effect Trade-Off: Expanding her Silence helps suppress more of Pestilence's manifestations, but maintaining a broad zone while defending herself becomes increasingly difficult. The Eldritch domain further limits her ability to impose Silence across the battlefield.[2][3]
- Eldritch Domain Disadvantage: Once [[Gol-goroth]] establishes its Eldritch domain, the Horsemen's influence overrides the gods' control of the affected space. Nerath's Silence cannot neutralize their coordinated assault under those conditions.[2]
- Dependence on External Restoration: Nerath is restored by [[Vaelthar]] after the Eldritch confrontation and later benefits from his shared power. These interventions are necessary events in her continued participation in the conflict and do not establish that she can independently recover from every fatal or existential attack.[4][5]
