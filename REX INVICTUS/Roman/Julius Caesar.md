### Appearances

#### Debut
Chapter 958
I understand...The time of Ragnarok has come... As promised, I will fulfill the agreement, Goddess of Death. Feel grateful for the benevolence of this Emperor.
—Julius upon his arrival, Chapter 958
Gaius Julius Caesar [[Octavian (Roman Emperor)|Octavianus]] Augustus, referred to in the novel as Julius Caesar, was an ancient Roman Emperor and one of the exceptional historical Humans whose Soul was claimed and preserved by Hela in preparation for Ragnarök. Unlike many of Hela's other contracted warriors, from whom she obtained only a fragment or portion of their Souls, Hela took Julius' entire Soul after his death and retained it until the war against [[Odin]].[2]
Julius' agreement with Hela was motivated by his attachment to the civilization he had ruled. Even after death, he wanted to observe the continued progress of his Empire. Hela's position as a Goddess possessing extraordinary authority over Death allowed her to offer possibilities that an ordinary deceased Human could never obtain, and Julius consequently entered an agreement requiring him to answer her summons when Ragnarök finally began.[2]
After thousands of years of preparation, Hela resurrected Julius together with more than 700,000 Roman soldiers. His army formed one of the largest historical military forces summoned during Ragnarök, greatly exceeding the 30,000 Spartans commanded by Leonidas and the more than 20,000 Wallachian soldiers accompanying [[Vlad III Tepes]].[1] Hela subsequently empowered the Roman, Spartan and Wallachian forces with her Divine Power, transforming the resurrected Humans into supernatural soldiers capable of participating in a war between Gods.[1]
The character presented by the novel contains a notable historical inconsistency. Chapter 958 introduces him by the full name "Gaius Julius Caesar Octavianus Augustus" and calls him the First Roman Emperor, identifying the historical figure normally known as Augustus, while simultaneously referring to him as Julius Caesar. The article therefore follows the novel's own identification rather than attempting to silently replace it with real-world historical genealogy.

### Physical Appearance
Julius appeared as a man with short hair seated upon an Imperial throne.[1]
Unlike the earlier resurrected armies, whose soldiers simply emerged from the ground, Julius' arrival was accompanied by an entire Imperial structure centered around his throne. He remained seated for several moments while observing Hela and [[Asgard]] before standing to formally acknowledge the beginning of Ragnarök.[1]
The novel does not provide enough additional information to canonically establish his hair color, eye color, exact height, skin tone or specific facial features. His current artwork should therefore be treated as an artistic representation rather than a definitive physical reference.

### Personality
Julius is portrayed as enormously proud, authoritative and conscious of his position as an Emperor. His first words after resurrection demonstrate that death had done little to diminish his Imperial attitude: despite being summoned through Hela's Power and obligated by their ancient agreement, he told the Goddess of Death that she should feel grateful for the benevolence of this Emperor in fulfilling their arrangement.[1]
His arrogance differs from mindless vanity. Julius remembers the agreement he made with Hela and immediately honors it once he realizes Ragnarök has begun. He does not attempt to evade his obligation or renegotiate the terms after resurrection, instead standing from his throne and preparing his enormous Roman army for war.[1]
Julius also possessed a strong attachment to his legacy. Unlike Leonidas, whose principal wish after death was simply to participate in a war greater than anything he experienced while alive, Julius' desire concerned the civilization he left behind. He wanted to continue observing the development of his Empire even after his death.[2]
This desire was important enough for him to make a supernatural agreement with Hela before his final death. It suggests that Julius valued the long-term consequences of his rule and remained interested in what Rome would become after he was no longer personally capable of guiding it.
His brief appearance also demonstrates considerable composure. Julius awakens in the middle of a Divine war involving Gods, Dragons, armies of the dead and creatures from The Abyss, yet shows no panic. His reaction is simply to determine that Ragnarök has begun, acknowledge Hela's summons and fulfill his side of the agreement.[1]

### Background
Julius was originally an exceptional Human ruler from ancient Rome.
The novel gives very little information concerning his Mortal life because his narrative role concerns Hela's preparation for Ragnarök rather than Roman history itself. Most details commonly associated with Julius Caesar or Augustus in real-world history—including parents, spouses, children and political campaigns—are not directly established within the novel and should therefore not automatically be placed in his MTW infobox as canonical family relationships.
What the novel does establish is that Julius became an Emperor whose attachment to his Empire remained strong enough to survive his own death.[2]
At some point before his death, Julius encountered Hela. As a Goddess possessing an abnormally powerful Affinity for Death, Hela could sense when exceptional Humans were approaching the end of their lives and spent millennia forming agreements with individuals she considered useful for her eventual war against [[Odin]].[2]
Hela offered different terms according to the desires of each individual. [[Vlad III Tepes]] wanted protection for his descendants, while Leonidas wanted the opportunity to participate in a Divine War. Julius' desire was to continue observing what became of his Empire after his own death.[2]
The agreement was significant enough that Hela ultimately claimed Julius' entire Soul rather than merely taking a small fragment.
She preserved that Soul for thousands of years as one of the assets intended for Ragnarök.[2]

### Synopsis

#### Dragon God of Chaos
After thousands of years of preparation, Hela finally began Ragnarök and revealed that her military resources extended far beyond the dead normally associated with the Norse [[Greek Underworld|Underworld]]. Through agreements made with exceptional Humans across history, she had secretly collected rulers, warriors and armies without allowing the other Pantheons to understand how large her forces had become.[1][2]
The first great historical army she revealed consisted of Leonidas and 30,000 Spartans. Hela had preserved the souls of the legendary warriors and allowed them to return for a war vastly greater than the conflicts they had experienced during their Mortal lives. A second army followed: more than 20,000 Wallachian soldiers led by [[Vlad III Tepes]].[1]
Hela then summoned an even larger force.
More than 700,000 people rose from the ground while an entire Imperial structure appeared with them. At its center sat a short-haired man upon a throne. After looking at Hela and then toward [[Asgard]], the man immediately understood why he had been summoned.[1]
He declared that the time of Ragnarök had arrived and promised to fulfill the agreement he had made with the Goddess of Death. Despite being the one resurrected through Hela's power, Julius characteristically told her to feel grateful for the benevolence of an Emperor who was honoring their deal.[1]
[[Aphrodite]] Elderblood identified him in shock as:
Gaius Julius Caesar [[Octavian (Roman Emperor)|Octavianus]] Augustus
and described him as the first Roman Emperor, while the narration refers to him as Julius Caesar.[1]
His appearance forced [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s observers to reconsider the scale of Hela's preparation. She had not simply gathered Norse dead and creatures associated with Ragnarök; she had spent millennia secretly taking the Souls of exceptional individuals belonging to human civilizations connected to other Pantheons.[1]
Julius' Roman army became one of the three principal historical Human armies initially deployed by Hela:

Hela then released Divine Power over the resurrected armies. Black full-plate armor marked by green coloration appeared over the Spartans, Romans and Wallachians while creatures from The Abyss were provided as supernatural mounts. The narration explicitly notes that the warriors Hela had resurrected were no longer merely ordinary Humans.[1]
Hela ordered the assembled kings, emperors, commanders and warriors to destroy Asgard. Julius and his Roman forces subsequently participated in the massive offensive alongside Leonidas, Vlad, Hela's undead, [[Fenrir]], [[Jormungandr]], Nidhoggr and the Abyssal creatures.[1]
The novel focuses more heavily upon Leonidas' direct combat after the order is issued, so Julius does not receive an individual duel or named technique during the battle. His principal demonstrated contribution is the command of the enormous Roman army Hela had preserved specifically for Ragnarök.
After the fall of [[Odin]], Victor questioned Hela about how she had obtained several historical Souls without the corresponding Pantheons noticing.[2]
Hela explained that her unusually strong Affinity for Death allowed her to predict when exceptional individuals would die or cease to exist. She used this knowledge throughout history to approach useful people before their deaths and negotiate agreements with them.[2]
The terms differed according to what each person wanted. Vlad wanted Hela to protect his descendants. Leonidas wanted another opportunity to fight, specifically in a war worthy of his warrior spirit. Julius wanted to observe the progress of his Empire even after his death.[2]
Hela normally collected only a fraction of a contracted person's Soul, allowing her to nurture that fragment until it could later be used to summon them. Julius and Leonidas were special cases: Hela took their entire Souls for herself.[2]
She explained that this method allowed her to accumulate an enormous army across the ages without attracting enough attention for the Pantheons to discover what she was preparing. Hela was particularly willing to steal important dead associated with the Greek sphere because she possessed little respect for the [[Greek Pantheon]]'s management of its Underworld.[2]
Julius' continued existence during Ragnarök was therefore not an ordinary resurrection or random act of [[Necromancy]]. It was the fulfillment of an agreement made while he was alive, with Hela preserving his complete Soul specifically so that he could return when her war against Odin finally began.
His individual fate after Ragnarök has not been directly described.

### Skills & Abilities
Julius was originally an exceptional Human Emperor and military commander. His supernatural capabilities during Ragnarök came primarily from his resurrection through Hela and the Divine empowerment she granted to her historical armies.
The novel has not shown Julius using a personal Divinity, [[Magic]] system or individualized supernatural ability.

#### Resurrected Soul
Hela possessed Julius' entire Soul rather than merely a fragment.[2]
During Ragnarök, she restored Julius to an active physical manifestation capable of commanding his army and participating in the Divine War.[1]
This grants him, during his resurrected state:
- Resurrection (External; through Hela): Julius was restored from death by Hela after she had preserved his complete Soul for thousands of years.[1][2]
- Spiritual Existence: His continued existence after his Mortal death depended upon the preservation of his Soul.
- Physical Manifestation: Hela was capable of giving his preserved Soul an active body during Ragnarök.
- Soul Preservation (External): Hela prevented Julius' complete disappearance into the normal reincarnation cycle by retaining his entire Soul.[2]
These are consequences of Hela's Powers and agreement with Julius rather than abilities Julius independently possesses.

#### Divine Empowerment
After summoning her historical armies, Hela released Divine Power across the forces of Leonidas, Vlad and Julius.[1]
The narration explicitly notes that the resurrected warriors were no longer merely Humans.
Known effects included:
- Superhuman Physical Characteristics (Empowered): Hela's resurrected warriors became capable of physically participating in a Divine battlefield.
- Divine Power Enhancement (External): Hela directly reinforced the army with her own Divine Power.
- Supernatural Combat Capability: The resurrected soldiers became capable of fighting Asgardian Divine forces rather than functioning as ordinary Mortal troops.
- Enhanced Durability (Empowered)
- Enhanced Strength (Empowered)
- Enhanced Speed (Empowered)
The exact degree of enhancement received by Julius personally is not quantified.

#### Divine Armor
Hela's Divine empowerment manifested black full-plate armor with green coloration over her resurrected historical armies.[1]
This equipment provided the Romans and the other resurrected soldiers with supernatural protection appropriate for participation in Ragnarök.
Because the armor was generated by Hela, it should be indexed as external equipment rather than a personal ability belonging to Julius.

#### Military Leadership
Julius' clearest personal strength is his capability as an Emperor and commander.
He appeared commanding an army numbering more than 700,000 Roman soldiers, making his force by far the largest of the three historical Human armies initially summoned by Hela.[1]
- Military Command: Julius can command extremely large military formations.
- Leadership: More than 700,000 resurrected soldiers appeared beneath his authority.
- Army Coordination: His forces operated as part of Hela's broader multi-front assault upon [[Asgard]].
- Strategic Experience: Julius was selected by Hela from countless Humans throughout history specifically because she considered him and his forces useful enough for Ragnarök.
- Command Presence: Even immediately after being resurrected into a Divine War, Julius retained his Imperial authority and composure.
The novel does not provide enough detail regarding Julius' individual battlefield decisions to assign specific supernatural tactical abilities.

#### Exceptional Will
Julius retained his identity, memories, pride and sense of obligation despite having died thousands of years before his resurrection.
He immediately understood the circumstances of Ragnarök, remembered his ancient agreement with Hela and willingly fulfilled his promised role.[1]
This demonstrates an exceptionally stable sense of self following Soul preservation and resurrection.

### Power
Julius' original Human combat strength is unknown.
During Ragnarök, he was no longer operating as an ordinary Mortal. Hela empowered the historical armies with Divine Power, and the narration explicitly states that the resurrected warriors were no longer simply Humans.[1]
However, Julius himself is not shown personally fighting a named Norse God or demonstrating a unique offensive technique.
His greatest confirmed military asset is his army of more than 700,000 resurrected Roman soldiers.[1]
This made Julius strategically important even without requiring him to personally rival Divine beings in individual combat.
Any attempt to rank Julius at God, Combat God or similar levels would currently be speculative.

### Relationships

#### Hela
Hela is the Goddess responsible for preserving Julius' Soul and returning him to existence during Ragnarök.
Their relationship began through a supernatural agreement made before or around Julius' death. Hela spent millennia identifying exceptional Humans whose abilities, armies or reputations could become valuable during her future war against [[Odin]].[2]
Julius' personal desire was to continue observing the progress of the Roman civilization after his death. Hela possessed the Death-related abilities necessary to offer possibilities beyond those available to ordinary Humans, and Julius accepted her terms.[2]
Unlike contracts where Hela took only a fraction of the person's Soul, she claimed Julius' entire Soul and retained it for herself until Ragnarök.[2]
When resurrected, Julius immediately recognized that Hela had finally called upon their agreement and agreed to fulfill his promised role.[1]
Their relationship is contractual rather than one of ordinary loyalty. Julius serves Hela during Ragnarök because of an ancient agreement, while continuing to present himself with enough pride to suggest that Hela should be grateful for an Emperor's cooperation.

#### Roman Army
Julius commanded more than 700,000 Roman soldiers resurrected alongside him.[1]
The scale of the army far exceeded the other historical Human forces summoned at the beginning of Ragnarök.
Hela reinforced the Romans with Divine Power and supernatural armor before ordering her assembled armies to attack [[Asgard]].[1]

#### Leonidas
Leonidas was another legendary Human ruler and warrior whose entire Soul was taken by Hela.[2]
Unlike Julius, Leonidas requested almost nothing personal in exchange for his agreement. He had lived and died according to his own wishes and simply desired the opportunity to participate in a higher-level war—a war among Gods.[2]
Both men were subsequently resurrected as commanders during Ragnarök.

#### Vlad III Tepes
[[Vlad III Tepes]] was another historical ruler contracted by Hela.
Vlad's arrangement differed significantly from Julius'. Hela took only part of Vlad's Core existence rather than his entire Soul, while Vlad's payment was protection for his direct descendants after his death.[2]
All three rulers later appeared together among Hela's Ragnarök forces.

### Historical Identity
The novel's identification of Julius contains an internal historical contradiction.
Chapter 958 gives his full name as:
Gaius Julius Caesar [[Octavian (Roman Emperor)|Octavianus]] Augustus
and identifies him as the:
First Roman Emperor
while simultaneously referring to him as Julius Caesar.[1]
Historically, Gaius Julius Caesar Octavianus Augustus and the title of first Roman Emperor correspond to Augustus/Octavian rather than the earlier dictator normally known as Julius Caesar.
The previous version of this page attempted to combine both historical identities by listing Julius Caesar's spouses, parents, Cleopatra and various children while retaining the novel's description of the First Roman Emperor.
Because those family relationships are not established by the novel, they have been removed from the canonical infobox.
Until the story clarifies the discrepancy, the character should be indexed according to the identity explicitly used by Chapter 958 rather than attempting to reconstruct his genealogy through real-world Roman history.
