### Appearances

#### Debut
Chapter 675
Yamata-no-Orochi is an ancient mythological monster originating from Japan. By the time of the main story, Orochi had already been dead for an unspecified period, but its corpse was later resurrected through the forbidden [[Necromancy]] of [[Asmodeus]] during the Demonic War.[1]
Orochi was one of several dead mythological creatures recovered and transformed into undead weapons for [[Diablo]]'s war effort. Its resurrection, alongside [[Kumbhakarna]] and other legendary monsters, revealed that Diablo's forces had taken advantage of the Pantheons' distraction during the global conflict to obtain the remains of creatures previously slain by Gods and other mythological figures.[1]
Unlike a conventional resurrection, Orochi was not shown returning naturally to life. Asmodeus used Necromancy involving the Soul of an innocent being, breaking the Soul apart and preventing it from receiving judgment or passing into the afterlife in order to force the dead monster's body to function again.[1]

### Physical Appearance
Yamata-no-Orochi possesses the body of an enormous serpent with eight heads and eight tails. When its resurrection was displayed through a holographic recording, the gigantic creature was visibly surrounded by Miasma.[1]
The novel has not described the condition of Orochi's resurrected body in sufficient detail to establish whether it was decomposed, skeletal or otherwise visibly damaged. Its current artwork should therefore not be used as proof of the exact condition of the corpse.

### Personality
Yamata-no-Orochi has not displayed an independent personality in the modern story.
By Chapter 675, the original monster was already dead and its corpse had been forcibly reanimated through [[Asmodeus]]'s [[Necromancy]]. Nothing in the chapter confirms that Orochi's original consciousness, memories or personality were restored alongside the body.[1]
The revived monster should therefore be treated primarily as an undead weapon controlled through Necromancy rather than as evidence that the original Yamata-no-Orochi had genuinely returned to life.

### History
Very little is currently known about Yamata-no-Orochi's original life within the novel's continuity.
The story confirms that Orochi was a mythological monster belonging to Japan's supernatural history and that it had already been killed before the events of the Demonic War.[1]
Although traditional Japanese mythology associates Yamata-no-Orochi's death with Susanoo, Chapter 675 itself does not retell the circumstances of Orochi's original defeat. Until the novel directly establishes those events, the exact history of its death should not be expanded using mythology alone.

### Synopsis

#### Demonic War
Yamata-no-Orochi was revealed during the escalation of the Demonic War in Chapter 675. After the war between Heaven, Hell and the supernatural factions intensified, Haruna arrived with information she had received from [[Amaterasu]] Elderblood. Amaterasu was strengthening Japan's defenses by assigning Gods who remained healthy enough to fight because enormous mythological monsters were advancing toward the battlefield.[1]
Among the approaching creatures were [[Kumbhakarna]] and other monsters connected to the [[Indian Pantheon|Hindu Pantheon]], as well as Yamata-no-Orochi from the Japanese supernatural world. [[Jeanne Elderblood]] immediately questioned how these creatures could be advancing toward the battlefield when they were supposed to have been dead for a long time. Haruna answered with disgust: [[Necromancy]]. The monsters had been resurrected as undead.[1]
Kaguya then projected a holographic recording showing what had happened. The group watched a Miasma-covered entity resurrecting the enormous eight-headed and eight-tailed serpent. [[Morgana Elderblood]] identified the necromancer as [[Asmodeus]].[1]
The revelation caused immediate concern because the technique was fundamentally different from merely animating a corpse. The group explained that true Necromancy could interfere directly with deceased Souls, preventing them from being judged or passing into the afterlife. Asmodeus's method was even more abhorrent: an innocent Soul was used to force the ancient monster back into activity, with the Soul being shattered into thousands of pieces and prevented from reaching its proper end.[1]
The implications extended far beyond Orochi itself. Every major Pantheon possessed ancient monsters that its Gods and heroes had killed throughout history. With the Pantheons occupied by the Demonic War, [[Diablo]]'s forces had been given an opportunity to obtain those remains. The resurrection of Orochi and Kumbhakarna consequently suggested that Diablo could possess an entire army of undead mythological creatures created through Asmodeus's Necromancy.[1]
Yamata-no-Orochi was therefore not introduced as an isolated monster attack. Its resurrection was evidence of a much larger strategic operation intended to weaponize the supernatural world's dead against the factions currently fighting Diablo.[1]

### Skills & Abilities
Very little of Yamata-no-Orochi's original power has been demonstrated directly in the novel. Its modern appearance is limited to the holographic recording of its resurrection, so abilities originating only from external Japanese mythology should not be treated as confirmed novel powers.
- Gigantic Size: Yamata-no-Orochi was identified as one of the gigantic mythological monsters advancing toward the battlefield. Its resurrected serpentine body was enormous even when viewed through the holographic recording.[1]
- Eight-Headed Physiology: Orochi possesses eight separate serpent heads. Chapter 675 visually confirms the anatomy but does not show how the heads function during combat.[1]
- Eight Tails: Orochi possesses eight tails, matching its identification as Yamata-no-Orochi.[1]

#### Necromantic State
The following characteristics come from [[Asmodeus]]'s resurrection technique rather than Yamata-no-Orochi's original abilities.
- Undead Resurrection (Granted): Orochi's dead body was forced back into activity through Asmodeus's [[Necromancy]].[1]
- Soul-Based Necromantic Animation (Granted): The resurrection process used an innocent Soul to animate the monster rather than simply restoring Orochi through natural resurrection. The Soul involved in the process was broken apart and prevented from receiving judgment or moving into the afterlife.[1]
- Miasma Envelopment: Orochi's resurrected body was visibly surrounded by Miasma in the recording shown by Kaguya. The chapter does not establish whether this Miasma was generated naturally by Orochi or resulted from Asmodeus's Necromancy, so it should not currently be treated as one of Orochi's original personal abilities.[1]

### Power
Yamata-no-Orochi's original combat level is unknown.
Chapter 675 establishes only that it was one of the gigantic mythological monsters sufficiently dangerous for [[Amaterasu]] to reinforce Japan's defenses after learning that it and other resurrected monsters were advancing toward the battlefield.[1]
It was discussed alongside [[Kumbhakarna]] and other legendary creatures formerly slain within the various Pantheons, indicating that its resurrection was treated as a serious military threat. However, no direct fight involving the resurrected Orochi is shown in the chapter and no numerical or comparative scaling is provided.[1]
Claims such as:
- possessing venom capable of killing Gods;
- breathing poison;
- simultaneously attacking eight opponents;
- possessing regeneration;
- controlling weather;
- or scaling directly to Susanoo
are currently unsupported by the novel's demonstrated material and should not be included as established abilities unless a later chapter confirms them.

### Necromantic Resurrection
Yamata-no-Orochi's modern existence is particularly important because it provides one of the clearest explanations of high-level [[Necromancy]] in the setting.
Ordinary corpse animation is only one possible use of the art. The form used by [[Asmodeus]] interacts directly with Souls and interferes with the normal supernatural process of death.[1]
The process shown with Orochi can be summarized as:
Ancient mythological monster dies
↓
Corpse remains in the supernatural world
↓
Asmodeus obtains the remains
↓
An innocent Soul is used
↓
Soul is shattered into thousands of fragments
↓
Soul is prevented from judgment / afterlife
↓
Fragments are used through Necromancy
↓
Yamata-no-Orochi's corpse is forced back into activity
↓
Resurrected monster becomes an undead weapon
The horror expressed by Haruna, Morgana and the others came primarily from this abuse of the Soul rather than from the simple fact that a corpse had been animated.[1]

### Relationship with Asmodeus
[[Asmodeus]] was responsible for Yamata-no-Orochi's resurrection.
Nothing shown in Chapter 675 indicates that Orochi cooperated willingly with him. The ancient monster had already died, and Asmodeus used [[Necromancy]] to force its corpse back into action.[1]
Their relationship should therefore be understood as necromancer and undead weapon rather than master and willing subordinate.

### Relationship with Diablo
Yamata-no-Orochi became part of [[Diablo]]'s war effort after its resurrection.
The characters concluded that if Diablo had ordered the resurrection of both Orochi and [[Kumbhakarna]], it was reasonable to suspect that [[Asmodeus]] had been sent across multiple Pantheon territories to recover and resurrect other dead mythological monsters while the supernatural factions were distracted by the war.[1]
Orochi therefore became one component of a potential undead army created for Diablo.

### Trivia
- Yamata-no-Orochi's first appearance occurs only after its original death; the monster is introduced to the main story as an undead resurrection.[1]
- Its body possesses eight heads and eight tails.[1]
- The revived Orochi was visibly surrounded by Miasma.[1]
- Its resurrection was performed by [[Asmodeus]].[1]
- Kaguya showed the others a holographic recording of Orochi's resurrection.[1]
- Orochi was resurrected during the same operation that revived [[Kumbhakarna]] and other ancient mythological monsters.[1]
- The novel does not currently confirm that Orochi retained its original consciousness after resurrection.
- The novel does not directly confirm the circumstances surrounding Orochi's original death. Its association with Susanoo in real-world Japanese mythology should therefore not automatically be added to the novel's canon history.
- The old version's Venomous Breath ability was unsupported. Chapter 675 never shows Orochi breathing or using poison.
- The old claim that its resurrection made its legendary abilities stronger through "necromantic enhancement" was likewise unsupported.
