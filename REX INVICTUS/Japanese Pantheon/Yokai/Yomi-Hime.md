Yomi-Hime, commonly referred to simply as Yomi or Lady Yomi, is an ancient Yokai, the Queen of the Tengus and the Commander of the Ravens. She was formerly married to the Shinto Moon God [[Tsukuyomi]] and has served for thousands of years as one of the principal intermediaries between the Tengu Clan and the [[Shinto Pantheon]].
Thousands of years before the beginning of the story, Yomi personally approached the Shinto Gods and proposed an agreement intended to secure the survival of her people. Under the resulting pact, the Gods granted the Tengu their protection while Yomi's Clan became their messengers and observers, monitoring both Humans and supernatural beings throughout Japan.[1]
Her marriage to Tsukuyomi was likewise political rather than romantic, having been imposed as part of the relationship between the Tengu and the Shinto Gods. Although Yomi remained tied to the Pantheon for thousands of years, she never lost her concern for other Yokai and eventually divorced Tsukuyomi after later revelations concerning him and the deterioration of their already distant relationship.[1][2]

### Physical Appearance
Yomi is a beautiful woman with long black hair and two enormous raven-like wings extending from her back, clearly displaying her nature as a high-ranking Tengu. Her appearance is described as elegant and distinctly avian compared to ordinary humanoid Yokai.[2]
Her wings are functional and allow her to fly. During her first appearance, Yomi manifested her wings before leaving the Shinto Divine Realm, demonstrating that she can use them for rapid aerial travel.[1]
The novel has not presently provided an exact height, canonical eye color or highly detailed description of her clothing.

### Personality
Yomi is an intelligent, pragmatic and duty-oriented leader whose foremost concern is the survival and security of the Tengus. Her original agreement with the Shinto Gods demonstrates her willingness to compromise politically when doing so benefits her people. Rather than relying solely on strength or pride, Yomi chose to secure the protection of beings considerably more powerful than her Clan in exchange for providing a service valuable to them.[1]
Despite her long association with the Gods, Yomi retains genuine sympathy toward other Yokais. When [[Victor Elderblood]], [[Scathach]] and their allies began hunting those responsible for spreading information concerning Ophis and [[Nero Elderblood]], Yomi was disturbed by the number of Yokai being killed and directly questioned why [[Tsukuyomi]] was refusing to intervene.[1]
Her reaction became considerably more personal when Tsukuyomi dismissed the dying Yokai as filth. Because Yomi herself was a Yokai, his words reinforced something she had understood for thousands of years: Tsukuyomi had never overcome his prejudice toward her race and had never truly regarded her as an equal wife.[1]
Yomi had nevertheless retained some hope that thousands of years together might eventually change him. Their marriage had never been based on love, and Tsukuyomi had reportedly never even touched her as a wife, yet Yomi had still wondered whether his opinion of her might eventually improve. His attitude during Victor's retaliation demonstrated that nothing meaningful had changed.[1]
She is also perceptive enough to notice emotions that others attempt to conceal. Although Tsukuyomi tried to behave calmly while discussing Victor and Scathach, Yomi realized that he was genuinely afraid of provoking them and was avoiding interference partly because he understood how dangerous retaliation from Victor's faction could become.[1]
Yomi is considerably less comfortable when she herself becomes the subject of political arrangements. After divorcing Tsukuyomi, [[Amaterasu]] considered using Yomi in another political marriage during negotiations with Haruna. Yomi internally made it clear that she did not want to marry again, but her weakened political position left her feeling that she had little ability to refuse if Amaterasu seriously ordered it.[2]
Despite her dislike of being treated as a political bargaining piece, Yomi was capable of judging potential partners independently. When Victor was brought into the conversation, she acknowledged internally that she would not necessarily object to Victor himself, finding him physically attractive even though she disliked the circumstances surrounding the discussion.[2]
She can also become nervous around overwhelmingly dominant personalities. When Amaterasu left her to continue discussions with Haruna, [[Morgana Elderblood]] and [[Jeanne Elderblood]], Yomi was internally intimidated by the women around her but nevertheless maintained her position because she understood that she was representing the future of her people.[2]

### History

#### Ancient Pact with the Shinto Gods
Several thousand years before the main story, Yomi approached the [[Shinto Pantheon]] seeking protection for the Tengu Clan. Rather than simply submitting herself and her people to the Gods, she proposed a mutually beneficial agreement.[1]
Under the arrangement, the Shinto Gods would protect the Tengu from external threats. In return, the Tengu would act as their messengers and maintain an extensive observation network throughout Japan. Members of Yomi's Clan would monitor both Humans and supernatural beings and report significant developments to the Gods.[1]
The pact established the Tengu as one of the principal intelligence and communication networks available to the Shinto Pantheon. It also gave Yomi's people security in a supernatural society where comparatively weaker Yokai groups could easily be destroyed by stronger factions.
The agreement was not merely imposed upon Yomi. She was the person who originally sought it and therefore bears considerable responsibility for establishing the Tengu's long-term political alignment with the Gods.[1]

#### Marriage to Tsukuyomi
Yomi's increasing involvement with the [[Shinto Pantheon]] eventually resulted in her marriage to [[Tsukuyomi]]. The union was political from the beginning.
Tsukuyomi stated that he married Yomi because the council of Gods ordered him to do so, with [[Amaterasu]] being directly involved in the decision. Their marriage therefore functioned as another connection between the Tengu and the Shinto Gods rather than a relationship formed through mutual affection.[1]
Tsukuyomi's prejudice toward Yokai prevented the marriage from developing normally. Despite remaining married to Yomi for thousands of years, he never truly accepted her race and did not behave toward her as a conventional husband. Yomi was aware that he had never even touched her intimately despite the immense length of their union.[1]
Although their relationship was emotionally distant, Yomi appears to have developed at least some personal expectations over time. She hoped that living together for thousands of years might eventually cause Tsukuyomi to change his opinion of Yokai, only to repeatedly discover that his prejudice remained intact.[1]

### Synopsis

#### Discoveries, Changes and War
Yomi first appeared during the aftermath of the conflict surrounding Ophis and [[Nero Elderblood]]. After information concerning the two girls had been spread through Japan's supernatural community, [[Victor Elderblood]], [[Scathach]] and their allies began systematically eliminating the individuals responsible.[1]
A Tengu messenger rushed to the Shinto Divine Realm and reported the developing situation to Yomi and [[Tsukuyomi]]. The messenger used a supernatural talisman to display events occurring throughout the Mortal World, allowing Yomi and Tsukuyomi to observe the consequences of Victor's retaliation.[1]
Yomi was disturbed by the scale of the bloodshed and questioned why the Shinto Gods were permitting so many Yokai to die. Tsukuyomi showed little concern and made it clear that he had no intention of becoming involved simply to save unrelated Yokai. Instead, he instructed Yomi to concentrate on protecting her own Tengu Clan.[1]
When Tsukuyomi referred to the Yokai being eliminated as filth, Yomi was visibly affected. His words reminded her that despite several thousand years of marriage, he still regarded her race with contempt. Their union had never been based upon affection; Tsukuyomi had only married her because the Gods ordered the political arrangement.[1]
The discussion also revealed the origin of Yomi's relationship with the Pantheon. Thousands of years earlier, she had approached the Gods seeking their protection. In exchange, the Tengu became Divine messengers and observers responsible for watching both Humans and supernatural beings.[1]
Yomi nevertheless realized that Tsukuyomi's apparent indifference concealed fear. He understood that openly interfering with Victor and Scathach could result in consequences far worse than allowing the targeted Yokai to die. Yomi therefore accepted that intervention was impossible and resolved to prioritize the continued survival of her Clan.[1]
Before leaving, Tsukuyomi ordered Yomi to send a messenger to Inari, once again demonstrating the Tengu's traditional function as intermediaries between the Gods and other supernatural factions.[1]

#### Demonic War
By the time of the Demonic War, Yomi's circumstances had changed considerably. She and [[Tsukuyomi]] were no longer married, with their divorce having occurred after revelations concerning Tsukuyomi that Yomi found unacceptable.[2]
Yomi accompanied [[Amaterasu]] during negotiations with Haruna. She appeared through Amaterasu's flames and was immediately recognized by Haruna as the Queen of the Tengu and Commander of the Ravens.[2]
Haruna reacted with immediate hostility. From her perspective, Yomi and other Yokai who had aligned themselves with the Shinto Gods had betrayed their own race by choosing Divine protection over solidarity with other Yokai. Yomi's ancient pact consequently remained politically controversial even thousands of years after its creation.[2]
Yomi attempted to clarify that she was no longer Tsukuyomi's wife, separating her present position from her former marriage. This did little to erase Haruna's resentment, but the threat facing Japan made cooperation more important than their personal disagreements.[2]
Amaterasu offered the services of Yomi and the Tengu to Haruna, specifically emphasizing their exceptional capabilities as scouts. The arrangement placed Yomi's reconnaissance network at Haruna's disposal during the defense of Japan, allowing the unified Yokai forces to use the same intelligence system that had historically served the Shinto Gods.[2]
The negotiations then became considerably more uncomfortable for Yomi when Amaterasu suggested using marriage to strengthen the alliance. Because Yomi was now divorced, Amaterasu considered marrying her to one of Haruna's generals.[2]
Yomi did not want another marriage. After spending thousands of years in a political union with Tsukuyomi, she had little desire to repeat the experience. Nevertheless, she understood that her position had become politically fragile and believed she might have little choice if Amaterasu decided the marriage was necessary.[2]
Haruna rejected the proposal, refusing to use her generals as political marriage candidates. During the discussion, however, [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]] was indirectly considered as another possibility. Yomi privately acknowledged that she found Victor attractive enough that she would not personally object to him, although the larger circumstances still made her uncomfortable.[2]
Amaterasu ultimately left Yomi behind to continue negotiating with Haruna's group. Faced with Haruna, [[Morgana Elderblood]] and [[Jeanne Elderblood]], Yomi became internally nervous and questioned why Amaterasu had abandoned her to deal with the three intimidating women by herself. Despite her fear, she remained because her responsibilities as Queen required her to continue representing the Tengu.[2]
The arrangement marked an important transformation in Yomi's political position. Her people had once operated almost exclusively as the eyes and messengers of the Shinto Gods, but during the Demonic War the Tengu's scouting network was placed directly at the service of Haruna and the broader Yokai military effort.[2]

### Relationships

#### Tsukuyomi
[[Tsukuyomi]] is Yomi's former husband. Their marriage lasted for thousands of years but was political from its inception. Tsukuyomi married Yomi because of a decision by the council of Shinto Gods rather than because he loved her.[1]
His prejudice against Yokai prevented genuine intimacy from developing between them. Even after thousands of years, Yomi recognized that he had never truly viewed her as an equal and had reportedly never touched her as a wife.[1]
Yomi nevertheless appears to have hoped that time might gradually change him. His callous attitude toward the deaths of other Yokai during [[Victor Elderblood|Victor]]'s retaliation demonstrated otherwise and deeply disappointed her.[1]
By the Demonic War, Yomi and Tsukuyomi had officially divorced after Yomi discovered additional information concerning him that she could not accept.[2]

#### Amaterasu
[[Amaterasu]] is one of the Gods most directly associated with Yomi's political position. She participated in the decision that led to Yomi's marriage with [[Tsukuyomi]] and later continued treating Yomi as an important representative of the Tengu.[1]
During the Demonic War, Amaterasu brought Yomi into negotiations with Haruna and offered the Tengu's scouting abilities as part of the alliance. She was also willing to consider another political marriage involving Yomi if it helped stabilize relations between the Gods and Yokai.[2]
Their relationship therefore appears to be primarily political rather than personal.

#### Otsuki Haruna
Yomi and Haruna have a strained relationship rooted in their opposing histories.
Haruna views Yokai who willingly served the Gods as traitors to their own people. Because Yomi personally created the alliance that placed the Tengu under Divine protection, she represents one of the clearest examples of the political system Haruna disliked.[2]
Despite this hostility, circumstances forced them to cooperate during the Demonic War. [[Amaterasu]] assigned Yomi and her Tengu to provide reconnaissance for Haruna's forces, making Yomi an important support figure in Japan's defense.[2]

#### The Tengus
The Tengus are Yomi's people and her greatest responsibility.
Yomi has consistently prioritized their survival above her own personal desires. Her ancient pact with the Shinto Gods was created specifically to obtain protection for them, and even when she became disillusioned with [[Tsukuyomi]] she continued valuing the security provided by their agreement.[1]
Her willingness to tolerate difficult political circumstances, including an unwanted marriage and later [[Amaterasu]]'s attempts to arrange another one, is heavily influenced by her responsibility as Queen.

### Skills & Abilities
As an ancient Tengu Queen who has survived for several thousand years and leads one of Japan's established Yokai Clans, Yomi possesses significantly greater supernatural capabilities than ordinary Humans. However, the novel has shown relatively little of her direct combat ability, so several aspects of her full power remain unknown.
- Supernatural Physical Characteristics — As an ancient Yokai and the Queen of the Tengu, Yomi possesses supernatural physical capabilities beyond ordinary Humans.
- Flight — Yomi possesses two enormous raven wings that allow her to fly. She manifested them before leaving the Shinto Divine Realm during her debut.[1]
- Aerial Mobility — Her Tengu physiology provides natural mobility through the air, an ability particularly useful for reconnaissance and rapid transportation.
- Enhanced Senses — As a high-ranking Tengu and leader of a species specialized in observation and scouting, Yomi possesses supernatural sensory capabilities associated with her race.
- Reconnaissance — Yomi commands the Tengu intelligence network, whose members are explicitly regarded as expert scouts. The Tengu have monitored Humans, Yokai and other supernatural entities throughout Japan for thousands of years.[1][2]
- Information Gathering — Through her subordinates, Yomi can gather information regarding supernatural movements across large portions of Japan. This intelligence-gathering capability formed one of the principal services exchanged for Divine protection under her ancient pact with the Shinto Gods.[1]
- Surveillance — The Tengu were tasked with continually observing Humans and supernatural beings on behalf of the [[Shinto Pantheon]], placing Yomi at the head of one of Japan's oldest supernatural surveillance systems.[1]
- Messenger Network — Yomi can dispatch Tengu across Japan to deliver orders, warnings and reports between the Mortal World, Divine authorities and supernatural factions.[1]
- Talisman Usage — Members of Yomi's Tengu network possess supernatural talismans capable of transmitting or displaying information. During her debut, one messenger used a talisman to show Yomi and [[Tsukuyomi]] the events occurring in the Mortal World.[1]
- Yokai Physiology — As a Tengu, Yomi possesses the supernatural physiology and Energy characteristic of Yokai.
- Longevity — Yomi has existed for several thousand years while retaining the appearance of a young adult woman.[1]
- Leadership — Yomi has ruled the Tengu for thousands of years and successfully maintained her Clan's existence through changing Divine and Yokai political systems.
- Political Negotiation — Yomi personally negotiated the ancient agreement between the Tengu and Shinto Gods, demonstrating considerable political intelligence and long-term strategic judgment.[1]
- Mental Fortitude — Despite being placed in politically and personally uncomfortable situations, Yomi is generally capable of controlling her outward behavior and acting according to her duties as Queen.[2]
