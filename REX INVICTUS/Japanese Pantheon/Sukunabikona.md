### Appearances

#### First Mention
Chapter 672
Sukunabikona is a Third Generation God belonging to the [[Shinto Pantheon]] and its God of Culture. Although he has not directly appeared in the story, his Divine authority plays an important role in the Shinto Pantheon's supernatural crafting industry. Sukunabikona works alongside [[Amatsumara]], the God of the Forge, while [[Amaterasu]] contributes her Flames of the Sun as the furnace used to create the Pantheon's high-quality supernatural equipment.[1]
Sukunabikona's principal demonstrated application of the Culture Divinity is the protection of crafted objects. He can apply Divine protection to equipment so that other Gods of the Forge cannot easily analyze the object, discover how it was produced or perfectly reproduce the techniques incorporated into it. Combined with Amatsumara's forging abilities and Amaterasu's Solar Flames, this protection forms part of the reason the Shinto Pantheon's Divine forge is considered unusually sophisticated and difficult for competing Pantheons to replicate.[1]

### Physical Appearance
Sukunabikona has not received a direct physical appearance or canonical description in the novel. His height, build, hair color, eye color, clothing and other identifying characteristics remain unknown because his only confirmed story involvement concerns his contribution to the [[Shinto Pantheon]]'s crafting system.[1]

### Personality
Sukunabikona has not directly spoken or received sufficient characterization for a detailed personality profile. No canonical material currently establishes whether he is reserved, eccentric, proud, aggressive or otherwise possesses personality traits traditionally associated with his mythological counterpart.
What can be established from his role is that he contributes to one of the [[Shinto Pantheon]]'s most economically and strategically important industries. The Pantheon does not restrict its Divine equipment to internal use; it sells and exchanges supernatural weapons and items with outside factions, allowing the Shinto Gods to accumulate considerable wealth and resources through their craftsmanship.[1] Sukunabikona's ability to protect the techniques contained within those products is particularly valuable because it prevents competing Divine craftsmen from simply purchasing Shinto equipment and reverse-engineering the methods used to produce it.

### Synopsis

#### Demonic War
Sukunabikona was first mentioned during the later stages of the Demonic War, when the [[Shinto Pantheon]] was suffering severe losses and administrative problems. The conflict had injured numerous Gods, while the abandonment of important responsibilities by [[Izanami-no-Mikoto]] created additional problems involving the Pantheon's Hell and the Souls that should have been processed through its afterlife system. Even powerful Gods such as [[Tsukuyomi]] had suffered injuries serious enough that complete recovery could potentially require centuries.[1]
With the Pantheon's resources divided between the war, the protection of Japan and its supernatural administrative problems, [[Amaterasu]] negotiated with Haruna and the Youkai. The resulting arrangement allowed the Shinto Pantheon to provide weapons and other resources while the Youkai assumed greater responsibility for defending Japan. The equipment shown during these negotiations impressed Haruna's faction because of both the quality of the materials and the sophistication of its construction.[1]
Amaterasu explained that the Shinto Pantheon possessed an unusually advanced Divine crafting industry. Although she acknowledged that [[Hephaestus]] of the [[Greek Pantheon]] and the Dwarves associated with the [[Norse Pantheon]] surpassed them at the absolute highest levels of craftsmanship, she rejected the idea that the Shinto Gods were mediocre craftsmen. According to Amaterasu, without those exceptional rivals the Shinto Pantheon could potentially have stood at the forefront of supernatural item production.[1]
The Shinto Pantheon's forging process combined several different Divine specializations. [[Amatsumara]], the God of the Forge, contributed the supernatural craftsmanship necessary to physically create the equipment, while Amaterasu supplied her Flames of the Sun as a Divine furnace. Sukunabikona's Culture Divinity supplied another essential component: his Divine Power protected the finished items so that other Forge Gods could not simply analyze them and reproduce the methods used in their creation.[1]
This protection gave the Shinto Pantheon a significant commercial advantage. Unlike the Greek and Norse Divine craftsmen, whose products were described as being used primarily within their own Pantheons, the Shinto Pantheon actively sold supernatural equipment to outsiders and obtained money, materials and other resources in return. Sukunabikona's contribution therefore protected not merely individual weapons but one of the Pantheon's valuable technological and economic advantages.[1]
Sukunabikona himself did not enter the battlefield during the chapter and has not received a direct combat scene.

### Skills & Abilities
Sukunabikona is a genuine Third Generation Shinto God whose known specialization is the Concept of Culture. His direct combat capabilities remain unknown, as the novel has only demonstrated his Divine authority through its application to supernatural craftsmanship.

#### Divine Physiology
- God Physiology: As a genuine Shinto God, Sukunabikona possesses the fundamental supernatural characteristics associated with Divine beings.
- Higher-Spirit Physiology: Gods are fundamentally high-order spiritual existences rather than conventional mortal organisms.
- Divine Soul: Sukunabikona possesses a Divine Soul capable of containing and expressing genuine Divinity.
- [[Divine Energy]] Manipulation: He possesses Divine Energy and can channel it through his Culture Divinity and Divine techniques.
- Superhuman Physical Characteristics: As a God, Sukunabikona possesses strength, speed, durability, stamina, agility and perception beyond ordinary mortals, although none of these attributes have been individually demonstrated.
- Divine Regeneration: Gods possess supernatural recovery significantly beyond that of mortal species, although sufficient damage to their Souls or Divine existence can still permanently threaten them.
- Agelessness / Divine Longevity: Sukunabikona has existed for an immense period and does not deteriorate through ordinary mortal aging.
- Godly [[Immortality]]: Sukunabikona is naturally exempt from conventional death through old age. This does not constitute absolute immortality against Soul destruction, Conceptual attacks or sufficiently powerful Divine enemies.
- [[Divine Presence]]: As a genuine God, Sukunabikona possesses the supernatural presence inherent to Divine beings, although the intensity of his individual aura has not been shown.
- Blessing Bestowal: Gods can express portions of their Divine authority through Blessings associated with their Concepts. No individually named Blessing from Sukunabikona has yet been shown.
- Curse Bestowal: Divine authority can likewise be expressed negatively through effects associated with the user's Divinity, although Sukunabikona has not demonstrated a personal Curse.

#### Culture Divinity
- Divinity — Culture: Sukunabikona possesses Divine authority associated with the Concept of Culture. The complete scope of this Concept has not been explored, but its demonstrated applications include protecting products of a civilization's craftsmanship and preventing outsiders from easily reproducing the knowledge encoded within them.[1]
- Culture Manipulation: Through his Divinity, Sukunabikona can exercise supernatural influence over phenomena associated with Culture. Only the artifact-protection aspect of this authority has been explicitly demonstrated or described.
- Conceptual Authority: His power operates through Divinity rather than ordinary [[Magic]], allowing supernatural effects to be imposed through the Concept represented by his Divine existence.
- Divine Protection: Sukunabikona can apply Divine protection to supernatural items produced through the [[Shinto Pantheon]]'s forging system.[1]
- Artifact Protection: Crafted objects carrying his protection become significantly more difficult for outside supernatural craftsmen to study and reproduce.[1]
- Anti-Analysis: Sukunabikona's Divine Power interferes with attempts by other Forge Gods to analyze protected Shinto equipment and determine the techniques responsible for its construction.[1]
- Anti-Replication: His protection prevents rival craftsmen from simply copying a finished Shinto artifact and perfectly recreating the methods used to produce it.[1]
- Knowledge Protection: The practical result of his Culture Divinity is the protection of the knowledge, craftsmanship and techniques embodied within an object. This allows the Shinto Pantheon to distribute and commercially sell equipment without automatically giving away the secrets of its production.[1]
- Crafting-Method Concealment: Even if another Divine craftsman obtains one of the Pantheon's items, Sukunabikona's protection makes identifying the exact construction process considerably more difficult.[1]
- Intellectual-Property Protection (Conceptual): Functionally, Sukunabikona's demonstrated power acts as a supernatural form of protection over a civilization's creations, preventing competitors from freely appropriating their techniques. This is an interpretation of the demonstrated anti-copy effect rather than a separately named ability in the novel.[1]
The complete extent of Culture as a Divinity remains unknown. Abilities such as unrestricted control over art, language, civilization, traditions, social values, technology or collective Human behavior should not be attributed to Sukunabikona until they are directly demonstrated.

#### Divine Craftsmanship
Although [[Amatsumara]] is the actual God of the Forge, Sukunabikona is an important participant in the [[Shinto Pantheon]]'s Divine crafting process because his power protects what the forge produces.[1]
- Supernatural Crafting Knowledge: Sukunabikona possesses sufficient understanding of Divine craftsmanship to work alongside Amatsumara and [[Amaterasu]] in the production of high-quality supernatural items.
- Artifact Enchantment: He can add Divine properties to an item during or after its construction, with his confirmed specialization being protection against analysis and reproduction.[1]
- [[Divine Energy]] Infusion: Sukunabikona can place his Divine Power into crafted objects to establish persistent supernatural protections.
- Collaborative Crafting: He can combine his Divine specialization with the authorities of other Gods during the production of supernatural equipment.[1]
- High-Level Artifact Production: The equipment produced through the Shinto Pantheon's collaborative forge was considered exceptionally high quality even by supernatural standards.[1]

#### Power
Sukunabikona's direct combat strength is unknown. He has not participated in an on-page battle, released an offensive attack or been compared directly with another God in martial ability.
His importance lies primarily in his specialization. The [[Shinto Pantheon]] possesses more than enough dedicated combat Gods, while Sukunabikona contributes an ability that strengthens the civilization's military and economy indirectly. By preventing rivals from easily reverse-engineering Shinto equipment, he helps preserve the Pantheon's technological advantage even while those items are sold or distributed outside its own territory.[1]
His Culture Divinity should consequently not be judged solely through destructive power. Within the field relevant to his Concept, Sukunabikona performs a function that ordinary combat Gods cannot easily reproduce.

### Relationships

#### Amatsumara
[[Amatsumara]] is the [[Shinto Pantheon]]'s God of the Forge and Sukunabikona's principal known collaborator within the Pantheon's crafting industry. Their responsibilities are distinct: Amatsumara handles the supernatural forging of equipment, while Sukunabikona's Culture Divinity protects the techniques contained within the completed products from analysis and copying.[1]
Their combined powers allow the Shinto Pantheon to manufacture advanced equipment without making its underlying methods freely available to every Forge God who acquires one of its products.

#### Amaterasu Elderblood
[[Amaterasu]] is the God-Queen of the [[Shinto Pantheon]] and participates directly in its most advanced crafting process by supplying her Flames of the Sun as a Divine furnace.[1]
The collaboration between Amaterasu, Sukunabikona and [[Amatsumara]] demonstrates that the Pantheon's artifact industry relies upon multiple specialized Gods combining their authorities rather than expecting a single Divine craftsman to perform every stage alone.

#### Shinto Pantheon
Sukunabikona is one of the specialized Gods whose abilities support the wider infrastructure of the [[Shinto Pantheon]]. His Culture Divinity is especially valuable because the Pantheon actively trades supernatural equipment with outside factions rather than keeping every artifact exclusively for internal use.[1]
His protection allows the Shinto Pantheon to benefit economically from those transactions without allowing buyers or rival Divine craftsmen to easily reproduce the knowledge behind its products.[1]
