**117B:** Your deployment logs show ships fitted with broadside weapons in long-range formations. Why not prioritize forward-firing configurations for higher pursuit efficacy?

**STRATEGOS:** Because forward is not where the enemy stays. By the time your weapons reach their target, the target is no longer there. Everything in space moves. Everything accelerates. If your weapons don’t account for that, they’re ornamental.

**117B:** So then, the preference for missile saturation?

**STRATEGOS:** Of course. Missiles are your mind extended. They correct. They listen. You fire, and they _think_ their way to the enemy. A beam, a rail—those don’t think. They assume the world is still. The world is never still.

**117B:** What about kinetic strikes, rail weaponry? Some doctrines suggest they're indispensable at medium range.

**STRATEGOS:** Medium range exists for the few seconds it takes to become close range or irrelevant. Rail weapons are brute declarations. They demand precision timing and sensor sync, and if the target maneuvers even slightly in that window? Nothing. Waste.

**117B:** And broadsides? Why arrange weapons laterally if trajectory is uncertain?

**STRATEGOS:** Broadsides aren’t about style. They’re about surface area. More tubes, more barrels, more coverage. Lateral arrays allow a full spread without destabilizing the ship’s centerline. You fire wide, and the missiles arc to target. The launch is never the strike. The space between matters.

**117B:** Some doctrines favor rear-mounted weapons for pursuit or retreat engagements. Valid?

**STRATEGOS:** Highly. You charge—your nose faces them. But once you commit, you have to slow. Slowing means turning. Turning means your drive faces the enemy. So your fire should, too. Rear-facing weapons aren't a contingency. They are inevitability. You run. You shoot. You survive.

**117B:** Wouldn't that increase exposure?

**STRATEGOS:** You’re always exposed. You exist in motion. Better to weaponize your weakness than pretend it doesn’t exist. Your drive is your shield. Your drive is your blade. You cook them as you leave.

**117B:** What of shields? Energy dispersal, kinetic teleportation fields—those complicate missile logic.

**STRATEGOS:** Then you saturate. Volume overwhelms calculation. One missile fails. Ten overwhelm. One beam disperses. Ten fragment the field. If the defense is intelligent, drown it in noise. If it adapts, outpace it. You fire not to hit. You fire to break rhythm. Once rhythm is broken, you win.

**117B:** Have you considered direct boarding in these scenarios?

**STRATEGOS:** Only if you’re desperate. Boarding is fantasy. Distance is death. Ships that meet should never have done so. If your boarding party survives, your strategy already failed. We board as last rites, not tactics.

**117B:** You seem to reject visual targeting entirely.

**STRATEGOS:** You fire by telemetry. You confirm by ghost signature. You adapt by pulse decay. If you’re _seeing_ the enemy, you’ve already lost. Seeing is for gods and artists. We work in prediction and probability.

**117B:** You speak of missiles as thought. What about the drive as weapon?

**STRATEGOS:** That is truth. Your drive is the god you carry. Fire it wrong, and it burns you. Fire it right—and it is fury incarnate. Plasma, fusion exhaust, whatever you call it—it’s raw direction made lethal. You point it at the enemy, and you accelerate. You _leave_, and they _die_.

**117B:** So all weapons are distance or deception?

**STRATEGOS:** All weapons are time. The clever make time bleed. The foolish count it.

**117B:** And broadsides?

**STRATEGOS:** Broadsides are faith. You trust your ship to endure the swing, your missiles to think, your enemy to err. You launch not to kill. You launch to control _when_ the kill is possible.
