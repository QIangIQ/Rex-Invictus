# Character Roster (To Fly on Borrowed Wings)

Up: [[To Fly on Borrowed Wings]]. Names: [[TFBW Name Conversion Bank]].

> [!note] Converted 2026-11-26
> Placeholder tags were replaced with the new names from the conversion bank. Where the draft's placeholder pass had overwritten real names (for example Otto Moritz Walter Model, Georges Cuvier, Primo de Rivera, the footballer names) the real name was restored. Protagonist nicknames that the placeholder pass erased are marked "(nickname lost in the draft placeholder pass)". The premise section still reflects the older "Blue Star" origin; the UOE origin in [[Gabriel Jagdfeld]] supersedes it.

Compiled from per-chapter notes covering chapters 1-473. Chapter numbers are given as "ch N". From roughly ch451 on, file numbers and in-book chapter numbers drift apart by one or two because of a translator announcement (ch451) and a duplicated chapter (ch468). Citations here use the file numbers. Other duplicated or doubled files: ch34, ch113 (largely retells ch112), ch154/155, ch472.

Contradictions between chapters are flagged with **[Conflict]** and left unresolved. Where the notes disagree about whether a name refers to a real person, that is flagged too.

## Real vs original characters

Each character below is tagged in the roster. **[REAL]** means a real historical person used under their own name. **[REAL-BASED]** means modelled on a real person but renamed, gender-swapped or merged. **[OC]** means an original character, including characters given a celebrity or footballer name as a joke. **[UNSURE]** means the evidence is inconclusive. Several rows group people who share one roster entry.

Rows: 82 REAL, 6 REAL-BASED, 9 UNSURE, 124 OC.

| Ewald von Kleist | REAL | Ewald von Kleist (1881-1954), later a WWII field marshal. In 1914 he was a cavalry captain (1st Life Hussars) who fought at Tannenberg. The novel makes him about 37-38 (he was really 33) and a Guard infantry battalion adjutant and later chief of staff to [[Gabriel Jagdfeld]]. |

The novel makes him [[Gabriel Jagdfeld]]'s operations officer and promotes him to Major by May 1915. |

| Erwin Rommel | REAL | Erwin Rommel (1891-1944). In 1914 he really was a second lieutenant in the 124th Württemberg Infantry Regiment, and he fought in Romania in 1917 with the Württemberg Mountain Battalion. The novel moves him to [[Gabriel Jagdfeld]]'s unit and puts him at Predeal in May 1915. |
| Heinz Guderian | REAL | Heinz Guderian (1888-1954), later the WWII panzer general. In 1914 he really did command a heavy wireless station of the 5th Cavalry Division, and his father Friedrich was a lieutenant general. The novel transfers him to [[Gabriel Jagdfeld]]'s unit. |

The novel makes him a Lieutenant General in a Spanish-style civil war in 1914, then a Western Front army group commander at Amiens and Paris. His 'Forward Marshal' nickname and his Balkan fame are kept. |

In 1914 he was a colonel and chief of staff of III Corps, and in 1915 chief of staff of Mackensen's 11th Army. | Erich Ludendorff | REAL | Erich Ludendorff (1865-1937). At Liège he really did take over the 14th Brigade after Wussow was killed (6 August 1914). He was then sent east to Tannenberg; the novel instead keeps him in the West at Charleroi, Arras and Amiens. |

## 1. Premise and protagonist background

**Setting.** The story is an alternate-history Europe ("Europa") on the eve of and during the First World War, with D&D-style magic and "magitech" (ch5-6, ch65-66, ch119). Nations are renamed analogues: the **Saxon Empire** (Germany, ruled by the House of Wettin from Dresden), the **Holy Britannian Empire** (Britain, under an ageless "Eternal Queen" Victoria), the **Gallic Republic** (France), the **Kingdom of Aragon** (Spain), the **United Kingdom / Grand Duchy of Flanders** (merged Netherlands, Belgium and Luxembourg), and others (ch1, ch5-6, ch71, ch146). The story opens in early 1914 (ch70 telegram dated 11 February 1914) during the Aragonese civil war. It moves through the outbreak of the Great War (August 1914, ch144-145), the Western Front campaigns of 1914, and the Balkan and Romanian campaigns of spring 1915 (ch409-473). An author's note (ch73) says the map combines Victoria 3 with an "English Peace" mod for Hearts of Iron 4. In that map Britain holds over 90% of North America and most of Africa, and "Saxony" is a pun on the Holy Roman Empire.

**Who he is.** The protagonist is a 21st-century military-academy cadet from "Blue Star" (modern Earth; hints point to China: "Red Scarf and Party Badge", ch23; "a certain mysterious Eastern nation", ch463; studied "under the red flag", ch441). He was playing a game ("line-climbing") on a holiday when he woke inside the body of a Saxon Second Lieutenant, also named [[Gabriel Jagdfeld]] (ch1, ch9). The host body is **[[Gabriel Jagdfeld]]**, a newly commissioned (ten days) graduate of the Lichterfelde Central Military Academy (ch1). He comes from an impoverished Junker military family (ch4, ch120, ch417). His parents are dead and his father drank away the family fortune (ch4, ch104). He was a notorious Dresden ballroom playboy who learned languages to seduce women (ch19, ch107, ch255). Born 26 December 1892 in Dresden (ch294). **[Conflict]** ch334 links his birthday to 25/26 December and says it was also his birthday "in another world".

**How he got here.** He wakes captured and beaten in a Britannian cellar in Seville, alongside Lieutenant General Mackensen (ch1-2). Mackensen's armored "Iron Cans" rescue them (ch3). **[Conflict]** The notes give his time in this world inconsistently. ch26 says "less than 24 hours" before ch26. ch62 says "48 hours" at that point.

**His edge.**
- **The System / "Cheat Code" / "golden finger"** (ch1 on). It was first called the "Intelligence Management System". It is a game-like interface with these parts:
  - a God's-eye map with live friendly and enemy unit cards and fog of war (ch1, ch10, ch19);
  - Information and Intelligence tabs that fill in as he collects information (5% in ch1, 23% in ch48, Balkans at 40% in ch132);
  - a Technology tab that unlocks and optimizes captured or remembered technology (first entry, the HEAT round, ch49; the Griffin IV cannon at 100%, ch98; a cuirass rune array, ch178-179; tires and suspension, ch312);
  - a Magic/Spells tab with "My Spellbook" (ch58, ch65-66).
  
  Enemy units appear only once a friendly unit sees them (ch10). Inside buildings it shows only enemy counts (ch94). It does not reveal invisible units (ch409). He keeps it secret and uses a handwritten notebook as cover (ch26).
- **Magic.** He became a spellcaster after killing a High-Level Mage Apprentice and being "contaminated with Spell Plague", which the system cleared, unlocking a Magic System (ch65). He levels up by "consuming" captured spell manuals and notebooks (ch65-66, ch75, ch233). He specializes in Abjuration (Arcane Ward, ch66) and grows from Caster Level 0 (ch65) to Level 9 / 5th circle (ch473). See the arc table in section 2.
- **Modern military knowledge.** Fire and maneuver, dispersal, urban warfare doctrine, trench earthworks, infiltration and stormtrooper tactics, airborne assault, creeping barrage, combined arms, OODA loop (ch12-23, ch43-44, ch80-90, ch152, ch255, ch358). He also knows real WWI/WWII history and recognizes historical figures (ch77, ch123, ch151, ch214, ch304-305).
- **Weapons and engineering.** He designed or inspired:
  - the shaped-charge anti-armor warhead, the "can opener" or "Jagdfeld Effect" (ch45-49, ch253);
  - the MG14 light MG and MP14 SMG, built with Helga Schmeisser (ch128-134);
  - the Type 56 chest rig (ch133), tracer rounds (ch319), spaced and composite armor (ch254, ch306-307), Tachankas (ch203, ch210), the two-seat Armored Knight (ch243-244), and half-tracks (ch313, ch381).
- **Super-soldier body** (from ch369). In the Paris underground lab Fritz Haber injected him with a Gallic "Second Generation Sentinel" modification potion. The system purged its addiction (ch370). It gives superhuman strength, speed, healing and perception, plus a huge appetite (ch371, ch386, ch410-411, ch443, ch457).
- **Languages.** He automatically understands Britannian English, Aragonese and others. The host body was a polyglot (ch1-2, ch19), and later he speaks Gaulish, Netherlandic and Czech (ch152, ch281, ch396).

## 7. Factions, nations and world-building

### Nations

  - Annexed Hanover after the 1866 Saxon-Austrian war (ch417). Won the 1870-71 "Saxo-Gaulish / Sago War", taking Alsace-Lorraine and a Mediterranean outlet (Treaty of Frankfurt; ch77, ch281).
  - Internal factions: Military and Court Junkers, Social Democrats ("Castle Peace" at mobilization, ch143-144) and the Imperial Mage Association (ch379).
  - Saxon mages number 392, 92% of them 3rd circle or lower (ch97). They are mostly rear-area researchers, so the Empire relies on magitech rather than individual power (ch76, ch412).
- **Holy Britannian Empire.** Britain analogue and "old world hegemon", ruled by the Eternal Queen Victoria from London (ch71).
  - Holds North America as a colony, with a "North American Colony Conscript Legion" (ch324). Benedict Arnold took Washington (ch71). **[REAL]** *Benedict Arnold, a real American Revolutionary War figure. In this world his side won.*
  - Leads in Armored Knight and mage technology. Has 642 mages, 83% of them Evocation (ch66).
  - Organizations: Highland (High-Level) Mage Corps/Order with a High Council of Six and a Chairman; the Order of the Garter (St. George Mk3 Knights); MI6 / Section VI; Military Intelligence Directorate.
- **Gallic Republic.** France analogue.
  - Rivals Britannia over Africa and holds Gibraltar and North Africa (ch77).
  - Mage order: the Eye of the Loire, with 525 mages (ch264). Holy Sepulchre Knights fly "Charlemagne II" Knights (ch252); Templar Knights are also mentioned.
  - The government flees to Bordeaux. Paris becomes a "Domain of the Dead" (ch289, ch444). Gaul asks for a ceasefire (ch371) and signs the Sedan armistice on 6 May 1915, ceding Alsace-Lorraine (ch444-445).
- **Kingdom of Aragon.** Spain analogue, in a civil war. The Britannian-backed Royal/Kingdom Army fights the Saxon-backed National Army and International Brigades (ch1, ch6). Contested over a vast Luminite / "brilliant crystal" ore vein (ch6, ch85).

The Romanovs were hanged. Factions: the West-Lucia (Western Rus/Russia) Provisional National Government, which is Saxony's ally; Kolchak's Britannian-backed "Supreme Autocratic Principality"; Denikin; and a Black Sea Republic (ch48, ch141, ch373).
- **Balkans.** Greater Serbia leads a coalition with Montenegro, Greece, Romania and the Ottomans (after a Britannian "Project Beacon" coup). Bulgaria is on the Saxon side (ch419-420).

- **Kalmar Union.** North Sea power holding Sri Lanka and Indonesia (ch387, ch392).
- **East and Southeast Asia.** Behind a "Myththal" barrier since 1620 (ch386-387).

### Magic system
- **Rules.** D&D-like. Spells are ranked by Circle or Tier from 1 to 9, with cantrips, daily spell slots, preparation, schools, material components, scrolls, spell journals and magic items (ch24, ch65-66, ch233, ch389). Mages cannot cast in armor (ch317).
- **Origin of mana.** Magic crystal ore is an extraterrestrial meteorite deposit that leaks free mana (ch5, ch119, ch376, ch411).
  - **[Conflict / naming drift]** Early chapters split Britannian "Aetherium Crystals", which power magitech, from Saxon "Luminite Ore", which fuels diesel-type engines (ch5-6). Later chapters use "Magic Crystal", "brilliant crystal" and "Radiant/Glory Crystal" (ch70, ch85, ch112, ch246).
- **Spell Plague.** 243 years ago a plague from the Magic Crystal Mines punished multi-school mages, so only specialists rise above the 3rd circle (ch65-66).
- **Mana Capacity Ratio.** Saxon mages manage about 6x, Britannian 12x and Highland core mages 15-20x. Free mana is rising (ch411).
- **Arcane devices** need a mage or someone with "Magic/Mana Affinity" modification, as Armored Knight pilots and the [[The General's Guard]] have. Without a mage, Magic Crystal Cannons work at about a tenth of their power (ch117, ch119, ch235, ch414).
- **Scale.** 9th-circle spells such as Meteor Swarm act as strategic deterrents (ch351). Tier-8 Weather Control appears (ch350).
- **Necromancy and the divine.** Necromancy is despised. Holy oil, fire and blessed weapons work against undead, and Papal "divine arts" exist (ch275-283). Priests can cast Silence (ch139).
- **[[Gabriel Jagdfeld]]'s view.** He calls magitech a "sunset industry" because the ore is depleting (ch386).
