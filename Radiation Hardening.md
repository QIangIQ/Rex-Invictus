# [[Radiation Hardening]]

## Definition

(Not to be confused with [[Divine Hardening]])

**[[Radiation Hardening]]** is the process of designing and modifying [[electronic components]] and [[circuits]] to resist damage or malfunction caused by [[ionizing radiation]].

This includes exposure to:

- [[Particle Radiation]]
- [[High-Energy Electromagnetic Radiation]]

---

## Operational Environments

Radiation hardening is critical in environments with elevated radiation exposure, including:

- [[Outer Space]] (especially beyond [[Low Earth Orbit]])
- [[Nuclear Reactors]]
- [[Particle Accelerators]]
- [[Nuclear Accidents]]
- [[Nuclear Warfare]]

---

## Component Vulnerability

Most [[Semiconductor Devices]] are inherently susceptible to radiation-induced degradation.

As a result:

- [[Radiation-Hardened Components]] are derived from standard designs
- Modified through:
    - Structural design changes
    - Manufacturing process adjustments
- Goal:
    - Reduce susceptibility to radiation damage
    - Maintain operational integrity under exposure

---

## Development Constraints

Radiation-hardened systems face several limitations:

- Low demand compared to commercial electronics
- Extensive [[Testing Requirements]]
- Increased [[Design Complexity]]

### Resulting Effects

- Technology often lags behind cutting-edge commercial chips
- Significantly higher production cost

---

## Testing Methodologies

[[Radiation-Hardened Systems]] are validated through specialized testing protocols:

### [[Total Ionizing Dose (TID)]]

- Measures cumulative radiation damage over time

### [[Enhanced Low Dose Rate Effects (ELDRS)]]

- Evaluates degradation under prolonged low-dose exposure

### [[Displacement Damage]]

- Caused by:
    - [[Neutron Radiation]]
    - [[Proton Radiation]]
- Results in lattice defects within materials

### [[Single Event Effects (SEE)]]

- Caused by individual high-energy particles
- Includes:
    - Bit flips
    - Latch-up
    - Transient faults

---

## Summary

[[Radiation Hardening]] represents a trade-off between:

- Performance
- Cost
- Reliability under extreme conditions

It is a foundational requirement for any system operating in:

- [[High-Radiation Environments]]
- [[Spaceborne Systems]]
- [[Nuclear Infrastructure]]
