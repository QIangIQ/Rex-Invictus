**PSF Heavy Security** units constitute the Planetary Security Forces' primary heavy-duty security and public-order formation. They are deployed when ordinary security personnel are insufficient to safely control a situation, particularly where there is a credible threat of organized violence, mass civil disorder, or attacks against protected personnel and facilities.

Heavy Security personnel are commonly assigned to **riot control, prisoner pacification, high-profile escorts, military-security duties, and the protection of strategically important locations**. Unlike PSF Medium Security, which is primarily concerned with guarding important civilian and governmental infrastructure, Heavy Security is equipped and trained to actively intervene when a situation has already escalated or is expected to become violent.

### Primary Duties

- Riot and civil-disturbance control
- Suppression of violent demonstrations and organized unrest
- Prison and detention-facility security
- Pacification of violent or resistant prisoners
- High-profile civilian and governmental escorts
- Protection of dignitaries and senior officials
- Military installation and military personnel security
- Security of critical infrastructure during periods of unrest
- Reinforcement of Medium Security units during major incidents
- Establishment of security cordons around dangerous areas
- Extraction of threatened personnel from hostile crowds
- Response to heavily armed criminal or insurgent groups when conventional PSF units are inadequate

### Equipment

Heavy Security personnel wear substantially more protective equipment than Medium Security personnel, while retaining sufficient mobility to operate in dense urban environments.

Their standard armor typically consists of a **full-body protective system**, incorporating reinforced ballistic plates, impact-resistant components, armored shoulder and limb protection, a ballistic helmet, and a protective tactical vest. Additional riot-control equipment can be attached depending on deployment requirements.

Common equipment includes:

- Heavy ballistic vest with front, rear, and side protection
- Reinforced ballistic plates
- Armored shoulder, elbow, knee, and shin protection
- Ballistic helmet
- Full-face ballistic visor or riot shield
- Respiratory protection for chemical agents and smoke
- Tactical gloves
- Reinforced boots
- Communications headset and encrypted PSF radio
- Body camera and incident recorder
- Identification transponder
- Flashlights and helmet-mounted illumination
- Restraint devices
- Medical trauma kit
- Less-lethal ammunition and equipment
- Riot-control shields
- Portable barrier systems
- Chemical-agent detection equipment

For particularly dangerous deployments, Heavy Security personnel may be issued heavier protective systems capable of resisting rifle fire and fragmentation. These configurations are generally reserved for military-security assignments, major riots, or operations involving known armed resistance.

### Arms

Heavy Security units possess considerably greater firepower than lower PSF formations.

Their standard firearm is generally a **service rifle or carbine**, allowing them to engage threats at substantially greater distances than Orderlies or Light Security personnel. Weapons are typically equipped with modular optics, weapon lights, and less-lethal ammunition capabilities where appropriate.

Typical armament includes:

- Service rifle or carbine
- Service pistol
- Stun gun or electroshock weapon
- Less-lethal launcher
- Conducted-energy weapons
- Riot-control shotgun
- Flashbang and distraction devices
- Smoke grenades
- Chemical irritant projectors
- Breaching equipment

Certain Heavy Security teams may receive specialized weapons depending on their assignment. Prison-security formations, for example, may emphasize less-lethal weapons and restraint equipment, while military guards may carry weapons optimized for conventional firefights.

Heavy Security personnel are generally expected to exercise greater restraint than military infantry when operating among civilians. Their purpose is **security and controlled force**, rather than battlefield destruction.

### Riot-Control Configuration

The most recognizable Heavy Security configuration is their riot-control loadout.

Personnel assigned to major civil disturbances may replace portions of their conventional armor with specialized riot equipment, including large ballistic/riot shields, reinforced helmets, face protection, respiratory equipment, and less-lethal weapons.

A Heavy Security formation can establish a physical line between opposing groups, advance methodically through a hostile crowd, protect evacuation routes, or reinforce weaker PSF formations.

Their doctrine emphasizes **containment before dispersal**. Whenever practical, Heavy Security attempts to isolate the disturbance, prevent additional civilians from entering the affected area, establish evacuation corridors, and separate violent participants from the wider population.

### Prisoner Pacification

Heavy Security units are frequently used when dealing with prisoners who present a significant physical threat.

They provide security during:

- Prisoner transfers
- Mass prisoner disturbances
- Detention-facility riots
- High-risk arrests
- Prisoner extraction
- Restraint of exceptionally dangerous detainees

Their armor makes them considerably more resistant to improvised weapons and physical attacks, while their less-lethal equipment allows them to subdue resistant prisoners without immediately resorting to lethal force.

### Military Security

Heavy Security also serves as the PSF's principal **military-security formation**.

Although they are not regular soldiers, their training and equipment allow them to guard military installations, arsenals, command facilities, transport hubs, and military personnel operating within civilian jurisdictions.

During periods of martial emergency or planetary mobilization, Heavy Security units may be placed alongside military formations to provide local security and law-enforcement capabilities while the military concentrates on external threats.

### High-Profile Escort

Heavy Security personnel provide the protective cordon for individuals whose presence creates an elevated security risk.

Typical protected persons include:

- Government ministers
- Senior PSF commanders
- Military officers
- Diplomats
- High-ranking officials
- Important scientific personnel
- Members of powerful political or corporate institutions
- Individuals targeted by organized criminal or insurgent groups

Unlike specialized protective details, Heavy Security's primary responsibility is usually **area security** rather than close personal protection. They establish the outer security perimeter, control crowds, secure transportation routes, and respond to threats approaching the protected individual.
