[[Pulse Compression]] is a defining [[Technical Characteristic]] of certain [[Radar Systems]] in which a transmitted [[Radar Pulse]] is encoded using a structured [[Modulation Scheme]]—such as [[Linear Frequency Modulation (LFM)]], [[Phase Modulation (PM)]], or [[Binary Phase Shift Keying (BPSK)]]—and subsequently processed through a [[Matched Filter]] or [[Correlation Receiver]] to produce a compressed output pulse with reduced effective [[Pulse Duration]], thereby improving [[Range Resolution]] while preserving long-pulse detection performance.

Within [[Electronic Intelligence (ELINT)]], [[Pulse Compression]] is identified through [[Interception]] and detailed [[Signal Analysis]] of non-communication emissions from [[Radar Systems]].

---

## Detection of [[Pulse Compression]] in [[Electronic Intelligence (ELINT)]]

### During [[Interception]]

Sensors such as [[Electronic Support Measures (ESM)]] or [[Radar Warning Receiver (RWR)]] capture the transmitted waveform.

---

### During [[Signal Analysis]]

[[Pulse Compression]] is revealed through:

- [[Frequency Analysis]] → Detection of continuous [[Frequency]] sweep (indicative of [[Linear Frequency Modulation (LFM)]])
    
- [[Time Domain Analysis]] → Measurement of extended [[Pulse Duration]]
    
- [[Spectral Analysis]] → Measurement of wide [[Bandwidth]]
    
- [[Pulse Analysis]] → Determination of internal modulation structure
    

These parameters collectively form part of the emitter’s [[Signal Fingerprinting]] profile.

---

## Role in [[Emitter Identification]]

Because [[Pulse Compression]] waveforms possess distinctive characteristics:

- Specific [[Bandwidth]]
    
- Specific [[Chirp Rate]]
    
- Specific phase coding patterns
    
- Specific [[Compression Ratio]]
    

They enable:

- [[Emitter Identification]]
    
- Classification of [[Radar System]] type
    
- Determination of [[Operational Capabilities]]
    

These characteristics are stored in [[Emitter Libraries]] and contribute to the construction of the [[Electronic Order of Battle (EOB)]].

---

## Intelligence Value of [[Pulse Compression]]

From an [[Electronic Intelligence (ELINT)]] perspective, detection of [[Pulse Compression]] implies:

- Advanced signal processing capability
    
- Enhanced [[Range Resolution]]
    
- Increased resistance to [[Electronic Countermeasures (ECM)]]
    
- Modernized [[Radar System]] architecture
    

Thus, [[Pulse Compression]] is not merely a signal processing method, but an exploitable indicator of a radar’s [[Technical Characteristics]] and [[Operational Capabilities]].

## Doctrinal ELINT-Aligned Definition

[[Pulse Compression]] is a structured [[Radar Signal Processing Technique]] observable through [[Electronic Intelligence (ELINT)]] that involves the transmission of a long, internally modulated [[Radar Pulse]]—typically using [[Linear Frequency Modulation (LFM)]] or [[Phase Modulation (PM)]]—followed by receiver-side matched processing to achieve reduced effective [[Pulse Duration]]. Its measurable parameters, including [[Bandwidth]], [[Chirp Rate]], and internal coding structure, constitute identifiable [[Technical Characteristics]] used in [[Signal Fingerprinting]], [[Emitter Identification]], and assessment of [[Radar System]] [[Operational Capabilities]].
